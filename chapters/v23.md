# Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa

PART XXIII - How the Sacred Alphabet Becomes Bondage

*Abhinavagupta takes the very same phonemes that express Bhairava's supreme freedom and explains how they can become the instruments of ignorance. Sāṃkhya arrives carrying fifty classifications, the human mind turns out to be a magnificent factory for mistaking things, Kṣemarāja's beloved Spanda tradition reveals why thought is inseparable from the power of speech, and the Pūrvaśāstra describes the Supreme Lord producing ordinary audible letters from their divine prototypes.*

*The alphabet hasn't changed its ultimate nature. But the poor finite knower has forgotten what the alphabet actually is.*

## Previously, in the cosmic madhouse…

Twenty-two installments.

We've followed the Sanskrit alphabet from the unsurpassable **अ - a**, through the vowel-powers, the consonantal matrices, the thirty-six *tattvas*, Mātṛkā, the mysteries of Visarga, the divine **अहम् - aham**, and finally the wonderful doctrine of **Spanda**.

Last time, Somānanda entered and explained how Mātṛkā could be simultaneously transformed and untransformed.

Abhinavagupta then subjected movement itself to philosophical interrogation.

If consciousness moves into another independent substance, it isn't nondual.

If nothing whatsoever manifests, why describe it as dynamic?

The answer was Spanda: the intrinsic upsurge of awareness, a movement that doesn't require the Supreme to relocate into somebody else's metaphysical property.

Then came the Goddess's two apparently contradictory descriptions.

**अमूला - amūlā:** Rootless, yet rooted in the primordial vowel *a*.

**अज्ञेया - ajñeyā:** Not knowable as an independently external object, yet recognizable as the very ground of knowing.

And finally, the whole phonemic creation was declared to be the source of mantras, *vidyās*, activities and attainments.

But now we reach the uncomfortable question.

If the phonemes are divine powers, why don't they automatically liberate everybody?

Why can speech conceal rather than reveal?

Why does someone hear the word "I" and immediately identify with a finite body rather than recognize universal *Aham*?

Why does the alphabet become entangled with ignorance?

Abhinavagupta's answer is extraordinary.

The same phonemes, previously understood as pure mantra-powers, enter the manifestation of differentiated cognitive states.

Those states can conceal their own conscious ground.

The power of speech becomes involved in the finite knower's construction of reality.

And through that process, the letters participate in bondage.

**The Goddess doesn't have to cease being consciousness for consciousness to appear limited.**

That's the problem of this installment.

Right.

The divine alphabet has arrived in the domain of finite cognition.

Welcome to the Sāṃkhya department.


## 1. त एव शुद्धमन्त्ररूपा वर्णाः - The very same letters

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN PROSE · KASHMIR SERIES P. 209

**त एव शुद्धमन्त्ररूपा वर्णाः प्रथमं…**

*Ta eva śuddhamantrarūpā varṇāḥ prathamaṃ…*

"Those very phonemes, whose nature is pure mantra, first…"

Stop there.

Because the very first two words matter enormously.

**त एव - ta eva.**

Those very ones.

Not different letters.

Not a completely separate alphabet invented by Māyā.

Not some evil collection of counterfeit phonemes secretly operating outside Bhairava's jurisdiction.

**The same letters.**

The same powers that previously expressed the uncontracted fullness of consciousness.

Now entering a different mode of manifestation.

Let's dismantle the Sanskrit.

### Ta - ते

Those.

Masculine nominative plural of the demonstrative pronoun *tad*.

**ते - te**

Those.

### Eva - एव

Precisely, indeed, those very ones.

An emphatic particle.

Thus:

**त एव - ta eva**

Those themselves.

This is a statement of continuity.

And that continuity is central to the argument.

### Śuddha - शुद्ध

Pure, unmixed, undefiled.

Related to √**शुध् - śudh**, to become pure.

Now *śuddha* here doesn't mean the written letters have recently been washed.

It identifies their pure, non-Māyic mode of manifestation.

### Mantra - मन्त्र

Sacred formula, mantric power.

In this passage, the word is being used within the doctrine of the divine phonemic energies, not merely for a publicly recited sequence of syllables.

### Rūpa - रूप

Nature, form.

The compound:

**शुद्धमन्त्ररूप - śuddhamantrarūpa**

Having the nature of pure mantra.

Masculine nominative plural:

**शुद्धमन्त्ररूपाः - śuddhamantrarūpāḥ**.

Now in connected Sanskrit:

**शुद्धमन्त्ररूपा वर्णाः - śuddhamantrarūpā varṇāḥ**.

The final visarga of *-rūpāḥ* is lost before the following voiced consonant *v* under the relevant sandhi.

### Varṇa - वर्ण

Phoneme, letter, sound-category.

Masculine nominative plural:

**वर्णाः - varṇāḥ**.

Now the noun can mean colour or social class in other Sanskrit contexts.

But here it means phonemes.

The context is the divine Mātṛkā and her differentiated sound-powers.

### Prathamam - प्रथमम्

First, initially.

An adverbial form.

Related to:

**प्रथम - prathama**

First.

Now here's the crucial philosophical point.

Abhinavagupta hasn't said that pure mantras become ontologically unrelated to their source.

He's describing how the same phonemic powers enter a mode of cognition in which their nature becomes concealed.

And that's why the next verb matters.

**Āvṛṇvate.**

They cover.

They conceal.

They veil their own nature.

Now, if we misunderstand that, we'll misunderstand the whole passage.

Because it's very tempting to imagine an evil second force attacking the divine letters from outside.

But Trika doesn't need an independent second Absolute to explain bondage.

It explains limited manifestation through Śakti's own power.

And this is where *Māyā* becomes relevant.

Not as an alien substance stronger than Bhairava.

But as a differentiating mode within the overall metaphysics of conscious freedom.


## 2. भावसृष्टि - Creation first takes the form of cognitive dispositions

Now we reach the central compound.

The Kashmir Series has:

**पञ्चविधविपर्ययशक्त्यादिरूपप्रत्ययात्मकभावसृष्टितामेत्य।**

The independent GRETIL witness reads:

*pañcavidhaviparyayāśaktyādirūpapratyayātmakabhāvasṛṣṭitām etya.*

The difference is significant: GRETIL makes **अशक्ति - aśakti**, incapacity, explicit after *viparyaya*, whereas the Kashmir Series's continuous spelling reads *viparyayaśaktyādi* at the corresponding position. The intended Sāṃkhya grouping of error, incapacity, satisfaction and accomplishment is confirmed by the immediately following citation. For our philological analysis, I'll use the GRETIL segmentation while retaining the Kashmir Series reading as a variant. ; \]

Here's the phrase in Devanāgarī following the GRETIL reading:

**पञ्चविधविपर्ययाशक्त्यादिरूपप्रत्ययात्मकभावसृष्टितामेत्य।**

*Pañcavidhaviparyayāśaktyādirūpapratyayātmakabhāvasṛṣṭitām etya.*

"Having entered the condition of a creation of cognitive dispositions, consisting of determinate cognitions such as fivefold error, incapacity and the other categories…"

Now that is a classic Abhinavagupta compound.

The man has apparently decided that spaces between words are an optional luxury.

Let's take it apart.

### Pañcavidha - पञ्चविध

**पञ्च - pañca**

Five.

**विध - vidha**

Kind, type, manner.

Thus:

**पञ्चविध - pañcavidha**

Fivefold, of five kinds.

Now don't confuse:

**विध - vidha**, kind.

With:

**विद्या - vidyā**, knowledge.

The words have different functions.

### Viparyaya - विपर्यय

Error, reversal, mistaken cognition.

From the family of:

**वि - vi**

Differently, apart.

**परि - pari**

Around, completely.

And verbal formations related to reversal or inversion.

Thus:

**विपर्यय - viparyaya**

Error or misapprehension.

Now this doesn't merely mean knowing nothing.

It can mean apprehending something incorrectly.

Recognizing an impermanent condition as permanent.

Taking what isn't the Self to be the Self.

Or attributing reality to an inappropriate object.

The exact Sāṃkhya classes will follow.

### Aśakti - अशक्ति

Incapacity, inability, powerlessness.

**अ - a-**

Negative prefix.

**शक्ति - śakti**

Power.

Thus:

**अशक्ति - aśakti**

Incapacity.

Now this is a particularly striking word in a Śākta context.

We've spent twenty-two installments celebrating **Śakti** as the universal divine power.

And now the Sāṃkhya classification introduces **aśakti**, the condition of incapacity.

The same conscious ground can appear through a finite system with limited capacities.

But limited capacity is not the intrinsic nature of the supreme Śakti.

That's the philosophical distinction.

### Ādi - आदि

And so forth, beginning with.

In the compound, the remaining categories include *tuṣṭi* and *siddhi*.

### Pratyaya - प्रत्यय

Cognition, conception, determinate mental state.

This word has several technical meanings in Sanskrit grammar and philosophy.

In the present context, **pratyaya** concerns a cognitive disposition or formation.

Not a grammatical suffix.

### Ātmaka - आत्मक

Consisting of, having the nature of.

Thus:

**प्रत्ययात्मक - pratyayātmaka**

Consisting of determinate cognitive states.

### Bhāva - भाव

Being, state, disposition, mental condition.

From √**भू - bhū**, to be or become.

### Sṛṣṭi - सृष्टि

Creation, manifestation.

From √**सृज् - sṛj**, to emit or bring forth.

Thus:

**भावसृष्टि - bhāvasṛṣṭi**

Creation of states or dispositions.

And:

**भावसृष्टिता - bhāvasṛṣṭitā**

The condition of being a creation of cognitive dispositions.

Accusative singular:

**भावसृष्टिताम् - bhāvasṛṣṭitām**.

### Etya - एत्य

Having reached, entered or attained.

From √**इ - i**, to go, with the appropriate absolutive formation.

Thus:

**एत्य - etya**

Having entered.

Now the grammar becomes clear.

The divine phonemes enter the condition of **bhāvasṛṣṭi**, the manifestation of determinate cognitive dispositions.

And through that condition, their own pure nature is concealed.

### The extraordinary psychological implication

Imagine hearing a word.

You recognize the sound.

It evokes a meaning.

The meaning activates associations.

Those associations become expectations.

The expectations shape your next thought.

Suddenly you're operating within a highly differentiated conceptual world.

Now none of that establishes that conceptual cognition is automatically false.

Much ordinary cognition is valid and useful.

But the philosophical possibility arises that **the conceptual structures through which the world is apprehended can also obscure recognition of their conscious ground**.

And that's what Abhinavagupta is explaining.

The divine phonemic power doesn't cease to exist.

Its pure nature is concealed within a contracted mode of cognition.

### The cosmic bureaucracy of mistaken understanding

Imagine Mātṛkā entering the world of ordinary minds.

The first department issues categories.

"Name."

"Object."

"Self."

"Other."

"Pleasure."

"Pain."

"Success."

"Failure."

Then the second department begins attaching opinions.

"This belongs to me."

"I am this body."

"I must acquire that."

"I cannot tolerate this."

Within minutes, the infinite fullness of consciousness has become a finite personality arguing with a customer-service chatbot.

What an absolute catastrophe.

But the joke illustrates the problem.

The phonemes enable meaningful differentiation.

That differentiation can become the medium of limiting identification.

And therefore the alphabet participates in bondage.

Not because sound is intrinsically evil.

But because **the same expressive power can operate through different modes of recognition and nonrecognition**.

And now the Sanskrit gives the decisive verb.


## 3. स्वरूपमावृण्वते - The letters cover their own nature

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S PROSE · SENTENCE RESUMING ON KASHMIR SERIES P. 209

**स्वरूपमावृण्वते।**

*Svarūpam āvṛṇvate.*

"They conceal their own nature."

That's the grammatical climax of the sentence.

And it's a beautifully direct statement.

Now let's learn the terms.

### Svarūpa - स्वरूप

**स्व - sva**

Own.

**रूप - rūpa**

Nature, form.

Thus:

**स्वरूप - svarūpa**

One's own intrinsic nature.

Accusative singular:

**स्वरूपम् - svarūpam**.

### Āvṛṇvate - आवृण्वते

They cover, conceal, veil.

From:

**आ - ā**

Over, toward.

**√वृ - vṛ**

To cover, envelop or conceal, in the relevant verbal root.

Third-person plural middle voice:

**आवृण्वते - āvṛṇvate**.

Now notice the conjunct and vowel:

**वृ - vṛ**.

The syllabic **ऋ - ṛ** is a genuine vowel.

And in:

**वृण्वते - vṛṇvate**

The sequence contains the nasal **ण् - ṇ** and the conjunct **न्व - nva**.

It's an excellent word for practising Devanāgarī.

### What exactly gets concealed?

Not necessarily the ordinary acoustic sound.

You can still hear the syllable.

You can still pronounce the word.

You can still study its grammar.

What is concealed is its **pure mantric nature as an expression of universal conscious freedom**.

The sound becomes apprehended within limited, differentiated cognition.

And the finite knower may no longer recognize the ground.

### The distinction between concealment and destruction

This matters enormously.

If the pure nature were literally destroyed, how could recognition ever occur?

And how could the same phonemic powers still belong to Śiva?

But concealment is different from annihilation.

Something can be present without being recognized for what it is.

Now, within Trika, this is tied to the metaphysics of self-contraction.

Consciousness can manifest a limited mode of apprehension without ceasing to be consciousness.

And this is why *āvṛṇvate* is such a powerful verb.

**The powers cover their own nature.**

They don't need to be attacked by an independently existing evil substance.

And, crucially, the process is not merely about incorrect pronunciation.

A perfectly pronounced mantra can remain involved in ordinary conceptual limitation if the ground of consciousness is not recognized.

Conversely, the doctrine of Mātṛkā is not simply a technical exercise in articulatory precision.

It concerns the very relationship between speech and self-awareness.

Now comes the great classification.

Fifty cognitive dispositions.

Five errors.

Twenty-eight incapacities.

Nine satisfactions.

Eight attainments.

And their relationship with the fiftyfold alphabet.

The entire Sāṃkhya system has arrived carrying a numerical chart.

Bhairava, apparently, has requested an audit.

Immediately after Abhinavagupta's claim, the Kashmir Series inserts a long explanation in square brackets.

Its first major quotation is **Sāṃkhyakārikā 46**.

**एष प्रत्ययसर्गो विपर्ययाशक्तितुष्टिसिद्ध्याख्यः।**

**गुणवैषम्यविमर्दात्तस्य च भेदास्तु पञ्चाशत्॥**

## 50

Now this is an extraordinarily important distinction.

There are **four principal categories**, comprising **fifty subdivisions**.

Sometimes a quick summary can make it sound as though Sāṃkhya directly names fifty different errors.

It doesn't.

The fifty comprise errors, incapacities, satisfactions and accomplishments.

And we need to preserve that structure.

### Pratyayasarga - प्रत्ययसर्ग

**प्रत्यय - pratyaya**

Cognitive disposition, determinate condition of understanding.

**सर्ग - sarga**

Creation, emanation, manifestation.

Thus:

**प्रत्ययसर्ग - pratyayasarga**

Creation of cognitive dispositions.

Now this is closely related to Abhinavagupta's:

**भावसृष्टि - bhāvasṛṣṭi**.

Creation of states or dispositions.

### Viparyayāśakti - विपर्ययाशक्ति

The conjunction of:

**विपर्यय - viparyaya**

Error.

**अशक्ति - aśakti**

Incapacity.

The sandhi:

*viparyaya + aśakti → viparyayāśakti.*

Notice the long **आ - ā** at the junction.

This is exactly why the segmentation matters in our opening passage.

### Tuṣṭi - तुष्टि

Satisfaction, contentment, complacency.

Related to √**तुष् - tuṣ**, to be satisfied or pleased.

Now the philosophical point isn't that all satisfaction is bad.

Within the Sāṃkhya classification, *tuṣṭi* can denote a state of settling into insufficient understanding, or contentment that halts further progress toward discriminative knowledge.

The precise technical meaning is broader than ordinary happiness.

### Siddhi - सिद्धि

Attainment, accomplishment, success.

Related to √**सिध् - sidh**, to succeed or accomplish.

Now here's a crucial qualification.

The eight Sāṃkhya *siddhis* in this classification are **not simply eight supernatural powers**.

They include reasoning, instruction, study, overcoming suffering and obtaining helpful conditions for understanding.

So when we reach the list, don't suddenly imagine flying, levitating and producing gold from your ears.

The technical classification is different.

### Guṇa - गुण

Constituent quality.

In classical Sāṃkhya, the three guṇas are:

**सत्त्व - sattva**

Lucidity, manifestation or clarity.

**रजस् - rajas**

Activity, stimulation.

**तमस् - tamas**

Inertia, obstruction.

Now Sāṃkhya gives these guṇas a specific ontological role in the transformation of *Prakṛti*.

Abhinavagupta borrows the classification into a Śaiva argument about the divine phonemic powers.

**Borrowing a Sāṃkhya taxonomy does not mean accepting every metaphysical premise of classical Sāṃkhya unchanged.**

That's important.

In classical Sāṃkhya, *Prakṛti* and *Puruṣa* are distinct principles.

In Abhinavagupta's Trika, all manifestation is ultimately grounded in the freedom of consciousness.

The same classification can therefore serve a different overarching metaphysics.

### Vaiṣamya - वैषम्य

Inequality, disequilibrium, imbalance.

From:

**विषम - viṣama**

Unequal, uneven.

The abstract noun:

**वैषम्य - vaiṣamya**.

### Vimarda - विमर्द

Pressure, collision, mutual interaction, friction.

Related to *vi-√mṛd*, to press or crush.

Now the compound:

**गुणवैषम्यविमर्द - guṇavaiṣamyavimarda**

The interaction or conflict arising from the guṇas' disequilibrium.

The ablative:

**गुणवैषम्यविमर्दात् - guṇavaiṣamyavimardāt**.

Thus, the fiftyfold cognitive manifestation arises through differentiation associated with the guṇas.

### The cosmic cognitive accounting department

Imagine the Sāṃkhya philosophers arriving at Abhinavagupta's office.

"We have fifty classifications."

"Excellent."

"Five errors."

"Fine."

"Twenty-eight incapacities."

"Very thorough."

"Nine satisfactions."

"Good."

"Eight accomplishments."

"Wonderful."

"And we can explain them through the guṇas."

Abhinavagupta smiles.

"Excellent classification. Now I shall explain how the whole system arises within the self-concealing manifestation of divine Mātṛkā."

The Sāṃkhya philosophers look at one another.

They were expecting a polite quotation.

Their entire ontology has just been incorporated into a Tantric theory of language.

### Why fifty matters

Now the numerical parallel is striking.

The sacred Mātṛkā is traditionally enumerated as fifty phonemic powers in this arrangement.

The Sāṃkhya *pratyayasarga* is divided into fifty states.

Abhinavagupta brings these into relation.

But the connection is a **doctrinal and interpretive correspondence**.

It isn't an empirical discovery that every Sanskrit phoneme activates exactly one neurological defect or cognitive function.

The fiftyfold arrangement gives a systematic way to discuss how the pure phonemic powers enter differentiated cognition.

And this is why the next classification matters.

We must distinguish:

**Bhāvasṛṣṭi** - creation of cognitive dispositions.

From:

**Bhūtasṛṣṭi** - creation of embodied or elemental existents.

The editor explains both.

And the distinction is subtle.


## 5. भावसृष्टि and भूतसृष्टि - The world of dispositions and the world of beings

The bracketed explanation begins:

**द्विधा हि सृष्टिः - भावसृष्टिर्भूतसृष्टिश्च।**

*Dvidhā hi sṛṣṭiḥ - bhāvasṛṣṭir bhūtasṛṣṭiś ca.*

"Creation is twofold: the manifestation of dispositions and the manifestation of beings or elements."

Now these are two different classificatory levels.

They shouldn't be confused.

### Dvidhā - द्विधा

In two ways, twofold.

From:

**द्वि - dvi**

Two.

With the adverbial formation:

**-धा - -dhā**.

Thus:

**द्विधा - dvidhā**

Twofold.

### Bhāvasṛṣṭi - भावसृष्टि

We know this.

**भाव - bhāva**

State, disposition.

**सृष्टि - sṛṣṭi**

Manifestation.

Thus:

**भावसृष्टि - bhāvasṛṣṭi**

Creation of cognitive or dispositional states.

### Bhūtasṛṣṭi - भूतसृष्टि

Now the important distinction.

**भूत - bhūta**

Being, existent, element, that which has become.

From √**भू - bhū**, to be or become.

Thus:

**भूतसृष्टि - bhūtasṛṣṭi**

Creation of beings or elemental existents.

Now the editor associates the former with *pratyaya*, cognitive formation, and the latter with a more object-oriented or elemental creation.

### The difference between a disposition and an embodied being

Consider fear.

Fear is a psychological condition.

Now consider a dog.

The dog is an embodied being.

These are not the same category.

A dog may experience fear.

A human may experience fear.

But the disposition and the bearer of the disposition can be distinguished.

Similarly, the Sāṃkhya system differentiates mental dispositions from the classification of embodied existents.

The two are related within its cosmological explanation.

But they're not identical.

### The connection to Sāṃkhyakārikā 52

The broader Sāṃkhya text itself describes a relationship between dispositions and the embodied or subtle-body processes through which they become manifest.

It doesn't simply claim that a mental state and a physical organism are interchangeable.

The distinction belongs to its account of manifestation.

And the Kashmir Series editor uses that framework to clarify Abhinavagupta's short phrase.

### Why Abhinavagupta cares

Now recall the main claim:

The pure mantra-powers enter **bhāvasṛṣṭi**.

That's the immediate issue.

Not merely the existence of mountains, trees and animals.

The emergence of determinate cognitive dispositions.

Because the philosophical problem is bondage through misrecognition.

A person can encounter the world through conceptual structures that obscure the conscious ground.

And the expressive power of sound is involved in those structures.

This is why the commentator moves first through *pratyayasarga*, not simply through physical cosmology.

### The difference between ontology and epistemology

Now this is a central distinction worth retaining.

**Ontology:** What kinds of entities or manifestations are there?

**Epistemology:** How are they apprehended, misapprehended or known?

The Sāṃkhya distinction helps separate the cognitive dispositions from the classes of beings.

Abhinavagupta then relates both to his broader metaphysics of conscious manifestation.

But the immediate mechanism of bondage concerns the **pratyayas**.

The determinate cognitions.

Their capacity to conceal their own conscious source.

And that is why the next quotation describes the elemental creation.

Eight divine classes.

Five subhuman classes.

One human class.

Fourteen in all.

A classification that would be extremely questionable as modern zoology.

But it's historically important as part of the Sāṃkhya cosmological framework.

Let's read it correctly.


## 6. The fourteen classes of embodied creation - Sāṃkhya's cosmic census

**अष्टविकल्पो दैवस्तैर्यग्योनिश्च पञ्चधा भवति।**

**मानुष्यश्चैकविधः समासतो भौतिकः सर्गः॥**

*Aṣṭavikalpo daivas tairyagyoniś ca pañcadhā bhavati.*

*Mānuṣyaś caikavidhaḥ samāsato bhautikaḥ sargaḥ.*

"The divine order is eightfold, subhuman life is fivefold, and the human order is onefold: such, in summary, is the elemental creation."

The quotation is genuinely from Sāṃkhyakārikā 53. The exact transmission differs slightly in some witnesses, including *mānuṣya/mānuṣaka* and the treatment of the final phrase. ; \]

Now the numerical structure is straightforward.

| Class | Number of types |
|--------|--------|
| Divine or celestial | 8 |
| Subhuman, including animals and stationary beings | 5 |
| Human | 1 |
| **Total** | **14** |

### Aṣṭa - अष्ट

Eight.

### Vikalpa - विकल्प

Difference, alternative, category.

Here:

**अष्टविकल्प - aṣṭavikalpa**

Having eight divisions.

### Daiva - दैव

Divine, celestial.

Related to:

**देव - deva**

God, deity.

The adjective:

**दैव - daiva**.

### Tiryagyoni - तिर्यग्योनि

Now this is a useful compound.

**तिर्यक् - tiryak**

Across, transverse, nonhuman in traditional classifications.

**योनि - yoni**

Birth-source, species-class, generative category.

Thus:

**तिर्यग्योनि - tiryagyoni**

Subhuman birth-class, in this traditional cosmological taxonomy.

Now the phrase is not a modern biological category.

And it's not an ethical verdict on the value of animals.

It's a historical classification of embodied existence.

### Pañcadhā - पञ्चधा

In five ways.

**पञ्च - pañca**

Five.

**-धा - -dhā**

Adverbial suffix.

Thus:

**पञ्चधा - pañcadhā**

Fivefold.

### Mānuṣya - मानुष्य

Human.

Related to:

**मनुष्य - manuṣya**

Human being.

Now the form:

**मानुष्य - mānuṣya**

Human condition or human category.

### Ekavidha - एकविध

Of one kind.

**एक - eka**

One.

**विध - vidha**

Kind.

Thus:

**एकविध - ekavidha**.

### Bhautika - भौतिक

Elemental, material, relating to beings constituted through the elements.

Related to:

**भूत - bhūta**

Being, element.

Now the distinction.

The Sāṃkhya classification addresses categories of embodied existence.

It does not itself provide a biological evolutionary tree.

Nor does the fact that it contains fourteen classes establish an empirical count of all living species.

We should keep the historical ontology separate from modern natural science.

### The eight divine classes

The Kashmir Series editor lists eight types associated with divine or supernatural existence, including categories named for Brahmā, Prajāpati, Indra, ancestral beings, Gandharvas, Yakṣas, Rākṣasas and Piśācas.

Now these are traditional cosmological classes.

Their internal taxonomy varies among sources.

But the structure is eightfold.

### The five subhuman classes

The editor names:

**पशु - paśu**

Domestic or quadrupedal animals.

**मृग - mṛga**

Wild animals.

**पक्षि - pakṣi**

Birds.

**सरीसृप - sarīsṛpa**

Creeping creatures.

**स्थावर - sthāvara**

Immobile or stationary beings.

Now this is historically interesting because plants and stationary beings are included within the broader classification of life.

But again, these are traditional categories, not modern phylogenetic divisions.

### The human class

The classification groups humans as one type for the purpose of the scheme.

This is a particular cosmological abstraction.

It shouldn't be mistaken for a complete anthropological account.

### The guṇa distribution

The next quoted verse is Sāṃkhyakārikā 54.

Its main contrast is:

**ऊर्ध्वं सत्त्वविशालः - ūrdhvaṃ sattvaviśālaḥ**

Above, predominance of *sattva*.

**मूले तमोविशालः - mūle tamoviśālaḥ**

Below, predominance of *tamas*.

**मध्ये रजोविशालः - madhye rajoviśālaḥ**

In the middle, predominance of *rajas*.

Now the purpose is to relate the different cosmic domains to varying predominance of the guṇas.

This is a doctrinal structure in classical Sāṃkhya.

It should not be treated as a modern measurable distribution of psychological chemicals across altitudes.

### The philosophical relevance

Now here's why this substantial editorial digression matters.

The original commentary describes the emergence of cognitive dispositions.

The editor supplies the broader Sāṃkhya framework of dispositions and embodied existents.

The cognitive classifications relate to the manifestation of limited beings.

And the limited being can become caught within the very conceptual structures through which it apprehends its world.

That's the bridge back to Abhinavagupta.

The external census is not the climax.

**The relationship between the powers of cognition and the concealment of their source is the climax.**

And now we return to the first of the fifty categories.

Error.

Fivefold.

One of the most important concepts in Indian philosophy.

Because, as it turns out, the ordinary mind can make an astonishing number of very sophisticated mistakes.


## 7. पञ्च विपर्ययभेदाः - Five ways cognition gets the universe spectacularly wrong

Now we enter the first division of the fiftyfold *pratyayasarga*.

**पञ्च विपर्ययभेदाः - pañca viparyayabhedāḥ.**

Five kinds of error.

The Kashmir Series editor explains them through two associated series.

First, the five afflictions familiar from Yoga:

**अविद्या - avidyā**

Ignorance.

**अस्मिता - asmitā**

Ego-identification.

**राग - rāga**

Attachment.

**द्वेष - dveṣa**

Aversion.

**अभिनिवेश - abhiniveśa**

Clinging or deeply rooted insistence upon continued existence.

Second, the five Sāṃkhya error-categories:

**तमस् - tamas**

Darkness or ignorance.

**मोह - moha**

Delusion.

**महामोह - mahāmoha**

Great delusion.

**तामिस्र - tāmisra**

Darkness associated with frustration or aversion.

**अन्धतामिस्र - andhatāmisra**

Blind darkness, the culminating error-category.

These two lists are **not simply the same historical terminology**. The editorial gloss correlates them. The underlying Sāṃkhya classification employs the *tamas-moha* sequence; the *avidyā-asmitā* sequence is especially familiar from Patañjali's Yoga. ; ; \]

Now let's examine them.

### Avidyā - अविद्या

**अ - a-**

Negative prefix.

**विद्या - vidyā**

Knowledge.

Thus:

**अविद्या - avidyā**

Ignorance, absence of correct knowledge.

But here's the important distinction.

*Avidyā* is not merely a lack of information.

Someone might know a thousand Sanskrit verses and remain ignorant in the relevant liberating sense.

The problem concerns misapprehension of reality.

In Yoga, ignorance is particularly associated with mistaking the impermanent for permanent, the impure for pure, suffering for happiness, and what is not the Self for the Self.

In Trika, the deepest problem concerns failure to recognize the universal nature of consciousness.

Those are related concerns, but not identical philosophical systems.

### Asmitā - अस्मिता

Now this is a beautiful word.

From:

**अस्मि - asmi**

I am.

The first-person singular present of √**अस् - as**, to be.

With the abstract suffix:

**-ता - -tā**.

Thus:

**अस्मिता - asmitā**

I-am-ness.

Now compare:

**अहम् - aham**

I.

And:

**अस्मि - asmi**

I am.

The grammatical connection is memorable.

But here's the crucial philosophical distinction.

**Limited *asmitā* is not identical with universal *Ahaṃbhāva*.**

We've just studied the supreme *Aham* in Part XIX.

The error arises when finite identification is mistaken for the entire reality of selfhood.

A particular body.

A role.

A memory.

An opinion.

A limited cognitive subject.

These can become the content of "I."

But Abhinavagupta's supreme I-consciousness isn't exhausted by those finite determinations.

### Rāga - राग

Attachment, desire, colouring, passion.

From √**रञ्ज् - rañj**, to colour or become affected.

Now this root is lovely because it connects emotional attachment with colouring.

The mind is coloured by what it wants.

The same word-family also has important aesthetic and musical associations.

But we should not conflate all those technical uses.

Here *rāga* names attachment.

### Dveṣa - द्वेष

Aversion, hostility, repulsion.

From √**द्विष् - dviṣ**, to hate or dislike.

Now compare:

**राग - rāga**

Attraction toward.

**द्वेष - dveṣa**

Aversion from.

Two important patterns of conditioned cognition.

### Abhiniveśa - अभिनिवेश

Deep attachment, insistence, clinging.

Now dismantle it:

**अभि - abhi**

Toward.

**नि - ni**

Into, down.

**√विश् - viś**

To enter.

Thus:

**अभिनिवेश - abhiniveśa**

Deeply entering into or becoming fixed upon something.

The established psychological meaning includes tenacious clinging, particularly to continued embodied existence in the Yoga context.

Now here's a fascinating historical coincidence.

In Part XVIII, Abhinavagupta used **anabhiniveśa**, absence of fixation, when warning against becoming obsessed with one phonemic enumeration.

Now *abhiniveśa* returns as an afflictive fixation.

The words have different contextual applications, but the shared sense of deeply rooted adherence is worth noticing.

### The five Sāṃkhya names

The editor correlates these with:

**Tamas:** Darkness.

**Moha:** Delusion.

**Mahāmoha:** Great delusion.

**Tāmisra:** An intensified darkening associated with frustration or hostility.

**Andhatāmisra:** Blind darkness, connected in traditional explanations with profound clinging and the fear of loss.

Now, the categories should be understood within the historical philosophical framework.

They're not five laboratory-defined neurological diseases.

Nor does every ordinary moment of preference amount to a complete metaphysical bondage-state.

The taxonomy identifies characteristic modes of error.

### The fivefold comedy of the finite ego

Imagine an ordinary finite ego encountering the world.

First:

"I am this limited personality."

Then:

"I want that."

Then:

"I hate the thing preventing me from obtaining it."

Then:

"I must preserve all these identifications forever."

Then:

"Why am I suffering?"

Abhinavagupta's response is not to deny that ordinary wants and aversions exist.

It's to examine the misrecognition through which their limited contents are mistaken for the whole of selfhood.

Now here's the central Trika point.

**Error is an event within consciousness.**

It does not create a second consciousness outside Bhairava.

Nor does it turn the supreme reality into an intrinsically ignorant substance.

It is a contracted mode of manifestation.

And the power of speech is involved because determinate cognition is articulated through distinctions, recognitions and conceptual associations.

But how are the five kinds of error counted?

There's a subtle numerical point.

### Five categories are not the same as their internal subcategories

Sāṃkhyakārikā 48 gives further numerical divisions of the five error-families.

They are traditionally explained as:

| Error-family | Further subdivisions |
|--------|--------|
| Tamas | 8 |
| Moha | 8 |
| Mahāmoha | 10 |
| Tāmisra | 18 |
| Andhatāmisra | 18 |

These additional enumerations **do not replace the five-category contribution to the fiftyfold *pratyayasarga***.

If we summed all those secondary subdivisions instead of treating the five error-headings as the relevant count, we'd be answering a different classificatory question.

This is exactly the sort of numerical distinction that becomes important when reading Sanskrit philosophical systems carefully.

Now, after the five errors, comes the next category.

And it's enormous.

Twenty-eight incapacities.

The Sāṃkhya philosophers have apparently examined the entire structure of cognition and produced an unusually bleak equipment-inspection report.

But the classification is more subtle than a list of damaged sense organs.

It includes limitations of the cognitive faculties themselves.


## 8. अष्टाविंशतिभेदा अशक्तिः - Twenty-eight incapacities

The second major category is:

**अशक्ति - aśakti**

Incapacity.

And it has:

**अष्टाविंशति - aṣṭāviṃśati**

Twenty-eight.

Now that's a lovely Sanskrit numeral.

### Aṣṭa - अष्ट

Eight.

### Viṃśati - विंशति

Twenty.

Thus:

**अष्टाविंशति - aṣṭāviṃśati**

Twenty-eight.

Now the compound:

**अष्टाविंशतिभेद - aṣṭāviṃśatibheda**

Having twenty-eight divisions.

The form appears in the traditional verse as:

**अष्टाविंशतिभेदा - aṣṭāviṃśatibhedā**.

Now here's the structural explanation supplied by the Kashmir Series editor.

**Eleven impairments of the sense and action faculties.**

**Seventeen impairments of the intellect.**

Thus:

| Group | Count |
|--------|--------|
| Indriya-related incapacity | 11 |
| Buddhi-related incapacity | 17 |
| **Total** | **28** |

This matches Sāṃkhyakārikā 49, which explicitly explains the eleven faculties and seventeen intellectual impairments.

### Indriya - इन्द्रिय

Sense or faculty.

The traditional Sāṃkhya enumeration recognizes eleven *indriyas*:

Five cognitive faculties.

Five action faculties.

And mind, *manas*, as the coordinating eleventh.

Now these are categories within a philosophical model of embodied cognition.

They shouldn't be equated without qualification with eleven anatomical organs.

Some are functional capacities rather than physically discrete structures.

### Vadha - वध

Striking, injury, destruction, impairment.

Thus:

**इन्द्रियवध - indriyavadha**

Impairment of a faculty.

And:

**एकादशेन्द्रियवध - ekādaśendriyavadha**

Eleven faculty-impairments.

### Buddhi - बुद्धि

Intellect, discriminative cognitive faculty.

From √**बुध् - budh**, to know or awaken.

Now:

**बुद्धिवध - buddhivadha**

Impairment of the intellect.

And:

**सप्तदश - saptadaśa**

Seventeen.

The seventeen intellectual impairments are explained in the traditional Sāṃkhya scheme through the inversions or failures corresponding to the nine satisfactions and eight attainments.

That is:

$$
9+8=17.
$$

Now here's the important point.

The twenty-eight incapacities are **not simply twenty-eight different physical disabilities**.

The classification includes cognitive and discriminative limitations.

### The editor's historical list of bodily impairments

The Kashmir Series supplies a bracketed verse naming various physical conditions as examples of impairment, including loss of hearing, loss of sight, speech impairment and difficulties of movement or bodily function.

That list reflects the medical and philosophical language of its historical context.

It should not be mistaken for a modern clinical classification.

Nor does the inclusion of disability within an ancient philosophical taxonomy justify treating disabled people as spiritually inferior.

The text is discussing functional incapacities within a specific theory of means and obstacles, not establishing a modern ethical hierarchy of human worth.

### Karaṇa - करण

Instrument, faculty, means of action.

From √**कृ - kṛ**, to do.

The Sāṃkhya verse explains incapacity through:

**करणवैकल्यात् - karaṇavaikalyāt**

Because of impairment of the instruments.

Now let's dismantle it.

**करण - karaṇa**

Instrument.

**वैकल्य - vaikalya**

Defect, impairment, incompleteness.

The ablative:

**वैकल्यात् - vaikalyāt**

Because of impairment.

Thus:

**करणवैकल्यात् - karaṇavaikalyāt**.

The limitation arises through impairment of a faculty.

### The philosophical significance for Abhinavagupta

Now imagine a finite knower.

They possess a limited sensory and cognitive apparatus.

Some information is available.

Other information isn't.

Some discriminations are made correctly.

Others fail.

The structure of knowledge is therefore constrained by the limited condition of the knower.

And the mind's determinate concepts are shaped by that condition.

Now, in Trika, this doesn't mean universal consciousness has literally lost its intrinsic power.

It means that **a contracted mode of manifestation appears with limited capacities**.

The pure phonemic powers are involved in the finite cognitions.

But their universal nature isn't recognized.

That's the point.

### The divine alphabet's faulty equipment department

Imagine Mātṛkā distributing the powers of cognition.

The universal form includes the whole divine alphabet.

The finite individual receives a highly restricted operating configuration.

"Can I know everything?"

"No."

"Can I perceive every object?"

"No."

"Can I act without limitation?"

"No."

"Can I at least correctly understand all the Sanskrit compounds?"

"Absolutely not."

The individual looks at the equipment inventory.

Twenty-eight possible incapacities.

And a very long queue for technical support.

But the joke illustrates the metaphysics.

**The individual limitation belongs to the contracted mode, not to an independent destruction of the supreme conscious power.**

And now the classification moves somewhere more surprising.

Nine forms of satisfaction.

Why would satisfaction be an obstacle?

Because being satisfied can sometimes prevent further discrimination.

And the Sāṃkhya system takes that possibility extremely seriously.


## 9. नवधा तुष्टिः - Nine ways to become satisfied before understanding anything

Now we reach:

**तुष्टि - tuṣṭi**.

Satisfaction, contentment, complacency.

And the classification is:

**नवधा - navadhā**

Ninefold.

Now the Sāṃkhya scheme divides these into:

**Four internal satisfactions.**

And:

**Five external satisfactions.**

### The four internal satisfactions

The Kashmir Series editor lists:

**प्रकृति - prakṛti**

Nature.

**उपादान - upādāna**

Means or instrumental cause.

**काल - kāla**

Time.

**भाग्य - bhāgya**

Fortune or fate.

Now here's the underlying attitude these represent in traditional interpretation.

Someone may become satisfied that liberation or knowledge will arise simply through nature.

Or through acquiring a particular external means.

Or merely through waiting for time.

Or through relying on fortune.

The dissatisfaction driving genuine inquiry is prematurely resolved.

### Prakṛti - प्रकृति

From:

**प्र - pra**

Forth.

**√कृ - kṛ**

To make or act.

The noun:

**प्रकृति - prakṛti**

Nature, primal material principle in classical Sāṃkhya.

Now the philosophically problematic satisfaction is not simply understanding *Prakṛti*.

It is taking some insufficient relation to it as the completion of the path.

### Upādāna - उपादान

Taking up, acquiring, means or material basis.

From:

**उप - upa**

Near.

**आ - ā**

Toward.

**√दा - dā**

To give, in one relevant lexical formation; the noun has multiple established philosophical uses.

Here it functions within the traditional classification of means-dependent satisfaction.

### Kāla - काल

Time.

A wonderfully relevant category given our previous installments.

Imagine someone saying:

"I'll attain understanding automatically if I simply wait long enough."

Now time certainly matters for learning and practice.

But the Sāṃkhya category concerns premature satisfaction in time as though it were a sufficient explanation or means by itself.

### Bhāgya - भाग्य

Fortune, luck.

Related to:

**भाग - bhāga**

Share, portion.

Now the problem is relying upon fortune instead of addressing the requirements for knowledge.

### The five external satisfactions

The editor connects them with turning away from the five external sense-objects.

Traditionally:

Sound.

Touch.

Form or colour.

Taste.

Smell.

The relevant Sanskrit family is:

**उपरम - uparama**

Cessation, desisting, withdrawal.

From *upa-√ram*, to stop or rest.

Now here's the important distinction.

Withdrawal from sense-objects is not automatically condemned.

The Sāṃkhya issue is **mistaking a merely external change of behaviour for the completed acquisition of discriminative knowledge**.

Someone may withdraw from particular pleasures.

But still not understand the ultimate distinction the system regards as liberating.

### The ninefold structure

| Type | Number | Traditional bases |
|--------|--------|--------|
| Internal | 4 | Prakṛti, upādāna, kāla, bhāgya |
| External | 5 | Withdrawal from the five sense-object classes |
| **Total** | **9** | |

This is the Sāṃkhya taxonomy underlying the editor's explanation. ; \]

### The philosophical problem of being prematurely satisfied

Imagine studying Sanskrit.

You learn the vowels.

Excellent.

Then the consonants.

Wonderful.

Then you discover that *aham* has an extraordinary nondual interpretation.

And you say:

"Brilliant. I understand the whole of Trika now."

But you haven't learned the cases.

You haven't examined the commentaries.

You haven't distinguished the root verses from editorial additions.

You haven't actually understood the arguments.

You've simply become satisfied.

Now the problem isn't happiness.

It's **mistaking a preliminary achievement for completed knowledge**.

And that is precisely why *tuṣṭi* can appear as an obstacle-category.

### A subtle Trika contrast

Now compare the highest **viśrānti**, self-repose in consciousness, with premature **tuṣṭi**, satisfaction.

Both can be translated using words related to resting or contentment.

But they're not identical.

Supreme *viśrānti* concerns the self-revealing fullness of consciousness.

The Sāṃkhya *tuṣṭi* categories concern forms of settling into insufficient understanding.

The surface resemblance is instructive, but the technical distinctions must remain intact.

**Contentment is not necessarily realization.**

And the reverse is also true.

Realization is not adequately defined as a mood of ordinary contentment.

Now we come to the final eight.

*Siddhi.*

Accomplishment.

And, in this Sāṃkhya context, the list is surprisingly practical.

Not levitation.

Not invisibility.

Not communicating with geckos.

Reasoning.

Instruction.

Study.

The resolution of suffering.

Helpful companions.

Giving.

The Sāṃkhya philosophers have somehow reinvented a study group.


## 10. अष्टधा सिद्धिः - Eight attainments, and not one of them requires flying

Now the last category:

**सिद्धि - siddhi**

Attainment, accomplishment.

**अष्टधा - aṣṭadhā**

Eightfold.

The traditional Sāṃkhya explanation gives:

**ऊह - ūha**

Reasoning or inquiry.

**शब्द - śabda**

Authoritative verbal instruction.

**अध्ययन - adhyayana**

Study.

**त्रिविधदुःखविघात - trividha-duḥkha-vighāta**

The overcoming of three kinds of suffering.

**सुहृत्प्राप्ति - suhṛtprāpti**

Obtaining a helpful friend or companion.

**दान - dāna**

Giving.

Now count carefully.

One reasoning.

One verbal instruction.

One study.

Three distinct modes of overcoming suffering.

One helpful companion.

One giving.

$$
1+1+1+3+1+1=8.
$$

That's the eightfold scheme.

### Ūha - ऊह

Reasoning, inference, thoughtful examination.

A very useful philosophical word.

It concerns working through the structure of a problem.

### Śabda - शब्द

Word, verbal testimony.

Now in this context, it can refer to receiving authoritative instruction.

The important point is that language can function as a means toward knowledge.

Remember that.

Because Abhinavagupta is about to show how the very same power of speech can also participate in bondage.

That's the extraordinary duality.

Speech can assist knowledge.

Speech can condition misrecognition.

The difference concerns how it functions within cognition.

### Adhyayana - अध्ययन

Study, disciplined learning.

From:

**अधि - adhi**

Upon, over.

The verbal family of studying or reciting.

Thus:

**अध्ययन - adhyayana**

Study.

This is the activity we're actually engaged in now.

Except our teacher happens to be a tenth-eleventh-century Kashmiri philosopher who refuses to write short sentences.

### Trividha - त्रिविध

Threefold.

**त्रि - tri**

Three.

**विध - vidha**

Kind.

### Duḥkha - दुःख

Suffering, distress, unsatisfactoriness.

### Vighāta - विघात

Removal, obstruction, striking down.

From:

**वि - vi**

Apart.

**√हन् - han**

To strike.

Thus:

**विघात - vighāta**

Destruction or removal.

The traditional threefold suffering-classification concerns suffering arising internally, from other beings or things, and from forces beyond ordinary control in the historical cosmological scheme.

### Suhṛd - सुहृद्

Friend, well-wisher.

A beautiful Sanskrit word.

**सु - su**

Good.

**हृद् - hṛd**

Heart.

Thus:

**सुहृद् - suhṛd**

Good-hearted friend.

Now:

**प्राप्ति - prāpti**

Attainment, obtaining.

Thus:

**सुहृत्प्राप्ति - suhṛtprāpti**

Obtaining a helpful companion.

Notice the final dental consonant of *suhṛd* adjusts in the compound before *prāpti*.

### Dāna - दान

Giving, donation.

From √**दा - dā**, to give.

Now here's the philosophical beauty.

Knowledge isn't approached solely through isolated private thought.

Reasoning matters.

Instruction matters.

Study matters.

The conditions in which one learns matter.

And the relation to others matters.

That's what makes this list surprisingly human.

### The indispensable distinction

The *Sāṃkhyakārikā*'s eight *siddhis* aren't identical with the extraordinary powers often called *siddhis* in Tantric literature.

We must distinguish the technical systems.

If we don't, we create a false account of the verse.

And that would be a particularly ironic way to study *viparyaya*, error.

### The eightfold divine study group

Imagine a Sāṃkhya philosopher announcing his eight attainments.

Abhinavagupta leans forward.

"Can you become invisible?"

"No."

"Can you fly?"

"No."

"Can you manifest flowers out of thin air?"

"No."

"What can you do?"

"Reason carefully, study, understand instruction, overcome suffering, seek good companions and practise giving."

Abhinavagupta pauses.

"Actually, that's rather respectable."

The Sāṃkhya philosopher looks relieved.

Then Abhinavagupta adds:

"Now let's discuss how your entire cognitive structure is a manifestation of Śakti."

And the relief disappears.


## 11. The authentic Sāṃkhya verse Abhinavagupta actually quotes

Now we reach a particularly important textual detail.

The large explanatory passages we just studied are editorial insertions.

But Abhinavagupta's **main prose itself quotes Sāṃkhyakārikā 47**.

The Kashmir Series inserts a long square bracket **between the word पञ्च - pañca and the rest of the quoted verse**.

That is an editorial interruption of a continuous quotation, not a signal that Abhinavagupta suddenly changed topics. ; \]

SĀṂKHYAKĀRIKĀ 47 · QUOTED IN ABHINAVAGUPTA'S UNBRACKETED PROSE

**पञ्च विपर्ययभेदा भवन्त्यशक्तिश्च करणवैकल्यात्।**

**अष्टाविंशतिभेदा तुष्टिर्नवधाष्टधा सिद्धिः॥**

*Pañca viparyayabhedā bhavanty aśaktiś ca karaṇavaikalyāt.*

*Aṣṭāviṃśatibhedā tuṣṭir navadhāṣṭadhā siddhiḥ.*

"There are five kinds of error; incapacity, arising through impairment of the instruments, has twenty-eight divisions; satisfaction is ninefold, and attainment eightfold."

Now let's learn the actual grammar.

### Pañca - पञ्च

Five.

### Viparyayabhedāḥ - विपर्ययभेदाः

**विपर्यय - viparyaya**

Error.

**भेद - bheda**

Division, kind.

Masculine nominative plural:

**भेदाः - bhedāḥ**.

Thus:

**विपर्ययभेदाः - viparyayabhedāḥ**

Kinds of error.

### Bhavanti - भवन्ति

They are, they occur.

From √**भू - bhū**, to be or become.

Third-person plural present:

**भवन्ति - bhavanti**.

### Aśaktiś ca - अशक्तिश्च

Incapacity, and.

**अशक्तिः - aśaktiḥ**

Incapacity.

**च - ca**

And.

Through visarga sandhi:

**अशक्तिश्च - aśaktiś ca**.

### Karaṇavaikalyāt - करणवैकल्यात्

We already studied the components.

Because of impairment of the faculties or instruments.

The ablative expresses the source or cause.

### Navadhā - नवधा

Ninefold.

From:

**नव - nava**

Nine.

**-धा - -dhā**

In so many ways.

### Aṣṭadhā - अष्टधा

Eightfold.

Now the sandhi:

*navadhā + aṣṭadhā → navadhāṣṭadhā.*

The adjacent **ā + a** becomes **ā**.

And the written phrase:

**नवधाष्टधा - navadhāṣṭadhā**.

### The philosophical point

The verse isn't merely a numerical curiosity.

It supplies the cognitive taxonomy Abhinavagupta is incorporating into his theory of phonemic self-concealment.

The pure mantra-powers enter the structure of differentiated cognition.

And that structure comprises error, incapacity, satisfaction and attainment.

Now here's the truly important difference between Sāṃkhya and Trika.

In Sāṃkhya, these are dispositions within the manifest processes of *Prakṛti*.

In Abhinavagupta's Śaiva interpretation, they belong ultimately to the manifestation of conscious Śakti.

He is using a recognized classification to explain how the phonemic powers participate in the finite knower's condition.

**The same taxonomy is incorporated into a different metaphysical explanation.**

And the result is rather astonishing.

The conceptual formations themselves can become **pāśas**.

Bonds.

Fetters.

That bind the limited being.

This is where the cognitive classification becomes a theory of bondage.

The next word is:

**पाश - pāśa**.

A bond, a fetter.

And now the *Spandakārikā* enters with one of its most important statements.

Every determinate cognition arises through the penetration of speech.

The precise Sanskrit is worth learning word by word.

Because it has enormous implications for the relation between language and bondage.


## 12. पाश - The powers of knowing become the bonds of the limited knower

Now Abhinavagupta draws the classification together.

The unbracketed prose says:

**एत एव प्रत्ययाः पाशवसृष्टिरूपाः पाशा मुख्यतया।**

*Eta eva pratyayāḥ pāśavasṛṣṭirūpāḥ pāśā mukhyatayā.*

"These very cognitive dispositions, belonging to the creation of the bound individual, are principally bonds."

Now that is a remarkable statement.

Because the preceding list included not only ignorance and incapacity, but also satisfaction and accomplishment.

So the problem can't be reduced to:

"Bad thoughts are bondage, good thoughts are freedom."

The distinction is more fundamental.

Let's learn the vocabulary.

### Eta eva - एत एव

These very ones.

Notice the similarity with the beginning of our installment:

**त एव - ta eva**

Those very ones.

At the beginning, the phrase referred to the pure phonemic powers.

Now it refers to the *pratyayas*, the determinate cognitive dispositions.

The repetition emphasizes that the same manifested structures can be considered from different levels.

### Pratyayāḥ - प्रत्ययाः

Cognitive formations, determinate cognitions.

Masculine nominative plural.

### Pāśava - पाशव

Belonging to the *paśu*, the bound individual.

Now this is a crucial Śaiva technical word.

**पशु - paśu**

Animal in ordinary Sanskrit.

But in Śaiva theology, *paśu* can designate the finite, bound subject.

The theological use doesn't simply mean a person is biologically an animal.

It concerns the condition of limitation.

### Pāśavasṛṣṭi - पाशवसृष्टि

**पाशव - pāśava**

Belonging to the bound subject.

**सृष्टि - sṛṣṭi**

Manifestation.

Thus:

**पाशवसृष्टि - pāśavasṛṣṭi**

Manifestation associated with the bound subject.

### Pāśa - पाश

Bond, fetter, noose.

Masculine nominative plural:

**पाशाः - pāśāḥ**.

Now the Sanskrit phonemes become *pāśas* through their involvement in contracted cognition.

This is the heart of the whole argument.

### Mukhyatayā - मुख्यतया

Principally, primarily.

From:

**मुख्य - mukhya**

Chief, primary.

With the abstract formation:

**-ता - -tā**.

Instrumental:

**मुख्यतया - mukhyatayā**.

Now the philosophical meaning.

The determinate cognitive formations become the principal bonds of the limited individual.

But don't misunderstand that as a universal condemnation of thought.

Abhinavagupta is himself using an extraordinary amount of conceptual reasoning.

If conceptual activity were simply intrinsically evil, his entire commentary would be a rather self-defeating enterprise.

Instead, the issue is **how cognition is apprehended**.

Does it reveal the conscious ground?

Or does the finite subject identify exclusively with the differentiated content?

Does the word function as a means toward recognition?

Or does the word become part of a limiting conceptual construction?

The same power of speech can participate in both.

### The terrifyingly intelligent prison

Imagine a prison.

But the prison isn't built from bricks.

It's built from concepts.

"I am only this."

"That is absolutely separate from me."

"This possession constitutes my identity."

"This thought is the entirety of reality."

The walls are not physical.

They're structures of identification.

Now here's the remarkable thing.

The same intelligence that constructs them can investigate them.

The same power of language that supplies the limiting concepts can express the teaching of recognition.

And that is why the divine phonemes are not intrinsically demonic.

Their Māyic manifestation conceals their pure nature.

But their conscious source remains.

### The distinction between practical individuality and bondage

We should not infer that all functional distinctions must disappear.

You still need to distinguish yourself from an approaching vehicle.

You still need to know which food belongs to you.

You still need language to understand a Sanskrit text.

The error concerns ultimate identification with limitation, not the ordinary practical usefulness of distinguishing objects.

In Trika, liberation doesn't require pretending pots and people are conventionally interchangeable.

It concerns recognition of the nondual conscious ground within differentiated experience.

And now Abhinavagupta quotes one of the most important statements in the Spandakārikā.

The verse links the powers of concealment directly to language.

Here is the actual Sanskrit.


## 13. शब्दानुवेध - No determinate cognition arises without the penetration of speech

SCRIPTURAL/PHILOSOPHICAL VERSE QUOTED BY ABHINAVAGUPTA · SPANDAKĀRIKĀ 3.15 IN A COMMON MODERN NUMBERING

**स्वरूपावरणे चास्य शक्तयः सततोत्थिताः।**

**यतः शब्दानुवेधेन न विना प्रत्ययोद्भवः॥**

*Svarūpāvaraṇe cāsya śaktayaḥ satatotthitāḥ.*

*Yataḥ śabdānuvedhena na vinā pratyayodbhavaḥ.*

"The powers are constantly arising to conceal his own nature, since no determinate cognition occurs without the penetration of speech."

Now this is a particularly important verification.

The verse is independently attested in the *Spandakārikā*, including in Kṣemarāja's *Nirṇaya* tradition. Abhinavagupta quotes it in the main prose, not inside the Kashmir Series's long Sāṃkhya bracket. Some editions number the relevant verses differently according to their arrangement of the Spanda text; the common numbering is 3.15, immediately following the verse on the loss of the taste of supreme nectar. ; ; \]

Now let's dissect it.

### Svarūpāvaraṇa - स्वरूपावरण

**स्वरूप - svarūpa**

Own nature.

**आवरण - āvaraṇa**

Covering, concealment.

Thus:

**स्वरूपावरण - svarūpāvaraṇa**

Concealment of one's own nature.

Now this returns us directly to the verb:

**आवृण्वते - āvṛṇvate**

They conceal.

The root-family is the same.

Abhinavagupta is deliberately linking the main argument with the earlier Spanda teaching.

### Asya - अस्य

Of this one, of him.

Genitive singular pronoun.

Within the Spanda context, the referent is the conscious subject whose own nature becomes concealed.

### Śaktayaḥ - शक्तयः

Powers.

Feminine nominative plural.

Now here's the astonishing thing.

The verse does not simply say that ignorance is an absence of power.

It says **powers** arise to conceal.

The same theological universe contains powers of manifestation and concealment.

### Satatotthitāḥ - सततोत्थिताः

Constantly arising.

**सतत - satata**

Continuous, constant.

**उत्थित - utthita**

Risen, arisen.

From *ud-√sthā*, to rise.

Thus:

**सततोत्थित - satatotthita**

Constantly arising.

Feminine nominative plural:

**सततोत्थिताः - satatotthitāḥ**.

### Yataḥ - यतः

Because, since, for the reason that.

Now the verse provides an explanation.

### Śabda - शब्द

Word, sound, linguistic expression.

### Anuvedha - अनुवेध

Penetration, permeation, interweaving.

From:

**अनु - anu**

Along, through.

**√व्यध् - vyadh**

To pierce or penetrate.

The noun:

**अनुवेध - anuvedha**

Penetration.

Now:

**शब्दानुवेध - śabdānuvedha**

Penetration by speech or word.

Instrumental singular:

**शब्दानुवेधेन - śabdānuvedhena**.

Through the penetration of linguistic expression.

### Na vinā - न विना

Not without.

Now this is crucial.

The verse doesn't simply say:

"All concepts are spoken aloud."

It says determinate cognition is not without *śabdānuvedha*.

The involvement of speech can occur in subtler cognitive forms.

It isn't restricted to audible physical articulation.

### Pratyayodbhava - प्रत्ययोद्भव

**प्रत्यय - pratyaya**

Cognitive formation, determinate cognition.

**उद्भव - udbhava**

Arising, emergence.

From:

**उद् - ud**

Forth.

**√भू - bhū**

To become.

Thus:

**प्रत्ययोद्भव - pratyayodbhava**

The arising of determinate cognition.

Now the complete philosophical claim:

**Determinate cognition arises in relation to the expressive power of language.**

And that expressive power can participate in the concealment of the universal Self.

### Is the claim that all thought happens in spoken Sanskrit?

Absolutely not.

The word *śabda* is being used within a broad theory of speech and conscious manifestation.

The Spanda doctrine does not require that a child who has never learned Sanskrit secretly articulate all the inflectional paradigms of the *Aṣṭādhyāyī*.

Nor does it establish that every cognitive operation in modern psychological terminology requires explicit verbal rehearsal.

The traditional claim concerns the way differentiated cognition becomes articulated and determined through the power of expression.

And the metaphysical background is the fourfold understanding of speech.

Parā.

Paśyantī.

Madhyamā.

Vaikharī.

The overt audible sound is only one level.

### The extraordinary double function of language

Now think about the whole audiobook.

We're using Sanskrit words to investigate the nature of awareness.

Those words can assist understanding.

They can make distinctions precise.

They can expose errors.

But they can also become objects of identification.

You can memorize a concept without understanding its referent.

You can mistake the label for the reality.

You can become attached to an explanation.

You can use the concept "I" to reinforce a limited ego.

Or investigate the *Aham* through which cognition is manifest.

That's the remarkable duality.

**The same power of speech can articulate both bondage and recognition.**

And this is why the *Spandakārikā* verse is so important.

The power of language isn't external to consciousness.

It is one of its manifestations.

But in the contracted mode, its activity can conceal the universal ground.

### The alphabet as a veil

Imagine Mātṛkā offering a person the word:

**अहम् - aham**.

The supreme interpretation:

Universal I-consciousness.

The limited interpretation:

Only this body.

Only this biography.

Only these preferences.

Only this finite set of experiences.

Now the word hasn't necessarily changed its sound.

But its apprehension has contracted.

The same expressive power participates in a limiting identification.

That's exactly the kind of structure the verse illuminates.

And now Abhinavagupta quotes the previous Spanda teaching.

Because it explains the consequence.

When determinate cognition arises in this contracted way, the finite knower loses the **taste of supreme nectar**.

Not because the universal conscious reality has literally died.

But because its fullness is no longer recognized.

The Sanskrit is beautiful.


## 14. परामृतरसापायः - Losing the taste of the supreme nectar

SPANDAKĀRIKĀ 3.14 · SHORT FRAGMENT QUOTED BY ABHINAVAGUPTA

**परामृतरसापायस्तस्य यः प्रत्ययोद्भवः।**

*Parāmṛtarasāpāyas tasya yaḥ pratyayodbhavaḥ.*

"The arising of determinate cognition is, for that individual, a loss of the taste of supreme nectar."

Now this is independently attested as the beginning of the immediately preceding Spanda verse.

And the full original continues by describing the limited subject's loss of freedom and confinement to restricted objects. ; \]

Let's unpack the compound.

### Para - पर

Supreme, highest, beyond.

### Amṛta - अमृत

Deathless nectar.

From:

**अ - a-**

Negative prefix.

**मृत - mṛta**

Dead.

Thus:

**अमृत - amṛta**

Deathless.

### Rasa - रस

Taste, flavour, essence, relish.

Now remember Abhinavagupta's extraordinary use of *rasa* in aesthetics and spiritual philosophy.

The word can refer to something much deeper than a physical taste on the tongue.

It can express experiential relish.

### Parāmṛtarasa - परामृतरस

The taste or relish of supreme nectar.

The vowel sandhi:

*para + amṛta → parāmṛta.*

Thus:

**परामृत - parāmṛta**

Supreme nectar.

And:

**परामृतरस - parāmṛtarasa**

The taste of supreme nectar.

### Apāya - अपाय

Loss, departure, falling away.

From:

**अप - apa**

Away.

**√इ - i**

To go.

Thus:

**अपाय - apāya**

Departure or loss.

Now:

**परामृतरसापाय - parāmṛtarasāpāya**

Loss of the taste of supreme nectar.

### Pratyayodbhava - प्रत्ययोद्भव

Arising of determinate cognition.

We already learned it.

Now the phrase ties the two together.

In the condition of bondage, the emergence of object-differentiating cognition is associated with the loss of the relish of one's supreme nature.

### The crucial interpretive distinction

This does **not** establish that having any thought automatically destroys liberation.

Abhinavagupta himself uses thoughts, distinctions, Sanskrit grammar and conceptual argument throughout this entire treatise.

The issue is **contracted apprehension**.

The cognition becomes associated with the loss of recognition of the universal ground.

The Spanda tradition specifically discusses the way differentiated cognition can confine the limited subject.

But the existence of a distinct cognitive event does not prove that consciousness has ceased to be the ground of manifestation.

### The nectar hasn't become chemically unavailable

Imagine a person tasting the most wonderful tea imaginable.

Then becoming absorbed in a distracting argument.

They stop noticing the flavour.

The tea hasn't necessarily changed.

Their attention has shifted.

Now this is a limited analogy.

The supreme nectar of *Parāmṛta* isn't literal tea.

And realization isn't simply a matter of paying more attention to pleasant sensations.

But the comparison helps distinguish:

**A reality being absent.**

From:

**A reality no longer being apprehended in the same way.**

The text's theological interpretation is that supreme conscious fullness hasn't been destroyed by the finite cognitive state.

Its recognition is obscured.

And that's why the loss is described through *rasa*.

The taste.

The relish.

The direct experiential character of consciousness.

### The philosopher who loses the nectar while discussing nectar

Imagine a scholar explaining *Parāmṛta*.

They have memorized every occurrence.

They know the sandhi.

They can explain the compound.

They're extremely proud.

Then someone asks:

"Do you recognize the conscious nature through which this understanding is manifest?"

The scholar replies:

"Excuse me, I am currently busy proving that my preferred transliteration is superior."

A rather unfortunate spiritual outcome.

The Sanskrit scholarship may be excellent.

But the self-recognition has become obscured by identification with the activity.

That's the philosophical danger.

**The teaching can be conceptually understood without its ultimate significance being recognized.**

And this is why the Spanda verse is so profound.

It's not condemning knowledge.

It's warning about the contracted manner of knowing.

And now we arrive at the final transition of the installment.

From the inner formation of cognitive dispositions.

To the outwardly audible world of language.

The pure mantra-powers become manifest as ordinary sound-forms.

The Lord generates phonemes corresponding to the divine originals.

And the *Pūrvaśāstra* supplies an authentic quotation.

This is the birth of **Māyic letters**.

The transition from pure mantra to the differentiated sounds of ordinary experience.


## 15. प्रत्ययसृष्टित्वान्तरालीकरण - Cognitive manifestation forms the intermediate layer

Abhinavagupta has now established the sequence.

The pure mantra-powers enter a differentiated creation of cognitive dispositions.

Those dispositions can conceal the pure nature of the powers.

The limited knower becomes bound through the structures of determinate cognition.

And the *Spandakārikā* explains why speech is intimately involved.

Now comes the next move.

The Sanskrit says:

**एवं प्रत्ययसृष्टित्वान्तरालीकरणेन।**

*Evaṃ pratyayasṛṣṭitvāntarālīkaraṇena.*

"Thus, by the interposition of the condition of the creation of cognitive dispositions…"

This is a wonderfully precise phrase.

Because it describes **an intermediate level** between pure mantra and outwardly manifest sound.

Let's dismantle it.

### Pratyaya - प्रत्यय

Determinate cognition.

### Sṛṣṭi - सृष्टि

Manifestation.

Thus:

**प्रत्ययसृष्टि - pratyayasṛṣṭi**

Creation of cognitive dispositions.

### -Tva - त्व

The abstract suffix.

Thus:

**प्रत्ययसृष्टित्व - pratyayasṛṣṭitva**

The condition of being a creation of cognitive dispositions.

### Antarāla - अन्तराल

Interval, intermediate space, intervening condition.

Now this is a useful word.

**अन्तराल - antarāla**

Intervening space or interval.

### Karaṇa - करण

Making, causing, producing.

From √**कृ - kṛ**, to do.

Now:

**अन्तरालीकरण - antarālīkaraṇa**

Making something intermediate, interposing.

And the instrumental:

**अन्तरालीकरणेन - antarālīkaraṇena**.

Thus the whole expression concerns an interposed condition of cognitive manifestation.

### Why this matters

The passage isn't saying that pure mantra immediately transforms into ordinary sound without any intervening structure.

The cognitive dispositions have a role.

And that role is precisely why the text has spent so much time discussing the fiftyfold *pratyayasarga*.

The outwardly articulated phonemes are involved in a more elaborate manifestation of limited cognition.

Now here's the important philosophical distinction.

**The intermediate cognitive layer isn't a second independent conscious substance.**

It's a level of manifestation within the same divine power.

But its differentiation can produce the experience of limitation.

And the pure nature of the phonemic powers can become concealed through that process.

### The divine alphabet's unfortunate detour

Imagine the pure mantra-powers leaving Anuttara.

First:

"Excellent. Universal consciousness."

Then:

"Wonderful. Differentiated cognitive powers."

Then:

"Fine. Individual dispositions."

Then:

"Ah. Error."

Then:

"Incapacity."

Then:

"Premature satisfaction."

Then:

"Limited linguistic identification."

And finally:

"Why am I trapped inside a finite conceptual world?"

What an absolutely disastrous sightseeing tour.

But that's the doctrinal sequence.

The divine powers aren't fundamentally destroyed.

Their manifestation has become contracted.

And now the sound becomes overtly articulated.

The Sanskrit gives us another monstrous compound.

One that is almost a miniature theory of audible language.


## 16. स्फुटश्रूयमाण… - The birth of distinctly audible Māyic letters

**स्फुटश्रूयमाणश्रुत्यात्मकक्रमाभासमानमायीयवर्णसृष्टिः।**

"The creation of Māyic phonemes manifesting sequentially as distinctly audible sound."

Now that is an astonishing compound.

Let's take the whole bloody thing apart.

### Sphuṭa - स्फुट

Distinct, clear, explicit.

**ट - ṭa**

Retroflex.

Thus:

**स्फुट - sphuṭa**.

### Śrūyamāṇa - श्रूयमाण

Being heard.

**श्रूयमाण - śrūyamāṇa**.

Now this is an important distinction from:

**शृणोति - śṛṇoti**

He or she hears.

The latter describes the act of hearing.

### Śruti - श्रुति

Hearing, audible expression, that which is heard.

### Ātmaka - आत्मक

Having the nature of.

**श्रुत्यात्मक - śrutyātmaka**

Having the nature of audible sound.

### Krama - क्रम

Sequence.

### Ābhāsamāna - आभासमान

Appearing, becoming manifest.

**आभासमान - ābhāsamāna**.

Manifesting sequentially.

### Māyīya - मायीय

Pertaining to Māyā, the differentiated and limiting domain.

### Varṇa - वर्ण

Phoneme.

### Sṛṣṭi - सृष्टि

Creation, emission, manifestation.

Now:

**मायीयवर्णसृष्टि - māyīyavarṇasṛṣṭi**

Creation of Māyic phonemes.

And the entire compound describes the manifestation of **clearly audible, sequentially articulated sound**.

### The philosophical progression

Now we can distinguish the levels.

**Pure mantra-powers:** Their ultimate nature is the uncontracted divine consciousness.

**Māyic phonemes:** The differentiated sound-forms appear through audible, sequential articulation.

This isn't a claim that the individual phonemes are necessarily morally bad.

It's a claim about their level and manner of manifestation.

### The difference between Parā and Vaikharī returns

Remember the fourfold speech system.

Parā.

Paśyantī.

Madhyamā.

Vaikharī.

Now the present passage moves toward the overtly articulated sound-world.

The spoken word has a temporal sequence.

Distinct phonetic qualities.

Conventional uses.

And functions within finite cognition.

But it isn't independent of the conscious power.

That's why Abhinavagupta refuses to treat it as a separate ultimate substance.

### The crucial distinction between acoustic sound and mantric power

Imagine pronouncing:

**शिव - śiva**.

The ordinary phonetic event involves an audible sequence.

You can analyse the consonants.

The vowel.

Their timing.

Their articulation.

But the Tantric interpretation concerns the conscious power represented through the phonemic form.

The two levels are related.

They aren't simply identical.

And the fact that a sound is audibly pronounced doesn't automatically establish the realization of its divine significance.

The speaker may understand it conventionally.

Or through a ritual interpretation.

Or through a contemplative philosophical recognition.

These are distinguishable.

### Why sequential speech can participate in bondage

Now here's the philosophical issue.

An articulated word can evoke a determinate concept.

That concept can become part of a limited identity.

A person hears:

"I."

They identify a finite subject.

They hear:

"Mine."

They construct possession.

They hear:

"Other."

They apprehend separation.

Those distinctions have practical functions.

But they can also support misrecognition when treated as ultimately independent realities.

And that is how the powers of sound become implicated in bondage.

Not because the Sanskrit alphabet contains an intrinsically evil syllable.

But because **differentiated speech can become the medium of contracted self-apprehension**.

Now here's the beautiful turn.

The outward phonemic creation isn't completely severed from the pure source.

The Sanskrit says it remains connected with:

**आद्यपारमार्थिकशुद्धरूप - ādyapāramārthikaśuddharūpa**

Its original, ultimate, pure nature.

And the next compound is:

**आलिङ्गिता - āliṅgitā**

Embraced.

Held together.

The ordinary sound-world is still embraced by its original pure reality.

That is a profoundly important qualification.


## 17. आलिङ्गिता - The ordinary letters remain embraced by their pure source

The passage describes the Māyic phonemic creation as:

**आद्यपारमार्थिकशुद्धरूपालिङ्गिता।**

*Ādyapāramārthikaśuddharūpāliṅgitā.*

"Embraced by its original, ultimate, pure form."

Now that's beautiful.

The pure nature isn't annihilated.

It remains the ground of the differentiated manifestation.

Let's learn the compound.

### Ādya - आद्य

Original, primordial.

### Pāramārthika - पारमार्थिक

Pertaining to ultimate reality.

From:

**परमार्थ - paramārtha**

Ultimate truth or reality.

The adjective:

**पारमार्थिक - pāramārthika**.

### Śuddha - शुद्ध

Pure.

### Rūpa - रूप

Nature, form.

### Āliṅgita - आलिङ्गित

Embraced, held, clasped.

From:

**आ - ā**

Toward.

**√लिङ्ग् - liṅg**, to embrace or attach in the relevant verbal family.

The past participle:

**आलिङ्गित - āliṅgita**.

Feminine nominative singular:

**आलिङ्गिता - āliṅgitā**.

Now the entire compound says that the outwardly manifested Māyic letters remain embraced by their original pure nature.

### The philosophical significance

This directly prevents a dualistic reading.

If Māyic sound were an independent substance outside Bhairava, how could it remain embraced by the pure power?

The text is maintaining continuity.

The different mode of manifestation doesn't create an independent ontological source.

### The beloved metaphor of an embrace

Imagine the divine Mātṛkā embracing her own differentiated expressions.

The letters become audible.

Sequential.

Conventional.

Associated with cognitive limitations.

But they're not thrown into an independently existing abyss outside consciousness.

Their ultimate source remains present.

The embrace is the image of nonseparateness.

And it has a beautiful Kaula resonance.

The relation between seed and generative matrix.

Śiva and Śakti.

Manifestation and its conscious ground.

### The capacity to produce results

The next expression is:

**तत्तत्कार्यफलप्रसवदायिनी।**

*Tattatkāryaphalaprasavadāyinī.*

"Giving rise to the corresponding effects and results."

Let's break it down.

**तत्तत् - tattat**

This or that, each respective.

**कार्य - kārya**

Action, product, effect.

**फल - phala**

Fruit, result.

**प्रसव - prasava**

Production, bringing forth.

**दायिनी - dāyinī**

Giving.

Thus the Māyic phonemes participate in the production of various effects and consequences within their domain.

Now here's the important qualification.

The text isn't asserting that every arbitrary utterance will automatically produce a supernatural result.

It describes the ritual and cosmological potency of differentiated phonemic manifestation within the traditional framework.

The effects belong to particular relations of powers, acts and conditions.

And the passage now invokes a scriptural authority for this emergence.

The **Śrīpūrvaśāstra**.

This is where the distinction between pure and Māyic letters becomes explicitly scriptural.

And we have to handle the quotation carefully, because one of the quoted lines is incomplete in the surviving transcription.

We will not reconstruct it.


## 18. The Pūrvaśāstra - The Lord produces letters corresponding to the divine originals

Abhinavagupta introduces the teaching as described in the:

**श्रीपूर्वशास्त्र - śrīpūrvaśāstra**.

The venerable *Pūrvaśāstra*.

Within Trika, the *Pūrva* tradition is associated with the scriptural material underlying the *Mālinīvijayottaratantra* and related doctrines.

But a quoted fragment here shouldn't be assigned a precise chapter and verse without independently verifying its location.

Now the first line is incomplete.

The Kashmir Series has:

**सर्वशास्त्रार्थगर्भिण्यै …**

*Sarvaśāstrārthagarbhiṇyai …*

And the GRETIL transcription explicitly marks the rest as missing.

That's an actual textual lacuna.

Not an invitation to invent Sanskrit.

### Sarva - सर्व

All.

### Śāstra - शास्त्र

Teaching, treatise, scripture, systematic instruction.

### Artha - अर्थ

Meaning, subject matter, purpose.

### Garbhiṇī - गर्भिणी

Pregnant, containing within.

Related to:

**गर्भ - garbha**

Womb, embryo, interior.

Thus:

**सर्वशास्त्रार्थगर्भिणी - sarvaśāstrārthagarbhiṇī**

She who contains the meanings of all scriptures.

The dative singular:

**सर्वशास्त्रार्थगर्भिण्यै - sarvaśāstrārthagarbhiṇyai**.

Now that's a magnificent phrase.

To her who contains the meaning of every scripture.

But the rest of the quoted sentence isn't securely preserved in these witnesses.

So we stop at the ellipsis.

We don't manufacture a complete verse.

### The next attested quotation

The Kashmir Series then gives:

PŪRVAŚĀSTRA QUOTATION · KASHMIR SERIES READING

**अनया सम्पबुद्धः सन् योनिं विक्षोभ्य शक्तितः।**

**तत्समानश्रुतीन् वर्णांस्तत्सङ्ख्यानसृजत्प्रभुः॥**

*Anayā sampabuddhaḥ san yoniṃ vikṣobhya śaktitaḥ.*

*Tatsamānaśrutīn varṇāṃs tatsaṃkhyān asṛjat prabhuḥ.*

"Awakened through her, the Lord, having stirred the generative matrix by his power, emitted phonemes corresponding in sound and number to those $$pure prototypes$$."

Now the variants matter.

The Kashmir Series has **सम्पबुद्धः - sampabuddhaḥ**, whereas GRETIL reads **saṃprabuddhaḥ**, the more regular-looking form meaning fully awakened. GRETIL also transmits **vamāṃs** where the Kashmir Series has **varṇān**, phonemes. The quotation above preserves the Kashmir Series reading; the differences are not silently harmonized. ; \]

Now let's unpack it.

### Anayā - अनया

By her, through this feminine power.

Instrumental singular of the feminine demonstrative pronoun.

The referent is the Goddess or the generative power being discussed.

### Saṃprabuddha - संप्रबुद्ध

Fully awakened, awakened through or in relation to.

From:

**सम् - sam**

Together, completely.

**प्र - pra**

Forth.

**√बुध् - budh**

To know, awaken.

The past participle:

**सम्प्रबुद्ध - samprabuddha**.

Masculine nominative singular:

**सम्प्रबुद्धः - samprabuddhaḥ**.

Again, this is the normalized formation supported by the GRETIL witness, while the Kashmir Series prints *sampabuddhaḥ*.

### San - सन्

Being.

The nominative singular masculine present participle of √**अस् - as**, to be.

### Yoniṃ - योनिम्

Generative matrix, womb, source.

Accusative singular of:

**योनि - yoni**.

### Vikṣobhya - विक्षोभ्य

Having stirred, agitated or activated.

From:

**वि - vi**

Apart, intensely.

**√क्षुभ् - kṣubh**

To stir or become agitated.

The causative absolutive:

**विक्षोभ्य - vikṣobhya**.

Now this is a particularly important verb.

It expresses the activation of the generative matrix.

The theological symbolism is continuous with the earlier discussions of Śiva's seed and Śakti's yoni.

### Śaktitaḥ - शक्तितः

Through power, by means of Śakti.

From:

**शक्ति - śakti**

Power.

With the adverbial/ablative-type ending:

**-तः - -taḥ**.

### Tatsamānaśrutīn - तत्समानश्रुतीन्

Now here's a beautiful compound.

**तत् - tat**

Those, referring to the divine prototype phonemes.

**समान - samāna**

Same, similar.

**श्रुति - śruti**

Audible form, sound as heard.

Thus:

**तत्समानश्रुति - tatsamānaśruti**

Having a corresponding or similar audible form.

Accusative masculine plural:

**तत्समानश्रुतीन् - tatsamānaśrutīn**.

Now this is important.

The Māyic phonemes correspond in audible form to the divine originals.

But correspondence doesn't mean complete identity of mode.

The pure phonemic power and the differentiated audible sound can be related without being identical in every respect.

### Varṇān - वर्णान्

Phonemes.

Accusative masculine plural.

Now the Kashmir Series reads:

**वर्णान् - varṇān**.

The GRETIL witness has a different, difficult reading.

So we should not claim that every textual witness transmits this word identically.

### Tatsaṃkhyān - तत्सङ्ख्यान्

Corresponding in number to those.

**तत् - tat**

Those.

**सङ्ख्या - saṃkhyā**

Number, enumeration.

The phrase concerns numerical correspondence.

Now the key idea.

The outward sound-series corresponds to the pure phonemic powers in both sound and number.

But it manifests in a different mode.

### Asṛjat - असृजत्

He emitted, created or sent forth.

From √**सृज् - sṛj**, to emit.

Third-person singular imperfect:

**असृजत् - asṛjat**.

Now this is an excellent verb to learn.

Compare:

**सृष्टि - sṛṣṭi**

Creation.

**विसर्ग - visarga**

Emission.

**असृजत् - asṛjat**

He emitted.

All belong to the same semantic family.

### Prabhuḥ - प्रभुः

Lord, master, powerful sovereign.

From:

**प्र - pra**

Forth, intensive.

**√भू - bhū**

To be or become.

Thus:

**प्रभु - prabhu**

Lord.

Masculine nominative singular:

**प्रभुः - prabhuḥ**.

### The philosophical meaning

Now the quotation is extraordinarily important.

The Lord produces differentiated phonemes corresponding to the divine prototypes.

But those phonemes become manifest through the stirred generative matrix.

And the effect is the outward world of articulated sound.

The relationship isn't simply:

"Śiva has a collection of eternal letters in a box and copies them onto paper."

It's a symbolic account of manifestation.

The pure power becomes expressed through differentiated audible forms.

And those forms are not ontologically independent of their source.

### The difference between prototype and manifestation

Imagine the distinction between a musical composition and a performance.

The composition has an intelligible structure.

Different performances can realize that structure through actual sound.

Now an ordinary composition and performance are not identical with Tantric pure mantra and Māyic sound.

But the analogy captures the difference between **a generative pattern** and **its particular audible expression**.

The performance manifests a structure.

But its actual acoustic qualities depend upon circumstances.

And the performance isn't simply the same thing as the abstract pattern.

Similarly, Abhinavagupta distinguishes the pure mantric powers from their outward Māyic manifestation.

The latter can correspond to the former while displaying the characteristics of articulated, sequential sound.

### The divine photocopier is not a photocopier

Imagine Śiva announcing:

"I shall produce the ordinary phonemic series."

Mātṛkā supplies the divine prototypes.

The generative matrix is activated.

And the letters emerge.

Same count.

Corresponding sounds.

Different mode of manifestation.

Now a modern office worker might call that copying.

But the metaphysical symbolism concerns the differentiation of Śakti's own powers.

Not an external manufacturing process operating on independent material.

The source isn't separated from the result.

And the result isn't identical to the source in every respect.

**Continuity without simple equivalence.**

That's the important relationship.


## 19. तैस्तैरालिङ्गिताः - The differentiated letters are embraced by their powers

Abhinavagupta continues with another short Pūrva quotation:

**तैस्तैरालिङ्गिताः सन्तः सर्वकामफलप्रदाः।**

*Tais tair āliṅgitāḥ santaḥ sarvakāmaphalapradāḥ.*

"Being embraced by those respective powers, they grant the results of various desires."

Now here's the beautiful connection.

Earlier, the Māyic letters were described as:

**आलिङ्गिता - āliṅgitā**

Embraced by their original pure nature.

Now the Pūrva quotation again uses:

**आलिङ्गित - āliṅgita**

Embraced.

The relation is explicit.

### Tais taiḥ - तैस्तैः

By those respective ones.

Instrumental masculine plural, repeated distributively.

The repetition indicates various corresponding powers.

### Āliṅgitāḥ - आलिङ्गिताः

Embraced.

From *ā-√liṅg*, to embrace.

Masculine nominative plural:

**आलिङ्गिताः - āliṅgitāḥ**.

Now the divine powers are being described as embracing the manifested phonemes.

### Santaḥ - सन्तः

Being.

Masculine nominative plural of the present participle of √**अस् - as**, to be.

### Sarva - सर्व

All.

### Kāma - काम

Desire, wish, object of desire.

### Phala - फल

Fruit, result.

### Prada - प्रद

Giving, granting.

From:

**प्र - pra**

Forth.

**√दा - dā**

To give.

Thus:

**सर्वकामफलप्रद - sarvakāmaphalaprada**

Granting the results of various desires.

Masculine nominative plural:

**सर्वकामफलप्रदाः - sarvakāmaphalapradāḥ**.

### The theological meaning

The differentiated phonemic forms retain a relationship with their originating powers.

And, within the Tantric ritual framework, those forms can be associated with distinct effects.

Now we need an important qualification.

**This is a scriptural claim concerning the efficacy of mantric forms within a traditional system.**

It isn't independently established evidence that pronouncing any particular syllable produces every desired external result.

Nor does the statement specify that any phoneme, used arbitrarily, will grant any wish.

The context concerns differentiated powers and their established relationships.

But the philosophical point is secure.

The Māyic sound-form isn't merely a dead acoustic object cut off from its divine origin.

It remains embraced by the power from which it manifests.

### The distinction between a letter's efficacy and its ultimate nature

Now imagine somebody pronouncing a mantra.

At the level of ordinary acoustic sound, there's an articulatory event.

At the level of convention, the sound may possess an established meaning.

At the ritual level, it may belong to a specific tradition of practice.

At the metaphysical level, the sound is interpreted as a manifestation of conscious Śakti.

Those levels are not identical.

But neither are they unrelated.

And Abhinavagupta's concern is precisely their relationship.

### The alphabet becomes an instrument of freedom again

Now think back to the start of Part XXIII.

The pure phonemic powers entered the cognitive manifestation of limitation.

Their pure nature became concealed.

The *pratyayas* became bonds.

Language participated in misrecognition.

But the text now explicitly retains the connection with the pure source.

That means the doctrine isn't a simple condemnation of ordinary speech.

**The very powers that participate in bondage remain grounded in the conscious reality whose recognition is liberating.**

And that's a profound distinction.

A word can reinforce a limited identity.

Or it can express a teaching that questions that identity.

A mantra can be heard merely as sound.

Or interpreted through its traditional divine associations.

A philosophical term can become an object of sterile conceptual attachment.

Or a means of investigating the conscious nature of experience.

The sound isn't an independent creator of liberation.

But it can participate in the process of recognition.

And that's why Abhinavagupta's long commentary on Mātṛkā is philosophically meaningful.

The sacred alphabet isn't merely a system of magical sound-objects.

It's an expression of the relation between consciousness, language, limitation and freedom.

Now we're almost at the natural boundary.

But the final sentence makes one more important distinction.

The Goddess is hidden from those blinded by Māyā.

Yet she remains manifest as pure conscious illumination.

And the whole discussion returns to the meaning of *suvrate*.

The beautiful vow.


## 20. मायान्धानाम् अप्रकटा - Hidden to those blinded by Māyā

At Kashmir Series p. 211, Abhinavagupta returns to the Goddess's description.

He says that she is:

**अप्रकटा मायान्धानाम्।**

*Aprakaṭā māyāndhānām.*

"Unmanifest to those blinded by Māyā."

Yet he also describes her as:

**प्रकाशा शुद्धवेदनात्मिका।**

*Prakāśā śuddhavedanātmikā.*

"Manifest luminosity, having the nature of pure awareness."

Now that is the beautiful final paradox.

The Goddess is not absent.

But her pure nature is not recognized by the limited subject.

Let's learn the words.

### Aprakaṭā - अप्रकटा

Not manifest, not openly revealed.

From:

**अ - a-**

Negative prefix.

**प्रकट - prakaṭa**

Manifest, evident.

Feminine nominative singular:

**अप्रकटा - aprakaṭā**.

### Māyāndha - मायान्ध

Now this is an excellent compound.

**माया - māyā**

Māyā, the differentiating power.

**अन्ध - andha**

Blind.

Thus:

**मायान्ध - māyāndha**

Blinded by Māyā.

Genitive plural:

**मायान्धानाम् - māyāndhānām**.

Now, this is a traditional metaphor of spiritual nonrecognition.

It's not a literal claim about the visual abilities of blind people.

The philosophical meaning concerns inability to recognize the universal conscious ground.

### Prakāśā - प्रकाशा

Manifest, luminous.

Now the relation to:

**प्रकाश - prakāśa**

Luminosity.

The same important term from Pratyabhijñā.

### Śuddha - शुद्ध

Pure.

### Vedana - वेदन

Awareness, cognition, experience.

Related to √**विद् - vid**, to know.

### Ātmikā - आत्मिका

Having the nature of.

Thus:

**शुद्धवेदनात्मिका - śuddhavedanātmikā**

Having pure cognition or awareness as her nature.

Now the philosophical structure is clear.

From the perspective of the limited individual, the Goddess's supreme nature is not recognized.

But in herself she remains pure conscious illumination.

### The sun behind the clouds

Imagine the Sun hidden by clouds.

From the observer's perspective, the Sun is obscured.

But the Sun hasn't necessarily ceased shining.

Now, this is only an analogy.

The supreme consciousness isn't a physical star.

And Māyā isn't literally a weather system.

But the distinction is helpful.

**Failure to recognize something is not necessarily evidence that it has ceased to exist.**

And that's precisely the structure of *akhyāti*, nonrecognition.

The divine phonemic powers remain.

Their ultimate nature remains consciousness.

But the limited knower apprehends their differentiated manifestations without recognizing their source.

### The ultimate paradox of language

Now the most interesting thing is that Abhinavagupta is explaining all this **through language**.

He uses Sanskrit.

Compounds.

Quotations.

Logical distinctions.

Classifications.

He quotes Sāṃkhya.

He quotes Spanda.

He quotes the Pūrvaśāstra.

And through those linguistic acts he attempts to disclose a reality that is not simply reducible to conventional verbal construction.

This is not a contradiction.

It's the same distinction from Part XVIII.

**A conventional explanation can point toward a nonconventional conscious ground.**

The explanation doesn't manufacture the ground.

It can help disclose it.

Now the Goddess is described as *suvrate*, one of beautiful observances.

And the text associates her with the uninterrupted manifestation of her own luminous nature.

So the whole discussion ends where it began.

The pure phonemic powers aren't destroyed.

Their source remains.

And the possibility of recognition remains.


## 21. The genuine boundary - Abhinavagupta now begins a new metrical synthesis

We stop at the end of Kashmir Series p. 211, immediately before:

**तदयमत्र सङ्क्षेपार्थः।**

*Tad ayam atra saṃkṣepārthaḥ.*

"Now this is the concise meaning here."

That's the natural transition.

The following text, beginning on p. 212, is no longer the same prose analysis of the fiftyfold *pratyayasarga* and the emergence of Māyic letters.

Abhinavagupta begins a substantial metrical synthesis of the preceding doctrine.

Its opening verse is:

NEXT SECTION'S OPENING · ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN METRICAL SYNTHESIS

**स्वातन्त्र्यैकरसावेशचमत्कारैकलक्षणा।**

**परा भगवती नित्यं भासते भैरवी स्वयम्॥**

*Svātantryaikarasāveśacamatkāraikalakṣaṇā.*

*Parā bhagavatī nityaṃ bhāsate bhairavī svayam.*

"The supreme Goddess Bhairavī shines forth eternally of herself, characterized by the wonder of immersion in the single relish of freedom."

That is a beautiful beginning for Part XXIV.

And it deserves its own extended reading.

The next section will distinguish the universal self-revealing nature of consciousness from its differentiated manifestations, trace the relation between pure and Māyic phonemic powers, and return to the supreme *Aham*.

But the specific argument of Part XXIII is complete.

We've followed the original prose from p. 209 through p. 211.

The pure phonemes enter cognitive manifestation.

Their nature becomes concealed.

The fiftyfold Sāṃkhya classification is invoked.

The limited *pratyayas* are identified as bonds.

The *Spandakārikā* explains the penetration of cognition by speech.

The loss of supreme nectar is discussed.

Then the pure phonemic powers become audibly manifested in the Māyic sequence.

The Pūrvaśāstra describes the Lord's production of corresponding sound-forms.

And the passage ends by distinguishing the Goddess's pure conscious nature from her nonrecognition by the limited subject.

That's the complete movement.

### Four writing exercises

**1. The five afflictive modes**

## अविद्या - अस्मिता - राग - द्वेष - अभिनिवेश

*Avidyā - asmitā - rāga - dveṣa - abhiniveśa.*

Notice the consonant cluster **द्व - dv** in *dveṣa* and the long vowels in *avidyā* and *asmitā*.


**2. The verb of concealment**

## स्वरूपमावृण्वते

*Svarūpam āvṛṇvate.*

They cover their own nature.

Pay attention to the syllabic **ऋ - ṛ** and the conjunct **न्व - nva**.


**3. The penetration of speech**

## शब्दानुवेधेन न विना प्रत्ययोद्भवः

*Śabdānuvedhena na vinā pratyayodbhavaḥ.*

No arising of determinate cognition without penetration by speech.


**4. The Goddess's ultimate nature**

## प्रकाशा शुद्धवेदनात्मिका

*Prakāśā śuddhavedanātmikā.*

Manifest luminosity, having the nature of pure conscious awareness.


## 24. The final insight - The alphabet never ceased to be divine

Now here's the philosophical insight I want to retain.

We began with the pure Mātṛkā.

The divine Mother of phonemic manifestation.

Her powers expressed the uncontracted freedom of consciousness.

Then the same phonemes entered the manifestation of cognitive dispositions.

And those dispositions became involved in limitation.

Error.

Incapacity.

Premature satisfaction.

Differentiated accomplishment.

The finite subject apprehended the world through increasingly specific structures.

And those structures could conceal their conscious ground.

Now the crucial point is this:

**The phonemic powers did not become independently existing evil substances.**

Their ultimate nature was never outside consciousness.

The concealment belongs to the mode of manifestation and recognition.

That's why the *Spandakārikā* says that the powers arise to cover the subject's own nature.

And why it associates determinate cognition with the penetration of speech.

The very power that articulates the world can also reinforce the limited subject's identification with it.

But the same power can serve recognition.

That's why Abhinavagupta is able to use Sanskrit commentary as a means of philosophical disclosure.

He isn't attempting to abolish all language.

He is investigating its ultimate ground.

And the *Pūrvaśāstra* makes the relationship visible through the generation of Māyic phonemes corresponding to their pure prototypes.

The audible letters are different in mode.

Sequential.

Articulated.

Associated with finite cognition.

But they remain embraced by their divine source.

Now think about the word:

**अहम् - aham.**

At the level of the limited subject:

"I am this body."

"I am this opinion."

"I am this particular history."

At the supreme level:

The self-revealing conscious reality through which those very identifications become manifest.

The same ordinary word can be implicated in very different modes of understanding.

And that's the entire point of the *Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa*.

The sacred alphabet is not simply a collection of sounds that automatically confer realization.

Its deepest meaning belongs to the recognition of the conscious power through which all sounds become manifest.

**Bondage is not the destruction of consciousness. It is the failure to recognize the fullness of consciousness within the very powers through which experience appears.**

And that is why liberation remains possible.

The source has never been fundamentally lost.

The phonemes remain expressions of Śakti.

The supreme Goddess remains:

**शुद्धवेदनात्मिका - śuddhavedanātmikā.**

Having pure awareness as her nature.

The mind may construct a thousand identities.

The words may become entangled in misunderstanding.

The cognitive dispositions may become bonds.

But the conscious ground has not become a second, independently ignorant Absolute.

Its own freedom includes the possibility of recognition.

And that's where Abhinavagupta now turns.

From the mechanism of bondage to a beautiful synthesis of the supreme conscious reality.

The next installment opens with the Goddess Bhairavī shining forth of herself.

Not dependent upon another light.

Not requiring another consciousness to reveal her.

And not fundamentally divided by the differentiated language through which her own nature is explained.
