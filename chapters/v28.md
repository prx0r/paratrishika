# Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa

PART XXVIII - The Universe Emitted from Śiva

*Abhinavagupta has finished examining the entire human body as Bhairava's Heart. Breath, the subtle body, 112 vital points, ritual substances, Kālī, the destruction of constricting doubt-the whole magnificent business.*

*Now he returns to the alphabet. Fourteen vowels. The mysterious terminal Visarga. A consonant hiding between **ष** and **ह**. And the extraordinary proposition that everything from an ordinary clay pot to the highest divine power is continuously emitted from Śiva and reabsorbed into Śiva.*

*Then comes a truly diabolical philosophical question: if the very ground of existence is liberation, how the hell does it become the terrifying desert of saṃsāra?*

*And finally, Utpaladeva appears with a verse that overturns the simplistic notion that liberation means having no thoughts.*

Kashmir Series pp. 237-238 · Somadeva Vasudeva's GRETIL transcription, Gnoli pp. 267-268 · Abhinavagupta's prose, a difficult metrical quotation, and Utpaladeva's Īśvarapratyabhijñā 4.12


## 1. Previously, in the cosmic madhouse

In Part XXVII, Abhinavagupta explained **योनिव्याप्ति - yonivyāpti**, the pervasion of the generative matrix.

He began with the changing conditions of embodied existence:

Childhood, youth, old age and the assumption of another body.

Then the five elements.

Prāṇa and Apāna.

The eightfold subtle complex, *puryaṣṭaka*.

The void.

The five designations of the knowing subject.

And the 112 *marmas* through which bodily existence could be contemplated as Bhairava's manifestation.

But the really important move came afterward.

He described **शङ्का - śaṅkā**, constricting doubt, as the first sprout of the enormous tree of saṃsāra.

He examined the ultimate status of religious purity distinctions.

He invoked the *Spandakārikā*.

Then the *Sarvācāra*, declaring:

**मन्त्रा वर्णात्मकाः सर्वे वर्णाः सर्वे शिवात्मकाः।**

*Mantrā varṇātmakāḥ sarve varṇāḥ sarve śivātmakāḥ.*

"All mantras consist of phonemes; all phonemes have Śiva as their nature."

And the *Kramastotra* brought in Kālī, who draws even the power of limitation back into herself.

The section concluded:

**विज्ञानक्रमो विस्तरत उक्तः।**

*Vijñānakramo vistarata uktaḥ.*

"The sequence of understanding has been explained in detail."

Now, at Kashmir Series p. 237, the commentary turns back to the phonemic explanation of the root Tantra's Heart-seed.

And this is important: we're not starting a new root-Tantra verse. **Abhinavagupta is continuing his commentary on the previously quoted root verses**, especially the cryptic description of the fourteenfold vowel-power, *tithīśānta* and the third *brahma*.

The text is short enough to follow closely, but dense enough to contain an entire system of cosmology.

There are also two genuine textual disagreements:

- The Kashmir Series locates an element between **ओ - o** and **औ - au**; GRETIL instead transmits a location between **o** and **aṃ**. The latter fits the usual phonemic enumeration more coherently.
- The unusually elaborate stanza about fire arising from water and saṃsāra arising from liberation has different readings in its final pāda. Its broad argument is clear, but its entire syntax cannot be translated with equal certainty.

We will keep those uncertainties visible. ; ; \]

Right.

Back to the Goddess's vowels.

The cosmic accountant has returned.

And he's going to be rather surprised to learn that the entire universe is somehow hidden inside the letters between **ओ** and **अं**.


## 2. गुणा इच्छाद्याः - The three powers have already been established

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN PROSE · P. 237

**गुणा इच्छाद्या निर्णीताः।**

*Guṇā icchādyā nirṇītāḥ.*

"The qualities or powers beginning with Icchā have been established."

A compact statement.

But philosophically enormous.

Let's take it apart.

### Guṇa - गुण

Quality, constituent, property, strand.

Masculine nominative plural:

**गुणाः - guṇāḥ**.

Now be careful.

The word *guṇa* has many technical uses in Indian philosophy.

In classical Sāṃkhya it famously designates:

*Sattva.*

*Rajas.*

*Tamas.*

But that is not the present enumeration.

Here Abhinavagupta refers to the divine powers beginning with **Icchā**.

He isn't silently substituting the Sāṃkhya three guṇas for Śiva's three powers.

### Icchā - इच्छा

Will.

Desire or intention.

In Trika, an intrinsic power of the supreme consciousness.

### Ādi - आदि

Beginning with.

The compound:

**इच्छाद्याः - icchādyāḥ**.

"Those beginning with Icchā."

Now the context supplies the other two:

**ज्ञान - jñāna**

Knowledge.

**क्रिया - kriyā**

Action.

Together:

**इच्छाशक्ति - icchāśakti**

Will-power.

**ज्ञानशक्ति - jñānaśakti**

Knowledge-power.

**क्रियाशक्ति - kriyāśakti**

Action-power.

### Nirṇīta - निर्णीत

Established, determined, settled.

From:

**निर् - nir**

Out, thoroughly.

**√नी - nī**

To lead.

The past passive participle:

**निर्णीत - nirṇīta**.

Masculine nominative plural:

**निर्णीताः - nirṇītāḥ**.

The word agrees with *guṇāḥ*.

### The structure

Abhinavagupta isn't beginning by proving these powers again.

He says they've already been established.

The entire previous exposition has shown how manifestation unfolds through their distinguishable functions.

Now the phonemic Heart is interpreted in their light.

### The three cosmic departments

Imagine Śiva's administrative office.

Icchā:

"I will manifest the universe."

Jñāna:

"I illuminate and determine what manifests."

Kriyā:

"I bring the differentiated appearance into expression."

The accountant asks:

"Are you three independent Absolutes?"

All three stare at him.

"No."

"Do you operate one after another in measurable cosmic seconds?"

"Not ultimately."

"Then why are you numbered?"

"Because their functions are distinguishable in explanation."

This is the crucial point.

**The three Śaktis articulate the sovereign freedom of one consciousness. They are not three independently ultimate minds cooperating from separate offices.**

And that becomes even more important when the prose shortly describes *Trika* as the nature of everything that appears.

But first, the vowels.


## 3. चतुर्दश स्वरेभ्यः - Fourteen vowels, and a remarkably consequential variant

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S PHONEMIC IDENTIFICATION · P. 237

The Kashmir Series proceeds with:

**चतुर्दश स्वरेभ्यः।**

*Caturdaśa svarebhyaḥ.*

The phrase invokes the fourteen-vowel series already established in the sacred phonemic exposition.

The immediately following expression identifies an intervening position.

But the witnesses disagree about its boundaries.

**ओकार-औकारमध्यगः**

*Okāra-aukāra-madhyagaḥ.*

Now look at the distinction.

**Between *o* and *aṃ* stands *au*.**

But in the ordinary consecutive arrangement there isn't a separate phoneme between *o* and *au*.

So GRETIL's reading is internally intelligible.

The Kashmir Series reading is problematic if interpreted as a straightforward account of the position of the fourteenth vowel.

And here's something important: this is not merely our conjecture.

A scholarly study of mantra extraction quotes the corresponding passage with **okāra-aṃkāramadhyagaḥ**, matching GRETIL's reading and explicitly discussing the *sauḥ* seed. ; \]

### Does that prove the Kashmir Series is wrong?

Not conclusively.

We'd need a fuller critical comparison of the underlying manuscripts.

But the GRETIL reading has strong contextual support.

The phrase makes excellent sense as a *phonemic location clue* for **au**, the fourteenth vowel.

The Kashmir Series **o-au** reading should therefore be preserved as a divergent and difficult witness, not silently harmonized.

### Madhyaga - मध्यग

Situated in the middle.

**मध्य - madhya**

Middle.

**ग - ga**

Going, situated.

Related to √**गम् - gam**, to go.

Thus:

**मध्यग - madhyaga**

Located between.

Masculine nominative singular:

**मध्यगः - madhyagaḥ**.

### Okāra - ओकार

The phoneme *o*.

**ओ - o**

The vowel.

**कार - kāra**

Phonemic designation.

Now compare:

**औकार - aukāra**

The phoneme *au*.

And:

**अंकार - aṃkāra**

The designation of *aṃ*, the nasalized or anusvāra-associated element in this traditional listing.

### The cosmic accountant's worst manuscript variant

Imagine Abhinavagupta handing you a phonemic puzzle.

"Find the letter between *o* and *aṃ*."

You count.

"Au."

"Excellent."

Then the Kashmir Series hands you another copy.

"Find the letter between *o* and *au*."

You count again.

Nothing.

You check the alphabet.

Still nothing.

You check it upside down.

Still nothing.

The manuscript has created a philosophical traffic jam.

The correct scholarly response is not to invent a missing letter.

It is to preserve the witnesses and identify the reading that makes the better sense.

And that is precisely what we're doing.

Now the next phrase identifies Visarga.

And after that, the third *brahma*.

We're about to reconstruct the sacred seed from its component positions.


## 4. तिथीशान्तो विसर्गः - Visarga at the end of the lunar powers

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN PROSE · P. 237

**तिथीशान्तो विसर्गः।**

*Tithīśānto visargaḥ.*

"Visarga is the terminal principle associated with the lord of the lunar days."

There it is.

After the fourteen vowels, Abhinavagupta identifies the emissive power.

### Tithi - तिथि

Lunar day.

We've studied this repeatedly.

The lunar sequence corresponds symbolically to differentiated powers of manifestation.

### Īśa - ईश

Lord, ruler.

From √**ईश् - īś**, to rule.

Thus:

**तिथीश - tithīśa**

Lord of the lunar days.

### Anta - अन्त

End, extremity, terminal element.

Thus:

**तिथीशान्त - tithīśānta**

Terminal element associated with the lord of the lunar days.

The phrase is transmitted in the connected form:

**तिथीशान्तो - tithīśānto**.

The nominative ending *-aḥ* has become *-o* through external sandhi before the voiced sound of *visargaḥ*.

### Visarga - विसर्ग

Emission, release.

From:

**वि - vi**

Apart, outward.

**√सृज् - sṛj**

To emit, release or bring forth.

Thus:

**विसर्ग - visarga**

Emission.

Now the same word denotes the phonetic sign:

**ः**

But the commentarial meaning is larger.

Visarga is the power of emission through which manifestation unfolds.

It can be represented through a phonetic sign without being reducible to the printed mark.

### The difference between *aṃ* and *aḥ*

Now this is especially important for learning the alphabet.

**अं - aṃ**

Anusvāra-associated or nasal element.

**अः - aḥ**

Visarga.

Those are not interchangeable.

One involves nasal resonance in the relevant phonological description.

The other represents a postvocalic voiceless breath-like emission, with pronunciation varying according to traditional phonetic conditions.

In Tantric exegesis they acquire further symbolic significance.

But the basic written and phonological distinction remains.

### The sixteenfold arrangement

The symbolic sequence may therefore be understood through:

Fourteen vowels.

Anusvāra or Bindu as a further element.

Visarga completing the set.

That gives a sixteenfold structure.

And we've already seen how some systems interpret a seventeenth transcending power beyond the sixteen.

The important point here is not to flatten all those alternative enumerations into one universal Sanskrit alphabet.

Abhinavagupta is working with a sacred phonemic system.

**Its count depends upon the particular classification being used.**

### The cosmic emission valve

Imagine a philosopher looking at the Visarga sign.

Two tiny dots.

"That's it?"

"Yes."

"That's the symbol of divine emission?"

"Within this interpretation."

"You're telling me the entire universe is connected with those two dots?"

"The sign represents a power; it is not a little physical machine that manufactures galaxies."

The philosopher looks disappointed.

He had hoped for a very economical cosmological engine.

But the distinction matters.

The sign is a sacred designation.

The doctrine concerns consciousness's expressive freedom.

And now we need the final consonantal element.

The third *brahma*.

Its location is astonishingly precise.

**Between ṣa and ha.**


## 5. तृतीयं ब्रह्म ष-हमध्यगम् - The consonant hiding between Ṣa and Ha

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN PROSE · P. 237

**तृतीयं ब्रह्म ष-हमध्यगम्।**

*Tṛtīyaṃ brahma ṣa-ha-madhyagam.*

"The third *brahma* is situated between ṣa and ha."

Now this is one of the most beautiful little phonemic riddles in the commentary.

Because the answer is:

**स - sa**.

Let's see why.

The sacred consonantal ending is commonly arranged:

# श

śa

# ष

ṣa

# स

sa

# ह

ha

# क्ष

kṣa

Now, between:

**ष - ṣa**

And:

**ह - ha**

Stands:

**स - sa**.

That's the location clue.

### Tṛtīya - तृतीय

Third.

From the numeral:

**त्रि - tri**

Three.

The ordinal:

**तृतीय - tṛtīya**.

Neuter singular:

**तृतीयम् - tṛtīyam**.

### Brahma - ब्रह्म

The sacred or supreme principle.

Neuter noun.

The grammar matters.

We have:

**तृतीयं ब्रह्म**

Not:

**तृतीयो ब्रह्मा**.

The latter would be a different grammatical construction referring to masculine Brahmā.

In this sacred phonemic context, *brahma* belongs to the interpretation of the divine letter-group.

### Ṣa - ष

The retroflex sibilant.

In IAST:

**ṣa**.

### Ha - ह

The consonant *ha*.

### Madhyaga - मध्यग

Situated in the middle.

Thus:

**ष-हमध्यगम् - ṣa-ha-madhyagam**

Situated between *ṣa* and *ha*.

The neuter ending **-am** agrees with *brahma*.

### Why is *sa* the third?

Within the three-sibilant series:

**श - śa**

First.

**ष - ṣa**

Second.

**स - sa**

Third.

But in the broader fivefold sacred consonantal sequence:

**śa, ṣa, sa, ha, kṣa**

It is also the third member.

So the clue works through the traditional ordered sequence.

### The complete seed

We now have all three elements:

| Clue | Element |
|--------|--------|
| Fourteenth vowel, between *o* and *aṃ* in the better-supported reading | **औ - au** |
| Third *brahma*, between *ṣa* and *ha* | **स - sa** |
| Terminal emissive principle | **ः - ḥ** |

Together:

# सौः

sauḥ

And that is why Abhinavagupta concludes:

**एतद्बीजं वस्तुतो विश्वस्य।**

*Etad bījaṃ vastuto viśvasya.*

"This is, in truth, the seed of the universe."

### Etat - एतत्

This.

Neuter singular.

### Bīja - बीज

Seed.

Neuter singular:

**बीजम् - bījam**.

### Vastutaḥ - वस्तुतः

In reality, actually, truly.

Related to:

**वस्तु - vastu**

Thing, reality.

The adverbial formation:

**वस्तुतः - vastutaḥ**.

### Viśvasya - विश्वस्य

Of the universe.

Genitive singular.

Now the sentence is extraordinary.

The three-element heart-seed is not presented merely as a formula for a particular ritual accomplishment.

It is described as **the seed of the universe**.

### What kind of seed?

Now we need to be precise.

A mango seed contains biological material and develops under suitable physical conditions.

The heart-seed is not that sort of seed.

It is a sacred concentration of the powers of consciousness.

The consonant.

The vowel.

The terminal emission.

Their arrangement represents the relation between the supreme ground and its manifestation.

**The seed is a phonemic expression of cosmic generation, not a tiny physical universe hiding inside an audible syllable.**

And the next sentence explains why.

Because absolutely everything that appears belongs to the three divine powers.

Then arises in the supreme Śiva-state.

And returns to it.

The metaphysical drama is about to begin.


## 6. यत्किञ्चित् सत् - Absolutely anything that appears

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN PROSE · PP. 237-238

**तथाहि - यत्किञ्चित् सत्।**

*Tathā hi - yat kiṃcit sat.*

"For whatever exists…"

Now that's a magnificent opening.

No exceptions established in advance.

No privileged class of objects.

No demand that the object be obviously sacred.

Whatever exists.

### Tathā hi - तथाहि

For indeed, because, thus.

An explanatory expression introducing the reason for the preceding claim.

### Yat kiṃcit - यत्किञ्चित्

Whatever, anything whatsoever.

**यत् - yat**

What, which.

**किञ्चित् - kiṃcit**

Something, anything.

Together:

**यत्किञ्चित् - yatkiṃcit**

Whatever.

### Sat - सत्

Existing, real, being.

A present participial form related to √**अस् - as**, to be.

Now this is a fundamental Sanskrit word.

**सत् - sat**.

Being.

The real.

That which is.

But here it functions within an argument about whatever appears as existent.

The next phrase explains the differentiated forms of such being.

**पार्थिवप्राकृतमायीय रूपम्।**

*Pārthivaprākṛtamāyīya-rūpam.*

"Having the form of what is earthly, Prakṛti-derived, or Māyic."

Let's break it down.

### Pārthiva - पार्थिव

Earthly, pertaining to earth.

Related to:

**पृथिवी - pṛthivī**

Earth.

The adjective:

**पार्थिव - pārthiva**.

This can represent the gross or materially differentiated end of the ontological field.

### Prākṛta - प्राकृत

Pertaining to Prakṛti.

From:

**प्रकृति - prakṛti**

The source or nature in the differentiated cosmological system.

Thus:

**प्राकृत - prākṛta**.

Now the adjective also has other meanings, including ordinary or natural, and names Prakrit language traditions.

But here it belongs to the ontological classification.

### Māyīya - मायीय

Belonging to Māyā.

From:

**माया - māyā**.

The adjective:

**मायीय - māyīya**.

Within Trika, this relates to the differentiated and limiting field.

### Rūpa - रूप

Form, nature.

The compound identifies various levels of manifest existence.

Now the philosophical point is that **anything that appears through those levels is not excluded from the Heart's power**.

### The pot returns

Imagine an ordinary pot.

Made of clay.

Sitting on a table.

It has a location.

A shape.

A history.

A material composition.

Now, within the Trika metaphysical framework, these features belong to differentiated manifestation.

But the pot doesn't therefore become an independently ultimate reality outside consciousness.

The same applies to the thoughts through which it is recognized.

### A crucial qualification

Abhinavagupta isn't saying that a pot's physical material properties are unreal in every practical respect.

The pot can hold water.

It can break.

It can be moved.

It differs from a person.

The distinction concerns the **ultimate ground of its manifest existence**, not the denial of its conventional functions.

And that's why the Sanskrit continues with the three powers.

Will.

Knowledge.

Action.

Whatever exists is associated with them.

And through their unity, the entire field is emitted into Śiva.

Then emitted from Śiva.

The sentence is beautifully paradoxical.

Let's read it.


## 7. इच्छायां ज्ञाने वा क्रियायां वा - Every manifestation belongs to will, knowledge or action

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN PROSE · P. 237

**तदिच्छायां ज्ञाने वा क्रियायां वा पतितमपि।**

*Tad icchāyāṃ jñāne vā kriyāyāṃ vā patitam api.*

"Even when that falls within will, knowledge or action…"

Now this is an extremely compact classification of manifestation.

Let's learn each term.

### Tad - तत्

That.

Referring to whatever exists in the preceding sentence.

### Icchāyām - इच्छायाम्

In will.

Locative singular.

**इच्छा - icchā**

Will.

The locative ending of this feminine *-ā* noun is:

**-आयाम् - -āyām**.

### Jñāne - ज्ञाने

In knowledge.

Locative singular of:

**ज्ञान - jñāna**.

Now compare the endings.

**इच्छायाम् - icchāyām**

Feminine *-ā* stem.

**ज्ञाने - jñāne**

Neuter *-a* stem.

Different declensions.

### Kriyāyām - क्रियायाम्

In action.

Locative singular of:

**क्रिया - kriyā**.

Again, feminine *-ā* stem.

### Vā - वा

Or.

Repeated in the passage.

### Patita - पतित

Fallen, entered, come within a condition.

From √**पत् - pat**, to fall.

Neuter singular:

**पतितम् - patitam**.

### Api - अपि

Even.

Now the wording doesn't necessarily mean every manifestation belongs exclusively to one of three mutually isolated departments.

The very next phrase says that it has the nature of **all**.

And is therefore Trika-form.

That's crucial.

### The three powers as functional distinctions

Imagine an event.

An object appears.

The conscious power of manifestation involves intention, disclosure and the actual appearing of a differentiated configuration.

Those can be described through:

Icchā.

Jñāna.

Kriyā.

But the powers are inseparable aspects of the supreme conscious freedom.

The distinctions belong to explanation.

Not to a competition between three independent divine subjects.

### The philosophical importance

If an object appears through the knowledge-power, that doesn't mean it's outside will and action at the ultimate level.

If action predominates in a particular manifestation, that doesn't make knowledge an alien substance.

The powers are distinguishable within the same ground.

Which leads to the next phrase:

**सर्वात्मकत्वात् त्रिकरूपम्।**

*Sarvātmakatvāt trikarūpam.*

"Because it has the nature of everything, it is Trika-form."

And that is the real point.


## 8. सर्वात्मकत्वात् त्रिकरूपम् - Everything is Trika-form

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN PROSE · P. 237

**सर्वात्मकत्वात् त्रिकरूपम्।**

*Sarvātmakatvāt trikarūpam.*

"Because it has the nature of everything, it is of the form of Trika."

Now here we reach the exact reason for bringing the three powers back into the discussion.

Abhinavagupta is not saying that some objects belong to Icchā, some exclusively to Jñāna, and the remainder exclusively to Kriyā.

He says that even when an appearance is considered under one of those powers, its ultimately all-inclusive nature makes it **Trika-form**.

### Sarva - सर्व

All.

Everything.

### Ātmaka - आत्मक

Having the nature of.

Thus:

**सर्वात्मक - sarvātmaka**

All-inclusive in nature.

### -Tva - त्व

Abstract condition.

Thus:

**सर्वात्मकत्व - sarvātmakatva**

The state of possessing the nature of everything.

### Ablative ending -āt - आत्

The complete form:

**सर्वात्मकत्वात् - sarvātmakatvāt**.

"Because of all-inclusiveness."

This is an ablative of cause.

The grammar tells us why the thing is *trikarūpa*.

### Trika - त्रिक

Triad, group of three.

Within this tradition, the threefold divine structure has several interconnected expressions.

Śiva, Śakti and Nara.

Parā, Parāparā and Aparā.

Will, knowledge and action.

But those different triads are not simply interchangeable lists whose members can be equated without reference to the particular context.

**Here the immediately preceding triad is Icchā-Jñāna-Kriyā.**

So that is our primary interpretive reference.

### Rūpa - रूप

Nature, form.

Thus:

**त्रिकरूप - trikarūpa**

Having the nature of Trika.

Neuter singular:

**त्रिकरूपम् - trikarūpam**.

### The crucial philosophical point

Now think about an ordinary act of cognition.

You look at a flower.

The flower appears.

You recognize it.

Perhaps you desire to pick it up.

Then you act.

We can distinguish:

Disclosure.

Intention.

Activity.

But the manifest occurrence of cognition and action doesn't establish three unrelated conscious substances.

Within Trika, these are powers of one conscious reality.

And the ultimate ground of the entire field is Śiva.

### The cosmic teamwork incident

Imagine the universe appearing.

Icchā says:

"My work."

Jñāna says:

"Excuse me. Without knowledge, how would the manifestation be determined?"

Kriyā says:

"And without action, what would be expressed?"

They begin arguing.

Śiva asks:

"Are you independent of the conscious freedom through which you operate?"

Silence.

The departmental rivalry has been resolved.

The same event can be approached through distinguishable powers.

But its ultimate nature is not divided into competing Absolutes.

That's why **sarvātmakatva** matters.

And the next phrase gives the decisive theological movement.

Whatever appears through the triad is emitted into the supreme Śiva-state.

Then the entire universe is emitted from that very state.

Yes.

Both directions.

And the same Sanskrit verb.

This is where the ontology becomes really beautiful.


## 9. परत्र शिवपदे विसृज्यते - The universe returns into the Śiva-state

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN PROSE · PP. 237-238

**परत्र शिवपदे विसृज्यते।**

*Paratra śivapade visṛjyate.*

"It is released or emitted into the supreme Śiva-state."

Now this is an important phrase.

And it creates a subtle difficulty in translation.

Because **visṛjyate** ordinarily belongs to the semantic field of release, emission, sending forth.

But here the direction is:

**Into the supreme Śiva-state.**

So we must allow the context to determine the sense of the release.

The differentiated field is released back toward its supreme ground.

### Para - पर

Higher, beyond, supreme.

### Paratra - परत्र

Beyond, elsewhere, in the higher state.

An indeclinable adverb in this usage.

### Śiva - शिव

Śiva.

### Pada - पद

State, station, position, realm.

Locative singular:

**पदे - pade**.

Thus:

**शिवपदे - śivapade**

In the Śiva-state.

### Visṛjyate - विसृज्यते

Is released, sent forth or emitted.

From:

**वि - vi**

Apart, outward, extensively.

**√सृज् - sṛj**

To release, emit or create.

The form:

**विसृज्यते - visṛjyate**

Third-person singular passive present.

Now the grammar matters.

The subject is the manifested reality we've been following.

The event is described as a release into the supreme Śiva-state.

### Why not simply translate "destroyed"?

Because that would suggest that the differentiated appearance becomes absolute nonexistence.

The language of the passage is more subtle.

The object belongs to the Trika powers.

And its ultimate ground is the Śiva-state.

Its return is not necessarily the annihilation of a second independent substance.

Rather, what was manifested within the conscious power is understood through its source.

### The relation to Saṃhāra

Recall:

**सृष्टि - sṛṣṭi**

Manifestation, emission.

**संहार - saṃhāra**

Reabsorption, withdrawal.

The present passage uses the verb of release in both directions, but its structure evokes this familiar dynamic.

Manifestation belongs to Śiva.

Withdrawal belongs to Śiva.

Nothing acquires an independently ultimate existence outside the same ground.

### The cosmic boomerang

Imagine the whole universe as a boomerang.

It appears.

Differentiates.

Objects emerge.

Subjects emerge.

Thoughts emerge.

Then the entire manifest field is understood as returning to its conscious source.

But here's the catch.

A literal boomerang travels through space.

The supreme conscious ground is not another physical destination at the far end of the universe.

So the analogy is limited.

**The return is a relation of ontological dependence and recognition, not necessarily physical travel.**

This is precisely the teaching of Spanda from our earlier installments.

Movement without ultimate displacement into another substance.

And the next phrase completes the paradox.

The universe doesn't merely return into Śiva.

It comes forth from Śiva.

Continuously.


## 10. सर्वं च शिवपदाद्विसृज्यते - Everything is emitted from Śiva

Now the Sanskrit says:

**सर्वं च शिवपदाद्विसृज्यते।**

*Sarvaṃ ca śivapadād visṛjyate.*

"And everything is emitted from the Śiva-state."

There it is.

The reverse direction.

And the same verb.

### Sarvam - सर्वम्

Everything.

Neuter singular, used collectively.

### Ca - च

And.

### Śivapadāt - शिवपदात्

From the Śiva-state.

Ablative singular.

Now compare the preceding form:

**शिवपदे - śivapade**

In the Śiva-state.

And:

**शिवपदात् - śivapadāt**

From the Śiva-state.

That is a beautiful case lesson.

| Form | Case | Meaning |
|--------|--------|--------|
| **शिवपदे** | Locative | In the Śiva-state |
| **शिवपदात्** | Ablative | From the Śiva-state |

Now the whole passage expresses:

**Into Śiva.**

**From Śiva.**

Both movements are manifestations of the same conscious reality.

### The philosophical point

If the world emerges from Śiva and is gathered into Śiva, then Śiva isn't simply an object situated before creation.

Nor is Śiva a destination that exists only after everything else has disappeared.

The Śiva-state is the ultimate ground through which both manifestation and reabsorption are intelligible.

### Does this require a temporal beginning?

Not necessarily.

A novice might picture:

First, Śiva was alone.

Then, at a particular moment, Śiva created the universe.

Later, Śiva destroyed it.

But Abhinavagupta's next sentence explicitly complicates that picture.

He calls this process **uninterrupted**.

And **nirvikalpaka**.

The distinction between temporal stages belongs to the mode in which manifestation is apprehended.

The supreme ground isn't dependent upon an independently existing clock.

### The cosmic shipping department

Imagine Śiva running a warehouse.

The universe ships out.

Then returns.

Then ships out.

Then returns.

The clerk asks:

"How many shipments?"

"Without interruption."

"When did the first one leave?"

"Your question presupposes a temporal sequence that belongs to the manifestation itself."

The clerk quits.

This is the fifth accountant we've lost.

But the analogy points toward the central problem.

**A temporal sequence can appear within consciousness without requiring the conscious ground itself to be produced at an earlier point in time.**

And the next phrase makes this especially clear.


## 11. अविरतमेष एव प्रबन्धो निर्विकल्पकः - The uninterrupted nonconceptual continuum

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN PROSE · P. 237

**इत्यविरतमेष एव प्रबन्धो निर्विकल्पकः।**

*Ity aviratam eṣa eva prabandho nirvikalpakaḥ.*

"Thus, this very continuum is uninterrupted and nonconceptual."

Now we have reached the philosophical centre of this passage.

Let's dismantle every important word.

### Avirata - अविरत

Uninterrupted, unceasing.

**अ - a-**

Not.

**विरत - virata**

Ceased, stopped, withdrawn.

Related to *vi-√ram*, to cease.

Thus:

**अविरत - avirata**

Unceasing.

The adverbial use:

**अविरतम् - aviratam**

Without interruption.

### Eṣaḥ - एषः

This.

Masculine nominative singular.

### Eva - एव

Precisely, indeed, this very.

### Prabandha - प्रबन्ध

Continuity, connected sequence, arrangement, sustained course.

Related to:

**प्र - pra**

Forth.

**√बन्ध् - bandh**

To bind or connect.

Thus:

**प्रबन्ध - prabandha**

Connected continuity.

Now the word is important.

It suggests a process or connected unfolding.

Not isolated events floating without relation.

### Nirvikalpaka - निर्विकल्पक

Free from conceptual differentiation.

From:

**निर् - nir**

Without.

**विकल्प - vikalpa**

Conceptual construction, determination.

With the adjectival formation:

**निर्विकल्पक - nirvikalpaka**.

Masculine nominative singular:

**निर्विकल्पकः - nirvikalpakaḥ**.

Now this adjective modifies the *prabandha*, the uninterrupted continuum.

### The subtle philosophical issue

How can a continuum include all differentiated manifestation and nevertheless be *nirvikalpaka*?

Because the distinction between intrinsic conscious manifestation and conceptual determination is fundamental.

The supreme reality doesn't need to formulate a separate linguistic judgment in order to manifest.

It doesn't require a sentence:

"Now I shall create blue."

Followed by:

"Now I shall create a pot."

Followed by:

"Now I shall create time."

The differentiations belong to its expressive freedom.

Their appearance doesn't presuppose an externally imposed conceptual plan.

### Nirvikalpaka does not mean unconscious

This is extraordinarily important.

People sometimes hear *nirvikalpa* and imagine:

Blankness.

Total absence.

A state of unconsciousness.

Or a metaphysical void resembling general anaesthesia.

But that isn't sufficient to express Abhinavagupta's claim.

The supreme consciousness is **self-revealing**.

Its nonconceptuality doesn't make it inert.

Nor does it remove its power to manifest.

### The distinction from Vikalpa

**निर्विकल्पक - nirvikalpaka**

Nonconceptual.

**विकल्प - vikalpa**

Conceptual construction.

Now here's the really interesting thing.

Abhinavagupta immediately says:

**विकल्पोऽपि - vikalpo 'pi.**

"Even conceptual determination…"

And then explains how conceptual differentiation arises through the same continuous conscious ground.

The argument is not:

"Nirvikalpa is holy; vikalpa is an independent evil substance."

The argument is much more subtle.

**Conceptual determination itself belongs to the expressive power of the nonconceptual ground.**

And that's precisely where the next example becomes useful.

The text introduces ordinary experience-apparently including the remembered enjoyment of a woman-and the three temporal forms:

Was.

Is.

Will be.

The universe has now become a grammar lesson about time.

Which is rather appropriate, because time itself is part of the problem.


## 12. विकल्पोऽपि - Even conceptual thought belongs to the same power

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN PROSE · KASHMIR SERIES PP. 237-238

**विकल्पोऽपि।**

*Vikalpo 'pi.*

"Even conceptual determination."

Those two words make an enormous difference.

Because Abhinavagupta has just characterized the uninterrupted continuum as nonconceptual.

Now he explicitly includes *vikalpa*.

Let's learn the grammar.

### Vikalpa - विकल्प

Conceptual determination, imagination, differentiation, construction.

The word can involve alternatives or the distinguishing of one thing from another.

Related to:

**वि - vi**

Apart, distinctly.

**√कॢप् - kḷp**

To arrange, order or conceive.

Thus:

**विकल्प - vikalpa**.

### Api - अपि

Even, also.

Now the sandhi:

*vikalpaḥ + api → vikalpo 'pi.*

The masculine nominative singular **-aḥ** becomes **-o**, and the initial *a* of *api* is elided.

The avagraha:

**ऽ**

Marks the elision in Devanāgarī.

### Why *api* matters

If Abhinavagupta had stopped with *nirvikalpakaḥ*, we might imagine that the supreme ground is completely unrelated to conceptual thought.

But **api** extends the argument.

Even conceptual differentiation belongs within the divine manifestation.

That's not the same thing as claiming every concept is correct.

A false judgment remains false at the conventional level.

But its occurrence as cognition does not create a second independent conscious substance.

### The difference between occurrence and accuracy

Imagine someone thinking:

"There's a tiger in the room."

But there's no tiger.

The cognition occurred.

The judgment was false.

Those are different facts.

Within Pratyabhijñā, the occurrence of the conceptual episode belongs to consciousness's power of manifestation.

Its correctness as a description of an object is another question.

This distinction is especially important because otherwise the doctrine can be misunderstood as claiming that everything anybody thinks must be true.

It doesn't.

**Consciousness's power to manifest a thought is not identical with that thought's factual accuracy.**

### The ordinary example

The text introduces an expression beginning:

**प्रमदारातिप्रभृति - pramadārātiprabhṛti**.

The compound appears to invoke a familiar domain of ordinary human experience, involving a woman and enjoyment or intimacy.

But the transmitted sentence is tightly compressed, and its exact segmentation is not entirely secure.

Let's separate what can be established.

### Pramadā - प्रमदा

Woman, often with a literary nuance of an attractive or charming woman.

### Rāti - राति

Pleasure, delight or an act of giving, depending on the lexical context.

The word can participate in expressions concerning intimate enjoyment.

The present compound admits a reading connected with enjoyment of a woman, but we should not pretend its entire syntax is transparent.

### Prabhṛti - प्रभृति

Beginning with, and so forth.

Now the general meaning is that **ordinary differentiated experiences**-not merely exalted spiritual events-can become subjects of temporal conceptual determination.

And that is the important feature of Abhinavagupta's example.

Something happened.

Something is happening.

Something will happen.

And the same conscious power makes all three judgments possible.

### The cosmic philosopher's romantic disaster

Imagine an ordinary person remembering an intimate experience.

"That happened."

Then thinking about the present.

"This is happening."

Then imagining a future encounter.

"This will happen."

The grammarian arrives:

"Excellent. Three tenses."

The philosopher arrives:

"Excellent. Three temporal determinations."

Abhinavagupta arrives:

"And what is the conscious ground through which all three are manifest?"

The romantic evening has now become an epistemological seminar.

Nobody asked for this.

But the point is genuinely important.

**The conceptual distinctions of past, present and future are themselves manifested within consciousness.**

Now let's look at the exact Sanskrit verbal forms.

Because Abhinavagupta deliberately places them side by side.


## 13. अभूत्, भवति, भविष्यति - Three times, one conscious manifestation

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN PROSE · P. 237

The transmitted passage contains:

**अभूत् … भवति … भविष्यति।**

*Abhūt … bhavati … bhaviṣyati.*

"Was … is … will be."

This is a wonderful Sanskrit lesson.

All three forms belong to √**भू - bhū**, to be or become.

But they present the event through different temporal determinations.

### Abhūt - अभूत्

Was, became, occurred.

A past verbal form.

The augment:

**अ - a-**

Marks the past formation.

The underlying root:

**√भू - bhū**.

Third-person singular:

**अभूत् - abhūt**.

### Bhavati - भवति

Is, becomes, occurs.

Present tense.

Third-person singular.

### Bhaviṣyati - भविष्यति

Will become, will be.

Future tense.

Third-person singular.

Now compare them.

| Sanskrit | IAST | Temporal orientation |
|--------|--------|--------|
| अभूत् | *abhūt* | Past |
| भवति | *bhavati* | Present |
| भविष्यति | *bhaviṣyati* | Future |

The common verbal root is important.

The event is conceptually placed into three different temporal relations.

But those relations don't require three independent conscious illuminators.

### The *evaṃkārī* expressions

The text also employs forms of:

**एवंकारी - evaṃkārī**.

"Acting in this way," or "one whose activity is of this kind."

Now this belongs to:

**एवम् - evam**

Thus, in this manner.

**कारिन् - kārin**

Doing, acting, producing.

From √**कृ - kṛ**, to do.

The compound can describe an agent or subject acting in a particular way.

The witnesses' word division around the past form is awkward, so we shouldn't manufacture a perfectly regular independent three-part Sanskrit sentence where the received text has a compressed continuous construction.

What is secure is the juxtaposition of the three temporal forms.

### Vartamānakālatraya - वर्तमानकालत्रय

Now comes the more difficult expression:

**वर्तमानकालत्रय…**

*Vartamānakālatraya…*

"Presentness and the three times," or the threefold temporal series apprehended in relation to the present.

Let's dismantle it.

### Vartamāna - वर्तमान

Present, occurring.

A participial formation from √**वृत् - vṛt**, to occur or turn.

### Kāla - काल

Time.

### Traya - त्रय

Triad, set of three.

Thus:

**कालत्रय - kālatraya**

The three times.

Traditionally:

Past.

Present.

Future.

### Anusaṃdhita - अनुसन्धित

Connected, brought into relation, synthesized.

Related to *anu-sam-√dhā*, to connect or bring together.

The transmitted compound is:

**वर्तमानकालत्रयानुसन्धितः**

*Vartamānakālatrayānusaṃdhitaḥ.*

Its exact case relationship to the surrounding words is compressed.

The general philosophical sense is the connection of temporal distinctions within the present manifestation of awareness.

### The subtle temporal paradox

Now think about remembering the past.

A past event is no longer occurring in the ordinary way.

But its memory is manifest now.

Think about anticipating the future.

The future event hasn't yet occurred in ordinary temporal experience.

But the anticipation is manifest now.

And the present event is also manifest.

So all three temporal determinations are apprehended within the current conscious field.

Now this doesn't mean past and future are conventionally identical.

An event that happened yesterday isn't the same temporal event as something expected tomorrow.

The distinction remains important.

But **the awareness through which they are apprehended is not divided into three independent ultimate substances**.

### The really profound point

Abhinavagupta has already argued that time is one of the differentiated powers of manifestation.

So the conscious ground cannot simply be understood as another object somewhere within the temporal series.

It is not located only in yesterday.

Or tomorrow.

Or one tiny instant called now.

Yet temporal cognition becomes apparent through it.

### The cosmic scheduling disaster

Imagine Śiva's assistant preparing the calendar.

"Monday: creation."

"Tuesday: maintenance."

"Wednesday: dissolution."

"What about the supreme conscious ground?"

"Is it available Thursday?"

"Yes."

"And Monday?"

"Yes."

"And before the calendar?"

"The calendar itself belongs to the manifestation."

The assistant is furious.

"How can I schedule a meeting with something that isn't confined to a single date?"

Exactly.

That is why the philosophy distinguishes the ground from the temporal determinations that appear within it.

### The connection with Vikalpa

A *vikalpa* can represent:

What was.

What is.

What will be.

But the occurrence of those conceptual determinations remains part of the supreme power of manifestation.

Therefore:

**Vikalpa is not an independent principle outside consciousness.**

And the phrase that follows makes this absolutely explicit.

The differences do not destroy the ultimate nonduality of Visarga.


## 14. अभेदपरमार्थतयैव विसर्गः - Emission remains ultimately nondual

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN PROSE · END OF P. 237

The sentence concludes:

**अभेदपरमार्थतयैव विसर्गः।**

*Abhedaparamārthatayaiva visargaḥ.*

"Visarga is precisely of the nature of ultimate nondivision."

Now that is a magnificent conclusion.

We've just encountered:

Three divine powers.

The entire manifest universe.

Emission into Śiva.

Emission from Śiva.

The uninterrupted nonconceptual continuum.

Conceptual determination.

Past, present and future.

And now all of it is gathered into the ultimate reality of **abheda**.

Nondivision.

Let's learn the Sanskrit.

### Bheda - भेद

Difference, division.

From √**भिद् - bhid**, to split.

### Abheda - अभेद

Nondivision, nondifference.

**अ - a-**

Not.

**भेद - bheda**

Division.

Thus:

**अभेद - abheda**.

### Paramārtha - परमार्थ

Ultimate reality, highest truth.

**परम - parama**

Supreme.

**अर्थ - artha**

Meaning, reality, object, purpose.

Thus:

**परमार्थ - paramārtha**.

### Paramārthatā - परमार्थता

The condition of being ultimate reality.

### Abhedaparamārthatā - अभेदपरमार्थता

The ultimate nature consisting in nondivision.

The instrumental singular:

**अभेदपरमार्थतया - abhedaparamārthatayā**.

Now add:

**एव - eva**

Precisely.

Through sandhi:

**अभेदपरमार्थतयैव - abhedaparamārthatayaiva**.

Because final **ā** plus initial **e** yields **ai**.

### Visarga - विसर्ग

Emission.

Now the claim is explicit.

**The emission of differentiation doesn't establish ultimate separation.**

And this is the philosophical heart of the entire passage.

### The difference between manifestation and dualism

Imagine a consciousness manifesting an image.

The image has a form.

A colour.

A position.

It can be distinguished from another image.

But the manifestation of difference does not by itself prove the existence of two independent conscious grounds.

Within Trika, the supreme manifestation belongs to the freedom of one conscious reality.

The difference is **real as manifestation**, but not independently ultimate.

### Why this matters for the Heart-seed

The whole discussion began by identifying **sauḥ**.

The seed of the universe.

Now we can understand why Visarga is essential to that identification.

The seed represents not simply a static divine object but the power of emission.

And the emission doesn't break the unity of the ground.

That's why the Heart can be both:

The source of manifestation.

And the ultimate reality in which manifestation finds repose.

### The dangerous oversimplification

Now don't reduce this to:

"Nothing is different, so nothing matters."

That's not the doctrine.

A person and a stone are distinguishable manifestations.

Actions have consequences.

Knowledge can be correct or incorrect.

A finite subject can experience bondage or liberation.

The distinctions are meaningful.

The philosophical claim concerns their **ultimate relation to the self-revealing ground**.

That is what *abhedaparamārthatā* expresses.

And it leads to the most disturbing question in this part of the commentary.

If the supreme Śiva-state is intrinsically liberation, how can the appearance of saṃsāra arise within it?

How can the very ground of freedom become the terrifying wilderness of bondage?

Abhinavagupta doesn't answer with a tidy diagram.

He gives us a rather spectacular image.

A desert.

A forest.

A fire emerging from water.

And a terrifying power arising from the very abode of nectar.

The cosmic universe has become a philosophical nightmare.


## 15. मोक्षमयशिवभूमिरेव संसारभयमरुमहाटवी - The forest of bondage grows inside liberation

"The very Śiva-ground whose nature is liberation has become the vast desert-wilderness of the fear of saṃsāra."

**सदैव दैवदग्धानाम् - sadaiva daivadagdhānām.**

Now that is an incredibly powerful image.

### Mokṣa - मोक्ष

Now the noun:

**मोक्ष - mokṣa**.

### Maya - मय

**मोक्षमय - mokṣamaya**

Consisting of liberation.

Now don't confuse this suffix with:

**माया - māyā**.

They're different forms.

### Śiva - शिव

Śiva.

### Bhūmi - भूमि

The Śiva-ground whose nature is liberation.

### Eva - एव

Precisely, itself.

The emphatic particle matters.

It isn't saying some separate place of bondage arose outside the Śiva-ground.

**The very ground of liberation** becomes apprehended in the terrifying form.

### Sadaiva - सदैव

Always.

**एव - eva**

Indeed.

Through sandhi:

### Daiva - दैव

Fate, divine destiny, fortune.

### Dagdha - दग्ध

Burned, scorched.

**दग्ध - dagdha**.

### Saṃsāra - संसार

The cycle of worldly existence and bondage.

### Bhaya - भय

Fear.

Thus:

**संसारभय - saṃsārabhaya**

Fear of saṃsāra.

### Maru - मरु

Desert, arid wilderness.

### Mahā - महा

Great.

### Aṭavī - अटवी

Forest, wilderness.

Now:

**मरुमहाटवी - marumahāṭavī**

A vast desert-wilderness.

This is wonderfully extravagant imagery.

Desert and forest brought together into one immense, terrifying landscape.

### Saṃpannā - सम्पन्ना

Become, arisen, turned into.

From *sam-√pad*, to arrive at or become.

Feminine nominative singular.

It agrees with the feminine *śivabhūmiḥ* and its resulting *aṭavī* characterization.

### The philosophical paradox

Now here's the question.

If the ultimate ground is liberation, why does bondage appear at all?

The text doesn't answer by introducing a second independent principle of evil.

Nor by declaring that Śiva has literally ceased to be Śiva.

The difference concerns **the mode of manifestation and apprehension**.

For the finite, contracted subject, the same conscious field appears as the threatening cycle of saṃsāra.

Birth.

Death.

Loss.

Fear.

Separation.

Desire.

Frustration.

All the miserable business.

But its ultimate ground remains the one consciousness described as Śiva.

### The world's worst holiday destination

Imagine a travel agent offering you two destinations.

"First, the Śiva-ground of liberation."

"Sounds wonderful."

"Second, the terrifying desert-forest of saṃsāra."

"Sounds awful."

"Which would you prefer?"

"Obviously liberation."

The agent looks at the map.

"Interesting. They're the same ultimate ground."

"Excuse me?"

"The difference lies in the mode through which the reality is apprehended."

The customer throws the brochure across the room.

An understandable reaction.

But philosophically, that's the point.

**The apparent separation between bondage and liberation is not a division into two independently ultimate conscious substances.**

### Does this deny suffering?

No.

That would be a serious mistake.

The bound subject's experience of suffering remains meaningful within manifestation.

The doctrine doesn't establish that pain is imaginary in the trivial sense.

Nor that suffering people should be told their distress is unreal.

It asks a different question.

**What is the ultimate conscious ground of the experience, including the experience of limitation?**

And whether recognition of that ground transforms the relation of the subject to its own manifestations.

That's where Pratyabhijñā comes in.

### The distinction between reality and its constricted apprehension

The same reality can become manifest through different configurations of knowledge.

At the finite level:

"I am this separate individual."

"I am restricted."

"I am subject to time."

"I am threatened by the world."

At the universal level:

The entire differentiated manifestation is recognized as belonging to conscious freedom.

The difference is not merely that one person has happier thoughts.

It's the **scope of self-apprehension**.

And that's why Abhinavagupta describes the transformation so dramatically.

Liberation becomes, for those scorched by fate, the terrifying wilderness of bondage.

And then he introduces a stanza with two astonishing natural impossibilities.

Fire springing from water.

And an image involving the full nectar-bearing Moon and the destructive force of the thunderbolt.

The point is the same paradox.

Saṃsāra arising from the very ground of freedom.


## 16. जलात्स्फूर्जज्ज्वाला - Fire emerging from water

METRICAL STANZA IN THE COMMENTARY · P. 238 · AUTHOR NOT EXPLICITLY IDENTIFIED

The stanza opens:

**जलात्स्फूर्जज्ज्वालाजटिलवडवावह्निनिवहः।**

*Jalāt sphūrjajjvālājaṭilavaḍavāvahninivahaḥ.*

"A multitude of submarine fires, tangled with flashing flames, arising from water."

Now that's absolutely magnificent Sanskrit.

And a nightmare for anybody trying to pronounce it without taking the compound apart.

Let's dismantle it.

### Jala - जल

Water.

Ablative singular:

**जलात् - jalāt**.

From water.

### Sphūrjat - स्फूर्जत्

Flashing, bursting forth, resounding or blazing.

Related to √**स्फूर्ज् - sphūrj**, to flash, thunder or burst forth.

Now in the compound:

**स्फूर्जत् - sphūrjat**

Becomes:

**स्फूर्जज्ज् - sphūrjajj-**

Before the following consonantal sound through the relevant sandhi and assimilation.

This is a particularly unpleasant cluster for pronunciation.

### Jvālā - ज्वाला

Flame.

From √**ज्वल् - jval**, to blaze.

Notice the conjunct:

**ज्व - jva**

**ज् + व**.

Now the compound:

**स्फूर्जज्ज्वाला - sphūrjajjvālā**

Flashing flames.

### Jaṭila - जटिल

Tangled, matted, dense.

Related to:

**जटा - jaṭā**

Matted hair.

Thus:

**जटिल - jaṭila**

Entangled, thickly interwoven.

The flames are described as densely tangled or blazing in profusion.

### Vaḍavā - वडवा

A mare.

But here it belongs to a mythological compound referring to the **submarine or mare-mouth fire**.

### Vahni - वह्नि

Fire.

Related to √**वह् - vah**, to carry.

Now:

**वडवावह्नि - vaḍavāvahni**

The submarine fire associated with the mare-mouth myth.

In Sanskrit cosmology and poetry, this is a familiar image of an extraordinary fire associated with the ocean.

### Nivaha - निवह

Mass, multitude, collection.

Thus:

**वडवावह्निनिवह - vaḍavāvahninivaha**

A mass of submarine fire.

Masculine nominative singular:

**निवहः - nivahaḥ**.

### The complete compound

Now put it together:

*jalāt*

From water.

*sphūrjajjvālā*

With flashing flames.

*jaṭila*

Thickly tangled.

*vaḍavāvahni*

Submarine fire.

*nivahaḥ*

A multitude.

The entire image is deliberately paradoxical.

**Water, the apparent opposite of fire, becomes the source of an immense blaze.**

### Why this image?

Because Abhinavagupta has just described the ground of liberation appearing as saṃsāra.

And the poet expresses the paradox through fire emerging from water.

But we should not misread the verse as an empirical claim that seawater ordinarily ignites itself.

The imagery draws upon mythological and poetic cosmology.

Its function here is philosophical.

### The cosmic fire department

Imagine the ocean calling the fire brigade.

"There's a fire."

"Where?"

"In the water."

"Excuse me?"

"Underwater."

"How extensive?"

"An enormous mass of flashing flames."

The firefighters consult the Sanskrit poets.

Apparently this is a perfectly normal occurrence in mythological cosmology.

But the point is wonderfully effective.

The thing that appears to contradict fire becomes the setting in which fire arises.

Likewise:

**The very ground of liberation becomes manifest, to the contracted subject, as the terrifying experience of bondage.**

### The crucial distinction

The analogy doesn't mean water and fire are conventionally identical.

Nor does it mean bondage and liberation are indistinguishable experiences.

It means that the relationship between ultimate ground and manifest appearance can be paradoxical from the limited standpoint.

And the next image intensifies that paradox.

The full abode of nectar.

The thunderbolt.

Fear.

And destruction.

Unfortunately, the Sanskrit transmission becomes difficult at precisely this point.

We can identify the imagery.

But we should not fabricate an exact grammatical construction where the witnesses are uncertain.


## 17. सुधाधाम्नः पूर्णात् - The nectar-abode and the destructive thunderbolt

SECOND PĀDA OF THE SAME STANZA · P. 238

The second pāda includes:

**सुधाधाम्नः पूर्णात्…**

*Sudhādhāmnaḥ pūrṇāt…*

"From the full abode of nectar…"

And later:

**दम्भोलि - dambholi**

A thunderbolt or lightning-like weapon.

Now the poem is constructing another shocking juxtaposition.

The full nectar-bearing Moon.

And a destructive force associated with fear and the thunderbolt.

### Sudhā - सुधा

Nectar, ambrosia.

### Dhāman - धामन्

Abode, luminous domain, radiance.

Thus:

**सुधाधामन् - sudhādhāman**

Abode of nectar.

Genitive or ablative singular:

**सुधाधाम्नः - sudhādhāmnaḥ**.

The ending **-naḥ** is characteristic of the *-man* stem's oblique forms.

### Pūrṇa - पूर्ण

Full, complete.

From √**पॄ - pṝ**, to fill.

Ablative singular:

**पूर्णात् - pūrṇāt**.

Now the combination evokes the full Moon as the abode of nectar.

That is a familiar Tantric and poetic association.

### Bhaya - भय

Fear.

### Sadana - सदन

Abode, dwelling-place.

From √**सद् - sad**, to sit.

### Dambholi - दम्भोलि

Thunderbolt.

A lexical term associated with a destructive celestial weapon.

### Dalana - दलन

Breaking, splitting, crushing.

From √**दल् - dal**, to break.

Now the compound joins these elements, but its precise internal syntax is difficult.

The GRETIL transcription and Kashmir Series share the broad wording, yet the resulting structure is not perfectly transparent.

So the responsible reading is:

**The full nectar-bearing source is juxtaposed with a destructive thunderbolt-like manifestation.**

We can explain the philosophical function without claiming certainty about every syntactic relationship.

### The full Moon as a source of terror?

Imagine the Moon.

Full.

Luminous.

Traditionally associated with nectar.

Now something terrifying appears from that very fullness.

The poet asks:

How can the source of nectar become associated with destructive fear?

And the philosophical answer is that the manifestation of apparent contradiction belongs to the freedom of consciousness.

### The relation to the Heart

Remember the Heart-seed.

The lunar powers.

The fifteen *tithis*.

Visarga.

The sixteenth phase.

The seventeenth transcending power.

The Moon is not a random decorative image.

It resonates with the entire sacred phonemic exposition.

But we should distinguish resonance from explicit proof.

This particular stanza doesn't independently restate all sixteen or seventeen powers.

It employs familiar lunar and destructive imagery to dramatize the paradox.

### The editor's inserted gloss

The Kashmir Series inserts a square-bracketed explanatory note within the poem.

It describes the Moon's fullness from the tenth lunar day toward the full Moon and connects that condition with the appearance of a lightning-like power.

**That note is editorial.**

GRETIL does not reproduce it as part of the metrical stanza.

It should not be recited as though Abhinavagupta wrote an extra line of poetry.

; \]

### The cosmic meteorological catastrophe

Imagine the full Moon announcing:

"I am the abode of nectar."

Wonderful.

Then the sky produces a terrifying thunderbolt.

The observer looks up.

"How can this destructive manifestation arise from a symbol of fullness?"

The philosopher replies:

"Precisely the problem."

Saṃsāra appearing within the ground of liberation.

Difference emerging without establishing a second ultimate source.

The cosmic weather report has become an argument about *svātantrya*.

And now the stanza reaches its explicit philosophical language.

**Vikalpa.**

The expansion of divine sovereignty.

And the terror of worldly existence.

The metaphors are about to become unmistakably doctrinal.


## 18. विकल्पादैश्वर्यप्रसरसरणेः - Conceptual construction arising from the course of divine sovereignty

THIRD PĀDA OF THE DIFFICULT STANZA · P. 238

Now the poem turns directly to philosophy.

**विकल्पादैश्वर्यप्रसरसरणेः संसृतिदरः।**

*Vikalpād aiśvaryaprasarasaraṇeḥ saṃsṛtidaraḥ.*

The essential elements are:

"From conceptual determination, through the course of the expansion of divine sovereignty, the terror of worldly circulation…"

The exact syntactic relation remains compressed, but the core philosophical contrast is clear.

Let's dismantle it.

### Vikalpa - विकल्प

Conceptual determination.

We've spent an entire section learning this word.

But here it becomes the starting point for the paradoxical appearance of bondage.

### Vikalpāt - विकल्पात्

From conceptual construction.

Ablative singular.

Now observe the sandhi:

*vikalpāt + aiśvarya → vikalpād aiśvarya.*

The final *t* becomes voiced before the following vowel.

### Aiśvarya - ऐश्वर्य

Lordship, sovereignty, divine power.

From:

**ईश्वर - īśvara**

Lord, sovereign.

The abstract noun:

**ऐश्वर्य - aiśvarya**.

Now this is one of the central words in Pratyabhijñā.

The supreme conscious reality is not merely passively luminous.

Its intrinsic power is sovereign.

### Prasara - प्रसर

Expansion, spreading forth.

From *pra-√sṛ*, to move or flow forth.

### Saraṇi - सरणि

Course, pathway, progression.

Now:

**ऐश्वर्यप्रसरसरणि - aiśvaryaprasarasaraṇi**

The course of the expansion of divine sovereignty.

The genitive or ablative singular of the feminine *-i* stem appears as:

**सरणेः - saraṇeḥ**.

Thus:

**ऐश्वर्यप्रसरसरणेः - aiśvaryaprasarasaraṇeḥ**.

### Saṃsṛti - संसृति

Worldly circulation, cyclic becoming.

From *sam-√sṛ*, to flow or move.

Related in meaning to the more familiar:

**संसार - saṃsāra**.

### Dara - दर

Fear, dread, splitting or fracture in different lexical contexts.

Here the compound's immediate imagery supports the sense of terror or dread.

Thus:

**संसृतिदरः - saṃsṛtidaraḥ**

The dread of worldly circulation.

### The philosophical paradox

Now notice what's happening.

The same divine sovereignty that manifests the universe also manifests differentiated conceptual structures.

Those structures can be apprehended through limitation.

And then the world appears threatening.

Conscious freedom becomes experienced as bondage.

**Not because divine sovereignty has been annihilated, but because its manifestation is apprehended through contraction.**

### Does this mean conceptual thinking is automatically bondage?

No.

This is absolutely essential.

The stanza involves *vikalpa* in the generation of the frightening appearance.

But the subsequent quotation from Utpaladeva explicitly says that **Mahā-īśatā**, supreme lordship, can remain even while *vikalpas* expand.

So the problem cannot simply be the existence of any conceptual thought.

Otherwise the two passages would contradict one another.

The deeper issue is the relation between thought and self-recognition.

### The cosmic anxiety machine

Imagine the supreme consciousness manifesting an enormous field of possibilities.

Objects.

People.

Memories.

Desires.

Plans.

Risks.

The finite subject begins conceptualizing them.

"What if this happens?"

"What if that happens?"

"What if I lose everything?"

"What if I die?"

"What if the world is fundamentally separate from me?"

Soon an entire desert-forest of fear has appeared.

Now this is a useful illustration, not a claim that all anxiety is merely a philosophical mistake.

But it makes the doctrinal distinction vivid.

The conceptual process can organize an experience of separation and threat.

The nondual recognition does not require the fabrication of an entirely different conscious substance.

It changes the subject's apprehension of what is already manifest.

### The astonishing next line

Now the stanza exclaims:

**कियच्चित्रं चित्रं।**

*Kiyac citraṃ citram.*

"How wondrous-how extraordinary!"

The repetition is memorable.

And the final phrase links the phenomenon to a difficult reading concerning fate, destruction or expansion.

This is where the witnesses diverge.

We must keep them separate.


## 19. कियच्चित्रं चित्रं - The wonder, and a genuine textual corruption

FOURTH PĀDA · KASHMIR SERIES VS GRETIL

The last pāda begins:

**कियच्चित्रं चित्रं।**

*Kiyac citraṃ citram.*

"How astonishing, how astonishing!"

Now that's a wonderful poetic exclamation.

### Kiyat - कियत्

How much, how great, to what extent.

Before the following palatal consonant, the final dental undergoes sandhi:

**कियत् + चित्रम् → कियच्चित्रम्**.

*Kiyat + citram → kiyac citram.*

### Citra - चित्र

Variegated, wonderful, astonishing, strange.

Neuter singular:

**चित्रम् - citram**.

The repetition:

**चित्रं चित्रं - citraṃ citram**

Intensifies the wonder.

Now the verse is saying, in effect:

"How extraordinary that this whole terrifying manifestation can arise from the sovereign ground!"

But the final causal phrase is textually unstable.

### Kashmir Series

**हतविधिविकासात्प्रसरति।**

*Hatavidhivikāsāt prasarati.*

### GRETIL

*hatavidhivighātāt prasarati.*

Now this is not a trivial spelling difference.

**विकास - vikāsa**

Expansion, blossoming.

**विघात - vighāta**

Obstruction, destruction, violent interruption.

The resulting compounds have different meanings.

Let's learn them.

### Hata - हत

Struck, destroyed, afflicted.

From √**हन् - han**, to strike.

### Vidhi - विधि

Order, arrangement, fate, rule.

Within the poem's poetic context, the word can contribute a sense of fate or destiny.

### Vikāsa - विकास

Expansion, blossoming.

From *vi-√kas*, to open or unfold.

Ablative:

**विकासात् - vikāsāt**.

### Vighāta - विघात

Obstruction, destruction, blow.

From *vi-√han*, to strike or interrupt.

Ablative:

**विघातात् - vighātāt**.

### Prasarati - प्रसरति

Spreads forth, expands, proceeds.

From *pra-√sṛ*, to move forth.

Third-person singular present.

### Which reading is better?

The surrounding argument concerns paradoxical manifestation through divine freedom.

Both *vikāsa*, expansion, and *vighāta*, disruption, can be made contextually intelligible.

But the lexical choice changes the sense.

The GRETIL reading has a more explicitly destructive force.

The Kashmir Series reading emphasizes expansion or blossoming.

Neither can be treated as secure merely because it produces a satisfying English sentence.

Without a fuller manuscript collation, we should record the disagreement.

### The philosophical conclusion of the stanza

The broad argument is nevertheless clear.

The supreme ground is liberation.

Yet through differentiated manifestation and conceptual apprehension, the terrible appearance of saṃsāra arises.

The poet responds with wonder.

Not because saṃsāra has become a second Absolute.

But because the sovereign power can manifest extraordinary apparent opposition within itself.

### The authorial question

One more important distinction.

The stanza appears as metrical material in Abhinavagupta's commentary.

GRETIL marks it as a quotation.

But the immediate passage doesn't supply an explicit named authority.

Nor does it say:

**मयैव स्तोत्रे - mayaiva stotre**, "as I myself said in a hymn."

So we shouldn't confidently attribute it to a particular original work or claim Abhinavagupta personally composed it.

Its location is secure.

Its named authorship is not.

### The philosophical wonder

Imagine the entire universe as one enormous theatre.

The stage is conscious freedom.

The actors are differentiated powers.

One actor plays liberation.

Another plays bondage.

Another plays the finite subject.

Another plays the terrifying forest of saṃsāra.

The theatre is the same.

The performances differ.

And the finite actor, having identified completely with the role, forgets the ground on which the performance takes place.

Now the analogy isn't perfect.

But it captures the central distinction.

**The manifestation of bondage does not require a second independently ultimate conscious reality.**

And then comes the crucial question.

What happens when the subject recognizes the whole manifestation as its own divine power?

Does the play have to end?

Do all thoughts have to disappear?

Must every conceptual distinction be annihilated?

This is precisely where Abhinavagupta invokes Utpaladeva.

But first, the poem's metre.

Because the very form of the stanza deserves attention.


## 20. Metre laboratory - The poem is in Śikhariṇī

The stanza uses the seventeen-syllable **Śikhariṇī** metre.

This is not the ordinary eight-syllable anuṣṭubh of the root Tantra.

It is a much more elaborate literary metre.

Its standard pattern is:

**ya - ma - na - sa - bha - la - ga**

In light and heavy syllables:

$$
\underbrace{\shortsmile,-,-}_{ya}\quad
\underbrace{-,-,-}_{ma}\quad
\underbrace{\shortsmile,\shortsmile,\shortsmile}_{na}\quad
\underbrace{\shortsmile,\shortsmile,-}_{sa}\quad
\underbrace{-,\shortsmile,\shortsmile}_{bha}\quad
\shortsmile\quad -
$$

Seventeen syllables in each pāda.

The usual caesurae occur after the sixth and eleventh syllables. ](https://www.wisdomlib.org/hinduism/essay/the-ratnavali-natika-and-the-chandrakala-natika-study/d/doc1888637.html); \]

Now let's examine the opening pāda.

**जलात्स्फूर्जज्ज्वालाजटिलवडवावह्निनिवहः।**

*Jalāt sphūrjajjvālājaṭilavaḍavāvahninivahaḥ.*

The syllabic division is:

ja-lāt-sphūr-jaj-jvā-lā &#124; ja-ṭi-la-va-ḍa &#124; vā-vah-ni-ni-va-haḥ

6 + 5 + 6 = 17 syllables

Now the weight pattern:

⏑ - - - - - &#124; ⏑ ⏑ ⏑ ⏑ ⏑ &#124; - - ⏑ ⏑ ⏑ -

This matches the expected Śikhariṇī structure.

### Why the consonant clusters matter

Look at:

**स्फूर्ज् - sphūrj**

The consonant sequence closes the syllable.

It is heavy.

Now:

**ज्ज्वा - jjvā**

A complex cluster involving *j*, *j* and *v* before the long *ā*.

The long vowel independently makes the syllable heavy.

Now:

**वडवा - vaḍavā**

Three syllables:

*va-ḍa-vā.*

The final *vā* is heavy because of its long vowel.

### Why metre can help with textual criticism

If a transmitted reading produces the wrong number of syllables, that may indicate corruption.

But there's a limitation.

Different words can occupy the same metrical shape.

For example, the disputed final-pāda readings:

*vikāsāt.*

And:

*vighātāt.*

Both can fit the relevant metrical position.

So the metre alone doesn't settle the variant.

That's an important lesson.

**Metre can constrain the possibilities without always identifying the correct reading.**

### The beautiful match between form and content

Now here's the literary achievement.

The poem describes:

Fire emerging from water.

Terror arising from nectar.

Saṃsāra appearing within the ground of liberation.

And it does so in a highly controlled seventeen-syllable metre.

The imagery is explosive.

The metre is disciplined.

That's a beautiful combination.

The philosophical universe is apparently on fire.

But the Sanskrit syllables remain perfectly organized.

Abhinavagupta would appreciate the irony.


## 21. ईश्वरप्रत्यभिज्ञायामप्युक्तम् - Utpaladeva enters

ABHINAVAGUPTA INTRODUCES AN INDEPENDENT PHILOSOPHICAL AUTHORITY · P. 238

After the extraordinary stanza, Abhinavagupta writes:

**ईश्वरप्रत्यभिज्ञायामप्युक्तम्।**

*Īśvarapratyabhijñāyām apy uktam.*

"And it has also been said in the Īśvarapratyabhijñā."

Now this is a major moment.

Because the preceding argument has raised a philosophical difficulty.

The supreme ground is nonconceptual.

Yet conceptual determinations arise.

They can sustain the appearance of bondage.

But does that mean the actual presence of thought is the fundamental problem?

Utpaladeva's answer is much more subtle.

And Abhinavagupta quotes him.

The authority is Utpaladeva.

His Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā is one of the major foundational works of the Recognition school.

Now let's learn the introduction.

### Īśvara - ईश्वर

Lord, sovereign.

From the √**ईश् - īś** family.

### Pratyabhijñā - प्रत्यभिज्ञा

Recognition.

This is one of the most beautiful words in the whole tradition.

Let's dissect it.

**प्रति - prati**

Toward, back, in relation to.

**अभि - abhi**

Toward, directly.

**√ज्ञा - jñā**

To know.

Thus:

**प्रत्यभिज्ञा - pratyabhijñā**

Recognition.

The knowing of something as what it already is.

In this philosophical context, recognition of one's supreme conscious nature.

### The locative

**ईश्वरप्रत्यभिज्ञायाम् - īśvarapratyabhijñāyām**.

"In the Īśvarapratyabhijñā."

Feminine locative singular.

### Api - अपि

Also.

### Uktam - उक्तम्

Said, stated.

From √**वच् - vac**, to speak.

Now the phrase is a direct attribution.

The following verse is not Abhinavagupta's newly composed poetry.

It is **Utpaladeva's**.

### The numbering question

The verse is conventionally numbered **Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā IV.12**, or **4.12**.

GRETIL's note beside the quotation in the *Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa* instead labels it *Āgamādhikāra 2.12*.

The independent Sanskrit edition of Abhinavagupta's *Īśvarapratyabhijñāvimarśinī* explicitly marks the verse **Ipk\_4.12**, and the independent kārikā text agrees.

We're discussing the same attested verse, not two different sayings. For consistent referencing, we'll use **IPK 4.12**. ; ; \]

Now here it is.

The philosophical climax of this installment.


## 22. सर्वो ममायं विभवः - The whole universe is my manifestation

UTPALADEVA · ĪŚVARAPRATYABHIJÑĀKĀRIKĀ 4.12 · AUTHENTIC VERSE

**सर्वो ममायं विभव इत्येवं परिजानतः।**

**विश्वात्मनो विकल्पानां प्रसरेऽपि महेशता॥**

*Sarvo mamāyaṃ vibhava ity evaṃ parijānataḥ.*

*Viśvātmano vikalpānāṃ prasare 'pi maheśatā.*

My close translation:

"For the one whose Self is the universe, who recognizes, 'All this is my manifestation and splendour,' there is the condition of being the Great Lord even while conceptual determinations continue to expand."

That's an extraordinary statement.

And an extraordinarily precise response to the preceding problem.

**Thought can still arise.**

**Conceptual differentiation can still expand.**

Yet the universal recognition is not thereby destroyed.

The difference lies in the nature of the apprehending subject.

Now let's learn every part.

### Sarvaḥ - सर्वः

All, the whole.

Masculine nominative singular.

The connected form is:

**सर्वो - sarvo**.

The final visarga becomes *o* before the following voiced consonant.

### Mama - मम

My, of me.

Genitive singular of the first-person pronoun.

Now this word is philosophically explosive.

Because the verse says:

**मम - mama**.

Mine.

And in ordinary life, "mine" is a favourite word of the finite ego.

My body.

My possessions.

My status.

My opinions.

My territory.

My bloody intellectual achievements.

But Utpaladeva's *mama* is qualified by what comes later.

**Viśvātman.**

The one whose Self is the universe.

So it is not merely the finite ego trying to expand its possessions.

That's crucial.

### Ayam - अयम्

This.

Masculine nominative singular.

Together:

**मम अयम् - mamāyam**.

The sandhi:

*mama + ayam → mamāyam.*

Because:

**a + a → ā**.

### Vibhava - विभव

Manifestation, power, splendour, prosperity, expansion.

Related to:

**वि - vi**

Distinctly, extensively.

**√भू - bhū**

To be or become.

Thus:

**विभव - vibhava**

Manifested power, splendour.

Now here's the subtlety.

The word can mean wealth or prosperity in ordinary Sanskrit.

But in this philosophical context, it has a much more fundamental meaning.

**The manifest splendour of consciousness's sovereign power.**

The entire world as the manifestation of the Self.

### The first clause

**सर्वो ममायं विभवः।**

*Sarvo mamāyaṃ vibhavaḥ.*

"All this is my manifestation."

That's the underlying quotation marked by *iti*.

The transmitted continuous verse has:

**विभव इत्येवं - vibhava ity evaṃ**.

The external sandhi explains the disappearance of the isolated nominative visarga before the following *i*.

### Iti - इति

Thus.

Marks the quoted cognition.

### Evam - एवम्

In this way.

### Parijānataḥ - परिजानतः

Of one who fully recognizes.

Now here's a great morphology lesson.

The verb:

**परिजानाति - parijānāti**

Fully knows, recognizes.

From:

**परि - pari**

Completely, around.

**√ज्ञा - jñā**

To know.

The present participle:

**परिजानत् - parijānat**.

Genitive singular:

**परिजानतः - parijānataḥ**.

Now this is crucial.

The verse is describing **the person who recognizes in this way**.

It isn't merely issuing a command to repeat a sentence.

### The difference between saying and recognizing

Imagine someone saying:

"All this is my manifestation."

Now that could be an empty declaration.

It could be a delusion of personal grandeur.

It could be a philosophical quotation memorized without understanding.

Or it could be an expression of the universal recognition Utpaladeva intends.

The Sanskrit distinguishes the latter through:

**परिजानतः - parijānataḥ**.

Of the one who *recognizes*.

Not merely the one who utters.

That's why this verse belongs to Pratyabhijñā.

The point is the transformation of self-apprehension.

Not the inflation of the finite ego.

### The cosmic property dispute

Imagine a person announcing:

"Everything in the universe is mine!"

A neighbour replies:

"Then please stop taking my furniture."

The philosopher asks:

"Do you mean you're a finite person who owns every object?"

"No. The verse concerns universal I-consciousness."

"Then does it abolish conventional property relations?"

"No."

"Does it justify ignoring other people's interests?"

"Also no."

Now we're getting somewhere.

**Universal self-recognition is not a claim of personal legal ownership over the world.**

It's a metaphysical recognition of the manifest universe as inseparable from the supreme conscious Self.

And the next half of the verse explains why that recognition matters.

Even conceptual thought can continue.

The condition of Mahā-īśa remains.


## 23. विश्वात्मनो विकल्पानां प्रसरेऽपि महेशता - Even while concepts expand, there is Lordship

UTPALADEVA · IPK 4.12, SECOND HALF

**विश्वात्मनो विकल्पानां प्रसरेऽपि महेशता।**

*Viśvātmano vikalpānāṃ prasare 'pi maheśatā.*

"For the one whose nature is the universe, even during the expansion of conceptual determinations, there is the state of the Great Lord."

This is perhaps the most important sentence in Part XXVIII.

Let's dismantle it carefully.

### Viśva - विश्व

Universe, all.

### Ātman - आत्मन्

Self, essential nature.

Thus:

**विश्वात्मन् - viśvātman**

One whose Self is the universe.

Genitive singular:

**विश्वात्मनः - viśvātmanaḥ**.

In sandhi:

**विश्वात्मनो - viśvātmano**.

Now this is grammatically linked to the preceding:

**परिजानतः - parijānataḥ**.

Of the one who recognizes.

The knowing subject is being described through universal selfhood.

### Vikalpa - विकल्प

Conceptual construction, determination.

Genitive plural:

**विकल्पानाम् - vikalpānām**.

Of conceptual determinations.

### Prasara - प्रसर

Expansion, spreading forth.

Locative singular:

**प्रसरे - prasare**.

In the expansion.

### Api - अपि

Even.

The phrase:

**प्रसरेऽपि - prasare 'pi**.

The avagraha marks the elided initial vowel of *api*.

Now this is the central contrast.

**Even during the expansion of vikalpas.**

Not:

"Only if every vikalpa has completely ceased."

### Maheśatā - महेशता

The state of the Great Lord.

Let's dismantle it.

**महा - mahā**

Great.

**ईश - īśa**

Lord, sovereign.

Through compound sandhi:

*mahā + īśa → maheśa.*

Because:

**ā + ī → e**.

Thus:

**महेश - maheśa**

Great Lord.

The abstract suffix:

**-ता - -tā**.

Thus:

**महेशता - maheśatā**

The condition of Great Lordship.

Feminine nominative singular.

Now that is the grammatical predicate of the verse.

The recognized subject has *maheśatā*.

Even while conceptual determinations continue.

### The philosophical distinction

Now this is extremely important.

The preceding prose characterized the continuous process of manifestation as *nirvikalpaka*.

And the stanza described the terror of saṃsāra arising through *vikalpa*.

A superficial reading might conclude:

"All thoughts are bondage."

But Utpaladeva refuses that simplification.

**Thoughts can remain without destroying the supreme recognition.**

Why?

Because their relation to the conscious subject is different.

For the bound subject:

Conceptual determinations can be experienced as external limitations defining the self.

For the liberated subject:

The same general capacity for conceptual manifestation is recognized as belonging to the universal conscious freedom.

The issue is not merely the numerical count of thoughts.

It's the nature of the **recognizing subject**.

### A modern psychological caution

This doesn't imply that every person who claims universal consciousness is fully liberated.

Nor that intrusive thoughts, mental distress or difficulties with attention are evidence of spiritual failure.

The verse is a philosophical statement about the relation of recognition and conceptuality.

It isn't a medical diagnostic rule.

And it doesn't establish that one must force thoughts to disappear.

### The liberation misconception

Imagine a meditator attempting to become free.

A thought arises.

"Bloody hell. Still bound."

Another thought.

"Still bound."

Another.

"I must eliminate this one."

Then another.

"I must eliminate the thought about eliminating thoughts."

The procedure has become an infinite thought-removal factory.

Utpaladeva's verse introduces another possibility.

**The decisive issue is the recognition of the ground through which the thoughts arise.**

A concept can occur without the subject taking it to be an independently ultimate division of consciousness.

Now, this doesn't make every concept wise.

But it changes the interpretation of their occurrence.

And that's why the verse is so important within the wider Pratyabhijñā doctrine.

It supports the possibility of liberation without requiring total permanent suspension of ordinary mental activity.


## 24. Metre laboratory - Utpaladeva's anuṣṭubh is absolutely precise

Unlike the preceding Śikhariṇī stanza, Utpaladeva's verse is composed in the familiar **anuṣṭubh**.

Four pādas.

Eight syllables each.

Let's scan them.

PĀDA 1

**सर्वो ममायं विभव**

*sar-vo-ma-mā-yaṃ-vi-bha-va*

8 syllables


PĀDA 2

**इत्येवं परिजानतः**

*it-ye-vaṃ-pa-ri-jā-na-taḥ*

8 syllables


PĀDA 3

**विश्वात्मनो विकल्पानां**

*viś-vāt-ma-no-vi-kal-pā-nāṃ*

8 syllables


PĀDA 4

**प्रसरेऽपि महेशता**

*pra-sa-re-'pi-ma-he-śa-tā*

8 syllables

Now notice the metrical elegance.

The first half introduces the recognition:

"All this is my manifestation."

The second half gives its consequence:

"Even while concepts expand, there is Great Lordship."

The philosophical structure and the metrical structure reinforce one another.

### The first eight syllables

*sar-vo-ma-mā-yaṃ-vi-bha-va.*

A complete quoted recognition in one pāda.

Now that's impressive.

### The second eight syllables

*it-ye-vaṃ-pa-ri-jā-na-taḥ.*

The one who recognizes thus.

Again, a complete grammatical unit.

### The third and fourth

The universal Self.

Conceptual determinations.

Their expansion.

And Great Lordship.

All in sixteen syllables.

A remarkably economical philosophical statement.

### The comparison with Abhinavagupta's preceding prose

Abhinavagupta's prose is dense.

Long compounds.

Multiple philosophical transitions.

Consonantal sandhi everywhere.

Then Utpaladeva's verse arrives and says the whole thing in thirty-two syllables.

The man has apparently condensed an entire metaphysical system into one little śloka.

Abhinavagupta must have admired that.

Especially because he spends thousands of words elsewhere explaining precisely why the verse is true.

### Why the metre doesn't settle the Kashmir Series's damaged mark

The Kashmir Series prints an unusual punctuation-like mark after:

**विकल्पाना.**

That is not a securely meaningful alternative Sanskrit ending.

The independent Īśvarapratyabhijñā and GRETIL both transmit:

**विकल्पानां - vikalpānām**.

The normal genitive plural.

And the metre supports it.

So we can restore the reading with confidence **because of independent textual attestation**, not merely because it sounds better.

That is a good example of genuine textual verification. ; \]


## 25. What Abhinavagupta says about this verse elsewhere

Here is where our source comparison becomes especially valuable.

The *Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa* quotes Utpaladeva's verse without providing a lengthy independent commentary in its main prose at this location.

But Abhinavagupta also wrote the great Īśvarapratyabhijñāvimarśinī.

And there, in his actual explanation of IPK 4.12, he makes the relation completely explicit.

He says the verse shows liberation **even without the disappearance of conceptual construction**.

This is not an inference we're imposing on the verse from modern nondual spirituality.

It's directly supported by Abhinavagupta's own explanation.

The introductory prose to IPK 4.12 says, in essence:

The preceding verse described liberation through the reduction of *vikalpa* and assimilation to the nonconceptual state. Now the next verse shows liberation even without that reduction.

That is a remarkable contrast.

### Two ways of understanding the relation to thought

In IPK 4.11, the philosophical context includes:

**विकल्पहान - vikalpahāna**

The disappearance or relinquishing of conceptual determinations.

And:

**एकाग्र्य - aikāgrya**

One-pointed concentration.

That represents one way of approaching the recognition of divine lordship.

But IPK 4.12 gives another.

The conceptual determinations may continue.

What matters is the recognition of their relation to the universal Self.

### The issue is not simply mental silence

Now here's a very common misunderstanding.

Someone reads about *nirvikalpa*.

They decide liberation must mean the permanent cessation of every thought.

But the Recognition tradition's treatment is more sophisticated.

The nonconceptual ground is fundamental.

Yet the presence of conceptual manifestation doesn't necessarily destroy realization.

That is what IPK 4.12 says.

### The difference between a bound and a liberated knower

Now imagine two people seeing a pot.

Both recognize it as a pot.

Both may form the thought:

"This is a pot."

So the verbal content can look similar.

But the philosophical account distinguishes their underlying self-apprehension.

One identifies as a finite subject fundamentally separated from the objects of cognition.

The other recognizes the manifest field as belonging to universal conscious freedom.

The same conventional judgment can occur.

But its ultimate interpretation differs.

### The *Vimarśinī*'s central phrase

Abhinavagupta explains that the conceptual creation itself is:

**स्वातन्त्र्यलक्षणो विभवः**

*Svātantryalakṣaṇo vibhavaḥ.*

"A manifestation characterized by sovereign freedom."

Now that's beautiful.

The very *vikalpa* that seems capable of sustaining bondage can be recognized as the expansion of conscious sovereignty.

Not because the concept's ordinary truth-conditions have disappeared.

But because its manifestation is no longer treated as a second independent ultimate reality.

### Svātantryalakṣaṇa - स्वातन्त्र्यलक्षण

Characterized by sovereign freedom.

**स्वातन्त्र्य - svātantrya**

Sovereign freedom.

**लक्षण - lakṣaṇa**

Characteristic.

Thus:

**स्वातन्त्र्यलक्षण - svātantryalakṣaṇa**.

### The *Vimarśinī*'s liberation claim

The commentary then describes a person whose recognition is firm and whose concepts have not necessarily disappeared.

Such a person may be:

**जीवन्नेव मुक्तः - jīvanneva muktaḥ**.

"Liberated while living."

Now remember Part XXIV.

We encountered *jīvanmukti* as a central feature of the tradition.

The present comparison gives an especially precise explanation.

**Liberation while living does not require that the person stop engaging in every conceptual and practical activity.**

The underlying relation to consciousness is what matters.

### The cosmic thought-eradication service

Imagine a meditation company advertising:

"Guaranteed liberation through total elimination of all thoughts."

A customer arrives.

"I had a thought."

"Failure."

Another thought.

"Failure."

A thought about having no thoughts.

"Catastrophic failure."

Then Utpaladeva arrives.

He says:

"Even when concepts expand, there can be Great Lordship for the one who recognizes the universe as the manifestation of the Self."

The company collapses.

A tragic commercial outcome.

But a significant philosophical development.

### The more demanding interpretation

Now don't mistake this for an easier shortcut.

It's not merely:

"Think whatever you like and call yourself liberated."

The verse requires **parijñāna**, full recognition.

And Abhinavagupta's commentary emphasizes the firmness of that recognition.

A conceptual assertion alone is not identical with realization.

We studied that in Part XXVI, when he warned against merely saying:

"I have entered the Heart."

The same distinction applies here.

The sentence:

"All this is my manifestation."

Can be memorized.

Repeated.

Written on a wall.

But the transformative recognition concerns the actual nature of the Self.

That is why the doctrine is called *Pratyabhijñā*.

Recognition.

Not mere affirmation.


## 26. The commentary inside the square brackets is not the main commentary

Now we must make a crucial textual distinction.

Immediately after the IPK verse, the Kashmir Series places a substantial explanation in **square brackets**.

It discusses the relationship between the finite subject, the universal Self, conceptual creation and liberation while living.

The important claim is that the apparent individual isn't another independently ultimate reality outside the supreme conscious Self.

It also says that when conceptual creation is recognized as an expression of divine freedom, liberation can remain even without the disappearance of all concepts.

Now here's the interesting discovery.

This bracket closely parallels Abhinavagupta's own explanation of IPK 4.12 in the separate *Īśvarapratyabhijñāvimarśinī*.

So its philosophical content is strongly related to authentic Abhinavaguptan material.

But that does **not** mean the bracket belongs to Abhinavagupta's uninterrupted prose in the *Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa* at this point.

That's the distinction.

### Three textual layers

We have:

**First:** Abhinavagupta's *Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa* prose.

It introduces the independent verse.

**Second:** Utpaladeva's IPK 4.12.

This is the authentic quoted kārikā.

**Third:** The Kashmir Series's square-bracketed explanatory note.

This supplies additional philosophical interpretation and substantially overlaps with the interpretation given in the separate *Īśvarapratyabhijñāvimarśinī*.

But it is editorially inserted into the first work.

We must not collapse those three textual layers.

; \]

### Why this matters

Imagine someone writing an academic paper.

They quote an explanatory passage from the Kashmir Series bracket.

Then cite it as:

"Abhinavagupta, Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa, p. 238."

Without explaining that the passage is bracketed.

Now the underlying philosophy may be authentically Abhinavaguptan.

But the cited location's textual status has been misrepresented.

A more careful treatment would distinguish:

The quoted IPK verse.

The edition's insertion.

And the parallel authentic explanation in the *Vimarśinī*.

That's what philological rigor requires.

### The bracket's final line

The Kashmir Series bracket also quotes:

**शङ्कापि नो विशङ्क्येत निःशङ्कत्वमिदं स्फुटम्।**

*Śaṅkāpi no viśaṅkyeta niḥśaṅkatvam idaṃ sphuṭam.*

"Even doubt should not itself become an object of anxious doubting; this is plainly freedom from constricting doubt."

The general sense is clear, but the nuance of *śaṅkā* and *viśaṅkyeta* is deliberately playful.

Now that phrase is especially appropriate after Part XXVII.

Remember the entire argument about *śaṅkā* as the first sprout of bondage.

Even the thought:

"Am I free from doubt?"

Can itself become another form of binding apprehension.

### The infinite doubt-recursion incident

Imagine someone attempting to become free of doubt.

"Do I doubt?"

"Yes."

"Then I must eliminate doubt."

"But am I doubting the elimination?"

"Yes."

"What if I doubt that doubt?"

"Then I need to eliminate the doubt about the doubt."

Soon the practitioner has created an infinite recursive program.

A conceptual machine with no exit condition.

The traditional verse points toward freedom from that constricting relation.

But again, it doesn't condemn critical inquiry.

It concerns the way the finite subject becomes trapped in the effort to secure itself through conceptual delimitation.

### Why this is the perfect conclusion

Now look at the entire progression.

At the start of Part XXVIII, Abhinavagupta identifies the sacred seed through the alphabet.

Then he says everything emerges from Śiva and returns to Śiva.

Conceptual determinations arise within that uninterrupted conscious ground.

Those determinations can sustain the appearance of the terrifying wilderness of saṃsāra.

But Utpaladeva tells us that conceptual manifestation can also continue within supreme recognition.

And the final bracketed explanation reinforces the possibility of freedom from the recursive grip of constricting doubt.

That's a beautiful philosophical arc.

**The same power through which conceptual distinctions become manifest does not require the subject to remain bound by them.**

The decisive difference is recognition.



## 27. The genuine stopping point - Before Abhinavagupta begins the next phonemic argument

The Īśvarapratyabhijñā quotation is followed by **इति - iti**, closing the quoted authority.

The next unbracketed words in the Kashmir Series begin:

**यथा चाकृतिमध्य एव…**

*Yathā cākṛtimadhya eva…*

"Just as, among forms themselves…"

That opens a new comparison.

Abhinavagupta moves toward the way certain forms stand out among other forms, just as particular substances stand out among substances.

He then returns to **सकार - sakāra**, the phoneme *sa*, and its relation to supreme bliss, the whole alphabet and the intrinsic expressive power of individual letters.

A subsequent argument concerns how even a single phoneme can communicate meaning, and he invokes Patañjali's *Yogasūtra* III.17 and Bhartṛhari.

That is a distinct exegetical development, beginning near the end of Kashmir Series p. 238 and continuing onto p. 239.

We stop immediately before it.

Not halfway through the Īśvarapratyabhijñā verse.

Not after drifting into the next argument.

The complete sequence from **guṇā icchādyā nirṇītāḥ** through Utpaladeva's statement of lordship in the presence of concepts has been followed in order. ; ; \]

**3. Utpaladeva's essential phrase**

## विकल्पानां प्रसरेऽपि महेशता

vikalpānāṃ prasare 'pi maheśatā

"Great Lordship even while conceptual determinations expand."

That is the single most important Sanskrit expression to retain from this installment.


## 30. The final synthesis - Liberation doesn't have to murder thought

Now think about the entire philosophical movement.

Abhinavagupta begins with the fourteen-vowel system.

The fourteenth vowel is connected with **au**.

The third *brahma* is identified through **sa**.

The terminal Visarga completes the sacred seed.

**Sauḥ.**

The Heart of Bhairava.

Then he explains why it is called the seed of the universe.

Everything that exists is manifested through the powers of will, knowledge and action.

Those powers aren't independent Absolutes.

Their ultimate ground is Śiva.

The universe is emitted from Śiva.

And returns to Śiva.

The entire movement is described as uninterrupted and nonconceptual in its ultimate nature.

But then comes the remarkable move.

**Vikalpo 'pi.**

Even conceptual determination.

Thought belongs to manifestation.

Past, present and future are apprehended through the same conscious ground.

And the emission of difference doesn't establish a second ultimate consciousness.

Yet the finite subject can experience that very ground as saṃsāra.

The terrible wilderness.

The fire emerging from water.

The thunderbolt from the abode of nectar.

The expansion of fear within the apparent limits of worldly existence.

And then Utpaladeva gives the resolution.

**Sarvo mamāyaṃ vibhavaḥ.**

"All this is my manifestation."

Not the possessions of a finite psychological ego.

Not the claim that personal desire gives one ownership over the universe.

But recognition of the universal conscious Self.

And the really radical part is what follows.

**Vikalpānāṃ prasare 'pi maheśatā.**

Even while thoughts expand, there can be supreme Lordship.

### The distinction is not between thinking and not thinking

That's too crude.

The deeper distinction is between:

**Conceptual manifestation apprehended through contracted identification.**

And:

**Conceptual manifestation recognized as belonging to conscious sovereignty.**

The object may still appear.

The sentence may still arise.

The person may still speak.

Remember.

Plan.

Study.

Translate Sanskrit.

Argue with a philosopher.

Or become infuriated by a manuscript variant.

None of those activities necessarily requires a second independently ultimate consciousness.

### The ultimate philological joke

Imagine spending twenty-eight installments learning the sacred alphabet.

You learn every phoneme.

Every case.

Every consonant cluster.

Every Sanskrit compound.

You study the *tattvas*.

The three divine powers.

The metrical structures.

The hidden heart-seed.

Then you discover Utpaladeva saying:

"Even when conceptual determinations expand, there is Great Lordship."

You stare at the thousands of words you've accumulated.

"Are you saying I don't have to destroy all these concepts?"

"Not if the universal nature is genuinely recognized."

"Then what have I been doing for twenty-eight installments?"

"Developing understanding."

"Through concepts?"

"Yes."

"And concepts aren't independently ultimate?"

"No."

"And their appearance doesn't necessarily prevent recognition?"

"Precisely."

You close the book.

Then reopen it.

Because now you want to understand the next Sanskrit sentence.

And the entire philosophical process continues.

### The real endgame

Abhinavagupta doesn't merely want to eliminate distinctions.

He's explaining their ultimate relation to consciousness.

The alphabet remains.

Language remains.

Cognition remains.

The world remains manifest in its varied forms.

But the supreme ground is not broken into independent pieces by their appearance.

**Nonduality is not the absence of expressive power. It is the freedom through which expression can appear without severing its conscious source.**

And that is exactly why Utpaladeva's verse is the perfect conclusion to this installment.

The Heart isn't silenced by the universe.

The Heart is the very ground through which the universe becomes manifest.

The conceptual world doesn't need to become another Absolute in order to appear.

And recognition doesn't necessarily require the annihilation of the conceptual world.

The seed is *sauḥ*.

The manifestation is Trika.

The power is *svātantrya*.

The ground is Śiva.

And the recognition is:

**सर्वो ममायं विभवः।**

*Sarvo mamāyaṃ vibhavaḥ.*

"All this is my manifest splendour."
