# Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa

PART XX - The Seventeenth Kalā and the Fractions of Infinity

*Abhinavagupta discovers that the universe has sixteen subdivisions of a breath, fifteen lunar days, a sixteenth power of emission, a seventeenth Goddess made from half of half a letter, and-because apparently that wasn't enough-some scriptures recognize an eighteenth. A philosopher objects that you cannot cut an indivisible phoneme into quarters. Abhinavagupta replies that the entire universe is indivisible consciousness, the objection collapses, and a Sanskrit grammar lesson somehow becomes an explanation of why you can hear sorrow in a voice before understanding a single word.*

Commentary on verses 5-8 · Kashmir Series pp. 200-204 · Primary comparison: Kashmir Series and Somadeva Vasudeva's GRETIL transcription


## Previously, in the cosmic madhouse…

Nineteen installments.

We began with **अनुत्तर - Anuttara**, the unsurpassable conscious reality of Śiva.

Then came the vowels. The divine powers of will, knowledge, bliss and action. The consonants. The thirty-six *tattvas*. Mātṛkā. Mālinī. The Goddess as the generative triangle.

Then language itself went on trial.

Why does *go* mean cow?

What makes convention possible?

Why does knowledge of an object require a conscious subject?

And finally, in Part XIX, the whole system came to rest in the glorious Sanskrit word:

# अहम्

Aham - the supreme I-consciousness

Abhinavagupta explained that this *Aham* is not simply the psychological ego.

It is *Prakāśa* resting in itself: the intrinsic self-awareness of consciousness.

Then the entire universe was emitted and reabsorbed through Visarga.

The divine power became *ha*, manifested the consonantal totality, passed through subtle Nāda, and returned to Anuttara.

One conscious reality.

No second Absolute.

No external clock forcing Śiva through a production schedule.

And yet we have a problem.

A very awkward problem.

Because we experience time.

Days.

Months.

Years.

The duration of a syllable.

The duration of a breath.

The time between two thoughts.

And the entire lunar calendar.

If supreme consciousness is intrinsically beyond temporal sequence, how does differentiated time appear?

More importantly, how does the Sanskrit alphabet itself become associated with measurable durations?

Abhinavagupta has already discussed sixteen vowel-powers.

Now he connects them with sixteen *tuṭis* in the movement of Prāṇa.

Then fifteen lunar days.

Then a sixteenth *kalā*.

Then a seventeenth.

And, just as we start getting comfortable, somebody asks:

"Excuse me. How can an indivisible letter have a half?"

A magnificent objection.

Because if a phoneme is truly indivisible, the arithmetic sounds absurd.

Half a letter?

Quarter of a letter?

What next, Bhairava, a decimal expansion of the Absolute?

But Abhinavagupta isn't simply doing fractional arithmetic.

He's explaining how consciousness can remain undivided while manifesting distinguishable parts.

And that's the central philosophical problem of this installment.

We resume at the exact break between Kashmir Series pp. 200-201. The Sanskrit and the independent GRETIL transcription agree closely on the central argument, although their punctuation and treatment of the supporting quotations differ. ; \]


## 1. अमी चाकाराद्याः - The vowel-powers enter the breath

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN PROSE · KASHMIR SERIES PP. 200-201

**अमी चाकाराद्याः स्थितिमन्तः प्राणे तुटिषोडशकादिस्थित्या।**

*Amī cākārādyāḥ sthitimantaḥ prāṇe tuṭiṣoḍaśakādisthityā.*

"And these $$phonemic powers$$ beginning with *a*, established within Prāṇa according to a configuration involving sixteen *tuṭis*..."

Now that's how the new movement begins.

Notice what he's doing.

Previously, we were studying the phonemes as powers of conscious manifestation.

Now he considers their relationship with the movement of vital energy and the division of time.

But don't immediately imagine sixteen independent objects sitting inside your lungs.

The passage concerns a traditional correspondence between sacred sound, Prāṇa and temporal manifestation.

Let's dismantle it.

### Amī - अमी

These.

A masculine nominative plural demonstrative pronoun.

It points back to the previously described phonemic powers.

### Cākārādyāḥ - चाकाराद्याः

This is a sandhi-rich expression.

Start with:

**च - ca**

And.

**अकार - akāra**

The vowel *a*.

**आदि - ādi**

Beginning with.

Now:

**अकारादि - akārādi**

Beginning with *a*.

Masculine nominative plural:

**अकाराद्याः - akārādyāḥ**

Those beginning with *a*.

With *ca*:

**चाकाराद्याः - cākārādyāḥ**.

The joining produces a long ā through the coalescence of adjacent *a*-vowels.

The phrase means:

"And those beginning with *a*."

### Sthitimantaḥ - स्थितिमन्तः

Established, possessing an abiding condition.

**स्थिति - sthiti**

Standing, establishment, abiding.

Related to √**स्था - sthā**, to stand.

The possessive formation:

**स्थितिमत् - sthitimat**

Possessing an established condition.

Masculine nominative plural:

**स्थितिमन्तः - sthitimantaḥ**.

Now this is a beautiful reminder that Sanskrit *-mat* adjectives inflect.

The stem isn't simply repeated unchanged in every case.

### Prāṇe - प्राणे

In Prāṇa.

**प्राण - prāṇa**

Breath, vital energy.

Locative singular:

**प्राणे - prāṇe**.

Now, the term can refer to the movement of breathing, but also to the wider vital process in the traditional yogic and Tantric understanding.

The present passage is clearly concerned with its relation to temporal divisions.

That does not mean the sixteenfold scheme supplies a modern physiological measurement of breathing.

### Tuṭi - तुटि

A minute division or unit of time.

This is one of today's central words.

**तुटि - tuṭi**.

Notice the retroflex consonant:

**ट - ṭa**.

The word is not the same as:

**तिथि - tithi**.

They have different meanings.

A *tuṭi* here is a very small temporal subdivision.

A *tithi* is a lunar day.

Abhinavagupta's argument connects them through a sacred cosmological scheme.

But they are not ordinary synonyms.

### Ṣoḍaśaka - षोडशक

A group of sixteen.

**षोडश - ṣoḍaśa**

Sixteen.

The collective suffix:

**-क - -ka**.

Thus:

**षोडशक - ṣoḍaśaka**

A set or group of sixteen.

### Sthityā - स्थित्या

Through its establishment, arrangement or condition.

Instrumental singular of:

**स्थिति - sthiti**.

Now combine everything.

**तुटिषोडशकादिस्थित्या - tuṭiṣoḍaśakādisthityā**

By a configuration beginning with a set of sixteen *tuṭis*.

The Sanskrit doesn't specify a simple modern conversion such as "one *tuṭi* equals exactly X milliseconds."

We should not invent such a measurement.

What it establishes is a sixteenfold division associated with Prāṇa.

### The philosophical question

Why connect the vowels with breath?

Because speech becomes manifest through breathing and articulation.

And because Prāṇa is a particularly important bridge between subtle power and embodied manifestation in Tantric cosmology.

The earlier vowel-powers have now entered a description of temporal and respiratory manifestation.

The conceptual relationship is intelligible.

**The same powers of consciousness that become manifest as phonemic distinctions can also be described through divisions of temporal activity.**

But this creates another problem.

How does an internally differentiated breath become the calendar?

Abhinavagupta answers immediately.

The sixteenfold structure is connected with fifteen externally manifested days.

And the language here is genuinely peculiar.

Because it involves the half of a half.


## 2. एकां तुटिं सन्धीकृत्य - The sixteen become fifteen

The next phrase is:

**एकां तुटिं सन्धीकृत्यार्धार्धभागेन।**

*Ekāṃ tuṭiṃ sandhīkṛtyārdhārdhabhāgena.*

"Having joined one *tuṭi* through a half-of-half subdivision..."

Now let's be careful.

The Sanskrit doesn't simply present a conventional arithmetic calculation.

It is describing how one unit is incorporated through a subtle subdivision, allowing the internal sixteenfold structure to be related to an external fifteenfold calendrical scheme.

And Abhinavagupta will shortly explain the half-of-half symbolism through the seventeenth *kalā*.

### Ekām - एकाम्

One.

Feminine accusative singular.

It agrees with:

**तुटिम् - tuṭim**.

So:

**एकां तुटिम् - ekāṃ tuṭim**

One *tuṭi*.

### Sandhīkṛtya - सन्धीकृत्य

Having joined or brought into conjunction.

**सन्धि - sandhi**

Junction, joining, connection.

**कृत्य - kṛtya** here belongs to a compounded absolutive construction from √**कृ - kṛ**, to do.

Thus:

**सन्धीकृत्य - sandhīkṛtya**

Having made a junction, having joined.

Now, this is a good opportunity to distinguish two meanings of *sandhi*.

In grammar, *sandhi* refers to sound-combination at phonological boundaries.

Here it means a junction or connection in the sixteenfold temporal arrangement.

The underlying notion of joining is related.

But this isn't simply a vowel-sandhi exercise.

### Ardha - अर्ध

Half.

Now:

**अर्धार्ध - ardhārdha**

Half of a half.

In ordinary arithmetic:

$$
\frac12\times\frac12=\frac14.
$$

But the theological interpretation is not exhausted by the numerical result.

The quarter-like division concerns a mode of manifestation or separation within the phonemic power.

### Bhāga - भाग

Part, portion, share.

From √**भज् - bhaj**, to divide or distribute.

Instrumental singular:

**भागेन - bhāgena**.

So:

**अर्धार्धभागेन - ardhārdhabhāgena**

By means of a half-of-half portion.

Now the phrase becomes clearer.

The sixteenfold internal arrangement is related to the fifteenfold external cycle through a special junction involving fractional representation.

And the resulting time-structure is described in the next clause.

### The calendars department is becoming unbearable

Imagine the divine accountant receiving sixteen *tuṭis*.

"Excellent. Sixteen units."

Then the lunar department demands fifteen days.

"Fine. What happens to the extra unit?"

"The difference is explained through a half-of-half."

"A quarter?"

"In the relevant representation."

"Of what?"

"The supreme phonemic power."

The accountant closes the spreadsheet.

The Goddess smiles.

We've officially moved beyond ordinary office arithmetic.

But the philosophical distinction matters.

**An internal sixteenfold sacred division and an external fifteenfold calendrical manifestation are related through an interpretive scheme, not by a straightforward claim that one literal human breath equals fifteen civil days.**

And now Abhinavagupta names the lunar days.


## 3. पञ्चदशदिनात्मककालरूपता - The fifteen lunar days

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S PROSE · P. 201

**प्रलयोदययोर्बहिरपि पञ्चदशदिनात्मककालरूपतां तन्वते।**

*Pralayodayayor bahir api pañcadaśadinātmakakālarūpatāṃ tanvate.*

"Even outwardly, in dissolution and emergence, they extend into a temporal form constituted by fifteen days."

This is the point at which the internal Prāṇa structure becomes connected to lunar time.

Let's break it down.

### Pralaya - प्रलय

Dissolution, withdrawal.

From:

**प्र - pra**

Forth, completely.

**लय - laya**

Absorption, dissolution.

Now:

**प्रलय - pralaya**

Dissolution.

### Udaya - उदय

Arising, emergence.

From:

**उद् - ud**

Up, forth.

A verbal family involving rising or emergence.

Thus:

**उदय - udaya**

Arising.

### Pralayodayayoḥ - प्रलयोदययोः

Now this is a particularly good compound.

**प्रलय - pralaya**

Dissolution.

**उदय - udaya**

Emergence.

Together:

**प्रलयोदय - pralayodaya**.

The ending:

**-योः - -yoḥ**

Genitive or locative dual.

So:

**प्रलयोदययोः - pralayodayayoḥ**

In or during dissolution and emergence.

Notice the dual.

It is not a plural.

And it refers to two complementary modes.

### Bahiḥ - बहिः

Outwardly, outside.

The connected form:

**बहिरपि - bahir api**.

Even outwardly.

### Pañcadaśa - पञ्चदश

Fifteen.

From:

**पञ्च - pañca**

Five.

**दश - daśa**

Ten.

Thus:

**पञ्चदश - pañcadaśa**

Fifteen.

### Dina - दिन

Day.

So:

**पञ्चदशदिन - pañcadaśadina**

Fifteen days.

### Ātmaka - आत्मक

Having the nature of, constituted by.

Now:

**पञ्चदशदिनात्मक - pañcadaśadinātmaka**

Consisting of fifteen days.

### Kālarūpatā - कालरूपता

**काल - kāla**

Time.

**रूप - rūpa**

Form.

**-ता - -tā**

Abstract condition.

Thus:

**कालरूपता - kālarūpatā**

The condition of possessing the form of time.

Accusative singular:

**कालरूपताम् - kālarūpatām**.

### Tanvate - तन्वते

They extend, spread or unfold.

From √**तन् - tan**, to stretch, extend or spread.

Third-person plural middle voice:

**तन्वते - tanvate**.

Now the whole sentence is intelligible.

The powers unfold into an externally differentiated fifteen-day temporal structure.

And the text immediately identifies this with:

**तिथि - tithi**

Lunar days.

And:

**कला - kalā**

Lunar digits or powers.

### What is a tithi?

A *tithi* is a traditional lunar day defined by the changing angular separation between the Sun and Moon, rather than a fixed twenty-four-hour civil day.

A lunar half-month normally comprises fifteen tithis.

So the fifteenfold grouping has a genuine calendrical reference.

But Abhinavagupta's philosophical claim concerns its sacred correspondence with the internal sixteenfold structure.

The two levels should remain distinct.

### The great lunar expansion

The Moon waxes.

Its appearance changes.

Then it wanes.

The cycle can be described through fifteenfold divisions of waxing or waning.

And the tantric symbolism relates those phases to manifestation and withdrawal.

Now this is a beautiful image.

A form emerges.

Increases toward fullness.

Then decreases.

The visible Moon changes.

But the reality of lunar phases isn't simply identical with the physical Moon ceasing to exist at the new moon.

The phases express relational appearance.

And Abhinavagupta uses the sacred lunar calendar to illuminate a deeper structure of differentiated manifestation.

The analogy isn't proof of the metaphysics.

But it helps explain why the lunar scheme is so powerful within the tradition.

And now we have fifteen external *tithis*.

The internal sixteenfold arrangement remains.

So what is the sixteenth?

The answer is:

**Visarga.**

And it becomes the foundation for the seventeenth Goddess.


## 4. षोडशी कला - The sixteenth power is Visarga

The next words are:

**षोडश्येव च कला विसर्गात्मा।**

*Ṣoḍaśy eva ca kalā visargātmā.*

"And the sixteenth *kalā* has Visarga as its nature."

Now this is a crucial identification.

The sixteenth isn't merely another ordinary day on the external calendar.

It expresses the special power of emission through which the fifteenfold phases are related to their more fundamental source.

### Ṣoḍaśī - षोडशी

Sixteenth.

Feminine nominative singular.

From the numeral:

**षोडश - ṣoḍaśa**

Sixteen.

The feminine form:

**षोडशी - ṣoḍaśī**.

Now this is appropriate because it qualifies:

**कला - kalā**.

A feminine noun.

### Visargātmā - विसर्गात्मा

**विसर्ग - visarga**

Emission.

**आत्मन् - ātman**

Self, essential nature.

Thus:

**विसर्गात्मन् - visargātman**

Having the nature of emission.

The form used in the prose is:

**विसर्गात्मा - visargātmā**.

Now here's the important point.

We are not being asked to imagine a sixteenth physically measurable piece of Moon-rock.

The passage concerns a sacred cosmological classification.

The sixteenth power is associated with the emissive capacity of consciousness.

And this is why the next term is so important.

**Viśliṣyantī.**

Relaxing, becoming separated, loosening its conjunction.

The sixteenth power unfolds further.

And the result is designated the seventeenth *kalā*.

This is where things become truly interesting.

Because the seventeenth is not simply another ordinary external lunar day.

It belongs to a subtler mode of Visarga.

And the language becomes astonishing.

**Half of half the phoneme *ha*.**



## 5. सप्तदशी कला - The seventeenth Goddess appears

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S PROSE · P. 201, INTRODUCING A SCRIPTURAL QUOTATION

**विश्लिष्यन्ती सप्तदशी कला श्रीवाद्यादिशास्त्रेषु निरूपिता।**

*Viśliṣyantī saptadaśī kalā śrīvādyādiśāstreṣu nirūpitā.*

"Upon further separation, $$the power$$ is described as the seventeenth *kalā* in the scriptures beginning with the venerable Vādya tradition."

Now the remarkable thing is the word **viśliṣyantī**.

The seventeenth doesn't simply appear because someone has counted another ordinary vowel.

It arises in the exposition through the further loosening or separation of the Visarga-power.

Let's learn it.

### Viśliṣyantī - विश्लिष्यन्ती

From:

**वि - vi**

Apart, asunder.

**√श्लिष् - śliṣ**

To cling, embrace, join.

The verbal form *viśliṣyate* can signify loosening, separating or becoming detached.

The feminine present participle:

**विश्लिष्यन्ती - viśliṣyantī**

Separating, becoming loosened.

Notice the conjunct:

**श्लि = श् + लि**

And the cluster:

**ष्य = ष् + य**.

The whole word deserves to be written several times.

### Saptadaśī - सप्तदशी

Seventeenth, feminine.

**सप्त - sapta**

Seven.

**दश - daśa**

Ten.

Together:

**सप्तदश - saptadaśa**

Seventeen.

The feminine ordinal:

**सप्तदशी - saptadaśī**.

And:

**कला - kalā**

Power, lunar digit, portion.

Thus:

**सप्तदशी कला - saptadaśī kalā**

The seventeenth *kalā*.

### Nirūpitā - निरूपिता

Described, defined, set forth.

From:

**निर् - nir**

Out, thoroughly.

**√रूप् - rūp**, in the derived verbal family of forming or describing.

The past participle:

**निरूपित - nirūpita**

Explained or defined.

Feminine nominative singular:

**निरूपिता - nirūpitā**.

Agreeing with *kalā*.

### What about the source?

The Kashmir Series gives **श्रीलाद्यादिशास्त्रेषु**, whereas the GRETIL transcription reads **śrīvādyādiśāstreṣu**.

The parallel interpretive transcription follows the latter.

That leaves a genuine question about the exact title or designation of the scripture being invoked.

We can securely say Abhinavagupta introduces the following statement as a **scriptural quotation**, but the consulted evidence doesn't justify inventing a fully identified chapter and verse for it. ; \]

And now the quoted line itself.

One of the strangest-and most beautiful-of the whole *Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa*.

# सा तु सप्तदशी देवी हकारार्धार्धरूपिणी।

*Sā tu saptadaśī devī hakārārdhārdharūpiṇī.*

"That seventeenth Goddess has the form of half of half the phoneme *ha*."

That's the complete quoted fragment transmitted at this location.

**Not a complete four-pāda stanza.**

Not an invitation to invent the missing three quarters of a verse.

And not a statement attributed to Bhaṭṭanārāyaṇa-that quotation comes later.

Now let's learn every element.

### Sā - सा

She, that feminine one.

Feminine nominative singular.

Referring to the divine *kalā*.

### Tu - तु

But, indeed, now.

It marks a distinction from the preceding power.

### Saptadaśī - सप्तदशी

The seventeenth.

### Devī - देवी

Goddess.

Feminine nominative singular.

### Hakāra - हकार

The phoneme *ha*.

### Ardhārdha - अर्धार्ध

Half of half.

### Rūpiṇī - रूपिणी

Having the nature or form of.

Feminine nominative singular.

Thus:

**हकारार्धार्धरूपिणी - hakārārdhārdharūpiṇī**

She whose form is the half of the half of *ha*.

### A useful sandhi lesson

Consider:

**हकार + अर्ध → हकारार्ध**

*hakāra + ardha → hakārārdha.*

The final *a* and following *a* merge into long **ā**.

Then:

**अर्ध + अर्ध → अर्धार्ध**

*ardha + ardha → ardhārdha.*

Again:

**a + a → ā**.

The resulting compound:

**हकारार्धार्ध**

*hakārārdhārdha.*

Now that is worth memorizing.

Because the Sanskrit's very structure expresses the double division.

### The theological question

What does it mean for a Goddess to be the quarter-like expression of a phoneme?

The key is that this isn't ordinary arithmetic performed on an alphabetic glyph.

Abhinavagupta is describing a subtle differentiation within the divine sound-power.

The fractional language distinguishes levels of manifestation and separation.

But it doesn't establish that consciousness literally consists of divisible physical syllable-fragments.

And that's precisely why the next sentence matters.

Because the commentator explains the relationship.

Then immediately allows an opponent to object.


## 6. Visarga is half of *ha*; the further separation is half again

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN EXPLANATION · P. 201

**विसर्गस्य हकारार्धत्वात् ततोऽपि विश्लेषस्यार्धत्वादिति।**

*Visargasya hakārārdhatvāt tato 'pi viśleṣasyārdhatvād iti.*

"Because Visarga has the character of half of *ha*, and the further separation is again half of that."

Now that's the logic.

We need to distinguish its steps carefully.

THE FRACTIONAL SYMBOLISM

# ह

**Ha**

The reference phonemic power

# ½

**Visarga**

Symbolically, half of *ha*

# ¼

**Seventeenth kalā**

Half of that half

This depicts the text's relative fractional symbolism. It is not a claim that Devanāgarī glyphs, acoustic frequencies or supreme consciousness can be literally cut into these physical fractions.

Now let's learn the grammar.

### Visargasya - विसर्गस्य

Of Visarga.

Genitive singular:

**विसर्गस्य - visargasya**.

### Hakārārdhatva - हकारार्धत्व

**हकार - hakāra**

The phoneme *ha*.

**अर्ध - ardha**

Half.

**-त्व - -tva**

Abstract state.

Thus:

**हकारार्धत्व - hakārārdhatva**

The condition of being half of *ha*.

Ablative singular:

**हकारार्धत्वात् - hakārārdhatvāt**.

Notice the ending **-āt**.

It explains the reason.

### Tato 'pi - ततोऽपि

And then, from that, even further.

**ततः - tataḥ**

From that, thereafter.

**अपि - api**

Also, even.

Through sandhi:

**ततोऽपि - tato 'pi**.

Notice the avagraha **ऽ**.

### Viśleṣa - विश्लेष

Separation, loosening, disjunction.

From:

**वि - vi**

Apart.

**√श्लिष् - śliṣ**

To cling or unite.

The noun:

**विश्लेष - viśleṣa**

Separation.

Now:

**विश्लेषस्य - viśleṣasya**

Of the separation.

And:

**अर्धत्वात् - ardhatvāt**

Because of being half.

So the whole explanation is a two-stage division:

The Visarga-power is considered as half of *ha*.

Its further loosening is considered half of that.

And this gives the seventeenth Goddess her quarter-like description.

### What about Bindu?

The independent parallel exposition interprets this further separated power through the Bindu-associated condition.

That fits the broader Śaiva phonemic system we've been following.

But the present sentence itself uses **viśleṣa**, separation.

The distinction is worth preserving.

We needn't erase Abhinavagupta's actual term in order to make the symbolism fit a familiar diagram.

### The Goddess's absurd fractional inheritance

Imagine the seventeenth Goddess arriving at a cosmic inheritance meeting.

"What have I inherited?"

"Half of half the phoneme *ha*."

"That sounds like a quarter."

"Yes, in the symbolic relation."

"Of what material?"

"Divine emission."

"So where do I put it?"

"In Anuttara."

"Does it occupy space?"

"Not in the sense you're imagining."

At this point the cosmic accountant resigns.

And the philosopher asks the only sensible question left.

**How can anything indivisible have parts?**

That is the serious objection.

And now we reach the philosophical heart of Part XX.


## 7. निरवयवस्यैकवर्णस्य कथम् - You cannot cut an indivisible letter into pieces

That is the central answer.

Now consider a pot.

A body.

The divisions belong to manifestation.

The supreme reality remains one.

### The fractal disaster

Imagine somebody trying to divide the Absolute.

"Here is the first half."

"Here is the second."

"And here is a quarter."

"Now I have four different Absolutes."

It produces distinguishable aspects within manifestation.

That's the point.

And therefore the opponent's objection has to be reframed.

The question isn't whether an ultimately partless reality can manifest the appearance of parts.

That is precisely what Trika's account of *svātantrya* allows.

The question is whether the apparent parts must be independently ultimate.

Abhinavagupta says no.

And that is why he can accept the fractional symbolism of *ha* without declaring that the underlying conscious reality has been physically dismembered.

### The philosophical sophistication

Now this is important.

Abhinavagupta isn't merely saying:

He is offering a specific account of the relation between unity and differentiation.

The distinction is manifest.

But the ultimate ground isn't divided.

And the power through which the distinction becomes manifest is sovereign freedom.

So the next sentence is essential.

**Even when parts appear, indivisibility remains.**

That is the full answer.


## 9. अवयवावभासेऽप्यनवयवतैव - The parts appear; the unity isn't lost

Through the intrinsic freedom of consciousness.

### The conclusion

The appearance of parts doesn't require the underlying reality to become independently fragmented.

**तालव्य - tālavya**

Palatal.

The point is that the articulatory process involves a breath moving through the vocal apparatus, while distinguishable phonemes arise through different articulatory conditions.

Thus:

*tālu + āhanti → tālv āhanti.*

### What the argument is doing

Abhinavagupta isn't giving a complete modern aerodynamics account.

He is discussing the relation between a continuous or coordinated physical process and the perception of distinguishable articulatory results.

If one insists that each sound must be an absolutely independent physical entity, difficulties arise in explaining how sounds are produced through one connected breath and articulatory process.

The sequence of striking or contacting different locations doesn't require the breath to become several unrelated substances.

And therefore ordinary phonetics itself supplies an analogy for the relationship between unity and differentiation.

### The little air particle's itinerary

First stop:

Throat.

Second stop:

Palate.

Third stop:

Lips.

The philosopher demands:

"At what exact location did the first sound become a completely separate ultimate substance?"

The air parcel replies:

"I'm just moving through the vocal tract, mate."

The point is not that every phoneme is literally made by air hitting every organ in succession.

Modern phonetics is far more detailed than that.

The point is that differentiated articulatory events can belong to one coordinated process.

And the relationship between continuity and differentiation is precisely what Abhinavagupta is exploiting.

Now he considers the alternative.

What if all the articulatory regions are filled at once?

Would all the sounds become simultaneous?

And what would that imply?

### Why this is relevant to the indivisible *ha*

And that leads naturally to another way of dividing a sound.

One of the most useful technical words in Sanskrit phonetics.

## 13. मात्रा - A vowel can have one, two or three timing units

Now we cross onto Kashmir Series p. 202.

Abhinavagupta writes:

**द्विमात्रत्रिमात्रेषु च द्विकादियोगो गर्भीकृतः।**

*Dvimātratrimātreṣu ca dvikādiyogo garbhīkṛtaḥ.*

"In forms of two and three mātrās, the inclusion of two and corresponding subdivisions is already implicit."

And then:

**तथैव मात्रकेऽप्यर्धमात्रादियोगः संवेद्यः।**

*Tathaiva mātrake 'py ardhamātrādiyogaḥ saṃvedyaḥ.*

"Likewise, even in a one-mātrā form, the possibility of a half-mātrā and other subdivisions is to be understood."

Now that is a direct extension of the fractional argument.

A syllable may be treated as one unit.

Yet have temporal duration.

That duration can be analysed through subintervals.

Such analysis doesn't require us to deny its unity as a recognizable syllable.

### Mātrā - मात्रा

Measure, quantity, unit of duration.

In Sanskrit phonetics:

A traditional unit used to describe vowel quantity.

The classical basic distinctions are:

| Sanskrit category | IAST | Conventional quantity |
|--------|--------|--------|
| **ह्रस्व** | *hrasva* | 1 mātrā |
| **दीर्घ** | *dīrgha* | 2 mātrās |
| **प्लुत** | *pluta* | 3 mātrās |

These are phonological and recitational categories; they do not define universally fixed numbers of milliseconds. ; \]

### Hrasva - ह्रस्व

Short.

For example:

**अ - a**

A short vowel.

### Dīrgha - दीर्घ

Long.

For example:

**आ - ā**

A long vowel.

Now the distinction matters in Sanskrit.

*Dīrgha* isn't simply "the same vowel pronounced more loudly."

It concerns quantity or duration.

### Pluta - प्लुत

Prolated, extended.

Conventionally associated with three mātrās.

In scholarly notation, pluta pronunciation can be represented with a superscript or numeral indicating the extended quantity.

Now the grammar in Abhinavagupta's sentence.

### Dvimātra - द्विमात्र

**द्वि - dvi**

Two.

**मात्र - mātra**

Having a given measure, in the compound.

Thus:

**द्विमात्र - dvimātra**

Of two mātrās.

### Trimātra - त्रिमात्र

Three mātrās.

From:

**त्रि - tri**

Three.

And:

**मात्र - mātra**.

Now:

**द्विमात्रत्रिमात्रेषु - dvimātratrimātreṣu**

In the two- and three-mātrā forms.

Locative plural:

**-eṣu**.

### Garbhīkṛta - गर्भीकृत

Now here's a wonderful compound.

**गर्भ - garbha**

Womb, interior, what is contained within.

**कृत - kṛta**

Made.

Thus:

**गर्भीकृत - garbhīkṛta**

Included, contained within, made internal.

The larger form carries the sense of having incorporated the relevant subdivisions.

### Ardhāmātrā - अर्धमात्रा

Half-mātrā.

**अर्ध - ardha**

Half.

**मात्रा - mātrā**

A measure.

The ordinary compound is:

**अर्धमात्रा - ardhamātrā**.

### Saṃvedyaḥ - संवेद्यः

To be apprehended, understood, recognized.

From the √**विद् - vid** family.

The gerundive:

**संवेद्य - saṃvedya**

To be known.

Masculine nominative singular:

**संवेद्यः - saṃvedyaḥ**.

Now the argument.

A one-mātrā unit can be treated as a whole.

And yet, if we're analysing its temporal manifestation, we can consider half-mātrās and smaller subdivisions.

Similarly, a two-mātrā or three-mātrā vowel contains an internally extended temporal structure.

The ability to distinguish temporal portions doesn't necessarily destroy the integrity of the sound as a unit.

### The important distinction between a part and a separate phoneme

Suppose you sustain:

**आ - ā**.

Now the vowel has duration.

You could describe the first half and second half of its realization.

But that doesn't mean ordinary Sanskrit contains two separate *ā* phonemes every time somebody pronounces one long *ā*.

You're analysing a single utterance through temporal subdivisions.

Likewise, the traditional half-*ha* symbolism doesn't automatically establish a separate conventionally contrastive phoneme for every fractional stage.

The distinction belongs to a particular scheme of phonemic manifestation.

And now Abhinavagupta supports this with a quotation from a poet we know.

**Bhaṭṭanārāyaṇa.**

The author of the magnificent *Stavacintāmaṇi*.

And the quoted verse is beautiful.

Because it describes Śiva as simultaneously smaller than the smallest imaginable fraction and greater than the universe.


## 14. Bhaṭṭanārāyaṇa - Smaller than half a mātrā, greater than the cosmic egg

QUOTED AUTHORITY · BHAṬṬANĀRĀYAṆA, STAVACINTĀMAṆI 7

The *Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa* quotes this shortened form:

**प्रणवोर्ध्वार्धमात्रातोऽप्यणवे महते नमः।**

*Praṇavordhvārdhamātrāto 'py aṇave mahate namaḥ.*

"Salutation to him who is subtler than even the upper half-mātrā of Praṇava, yet great."

Now this is genuinely attested in both consulted transcriptions of Abhinavagupta's commentary.

But we've also identified the **complete original verse** in Bhaṭṭanārāyaṇa's *Stavacintāmaṇi*, verse 7.

The full independent source reads:

**प्रणवोर्ध्वार्धमात्रातोऽप्यणवे महते पुनः।**

**ब्रह्माण्डादपि नैर्गुण्यगुणाय स्थाणवे नमः॥**

*Praṇavordhvārdhamātrāto 'py aṇave mahate punaḥ.*

*Brahmāṇḍād api nairguṇyaguṇāya sthāṇave namaḥ.*

"Salutation to the steadfast Lord, subtler than even the upper half-mātrā of Praṇava, yet greater than the cosmic egg, whose very excellence is freedom from limiting qualities."

The distinction matters: **Abhinavagupta quotes a shortened, adapted form, while the independently preserved hymn supplies the complete two-line stanza.** We don't need to invent the missing words: the full verse is verifiable in the *Stavacintāmaṇi*. ; ; \]

Now let's learn its Sanskrit.

### Praṇava - प्रणव

The sacred syllable **ॐ - Oṃ**.

The term belongs to the *pra-√ṇu* family of sounding or praising in traditional derivational discussions.

Within Indian religious traditions, *Praṇava* is one of the most important designations of Oṃ.

The verse invokes its subtle upper half-mātrā.

### Ūrdhva - ऊर्ध्व

Upper, above, upward.

Now:

**प्रणवोर्ध्व - praṇavordhva**.

The vowel sandhi:

**a + ū → o**.

So:

*praṇava + ūrdhva → praṇavordhva.*

A useful example of ordinary Sanskrit guṇa sandhi.

### Ardhamātrā - अर्धमात्रा

Half-mātrā.

Now the compound:

**प्रणवोर्ध्वार्धमात्रा - praṇavordhvārdhamātrā**

The upper half-mātrā of Praṇava.

### Aṇu - अणु

Minute, tiny, atomic, infinitesimal.

In different Indian philosophical systems, *aṇu* can designate an atom or minute unit.

Here it expresses extraordinary subtlety.

The dative singular:

**अणवे - aṇave**.

Now that's an important declension.

Compare:

**गुरु - guru**

Teacher.

**गुरवे - gurave**

To the teacher.

Likewise:

**अणु - aṇu**

Minute.

**अणवे - aṇave**

To the minute one.

### Mahat - महत्

Great, vast.

The dative singular:

**महते - mahate**.

So the verse places two apparently opposed descriptions together.

**अणवे - aṇave**

To the exceedingly subtle one.

**महते - mahate**

To the great one.

But they're not contradictions in the philosophical context.

They indicate that Śiva isn't confined by ordinary spatial magnitudes.

### Brahmāṇḍa - ब्रह्माण्ड

The cosmic egg, the universe.

**ब्रह्म - brahma**

Brahman/Brahmā-related cosmic designation in the compound's traditional context.

**अण्ड - aṇḍa**

Egg.

Thus:

**ब्रह्माण्ड - brahmāṇḍa**

Cosmic egg.

The ablative:

**ब्रह्माण्डात् - brahmāṇḍāt**.

And:

**अपि - api**

Even.

In sandhi:

**ब्रह्माण्डादपि - brahmāṇḍād api**.

The final *t* becomes voiced *d* before the following vowel.

### Nairguṇya - नैर्गुण्य

The condition of being without limiting *guṇas*.

From:

**निर्गुण - nirguṇa**

Without qualities, in the relevant philosophical sense.

And the abstract formation:

**नैर्गुण्य - nairguṇya**.

Now:

### Nairguṇyaguṇa - नैर्गुण्यगुण

A wonderful apparently paradoxical compound.

**नैर्गुण्य - nairguṇya**

Freedom from limiting qualities.

**गुण - guṇa**

Quality, excellence, characteristic.

Thus:

**नैर्गुण्यगुण - nairguṇyaguṇa**

One whose excellence is freedom from limiting qualities.

Dative singular:

**नैर्गुण्यगुणाय - nairguṇyaguṇāya**.

### Sthāṇu - स्थाणु

Firm, immovable, steadfast; a name of Śiva.

Related to √**स्था - sthā**, to stand.

The dative singular:

**स्थाणवे - sthāṇave**.

And:

**नमः - namaḥ**

Salutation.

Thus:

**स्थाणवे नमः - sthāṇave namaḥ**

Salutation to Sthāṇu.

### The philosophical brilliance

Now see why Abhinavagupta invokes this verse.

The opponent asks how an indivisible sound can have a fraction.

Abhinavagupta has explained the relation between unity and apparent parts.

Then Bhaṭṭanārāyaṇa praises Śiva as both infinitely subtle and infinitely extensive.

Smaller than a half-mātrā.

Greater than the cosmic egg.

The same divine reality cannot be exhausted by either spatial or temporal measurement.

And therefore a fractional description isn't necessarily a division of ultimate consciousness into pieces.

### The microscopic and the cosmic

Imagine Śiva being measured.

"How small are you?"

"Smaller than the upper half-mātrā of Oṃ."

"How large?"

"Greater than the cosmic egg."

"Which one?"

"Both descriptions fail to exhaust my nature."

The measuring technician looks at his instruments.

Then at the universe.

Then resigns.

Now, it's a poetic theological statement, not a laboratory measurement.

But it illustrates the point beautifully.

**The supreme ground is not limited by the divisions through which manifestation is described.**

And now Abhinavagupta returns to his own main prose.

The conclusion is extraordinarily direct.

All fifty letters.

Or the whole universe.

Ultimately one.

Without temporal succession.


## 15. पञ्चाशद्वर्णा विश्वमपि वा - Fifty letters, or the whole universe, are one

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S PROSE · P. 202

**इह तु पञ्चाशद्वर्णा विश्वमपि वा अक्रममेकमेव।**

*Iha tu pañcāśadvarṇā viśvam api vā akramam ekam eva.*

"Here, however, the fifty phonemes-or even the universe itself-are one, without sequence."

This is a decisive return to the original philosophical argument.

The fractional discussion has not destroyed the unity of consciousness.

Nor has the expanded phonemic inventory.

Nor has the lunar calendar.

The same underlying reality remains.

### Pañcāśat - पञ्चाशत्

Fifty.

The compound:

**पञ्चाशद्वर्ण - pañcāśadvarṇa**

The fifty phonemes.

The form undergoes consonantal voicing before the following *v*.

Now remember the difference between an ordinary phonological inventory and a Tantric Mātṛkā arrangement.

Different traditional systems can enumerate different numbers of sacred sound-powers.

The present statement concerns the fiftyfold system.

### Viśvam - विश्वम्

The universe.

Neuter singular.

### Vā - वा

Or.

### Akrama - अक्रम

Without sequence.

**अ - a-**

Negative prefix.

**क्रम - krama**

Sequence, progression.

Thus:

**अक्रम - akrama**

Nonsequential.

### Ekam eva - एकमेव

One alone.

**एकम् - ekam**

One.

**एव - eva**

Precisely, alone.

Now the philosophical point.

The sacred sounds are distinguishable in their manifestation.

But the supreme ground isn't a temporal construction made from fifty independently ultimate letters.

Similarly, the world possesses countless differences.

Yet its ultimate conscious nature isn't a pile of separate Absolutes.

**The variety belongs to manifestation; the nondual fullness remains undivided.**

Now the text adds something that would drive the accountant absolutely insane.

Some scriptures recognize an **eighteenth** *kalā*.

Yes.

We've only just survived the seventeenth.

And the next number is already here.


## 16. अष्टादशी कला - Some scriptures count an eighteenth

Abhinavagupta says:

**क्वचित्तु मतादिशास्त्रेषु ... अष्टादशी कला इत्यभ्युपगमः।**

*Kvacit tu matādiśāstreṣu ... aṣṭādaśī kalā ity abhyupagamaḥ.*

"In certain scriptures, including those of the Mata tradition, an eighteenth *kalā* is also accepted."

Now the phrase in between explains the relationship with the further separation of Visarga and the establishment of the Anuttara-state.

The important thing is that this is presented as a **variant traditional classification**.

Not as a contradiction accidentally discovered by modern scholars.

And not as a claim that the ordinary lunar month must suddenly contain eighteen days.

### Kvacit - क्वचित्

Somewhere, in some instances.

### Mata - मत

Here part of the name or designation of a scriptural tradition.

We shouldn't translate it casually as merely "opinion" and assume it has no textual reference.

The passage invokes the *Mata* scriptural systems.

### Aṣṭādaśī - अष्टादशी

Eighteenth.

**अष्ट - aṣṭa**

Eight.

**दश - daśa**

Ten.

Thus:

**अष्टादश - aṣṭādaśa**

Eighteen.

Feminine ordinal:

**अष्टादशी - aṣṭādaśī**.

And:

**कला - kalā**

Power or lunar digit.

Thus:

**अष्टादशी कला - aṣṭādaśī kalā**

The eighteenth *kalā*.

### Abhyupagama - अभ्युपगम

Acceptance, acknowledgement, adoption of a position.

From the *abhi-upa-√gam* family, to approach or accept.

The nominative singular:

**अभ्युपगमः - abhyupagamaḥ**.

Now the philosophical distinction.

One tradition articulates sixteen.

Another describes the further separation as seventeen.

Another includes an eighteenth.

This does **not** establish that the supreme conscious reality is changing its numerical size depending upon which manuscript a practitioner reads.

It means different scriptural systems can distinguish further aspects within the same underlying power.

And that returns us to the famous warning from Part XVIII.

**Pūrṇatā is the essential principle, not fixation on one particular enumeration.**

The counts have doctrinal significance.

But the Absolute isn't constituted by the count.

### What the eighteenth actually signifies

The phrase involves:

**विसर्गविश्लेष - visargaviśleṣa**

The separation or further loosening of Visarga.

And:

**अनुत्तरपदसत्ता - anuttarapadasattā**

The reality or being of the Anuttara-state.

The eighteenth is admitted through the relation of that further separation to the supreme ground.

So the logic isn't simply "seventeen plus one because we wanted another deity."

It's an additional differentiation in the way the Visarga-process is interpreted.

And the classification belongs to its cited scriptural context.

### The theological accounting nightmare

Sixteen.

Seventeen.

Eighteen.

The accountant returns.

"Surely one of these must be the correct number?"

Abhinavagupta replies:

"Correct for which system, and for which aspect of the power?"

"Can't we just have a single number?"

"The supreme reality is nondual."

"Then why are there eighteen?"

"Because the nondual reality can be described through differentiated powers."

The accountant walks into the Himalayas and is never seen again.

But the important thing is that Abhinavagupta **does not solve the variation by declaring every count identical in its literal ritual details**.

The classifications retain their functions.

Their ultimate ground remains one.

And now the commentary makes another beautiful turn.

We have been discussing vowels as lunar powers.

Now it asks why they're called **svaras**.

And the answer leads to one of the most sophisticated sections in the whole work.

Because vowels don't merely help us pronounce words.

They can carry feeling.

Delight.

Sorrow.

Tenderness.

Praise.

And the tone of a voice can communicate something before its lexical meaning becomes clear.

The alphabet has become an account of emotional expression.

And Abhinavagupta is about to make that explicit.


## 17. Why are vowels called *svaras*? Abhinavagupta's beautiful Tantric wordplay

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S PROSE · KASHMIR SERIES P. 202

**तदेवमेताः कला एव ... स्वरा इत्युक्ताः।**

*Tad evam etāḥ kalā eva ... svarā ity uktāḥ.*

"Thus these very *kalās* are called *svaras*."

Now we're returning to a fundamental Sanskrit term.

**स्वर - svara**.

Vowel.

Tone.

Musical pitch.

Depending upon context.

And this polyvalence is exactly what makes the word so interesting for Abhinavagupta.

The passage describes the vowel-powers as revealing or making experienced the movements of consciousness, including delight.

Then he supplies several interpretive explanations of the name *svara*.

Now there's an important philological qualification.

**These are traditional Tantric *nirvacanas*-meaning-revealing word interpretations-not necessarily historical linguistic derivations in the modern comparative sense.**

They're significant because they connect the Sanskrit word's expressive possibilities to the functions of Śakti.

We should learn them without pretending that symbolic wordplay and historical etymology are always identical.

### Svara - स्वर

Vowel, tone, pitch, sonic expression.

In ordinary Sanskrit grammatical terminology, *svara* can refer to vowels or accent.

In musicological terminology, it can designate a musical note.

And in Abhinavagupta's present interpretation, the word is related to the powers through which conscious states become manifest in sound.

### Svarayanti - स्वरयन्ति

They make sound.

They cause something to be voiced or expressed.

The causative present form:

**स्वरयन्ति - svarayanti**.

Third-person plural.

Now Abhinavagupta immediately glosses it with:

**शब्दयन्ति - śabdayanti**.

They make audible, express through sound.

And:

**सूचयन्ति - sūcayanti**.

They indicate.

Let's learn the three together.

| Sanskrit | IAST | Function |
|--------|--------|--------|
| **स्वरयन्ति** | *svarayanti* | They give vocal expression |
| **शब्दयन्ति** | *śabdayanti* | They make sound |
| **सूचयन्ति** | *sūcayanti* | They indicate or signify |

Now the point.

A vowel can carry or disclose the quality of a vocal expression.

An utterance may be recognizably sorrowful.

Tender.

Excited.

Calm.

Even before its words are understood.

The voice communicates through its sonic form.

And the vowel is central to sustained vocal sound.

### A second wordplay: Sva and Rā

Abhinavagupta then analyses *svara* through:

**स्व - sva**

Own, oneself.

And:

**रा - rā**

To give, in the traditional verbal explanation.

The commentary uses:

**रान्ति - rānti**.

They give.

He interprets the *svaras* as giving or communicating their own nature.

Now the Sanskrit is striking.

The vowels reveal something belonging to consciousness.

And through sonic expression, that is communicated to another conscious subject.

The commentator speaks of transfer toward:

**परप्रमातृ - parapramātṛ**

Another apprehender or conscious subject.

Let's learn that.

### Para - पर

Other, another.

### Pramātṛ - प्रमातृ

Knower, apprehender, cognizing subject.

Related to:

**प्रमा - pramā**

Valid cognition.

And the agent noun:

**प्रमातृ - pramātṛ**

Knower.

Thus:

**परप्रमातृ - parapramātṛ**

Another cognizing subject.

Now here's the philosophical point.

Vocal expression can communicate a conscious condition.

Not necessarily through words alone.

But through tone, articulation, resonance and emotional character.

### The communication of feeling

Imagine somebody says:

"I'm fine."

Now the lexical meaning is straightforward.

But consider three different ways of saying it.

With gentle warmth.

With exhausted sadness.

With barely concealed rage.

The written words may be identical.

But the expression differs.

And listeners can often recognize that difference.

Now this doesn't mean vocal tone communicates emotion infallibly.

Context and convention still matter.

People can act.

Feign emotion.

Misunderstand one another.

But the phenomenon is real.

**The qualities of the sound can contribute meaning beyond the literal lexical content.**

And that is exactly why Abhinavagupta moves from *svara* into *cittavṛtti*.

The movements of mind.

Or, more broadly in this passage, the conscious dispositions expressed through sound.

### The editorial root notes

The Kashmir Series places bracketed glosses after the discussion of *svarayanti* and *rānti*, supplying verbal-root interpretations.

Those are editorial explanations of the wordplay.

The unbracketed prose itself already supplies the main alternatives:

Vowels make sound.

They indicate conscious states.

They communicate their own expressive nature.

So we should distinguish Abhinavagupta's symbolic explanation from the editor's supplemental root citations.

And now we reach the most memorable part.

The vowel-powers can disclose:

**Karuṇā. Śṛṅgāra. Śānta.**

Compassion or pathos.

Erotic or affectionate sentiment.

Tranquillity.

The theory of *rasa* has entered the discussion of sacred speech.


## 18. करुणा शृङ्गार शान्त - The vowel that carries sorrow, love or peace

Abhinavagupta says:

**करुणाशृङ्गारशान्तादिकां चित्तवृत्तिम्।**

*Karuṇāśṛṅgāraśāntādikāṃ cittavṛttim.*

"A movement of consciousness characterized by pathos, erotic sentiment, tranquillity and related conditions."

Now this is fascinating.

Because it links the phonemic manifestation of vowels with affective expression.

And it's particularly interesting given Abhinavagupta's major contributions to Sanskrit aesthetics and the theory of *rasa*.

But we should keep the immediate argument in view.

He's not delivering a complete exposition of the *Nāṭyaśāstra*.

He's explaining how vocal sound can reveal states of consciousness.

### Karuṇā - करुणा

Compassion, tenderness, sorrowful or pathetic sentiment depending upon context.

In aesthetic theory, *karuṇa-rasa* is commonly associated with pathos.

The feminine form:

**करुणा - karuṇā**.

### Śṛṅgāra - शृङ्गार

Erotic, romantic or aesthetic love.

In the classical *rasa* system, *śṛṅgāra* is the erotic sentiment.

Now notice:

**ङ्ग = ङ् + ग**.

A velar nasal followed by a voiced velar stop.

The word is:

**शृङ्गार - śṛṅgāra**.

Not *sringara* if you're writing careful IAST.

### Śānta - शान्त

Peaceful, tranquil, pacified.

Related to √**शम् - śam**, to become calm or be pacified.

In aesthetic theory:

**शान्तरस - śāntarasa**

The tranquil sentiment.

Now the present passage groups:

**करुणा - karuṇā**

**शृङ्गार - śṛṅgāra**

**शान्त - śānta**

And:

**आदि - ādi**

And so forth.

So:

**करुणाशृङ्गारशान्तादिका - karuṇāśṛṅgāraśāntādikā**

Characterized by pathos, love, tranquillity and similar states.

### Cittavṛtti - चित्तवृत्ति

**चित्त - citta**

Mind, mental consciousness.

**वृत्ति - vṛtti**

Movement, mode, activity.

Thus:

**चित्तवृत्ति - cittavṛtti**

A mental mode or disposition.

Accusative singular:

**चित्तवृत्तिम् - cittavṛttim**.

Now the philosophical question.

How can the voice disclose an emotional condition?

Because sound has qualities beyond lexical denotation.

Its timing.

Pitch.

Rhythm.

Intensity.

Vocal timbre.

Contour.

And articulatory character.

These can contribute to how an utterance is apprehended.

Abhinavagupta's concern is that these properties belong to the expressive powers of consciousness.

### A voice without a dictionary

Imagine hearing someone weeping in a language you don't understand.

You cannot translate their words.

You don't know their grammar.

You may not even know which language they're speaking.

But you can recognize something about the affective condition conveyed by the voice.

Perhaps grief.

Perhaps distress.

Perhaps relief.

Now, these judgments aren't infallible.

But their possibility shows that vocal expression can communicate without depending exclusively upon conventional word-meaning association.

And that's precisely the point.

**The expressive power of sound exceeds the narrowly lexical function of language.**

Now Abhinavagupta supplies concrete forms of expression.

Crying.

Flattery or affectionate speech.

Praise.

And then he invokes animals and newborn beings.

Because the universal sound-power is not restricted to educated Sanskrit speakers.

And the argument becomes surprisingly beautiful.


## 19. आक्रन्दन चाटु स्तुति - Crying, flattering and praising

The next examples are:

**आक्रन्दन - ākrandana**

Crying out, lamentation.

**चाटु - cāṭu**

Flattering, pleasing or affectionate speech.

**स्तुति - stuti**

Praise.

Now let's learn them.

### Ākrandana - आक्रन्दन

From:

**आ - ā**

Toward, fully.

**√क्रन्द् - krand**

To cry, lament, call out.

The noun:

**आक्रन्दन - ākrandana**

Crying or lamentation.

Notice the cluster:

**क्र = क् + र**.

And:

**न्द = न् + द**.

### Cāṭu - चाटु

Pleasing, flattering, coaxing, affectionate speech.

The final vowel is short *u*.

And the retroflex consonant is:

**ट - ṭa**.

### Stuti - स्तुति

Praise.

From √**स्तु - stu**, to praise.

The noun:

**स्तुति - stuti**

Praise.

Now these aren't identical activities.

Crying expresses a condition of distress or intense emotion.

Flattery or affectionate speech can convey tenderness, coaxing or delight.

Praise communicates admiration or devotion.

Their phonetic expressions can differ.

And their emotional significance isn't reducible to a shared lexical formula.

### Kevalā vā - केवला वा

The text then says the relevant sound-powers can appear:

**केवला वा - kevalā vā**

Alone, by themselves.

Or:

**योनिवर्णनिविष्टा वा - yonivarṇaniviṣṭā vā**

Inserted into the consonantal or generative phonemic matrix.

Now this distinction is important.

The vowel may be considered independently.

Or as part of a more articulated syllabic structure.

The expressive character can belong to both.

### Yoni-varṇa - योनिवर्ण

**योनि - yoni**

Matrix, source.

**वर्ण - varṇa**

Phoneme.

The compound has a technical Tantric significance involving the consonantal matrix.

### Niviṣṭa - निविष्ट

Entered, placed within, established.

From *ni-√viś*, to enter.

Feminine nominative plural:

**निविष्टाः - niviṣṭāḥ**, or in the compound's relevant agreeing form.

The point is that the expressive vowel-power may be present as an independent sound or as part of a more complex articulation.

### What about babies and animals?

The next phrase includes:

**तिर्यक्तत्तदहर्जातादिष्वपि**

*Tiryaktattadaharjātādiṣv api.*

Even among animals and those born on a particular day-newborn beings-and similar cases.

Now here's the point.

Animals and newborns may produce cries that communicate affective or biological conditions without possessing full conventional human language.

Abhinavagupta takes this as evidence of the more fundamental expressive role of sound.

And that continues the argument from Part XVII.

Remember the crow.

The lion.

The camel.

The drum.

The sea.

All were included in the discussion of meaningful but not necessarily lexically articulated sound.

Now we're learning why.

The vocal expression can indicate a conscious disposition.

And this function can exist before mastery of a conventional language.

### The newborn's cry

Imagine a newborn crying.

It hasn't learned Sanskrit.

It hasn't memorized the *Aṣṭādhyāyī*.

It cannot explain the distinction between *udātta* and *anudātta*.

Yet the cry conveys something to caregivers.

The physical and emotional significance may be recognized.

Not always perfectly.

But enough to make communication possible.

Now Abhinavagupta's metaphysical interpretation is that this expressive capacity belongs to the power of consciousness.

The sound is not meaningless merely because it hasn't been assigned a conventional word-meaning relation.

That's the philosophical point.

### The tremendous difference between phoneme and affect

Now let's be precise.

The sound of crying isn't necessarily a distinct Sanskrit lexical vowel.

And the emotion communicated by a sound isn't always transparent.

The significance depends on context and the capacities of the listener.

But the general distinction is well established:

**Meaningful vocal expression is not exhausted by conventional word meanings.**

This is why Abhinavagupta can discuss newborn cries, animal vocalizations, aesthetic sentiment and sacred phonemes within a single broader philosophy of sound.

And now he introduces the term connecting these observations.

**Kāku.**

Vocal inflection.

Tone.

The expressive contour of speech.

And this leads directly to the Vedic accents.


## 20. स्वरकाकु - The emotion is sometimes in how you say it

Now we reach Kashmir Series p. 203.

The phrase is:

**स्वरकाक्वादिरूपतामश्नुवानाः प्रकाशयन्ति।**

*Svarakākvādirūpatām aśnuvānāḥ prakāśayanti.*

"Taking the form of vocal accent, inflection and related qualities, they reveal $$the conscious disposition$$."

Now here's where the entire argument becomes exquisitely concrete.

### Svara - स्वर

Vowel, tone, accent, musical pitch, according to context.

### Kāku - काकु

Vocal inflection, intonational expression, expressive modulation.

Now this is a particularly useful word.

**काकु - kāku**.

It refers to expressive qualities of the voice that can modify how an utterance is understood.

The word can be relevant to rhetorical, dramatic and aesthetic discussions.

And here Abhinavagupta invokes it as part of the expression of conscious dispositions.

### Rūpatā - रूपता

Condition of having a form.

From:

**रूप - rūpa**

Form.

**-ता - -tā**

Abstract condition.

### Aśnuvānāḥ - अश्नुवानाः

Attaining, assuming, entering into.

From √**अश् - aś**, to reach or attain, in the relevant verbal formation.

Masculine nominative plural:

**अश्नुवानाः - aśnuvānāḥ**.

### Prakāśayanti - प्रकाशयन्ति

They reveal, make manifest.

From:

**प्र - pra**

Forth.

**√काश् - kāś**

To shine or appear.

The causative:

**प्रकाशयति - prakāśayati**

Makes manifest.

Third-person plural:

**प्रकाशयन्ति - prakāśayanti**.

Now the philosophical claim is straightforward.

Vocal inflection can reveal something about the conscious condition being expressed.

And this can happen independently of a complete shared lexical convention.

### The classic example

Take the sentence:

"I see."

Now imagine different intonations.

One might express:

Recognition.

Suspicion.

Impatience.

Relief.

Sarcasm.

Or a sudden realization.

The lexical words remain the same.

But their expressive character changes.

This is the domain in which *kāku* becomes important.

### Why this isn't simply "emotion is vibration"

Now there's a temptation to make an extravagant claim.

"All emotions are frequencies."

"Every sacred feeling has an exact pitch."

"Śṛṅgāra must be 432 Hz."

Absolutely not.

That isn't what Abhinavagupta's Sanskrit establishes.

The text concerns the capacity of vocal qualities to communicate or reveal conscious dispositions.

The precise relation between vocal acoustics and emotion is complex.

No universal one-to-one frequency chart follows from this passage.

The philosophical point is subtler.

**The expressive power of speech is not confined to its lexical content.**

And this is what leads to the traditional accent categories.

The *udātta*, *anudātta* and *svarita*.

But before we examine those, one more qualification.

These three categories belong principally to the grammatical and Vedic accent system.

They aren't automatically identical with the emotional inflections studied through *kāku*.

Abhinavagupta's passage brings them into a broader account of vocal expression.

That doesn't erase the distinction between linguistic pitch accent and emotional intonation.

And this is where the Kashmir Series editor becomes extremely verbose.

Because the editor inserts a large grammatical explanation.

Which we must separate from Abhinavagupta's main prose.


## 21. उदात्त, अनुदात्त, स्वरित - The three traditional accents

At the end of the present passage, Abhinavagupta says that the expressive properties of sound are explained through:

**उदात्तादयः - udāttādayaḥ**

The *udātta* and related categories.

Now the principal three are:

| **उदात्त**<br><br>udātta | High accent |
|--------|--------|
| **अनुदात्त**<br><br>anudātta | Low accent |
| **स्वरित**<br><br>svarita | Compound or contour accent combining the preceding pitch characteristics |

Now these are genuine traditional Sanskrit grammatical categories.

And we can identify them precisely through Pāṇini's *Aṣṭādhyāyī*.

PĀṆINI'S SŪTRAS, CITED IN THE KASHMIR SERIES EDITOR'S BRACKETED NOTE - NOT A NEW DIRECT QUOTATION FROM ABHINAVAGUPTA

**उच्चैरुदात्तः।** - 1.2.29

*Uccair udāttaḥ.*

What is high is designated *udātta*.

**नीचैरनुदात्तः।** - 1.2.30

*Nīcair anudāttaḥ.*

What is low is designated *anudātta*.

**समाहारः स्वरितः।** - 1.2.31

*Samāhāraḥ svaritaḥ.*

The combination is designated *svarita*. ; \]

Now let's learn the terminology.

### Uccaiḥ - उच्चैः

High, on a high pitch, upwards in the relevant pronunciation.

An indeclinable adverbial form.

The important phonetic sense concerns pitch, not merely loudness.

### Udātta - उदात्त

High-accented, raised.

The term has wider meanings such as elevated or exalted in other contexts.

Here it is a technical phonetic category.

### Nīcaiḥ - नीचैः

Low, on a low pitch.

Again, an adverbial form.

### Anudātta - अनुदात्त

Low-accented, not bearing the high accent.

The initial **अनु-** in the established technical term should not simply be reduced to an unrelated negative prefix without regard for its grammatical history.

### Samāhāra - समाहार

Combination, gathering together.

From:

**सम् - sam**

Together.

**आ - ā**

Toward.

**√हृ - hṛ**

To gather or carry.

Thus:

**समाहार - samāhāra**

Combination.

### Svarita - स्वरित

The compound pitch-accent category.

The exact phonetic realization varies between traditional Vedic recitational systems.

And that matters.

Because we shouldn't pretend that the three accents have always been performed through one completely uniform melody across every historical school.

The traditional categories are stable as grammatical names; particular recitational practices can vary.

### The crucial distinction between pitch and loudness

Imagine someone reading:

"Udātta means high."

And deciding:

"Excellent. I'll shout."

No.

A high pitch is not necessarily a loud sound.

A low pitch is not necessarily a quiet sound.

And *svarita* is not merely a third arbitrary volume level.

The Vedic accent system primarily concerns pitch relationships.

The traditional phonetic explanations also discuss articulatory effort and related physical features, but the terminology should not be collapsed into ordinary emotional loudness.

### The editor's long phonetic explanation

Now here's something important.

The Kashmir Series includes a very substantial **square-bracketed explanation** after Abhinavagupta's brief reference to *udātta* and related categories.

The bracket discusses:

- Bodily effort and relaxation.
- Constriction or widening of the throat.
- Roughness and softness of sound.
- Patañjali's *Mahābhāṣya*.
- The explanation of the grammarian Kaiyaṭa.
- Pāṇini's three accent definitions.

Those are useful grammatical materials.

But they are not Abhinavagupta's uninterrupted prose in the present passage.

And it would be a significant textual mistake to present the whole bracket as though Abhinavagupta personally supplied this extended physiological commentary at p. 203.

The unbracketed sentence is much more concise.

It identifies accentual and expressive qualities as manifestations of the same vowel-powers.

Then proceeds directly to the musical notes. ; \]

### The more important philosophical connection

Why does Abhinavagupta mention the accents here?

Because he has been arguing that the powers of sound can communicate conscious dispositions.

And the sound's articulation isn't exhausted by the distinction between one written consonant and another.

There are differences of pitch.

Intonation.

Resonance.

Duration.

And expressive quality.

These differences participate in how vocal sound becomes meaningful.

Now the technical Vedic accents are not simply identical to emotional *kāku*.

But both show that vocal expression has properties beyond the basic sequence of consonants and vowels.

And this leads to the final phrase of the passage.

The musical notes beginning with **Ṣaḍja**.

The alphabet has become music.


## 22. षड्जादिस्वर - The sacred vowels become musical expression

The final unbracketed words before the next transition are:

**तेषामेव चित्तवृत्त्यनुभावकषड्जादिस्वरूपत्वात्।**

*Teṣām eva cittavṛttyanubhāvakaṣaḍjādisvarūpatvāt.*

"Because these very $$sound-powers$$ have the nature of the musical notes beginning with Ṣaḍja, through which movements of consciousness are made manifest."

Now that's a beautiful conclusion.

And it's the natural end of the discussion of vowels, emotional expression and pitch.

### Ṣaḍja - षड्ज

The musical note traditionally designated *ṣaḍja*, corresponding to *Sa* in Indian solmization.

Now the conventional seven-note names are:

| **षड्ज** | *Ṣaḍja* | Sa |
|--------|--------|--------|
| **ऋषभ** | *Ṛṣabha* | Re |
| **गान्धार** | *Gāndhāra* | Ga |
| **मध्यम** | *Madhyama* | Ma |
| **पञ्चम** | *Pañcama* | Pa |
| **धैवत** | *Dhaivata* | Dha |
| **निषाद** | *Niṣāda* | Ni |

The table supplies conventional musical background. Abhinavagupta names the sequence through *ṣaḍjādi*, "beginning with Ṣaḍja"; he does not enumerate all seven note names in this sentence.

Now the important distinction.

The musical *svaras* aren't simply the same thing as the Sanskrit vowel inventory.

The word *svara* operates in both phonetics and musicology, but the corresponding systems have different technical functions.

And the relation between a particular musical note and an emotional condition isn't universally fixed by this passage.

Abhinavagupta is making a broader claim.

**The sonic powers of consciousness can become manifest through tonal and musical expression.**

Now let's dismantle the actual Sanskrit.

### Cittavṛtti - चित्तवृत्ति

A movement or mode of mind.

### Anubhāvaka - अनुभावक

Making known, revealing, causing something to be experienced.

Related to:

**अनुभव - anubhava**

Experience.

And the verbal family *anu-√bhū*, to experience.

Thus:

**अनुभावक - anubhāvaka**

That which reveals or makes experienced.

### Ṣaḍjādi - षड्जादि

**षड्ज - ṣaḍja**

The musical note Ṣaḍja.

**आदि - ādi**

Beginning with.

Thus:

**षड्जादि - ṣaḍjādi**

Beginning with Ṣaḍja.

### Svarūpatva - स्वरूपत्व

**स्वरूप - svarūpa**

Own nature, form.

**-त्व - -tva**

Abstract condition.

Thus:

**स्वरूपत्व - svarūpatva**

The condition of possessing that nature.

Ablative singular:

**स्वरूपत्वात् - svarūpatvāt**

Because of possessing that nature.

Now the complete compound identifies the relevant tonal powers with the communication or manifestation of conscious dispositions.

### Why this is a perfect ending

We've moved from:

Sixteen divisions of Prāṇa.

To fifteen lunar days.

To the sixteenth Visarga.

To the seventeenth Goddess.

To the eighteenth variant.

To the problem of dividing an indivisible phoneme.

To the unity of consciousness.

To the temporal divisions of sound.

To a hymn describing Śiva as smaller than half a mātrā.

And then to the astonishing observation that vowels can communicate joy, sorrow, tenderness and tranquillity through tone.

Now the whole argument is connected.

The differentiated forms are meaningful.

The distinctions aren't simply false.

But none becomes an independent ultimate consciousness.

**One conscious power can manifest through different durations, pitches, phonemes, emotions and musical expressions without ceasing to be one.**

That is the culmination of Part XX.

And it's exactly where the next Sanskrit sentence begins a new movement.


## 23. Where the original commentary now turns

At Kashmir Series p. 204, immediately following the reference to the musical notes beginning with Ṣaḍja, Abhinavagupta writes:

**एवं सर्वत्र संवेदने सर्वा एवैता ... शक्तय आदिक्षान्ताः समापतन्त्यः।**

*Evaṃ sarvatra saṃvedane sarvā evaitā ... śaktaya ādikṣāntāḥ samāpatantyaḥ.*

"Thus, in every cognition, all these powers-from *a* to *kṣa*-come together."

That is the new argument.

The text moves from vowel-powers expressing conscious dispositions to the presence of **all phonemic Śaktis in every cognition**.

And from there it develops an account of space and time.

How the different powers manifest distinct locations.

How the diversity of action generates temporal sequence.

And how these differentiated manifestations remain grounded in nonsequential consciousness.

The following pages also return to the lunar and solar powers, the four modes of cognition, and the relation between the ordinary knower and the full divine *Aham*.

That's the natural start of Part XXI.

We stop here, at the end of Kashmir Series p. 203.

## 26. The final insight - A fraction of consciousness is still not a divided Absolute

This is the philosophical insight worth retaining.

The opponent asks:

"How can an indivisible phoneme have a half?"

And Abhinavagupta responds by questioning the assumption underneath the objection.

The opponent is treating the phoneme as though its apparent parts must be independently real pieces of an ultimate substance.

But the Trika account doesn't regard manifestation that way.

The divine power is fundamentally self-revealing consciousness.

Its differentiations appear through *svātantrya*.

Their appearance doesn't establish a second Absolute.

So when scripture speaks of a half-*ha*, a quarter-like seventeenth *kalā*, or an eighteenth power, these are ways of articulating differentiated manifestation.

They don't physically carve the supreme reality into separate pieces.

Now here's the beautiful part.

The same principle applies to ordinary speech.

A vowel has duration.

That duration can be divided.

A sound has distinguishable articulatory properties.

Its pitch can change.

Its emotional expression can change.

The same written word can be uttered lovingly, sorrowfully, angrily or peacefully.

The sound's identity and its manifested qualities are distinguishable.

Yet they belong to a connected act of vocal expression.

And Abhinavagupta interprets that entire structure through consciousness.

**The appearance of parts does not require a fundamental division of the reality through which they appear.**

That's the point.

And it leads to something especially beautiful.

The vowels aren't merely inert building blocks of words.

Within Abhinavagupta's symbolic account, they participate in the manifestation of conscious dispositions.

They communicate.

They reveal.

They carry the expressive quality of experience.

That's why the reading moves so naturally from lunar powers and fractions into emotion, voice and music.

One consciousness.

Many distinguishable sounds.

Many expressive possibilities.

No independently ultimate fragmentation.

And here's the final irony.

We began with an objection about the seventeenth fraction of a letter.

We ended with a discussion of how somebody can communicate sorrow through the sound of their voice.

The most abstract metaphysics has returned to the most ordinary human experience.

You hear someone cry.

You recognize something.

Before the dictionary.

Before the grammatical analysis.

Before the explicit verbal explanation.

And Abhinavagupta asks us to investigate the power through which that apprehension becomes possible.

**The differentiated expression is real as expression; the conscious ground remains one.**

That is the heart of Part XX.

And next, the commentary is going to show how those same powers manifest space, time and the astonishing variety of cognition-without introducing a second consciousness.

The universe is not merely speaking.

It is giving itself somewhere to appear, and something like time through which its activities can unfold.
