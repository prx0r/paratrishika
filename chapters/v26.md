# Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa

PART XXVI - Entering the Heart of Bhairava

*Abhinavagupta has spent twenty-five installments explaining how the entire universe can be expressed through sacred phonemes. Now he reaches the Heart and discovers a problem: people have started trying to enter it as though it were a room.*

*He explains why Śiva is the essence of Śakti's universe, why the Goddess's hips are suddenly a metaphysical subject, why worship can loosen bondage even before understanding has matured, and why declaring “I have entered the Heart” might be precisely the thought preventing you from recognizing it.*

*Then the ritual traditions arrive with triangles, divine unions, flowers, incense, two mudrās, sixteen powers, seventeen lunar principles and multiple ways of extracting an entire cosmology from the phrase “joined with fourteen.”*

Kashmir Series pp. 220 onward · Parallel GRETIL transcription, Gnoli pp. 261 onward · Commentary on Parātrīśikā 9cd-18ab


## 1. Previously, in the cosmic madhouse…

Last time, the Goddess revealed the Heart of Bhairava through the sacred formula:

# सौः

sauḥ - the Amṛtabīja, the nectar-seed

We studied its three phonemic components and the root Tantra's astonishing promises concerning mantra, mudrā, deity-visions and extraordinary attainments.

But those were the **root Tantra's words**.

Now Abhinavagupta begins explaining what the Heart means within his nondual philosophy.

And this distinction matters.

A sacred syllable is not simply a little piece of sound that somehow contains an independent deity.

The whole argument of the *Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa* concerns the relation between **consciousness and its manifestation**.

So when we encounter the Heart, we're going to have to understand it through:

**प्रकाश - prakāśa:** self-luminous consciousness.

**विमर्श - vimarśa:** intrinsic self-apprehension.

**स्वातन्त्र्य - svātantrya:** sovereign freedom.

**शक्ति - śakti:** expressive conscious power.

And **विसर्ग - visarga:** emission, the release of manifestation.

There is also a real textual complication immediately at the opening.

The Kashmir Series and GRETIL do not transmit exactly the same designation of the Goddess associated with the manifested universe. We'll preserve the disagreement.

One further boundary needs identifying: after this opening exposition and its first numerical interpretations, Abhinavagupta explicitly announces a **sixteenfold exposition of the Heart**. The subsequent sixteen interpretations are not editorial headings invented by a modern translator; their sequence can be checked against the Sanskrit itself. ; \]

Right.

The Heart is on the examination table.

Not an anatomical table.

A philosophical one.

And Abhinavagupta is about to show that the entire universe is involved.


## 2. भैरवरूपस्य विश्वस्य - The universe whose nature is Bhairava

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN PROSE · KASHMIR SERIES P. 220

The opening is:

**भैरवरूपस्य विश्वस्य।**

*Bhairavarūpasya viśvasya.*

"Of the universe having the form of Bhairava."

That is the starting point.

Not:

"Somewhere outside the universe there exists a separate Heart."

But:

**The universe itself is being interpreted through Bhairava-nature.**

Let's learn it.

### Bhairava - भैरव

Bhairava.

The supreme conscious reality of the teaching.

### Rūpa - रूप

Form, nature, essential configuration.

Now:

**भैरवरूप - bhairavarūpa**

Having Bhairava as its nature.

Genitive singular:

**भैरवरूपस्य - bhairavarūpasya**.

### Viśva - विश्व

Universe, all, the totality.

Genitive singular:

**विश्वस्य - viśvasya**.

Now the connected expression means:

"Of the universe whose nature is Bhairava."

The genitive prepares the subsequent discussion of **its Heart**.

### The crucial philosophical distinction

If we say the universe is Bhairava-form, we don't mean every particular object has identical observable properties.

A rock is not conventionally the same as a river.

A thought is not an audible consonant.

A child isn't a mountain.

The distinctions remain.

But the claim is that the entire manifested field has its ultimate ground in one self-revealing conscious reality.

Abhinavagupta has already established the structure through the Śākta powers.

Now he asks:

**What is the essence of that manifested totality?**

And his answer is:

Śiva-nature.

Embraced by the supreme Goddess.

Not abandoned by her.

Not standing in competition with her.

**Embraced.**

The Sanskrit is wonderfully precise.


## 3. शिवरूपं परमेश्वर्या समालिङ्गितम् - Śiva embraced by the Supreme Goddess

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S PROSE · PP. 220-221

**तस्य हृदयं सारं शिवरूपं परमेश्वर्या श्रीमत्पराभट्टारिकया समालिङ्गितम्।**

*Tasya hṛdayaṃ sāraṃ śivarūpaṃ parameśvaryā śrīmatparābhaṭṭārikayā samāliṅgitam.*

"Its Heart, its essence, has the nature of Śiva and is embraced by the venerable supreme Goddess Parā."

This is a particularly important passage.

Because it explains the Heart through the inseparability of Śiva and Śakti.

Let's dissect it.

### Tasya - तस्य

Of that.

Genitive singular.

Referring to the manifested Bhairava-universe.

### Hṛdayam - हृदयम्

Heart.

Neuter singular.

Now the ordinary word can mean the anatomical heart.

But here it identifies the essential conscious centre of the universe.

### Sāram - सारम्

Essence, core, substantial reality.

Neuter singular.

It stands in apposition to *hṛdayam*.

Thus:

**हृदयं सारम् - hṛdayaṃ sāram**

The Heart, the essence.

### Śivarūpam - शिवरूपम्

Having the nature of Śiva.

**शिव - śiva**

The auspicious Lord.

**रूप - rūpa**

Nature.

Neuter singular:

**शिवरूपम् - śivarūpam**.

### Parameśvaryā - परमेश्वर्या

By the supreme Goddess.

Instrumental singular.

From:

**परम - parama**

Supreme.

**ईश्वरी - īśvarī**

Goddess, sovereign Lady.

Together:

**परमेश्वरी - parameśvarī**.

Instrumental:

**परमेश्वर्या - parameśvaryā**.

### Parābhaṭṭārikā - पराभट्टारिका

The supreme venerable Goddess, Parā.

**परा - parā**

Supreme.

**भट्टारिका - bhaṭṭārikā**

Venerable Lady, honoured Goddess.

Instrumental singular:

**पराभट्टारिकया - parābhaṭṭārikayā**.

### Samāliṅgitam - समालिङ्गितम्

Embraced, clasped, held closely.

From:

**सम् - sam**

Together.

**आ - ā**

Toward.

**√लिङ्ग् - liṅg**

To embrace.

The past passive participle:

**समालिङ्गित - samāliṅgita**.

Neuter singular:

**समालिङ्गितम् - samāliṅgitam**.

Now the grammar makes the structure clear.

The Heart is Śiva-nature.

It is embraced by Parā Śakti.

**The Heart is not Śiva stripped of Śakti.**

Nor is Śakti merely an external object surrounding an otherwise inert Śiva.

The image expresses their inseparability.

### The world's least successful divorce

Imagine a metaphysician trying to separate Śiva and Śakti.

"Right. Śiva goes in this room."

"And Śakti?"

"Next door."

"But Śiva's luminosity is inseparable from his self-apprehending power."

"Yes."

"And Śakti is that intrinsic power."

"Yes."

"So what exactly have you separated?"

The metaphysician stares at the floor plan.

There are no independent rooms.

Because the very capacity to distinguish rooms is already a manifestation of consciousness.

That is the point.

**Śiva and Śakti are distinguishable in exposition without being independently ultimate substances.**

### An important textual variant

Here we need to be precise.

The Kashmir Series begins with wording that includes:

**नररूपापराभट्टारिकास्वभावः**

*Nararūpāparābhaṭṭārikāsvabhāvaḥ.*

GRETIL instead reads:

**परापराभट्टारिकास्वभावः**

*Parāparābhaṭṭārikāsvabhāvaḥ.*

The first suggests a designation involving *Nara*-form and Aparā; the second refers to the goddess Parāparā. These are different descriptions within the Trika's divine triadic terminology.

We cannot silently replace one with the other.

Both witnesses then proceed to identify the Heart as Śiva-nature embraced by **Parābhaṭṭārikā**. The broader nondual relation is therefore clear, even though the precise triadic designation at the beginning is unsettled. ; \]

Now that distinction is worth keeping.

The text's theology isn't improved by pretending every edition says the same thing.

The Goddesses deserve accurate grammar.


## 4. सर्वसर्वात्मकता - Everything in the nature of everything

The prose continues on p. 221 by interpreting the word *Bhairava* through the manifestation of the whole universe.

The crucial term is:

**सर्वसर्वात्मकता**

*Sarvasarvātmakatā.*

"The condition of everything having the nature of everything."

Now that's a formidable expression.

And a dangerous one if misunderstood.

### Sarva - सर्व

All, everything.

### Sarvātmakatā - सर्वात्मकता

**सर्व - sarva**

All.

**आत्मक - ātmaka**

Having the nature of.

**-ता - -tā**

Abstract condition.

Thus:

**सर्वात्मकता - sarvātmakatā**

The condition of being all-inclusive in nature.

The intensified compound:

**सर्वसर्वात्मकता - sarvasarvātmakatā**

An all-embracing universality.

### The philosophical problem

If everything is everything, why isn't a banana a philosophical manuscript?

Why can't we use a teacup as a railway locomotive?

Because the doctrine isn't abolishing every practical distinction.

It concerns the ultimate relation of the differentiated manifestations to their conscious ground.

The different forms do not establish independent supreme realities.

But they remain distinguishable within manifestation.

### Nara - नर

The limited human or embodied subject.

In Trika, *Nara* also has technical significance as one member of the Nara-Śakti-Śiva triad.

The present argument emphasizes that particular limited subjects do not possess an independently ultimate reality outside the universal Śakti.

The Heart is Śiva-nature precisely because the differentiated field is not fundamentally severed from its source.

### The missing second Absolute

Imagine every object requesting independent ultimate status.

The pot:

"I am a separate Absolute."

The table:

"Me too."

The mountain:

"Obviously."

The mind:

"I'm the most important Absolute."

Abhinavagupta asks:

"What makes their differences manifest?"

And all the supposed independent Absolutes are implicated in consciousness.

The argument doesn't eliminate their observable differences.

It rejects their independent ultimacy.

That is the force of *sarvasarvātmakatā*.

And now the Sanskrit makes a wonderfully unexpected turn.

From the cosmic universe.

To the Goddess's hips.

Because the root Tantra addressed her as:

**सुश्रोणि - suśroṇi**.

And Abhinavagupta has absolutely no intention of treating this as decorative poetry.

He turns the address into an entire metaphysical explanation.


## 5. सुश्रोणि - The Goddess's hips are not decorative poetry

ABHINAVAGUPTA EXPLAINS A ROOT-TANTRA VOCATIVE · P. 221

**सुश्रोणि इत्यामन्त्रणम्।**

*Suśroṇi ity āmantraṇam.*

"'O beautiful-hipped one' is an address."

Now we're getting to the really interesting stuff.

The root Tantra addressed the Goddess as:

**सुश्रोणि - suśroṇi**.

Ordinary translation:

"O beautiful-hipped one."

But Abhinavagupta doesn't leave it as a conventional compliment.

He connects the word to the **yoni-shaped Heart** and to its pure conscious nature even within the differentiated field.

### Su - सु

Good, beautiful, auspicious.

### Śroṇi - श्रोणि

Hip, pelvic region.

A feminine noun.

Now:

**सुश्रोणि - suśroṇi**

O beautiful-hipped one.

### Āmantraṇa - आमन्त्रण

Address, calling upon someone.

From:

**आ - ā**

Toward.

**√मन्त्र् - mantr**

To consult, address or utter in the relevant lexical family.

Thus:

**आमन्त्रण - āmantraṇa**

Address.

The commentary explicitly identifies *suśroṇi* as a vocative.

This matters: it isn't the subject of the sentence, and it isn't a locative naming somewhere the practitioner physically enters.

It's the Goddess being addressed.

### The yoni-shaped Heart

Abhinavagupta now recalls the earlier teaching concerning the Heart in the generative matrix.

The decisive terms are:

**योनिरूप - yonirūpa**

Having the form of the generative matrix.

**शुद्धचिन्मय - śuddhacinmaya**

Consisting of pure consciousness.

**अनपेत - anapeta**

Not departed, not separated, not lost.

Let's learn them.

### Yoni - योनि

Generative matrix, womb, origin.

It has literal bodily meanings and ritual meanings.

Within the metaphysics of Trika, it also represents the generative capacity of Śakti.

### Rūpa - रूप

Form or nature.

Thus:

**योनिरूप - yonirūpa**

Having the nature or form of a yoni.

### Śuddha - शुद्ध

Pure.

### Cit - चित्

Consciousness.

### Maya - मय

Consisting of.

Thus:

**शुद्धचिन्मय - śuddhacinmaya**

Consisting of pure consciousness.

Now don't confuse the suffix:

**-मय - -maya**, consisting of.

With the noun:

**माया - māyā**, Māyā.

They look similar but are not interchangeable.

### Anapeta - अनपेत

Not departed, not fallen away.

**अन् - an-**

Not.

**अपेत - apeta**

Gone away, departed.

From *apa-√i*, to go away.

Thus:

**अनपेत - anapeta**

Not separated.

### The textual variant that changes the adjective

Here the witnesses differ.

The Kashmir Series prints:

**अशोभनमायात्मकतायामपि**

*Aśobhanamāyātmakatāyām api.*

GRETIL reads:

**śobhanaṃ māyātmakatāyām apy anapetaṃ śuddhacinmayam**

The first reading contains an initial negative **a-**, while the second has **śobhanam**, beautiful or auspicious.

That is not a negligible difference.

The grammar surrounding the noun phrases is also compressed. We should not silently convert the Kashmir Series into GRETIL's reading, even though the latter gives a smoother description of the beautiful Heart as remaining pure-consciousness within Māyic manifestation. ; \]

### Why the bodily imagery matters

Now think about the philosophical structure.

Śakti is the generative power of consciousness.

The yoni symbolizes her capacity for bringing forth manifestation.

The Heart is the conscious essence of that manifestation.

And the root text addresses the Goddess through the pelvic region.

That is a deliberate congruence between bodily and cosmic generativity.

It isn't simply a prudish metaphor that must be translated into something less physical.

Nor is it necessarily an instruction to perform a specific sexual rite whenever the word appears.

The commentary connects multiple levels:

Bodily symbolism.

Ritual symbolism.

Phonemic symbolism.

Nondual metaphysics.

And that combination is characteristic of the Kaula environment.

### The absolutely impossible Sanskrit anatomy examination

Imagine a student saying:

"Master, why does the Tantra mention the Goddess's hips?"

"Because the imagery concerns the generative matrix of manifestation."

"So we should remove the bodily meaning?"

"No."

"So it's merely anatomical?"

"Also no."

"So what is it?"

"A bodily expression that acquires technical theological significance within the ritual and metaphysical system."

The student closes the dictionary.

It's going to be one of those days.

But the lesson is important.

**A Sanskrit word can retain its literal bodily meaning while acquiring an additional technical symbolic function.**

We don't have to erase one to understand the other.

Now Abhinavagupta moves from the Goddess's yoni to the universal **I**.

And here's where things get very interesting.

Because he includes the entire hierarchy of knowing subjects within the same Heart.

From the highest divine beings to animals and stationary life.

The universal Aham has returned.


## 6. From Mantra-Maheśvaras to stationary beings - The entire field of knowers

The commentary now speaks of:

**सकलमन्त्रमहेशादि…प्रमातृजाल**

*Sakalamantramaheśādi…pramātṛjāla.*

"The entire network of knowing subjects, beginning with the Mantra-Maheśvaras…"

The compound continues down to creatures and stationary beings.

This is an important ontological statement.

### Sakala - सकल

Entire, complete, with all parts.

### Mantra-Maheśvara - मन्त्रमहेश्वर

A high class of divine knowing subject in Śaiva cosmological classifications.

We should distinguish it from an ordinary human simply reciting a mantra.

The term has an established technical place in the hierarchy of *pramātṛs*.

### Pramātṛ - प्रमातृ

Knower, apprehending subject.

From:

**प्र - pra**

Forth, completely.

**√मा - mā**

To measure, ascertain or know in the relevant philosophical word-family.

The agent noun:

**प्रमातृ - pramātṛ**.

### Jāla - जाल

Net, network, collection.

Thus:

**प्रमातृजाल - pramātṛjāla**

Network of knowing subjects.

Now this is a lovely image.

The differentiated world isn't only a collection of objects.

It contains various levels of subjectivity.

Divine beings.

Finite beings.

Animals.

The entire traditional hierarchy of apprehenders.

### The universal Aham

The commentary invokes the word:

**अहम् - aham**

I.

And the corresponding expression:

**अस्माकम् - asmākam**

Of us, our.

Now this is philosophically significant.

In ordinary language, *aham* refers to the speaking individual.

But the divine interpretation concerns the universal conscious ground through which different finite subjects can be manifest.

The text's use of first-person language becomes an opportunity to distinguish finite self-reference from universal I-consciousness.

### The essential distinction

A finite subject says:

"I."

A second finite subject also says:

"I."

Do they thereby become one identical individual?

No.

Their empirical perspectives remain distinguishable.

But Abhinavagupta's nondual account asks what is ultimate in the conscious reality of those perspectives.

The supreme *Aham* isn't one finite ego competing with the others.

It is the underlying self-revealing reality expressed through different modes of apprehension.

### The cosmic census

Imagine the universal Heart conducting a census.

Mantra-Maheśvaras.

Divine knowers.

Human beings.

Animals.

Stationary forms of life.

Every level of the traditional hierarchy.

The clerk asks:

"Which of these is outside the supreme conscious reality?"

Abhinavagupta replies:

"None."

"Then are they all empirically identical?"

"Also no."

The clerk is furious.

He was hoping for one simple column.

But the metaphysical distinction is essential.

**Unity of ultimate ground does not erase differences among manifest subjects.**

And the Sanskrit now gives the positive description of the Heart.

Not merely a universal substance.

But fullness.

Emission.

Bliss.

And the entire universe already contained within its own generative potency.


## 7. विसर्गविश्लेषानन्दशक्ति - The Heart is condensed conscious bliss

Now we arrive at one of the densest compounds of p. 221.

**अन्तर्गतविश्ववीर्यसमुच्छलत्तात्मकविसर्गविश्लेषानन्दशक्त्यैकघनम्।**

*Antargataviśvavīryasamucchalattātmaka-visarga-viśleṣa-ānanda-śaktyaikaghanam.*

A close contextual rendering:

"An indivisible concentration of bliss-power associated with emission and unfolding, whose generative potency contains the universe within itself."

This is a compressed theological description; the exact internal grouping of the compound permits more than one emphasis.

Let's work through the securely identifiable components.

### Antargata - अन्तर्गत

Gone within, contained inside.

**अन्तर् - antar**

Within.

**गत - gata**

Gone, situated.

From √**गम् - gam**, to go.

### Viśva - विश्व

Universe.

### Vīrya - वीर्य

Potency, strength, generative capacity.

Now we've encountered *vīrya* in the seed-yoni discussions.

Here it describes a divine generative power containing the universe.

### Samucchalattā - समुच्छलत्ता

Upsurging, exuberant arising.

Related to *sam-ud-√chal*, to rise or surge.

This connects directly to the Spanda argument from Part XXII.

The manifestation is an upsurge of intrinsic power.

Not the relocation of one physical object into another independently existing substance.

### Visarga - विसर्ग

Emission.

From *vi-√sṛj*, to release or emit.

### Viśleṣa - विश्लेष

Separation, loosening, differentiation.

Related to *vi-√śliṣ*, to separate from contact.

### Ānanda - आनन्द

Bliss, fullness of conscious delight.

### Śakti - शक्ति

Power.

### Eka - एक

One.

### Ghana - घन

Dense, compact, solid, homogeneous in certain philosophical compounds.

Now:

**एकघन - ekaghana**

One homogeneous mass or undivided concentration.

And:

**शक्त्यैकघन - śaktyaikaghana**

An undivided concentration of Śakti.

### Why this description matters

The Heart is not a dead, passive object.

Its nature is:

Conscious.

Full.

Generative.

Blissful.

Expressive.

And indivisible in its supreme reality.

Now here's the key.

The differentiated universe is already included as the possibility of manifestation within the divine power.

The appearance of the world does not require some independently existing material outside consciousness.

This is why Abhinavagupta describes its potency as containing the universe.

### The difference between a physical seed and the divine bīja

A physical seed is a biological structure.

Its development depends upon conditions.

Water.

Temperature.

Genetic processes.

A suitable environment.

But the divine *bīja* is being used as a metaphysical symbol.

Its potency belongs to the supreme freedom.

It isn't a tiny biological object sitting inside the Absolute.

The analogy is generative, not literal.

### The enormous philosophical pressure inside one compound

Imagine a Sanskrit compound trying to contain:

The universe.

Potency.

Upsurging.

Emission.

Differentiation.

Bliss.

Power.

Unity.

The compound becomes almost as long as the universe it's describing.

Abhinavagupta appears completely untroubled.

Why use six sentences when one compound can terrify three generations of philologists?

But the structure is coherent.

**The divine Heart is undivided fullness whose own power manifests diversity.**

And this leads to the next word.

**Brahma.**

Not merely because the Heart is big.

But because it is expansive, pervasive and capable of making manifestation expand.

And Abhinavagupta now distinguishes his interpretation from a position he attributes polemically to certain Vedānta teachers.

That distinction deserves care.


## 8. बृहत्, व्यापक, बृंहित - The expanding meaning of Brahma

The commentary says:

**ब्रह्म बृहत् व्यापकं बृंहितं च।**

*Brahma bṛhad vyāpakaṃ bṛṃhitaṃ ca.*

"Brahma: great, pervasive, and expanded."

This is a traditional semantic explanation of *brahma* through the notion of expansion and greatness.

Now don't confuse the words.

### Brahma - ब्रह्म

Neuter.

The supreme or expansive principle.

### Brahmā - ब्रह्मा

Masculine nominative singular.

The creator-god in other theological contexts.

The present discussion concerns **brahma** in relation to the Heart.

### Bṛhat - बृहत्

Great, vast, expansive.

The connected form before a voiced consonant may appear as:

**बृहद् - bṛhad**.

### Vyāpaka - व्यापक

Pervading, encompassing.

From the verbal family of:

**वि - vi**

Apart, throughout.

**√आप् - āp**

To reach or pervade in the relevant derivational family.

Thus:

**व्यापक - vyāpaka**

Pervasive.

### Bṛṃhita - बृंहित

Expanded, made great, augmented.

Related to √**बृह् - bṛh**, to grow or enlarge.

Now here's the philosophical point.

The Heart is not merely one big object among smaller objects.

It's not a cosmic stone with an enormous diameter.

Its greatness concerns the all-inclusive nature of conscious reality.

And its expansion is the manifestation of its own powers.

### The polemic against certain Vedānta readings

Abhinavagupta contrasts his interpretation with a *brahma*-view that he characterizes as approaching sheer voidism.

The transmitted compound includes:

**केवलशून्यवादाविदूरवर्ति**

*Kevalaśūnyavādāvidūravarti.*

"Not far removed from a doctrine of mere emptiness."

Now that is explicitly polemical.

We should not repeat Abhinavagupta's characterization as a neutral statement that all Vedānta teaches nothingness.

Different Vedānta schools have sophisticated and varied doctrines of Brahman.

His objection here is directed at a conception he regards as insufficiently attentive to the intrinsic expressive and self-apprehending power of consciousness.

### The central philosophical difference

Abhinavagupta isn't content with describing the supreme reality as merely devoid of all difference.

He emphasizes its power to manifest.

Its self-awareness.

Its freedom.

Its generative fullness.

In his system, the supreme reality isn't rendered inert by nonduality.

**The Heart is the full, expressive ground of manifestation.**

Now we have reached the end of the opening explanation of the *brahma* that appears in the root verse.

And Abhinavagupta draws a conclusion.

The "third" is Śiva-nature relative to the preceding levels.

That is why the Heart is treated as the principal object of worship in these scriptures.

And then comes one of the most interesting practical statements in the entire passage.

A person may begin with external ritual.

Without fully recognizing the Heart.

And still gradually loosen the bonds of limitation.

This is where the commentary becomes remarkably generous.



## 9. Can external ritual loosen bondage before full realization?

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S PROSE · KASHMIR SERIES PP. 221-222

The text describes practitioners who have not yet entered the full essence of the Heart's potency, but who nevertheless practise outward observances.

The key words are:

**बाह्याचारपरिशीलन**

*Bāhyācārapariśīlana.*

"The sustained cultivation of external observance."

And then an extraordinary sequence describing bondage becoming progressively weaker.

### Bāhya - बाह्य

External, outward.

### Ācāra - आचार

Conduct, established practice, observance.

From *ā-√car*, to practise or conduct oneself.

Thus:

**बाह्याचार - bāhyācāra**

External ritual conduct or observance.

### Pariśīlana - परिशीलन

Repeated cultivation, diligent practice.

From the *pari-√śīl* family.

Thus:

**बाह्याचारपरिशीलन - bāhyācārapariśīlana**

The sustained practice of external observances.

Now the passage says that even through this limited practice, the bonds can weaken progressively.

The Sanskrit is wonderfully expressive:

**शिथिलीभवत् - śithilībhavat**

Becoming loose.

**शिथिलित - śithilita**

Loosened.

**विदलत् - vidalat**

Splitting apart.

**विदलित - vidalita**

Split open, broken apart.

All within one great compound describing the dissolution of the bonds of the finite subject.

### Śithila - शिथिल

Loose, relaxed, weakened.

### Śithilībhavat - शिथिलीभवत्

Becoming loosened.

From:

**शिथिल - śithila**

Loose.

**भवत् - bhavat**

Becoming.

### Vidal - विदल्

To split apart, burst open.

The related participial forms express the process of breaking.

### Pāśava - पाशव

Belonging to the bound individual.

### Bandhana - बन्धन

Bond, binding, restraint.

From √**बन्ध् - bandh**, to bind.

Now the whole sequence is almost cinematic.

The bonds loosen.

Then loosen further.

Then split.

Then break apart.

The image isn't instantaneous annihilation of the universe.

It's the progressive weakening of limitation.

### The remarkable philosophical generosity

Now this is important.

A practitioner may not possess full recognition at the beginning.

Yet the outward forms of practice can still serve a purpose within the traditional system.

They can loosen the structures of limitation.

But why?

Because the ritual isn't ultimately independent of the divine powers it expresses.

Even a preliminary method can orient the practitioner toward the Heart.

### Does this mean every ritual is automatically liberating?

No.

The text is speaking within a particular Śaiva ritual tradition.

It isn't establishing that any arbitrary physical action inevitably produces realization.

Nor does it abolish distinctions between appropriate and inappropriate practice.

But it does reject a simplistic opposition:

"Either you already possess perfect recognition, or external practice is entirely worthless."

Abhinavagupta offers a more graduated relationship.

**The method can contribute to the loosening of bondage before the ultimate recognition is fully established.**

### The metaphysical shoelace problem

Imagine the finite subject tied up in innumerable conceptual shoelaces.

"I am only this."

"I cannot know beyond this."

"This is separate from me."

"I must preserve this identity."

Now ritual practice begins loosening them.

One knot.

Then another.

Then another.

The practitioner isn't yet proclaiming the total recognition of universal consciousness.

But the structure of limitation becomes less rigid.

And eventually the very Heart that had seemed distant can be recognized.

This is the doctrine's practical point.

Now here's the extraordinary twist.

Abhinavagupta immediately warns against a much subtler problem.

Someone can declare:

"I have entered the Heart."

And that very declaration may become another conceptual trap.

The deepest obstruction isn't necessarily a lack of religious vocabulary.

It may be an attachment to the vocabulary itself.


## 10. एतद्धृदयेऽनुप्रविष्टोऽस्मि - “I have entered the Heart!”

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN PROSE · KASHMIR SERIES P. 222

Abhinavagupta examines the thought:

**एतद्धृदयेऽनुप्रविष्टोऽस्मि।**

*Etad hṛdaye 'nupraviṣṭo 'smi.*

"I have entered this Heart."

And another:

**इयं देवी परा।**

*Iyaṃ devī parā.*

"This Goddess is Parā."

Now these are not independently quoted Tantric verses.

They are examples within Abhinavagupta's own prose.

He uses them to expose a limitation of merely verbal identification.

### Etad - एतद्

This.

### Hṛdaye - हृदये

In the Heart.

Locative singular of:

**हृदय - hṛdaya**.

### Anupraviṣṭa - अनुप्रविष्ट

Entered into.

From:

**अनु - anu**

Along, into, following.

**प्र - pra**

Forth.

**√विश् - viś**

To enter.

Past passive participle:

**अनुप्रविष्ट - anupraviṣṭa**.

Masculine nominative singular:

**अनुप्रविष्टः - anupraviṣṭaḥ**.

Now the sandhi:

*anupraviṣṭaḥ + asmi → anupraviṣṭo 'smi.*

The visarga becomes *o*, and the initial *a* of *asmi* is elided.

### Asmi - अस्मि

I am.

First-person singular present of √**अस् - as**, to be.

Now notice how beautiful the Sanskrit is.

**अस्मि - asmi**

I am.

We're back to first-person self-reference.

But that very self-reference can be limited.

### Śābdavikalpa - शाब्दविकल्प

Verbal conceptual construction.

**शाब्द - śābda**

Pertaining to words.

**विकल्प - vikalpa**

Conceptual differentiation, determination.

Thus:

**शाब्दविकल्प - śābdavikalpa**

A verbal conceptual construction.

### Kalpya - कल्प्य

To be conceived, imagined, constructed.

From √**कॢप् - kḷp**, to arrange or conceive in the relevant formation.

Now the philosophical argument becomes clear.

**Entry into the Heart is not adequately constituted by entertaining the sentence “I have entered the Heart.”**

Nor does saying "this is Parā" necessarily establish recognition of Parā's supreme nature.

### Why this matters

Imagine someone reading the Sanskrit.

They learn:

*Sauḥ is the Heart of Bhairava.*

Then they announce:

"I understand the Heart."

But perhaps they've understood only the definition.

The difference between:

**Knowing a proposition about the Heart.**

And:

**Recognizing the conscious reality the proposition concerns.**

That is the issue.

### The conceptual trap

Now we must be careful.

Abhinavagupta is not condemning every use of language.

He is writing an exceptionally linguistic philosophical commentary.

The problem is not that a correct Sanskrit sentence is inherently evil.

It's mistaking the sentence for the complete realization.

### The search for another Heart

The prose explicitly warns that conceptual objectification can lead to:

**हृदयान्तरमार्गण - hṛdayāntaramārgaṇa**

"Searching for another Heart."

Let's dismantle it.

**हृदय - hṛdaya**

Heart.

**अन्तर - antara**

Other, different.

**मार्गण - mārgaṇa**

Seeking, searching.

Thus:

**हृदयान्तरमार्गण - hṛdayāntaramārgaṇa**

Searching for another Heart.

Now here's the really subtle problem.

You decide:

"The Heart must be some special object I haven't yet encountered."

Then you search for it.

And because you've already defined it as an object separate from awareness, you continue missing the nature of the awareness through which your search is possible.

The very concept of a special Heart can become a reason to look elsewhere.

### The mystical real-estate disaster

Imagine a practitioner announcing:

"I have entered the Heart."

"Excellent. Where is it?"

"Somewhere within."

"Within what?"

"My consciousness."

"And what is the nature of that consciousness?"

"Well, I'm still looking for the Heart."

Then they discover another method.

Another conceptual description.

Another object of attention.

Another imagined chamber.

Eventually they're searching for a Heart inside a Heart inside a Heart.

A metaphysical property ladder.

And Abhinavagupta's point is devastating:

**The ultimate Heart is not another independently existing object to be acquired by searching outside the self-revealing ground.**

The recognition concerns the nature of awareness itself.

And now he quotes a beautiful stanza.

A stanza that says scriptural bonds don't have the power to shrink the Heart.

Nor does the world possess an independently ultimate power to corrupt consciousness.

This is not a declaration that the world is morally harmless.

It's a metaphysical statement about the nondual ground.

And the Sanskrit is exceptionally beautiful.


## 11. सङ्कोचयन्ति हृदयं नहि शास्त्रपाशाः - Scripture cannot imprison the Heart

QUOTED STANZA IN ABHINAVAGUPTA'S PROSE · P. 222 · AUTHORSHIP NOT EXPLICITLY STATED

The stanza opens:

**सङ्कोचयन्ति हृदयं नहि शास्त्रपाशाः।**

*Saṃkocayanti hṛdayaṃ na hi śāstrapāśāḥ.*

"Scriptural fetters do not contract the Heart."

And continues by denying that the world can intrinsically defile consciousness.

The two transcriptions agree substantially on the stanza, although their spacing and minor transcription conventions differ. Importantly, Abhinavagupta introduces the **next** hymn quotation explicitly as his own; he does not give the same explicit self-attribution for this preceding stanza. We should therefore call it a quotation in his commentary, not confidently assign it to a particular independently identified hymn. ; \]

Now let's learn the Sanskrit.

### Saṃkocayanti - सङ्कोचयन्ति

They contract, cause to shrink.

From:

**सम् - sam**

Together.

**√कुच् - kuc**

To contract or bend in the relevant lexical family.

The causative:

**सङ्कोचयति - saṃkocayati**

Causes to contract.

Third-person plural:

**सङ्कोचयन्ति - saṃkocayanti**.

### Hṛdayam - हृदयम्

Heart.

Neuter accusative singular.

### Na hi - न हि

Certainly not, indeed not.

A strong negative expression.

### Śāstra - शास्त्र

Scripture, treatise, authoritative instruction.

### Pāśa - पाश

Bond, fetter, noose.

Now the compound:

**शास्त्रपाश - śāstrapāśa**

Scriptural fetter.

Nominative plural:

**शास्त्रपाशाः - śāstrapāśāḥ**.

Now this is an astonishing expression in a Tantric commentary.

Because Abhinavagupta isn't standing outside scripture criticizing religion in general.

He is an extraordinarily learned Tantric author.

He reveres the Āgamas.

And yet he can describe scriptural structures as *pāśas*, bonds, when considered through the limiting mode of apprehension.

### The meaning

The verse doesn't say scripture is useless.

It says the supreme Heart isn't ultimately contracted by scriptural bonds.

The distinction is essential.

A scripture can help orient the practitioner.

But a limited interpretation of scripture can become a structure of restriction.

The intrinsic fullness of consciousness is not destroyed by either.

### The world cannot intrinsically defile Saṃvid

The next phrase is:

**नो संविदं कलुषयेद्यदयं च लोकः।**

*No saṃvidaṃ kaluṣayed yad ayaṃ ca lokaḥ.*

The broad sense is that the world does not have the power to corrupt consciousness in its ultimate nature.

### Saṃvid - संविद्

Consciousness.

Accusative singular:

**संविदम् - saṃvidam**.

### Kaluṣayet - कलुषयेत्

Might make impure, muddy or defile.

From:

**कलुष - kaluṣa**

Impure, turbid, stained.

The causative optative:

**कलुषयेत् - kaluṣayet**.

### Lokaḥ - लोकः

World.

Masculine nominative singular.

Now the philosophical point.

The world can contain suffering.

Violence.

Confusion.

Error.

And moral evil.

Those are meaningful differentiated experiences.

But the supreme conscious ground isn't turned into a second intrinsically impure substance merely because such contents appear within manifestation.

### The full state

The stanza's conclusion describes:

**सम्यक्स्वभावपदवीपरिपूर्णरूपा**

*Samyaksvabhāvapadavīparipūrṇarūpā.*

"Full in the path of its own perfectly realized nature."

And:

**उल्लसल्लयभरा**

*Ullasallayabharā.*

"Abundant with the shining forth of absorption."

The imagery is of fullness rather than contraction.

### The theological implication

Scripture belongs to manifestation.

The world belongs to manifestation.

Their limiting forms don't possess an independent power to annihilate the conscious ground.

So the Heart remains full.

### Metre: Vasantatilakā

The stanza is composed in **Vasantatilakā**, with fourteen syllables per pāda.

The opening line divides as:

*saṃ-ko-ca-yan-ti-hṛ-da-yaṃ-na-hi-śās-tra-pā-śāḥ*

Fourteen syllables.

The heavy-light pattern is characteristic of the metre.

Notice particularly:

**शास्त्र - śāstra**

The conjunct **स्त्र - stra**.

It affects syllable quantity without adding a separate vowel.

And:

**सङ्कोच - saṃkoca**

The syllable **saṃ** is heavy because of the nasal.

Now the stanza is philosophically beautiful.

But Abhinavagupta doesn't stop there.

He introduces a second quotation.

And this time he explicitly says:

**यदुक्तं मयैव स्तोत्रे - yad uktaṃ mayaiva stotre.**

"What I myself have said in a hymn."

We have direct authorial attribution.

And the verse describes the astonishing effect of immersion in divine devotion.

A sudden clarity of mind.

And the state of the Heart becoming immediately evident.


## 12. भवद्भक्त्यावेशात् - Abhinavagupta quotes his own hymn

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN HYMN · EXPLICITLY ATTRIBUTED IN HIS PROSE · P. 222

The Kashmir Series begins:

**भवद्भक्त्यावेशात्**

*Bhavadbhaktyāveśāt.*

"Through immersion in devotion to you."

GRETIL instead reads:

**भगवद्भक्त्यावेशात्**

*Bhagavadbhaktyāveśāt.*

"Through immersion in devotion to the Lord."

This is a genuine textual variant.

The former is direct second-person address.

The latter uses the Lord as the object of devotion.

The readings are close in theological sense but not identical in grammar. ; \]

Let's learn the compound.

### Bhavat - भवत्

You, venerable you.

A respectful second-person pronoun.

In compounds, **bhavad-** may designate what pertains to you.

### Bhagavat - भगवत्

The blessed Lord, venerable divine being.

The form *bhagavad-* appears before a voiced consonant in compound formation.

### Bhakti - भक्ति

Devotion, participation, loving dedication.

Related to √**भज् - bhaj**, to share, participate or serve.

### Āveśa - आवेश

Entry, absorption, immersion.

From *ā-√viś*, to enter.

Thus:

**भक्त्यावेश - bhaktyāveśa**

Immersion in devotion.

The sandhi:

*bhakti + āveśa → bhaktyāveśa.*

The vowel **i** becomes the semivowel **y** before the following vowel.

### The ablative ending

**भक्त्यावेशात् - bhaktyāveśāt**

Because of or through devotional immersion.

The ablative can express cause.

Now the hymn describes a condition of greatly clarified mind.

The Kashmir Series reads a form ending in:

**मनस्यम् - manasyam**.

GRETIL reads:

**मनसाम् - manasām**.

The latter is the genitive plural "of minds"; the former would require a different grammatical construction and is textually awkward in the immediate context.

We should not silently rewrite the Kashmir Series.

### Viśadatara - विशदतर

Clearer, more lucid.

**विशद - viśada**

Clear.

**-तर - -tara**

Comparative suffix.

Thus:

**विशदतर - viśadatara**

More lucid.

### Saṃjāta - सञ्जात

Arisen, produced, become.

From *sam-√jan*, to arise or be born.

Now the expression describes a clearer mental condition arising through devotional immersion.

### Kṣaṇena - क्षणेन

In a moment.

Instrumental singular of:

**क्षण - kṣaṇa**

Moment.

### Eṣā avasthā - एषावस्था

This state.

**एषा - eṣā**

This, feminine.

**अवस्था - avasthā**

State or condition.

Through sandhi:

*eṣā + avasthā → eṣāvasthā.*

### Sphuṭam - स्फुटम्

Clearly, distinctly.

### Adhivasati - अधिवसति

Dwells, occupies, settles within.

From:

**अधि - adhi**

Upon, over.

**√वस् - vas**

To dwell.

Third-person singular:

**अधिवसति - adhivasati**.

The connected form:

**अधिवसत्येव - adhiv asaty eva** in continuous pronunciation, normally written *adhivasaty eva*.

### Hṛdayam - हृदयम्

Heart.

Now the hymn's meaning is powerful.

Through devotional immersion, clarity can arise, and the state of the Heart becomes present.

The point isn't merely the mechanical repetition of a formula.

It concerns the transformation of apprehension.

### A subtle philosophical relationship

We have just studied why merely saying "I entered the Heart" is insufficient.

Now Abhinavagupta describes a state of devotional absorption in which the Heart becomes directly evident.

The contrast matters.

**Conceptual declaration alone is not realization.**

But a transformative mode of awareness can disclose the Heart.

And the same sacred tradition includes devotion as one such mode.

### The scholar's embarrassing discovery

Imagine an academic expert on Bhakti.

They have written ten papers.

They know every grammatical form of *bhakti*.

They can identify historical variants of devotional hymns.

Then Abhinavagupta says:

"The relevant transformation concerns immersion in devotion."

The scholar replies:

"Yes, of course, but I'd prefer to remain an external observer."

Now that's a legitimate scholarly standpoint.

But it isn't identical with the experience described in the hymn.

And this distinction is important.

**A historical description of spiritual practice and the practitioner's claimed realization are different kinds of knowledge.**

The hymn concerns the latter.

The philological analysis concerns the former.

We need both, without confusing them.

And now Abhinavagupta turns back to ritual structure.

Three goddesses at the corners.

One in the centre.

A triangle.

And the astonishing claim that worship of the Heart is the **expansion of bliss**.



## 13. कोणेषु पूज्यास्तिस्रः - Three goddesses at the corners

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S PROSE · PP. 222-223

Now the commentary says:

**कोणेषु पूज्यास्तिस्रः।**

*Koṇeṣu pūjyās tisraḥ.*

"Three $$Goddesses$$ are to be worshipped at the corners."

And:

**मध्ये देवी।**

*Madhye devī.*

"The Goddess $$is worshipped$$ in the centre."

Now this is a wonderfully compact ritual statement.

Three corners.

Three divine powers.

One central Goddess.

It recalls the triangular Yoni we've studied throughout the commentary.

Let's learn the grammar.

### Koṇa - कोण

Corner, angle.

Locative plural:

**कोणेषु - koṇeṣu**.

At the corners.

Now the plural is important.

We're not describing one single point.

### Pūjyāḥ - पूज्याः

To be worshipped.

From √**पूज् - pūj**, to worship or honour.

The gerundive:

**पूज्य - pūjya**.

Feminine nominative plural:

**पूज्याः - pūjyāḥ**.

Through sandhi:

**पूज्यास्तिस्रः - pūjyās tisraḥ**.

### Tisraḥ - तिस्रः

Three, feminine nominative plural.

This is a particularly useful Sanskrit numeral.

Compare:

**त्रयः - trayaḥ**, three masculine.

**तिस्रः - tisraḥ**, three feminine.

**त्रीणि - trīṇi**, three neuter.

The feminine form agrees with the implied Goddesses.

### Madhye - मध्ये

In the middle.

Locative singular of:

**मध्य - madhya**.

### Devī - देवी

Goddess.

Feminine nominative singular.

Now the structure is explicit.

Three at the corners.

One in the centre.

### Which goddesses?

Within the Trika tradition, the threefold powers are associated with the divine triadic manifestation, commonly articulated through Parā, Parāparā and Aparā.

But the present sentence doesn't independently spell out all three names in this location.

It describes the triangular arrangement and the supreme central Goddess.

We should distinguish the general Trika symbolism from the literal contents of this particular clause.

### The central Goddess

Abhinavagupta describes the centre through:

**परानन्दभैरवनिर्मथनरूपा**

*Parānandabhairavanirmathanarūpā.*

The compound relates the Goddess to supreme bliss and the stirring or churning of Bhairava.

Let's dismantle it.

**पर - para**

Supreme.

**आनन्द - ānanda**

Bliss.

**भैरव - bhairava**

Bhairava.

**निर्मथन - nirmathana**

Churning, stirring, rubbing, agitation.

From *nir-√manth*, to churn.

**रूपा - rūpā**

Having the nature of.

Now the imagery is explicitly dynamic.

The centre isn't an inert point.

It is the locus of divine blissful dynamism.

### The triangular metaphysical theatre

Imagine a triangle.

Three corners.

Three differentiated divine powers.

At the centre, Parā.

Now a student asks:

"Which corner is the real Absolute?"

Wrong question.

The corners articulate differentiated powers.

The central principle expresses their unity.

But even that spatial arrangement is a ritual representation.

The supreme conscious reality isn't literally trapped inside a triangle drawn on paper.

### The profound distinction

**The geometric form is a means of representing the relationships among divine powers.**

Its ritual significance doesn't require the Absolute to be a physical object occupying a tiny central coordinate.

That's why Abhinavagupta can speak of the triangle and immediately relate it to the nondual expansion of bliss.

The geometry represents a metaphysical process.

And now the text enters explicitly Kaula territory.

Divine union.

The Yoginī and Vīra traditions.

The *dūtī*.

And the generative powers of bliss and Visarga.

We need to preserve the historical bodily dimension without inventing a complete ritual manual.


## 14. The Kaula triangle - Bliss, union and the *dūtī*

The prose distinguishes the ritual understanding associated with divine beings and that associated with Vīras.

It uses:

**यामलयोग - yāmalayoga**

The union of a pair.

And:

**दूतीसङ्घट्ट - dūtīsaṃghaṭṭa**

The conjunction or encounter involving the *dūtī*.

Now these are significant technical terms.

### Yāmala - यामल

Pair, coupled form, union.

In Tantric systems, *yāmala* can refer to paired divine principles or sacred textual and ritual structures involving a pair.

### Yoga - योग

Union, joining, integration.

Thus:

**यामलयोग - yāmalayoga**

The union of a pair.

### Dūtī - दूती

Female messenger or intermediary.

In Kaula contexts, *dūtī* can refer to a ritual female partner, with important initiatory and symbolic significance.

We should not erase that historical usage.

### Saṃghaṭṭa - सङ्घट्ट

Conjunction, collision, union, coming together.

From *sam-√ghaṭṭ*, to strike or join together.

Now the compound:

**दूतीसङ्घट्ट - dūtīsaṃghaṭṭa**

Conjunction with the dūtī.

### Ānandakṣobha - आनन्दक्षोभ

**आनन्द - ānanda**

Bliss.

**क्षोभ - kṣobha**

Agitation, stirring.

Thus:

**आनन्दक्षोभ - ānandakṣobha**

The stirring of bliss.

### Why the bodily dimension matters

The passage doesn't restrict itself to abstract metaphysics.

It brings together:

Ritual bodies.

Generative imagery.

The triangular matrix.

Divine union.

And the inner repose of consciousness.

This is consistent with the Kaula setting.

But we should avoid two errors.

First, treating the bodily imagery as irrelevant or embarrassing.

Second, assuming the prose supplies every operational detail of a historically specific ritual.

It does not.

The original uses compressed terminology belonging to a wider initiatory world.

### Ekavīra - एकवीर

The passage also mentions:

**एकवीरता - ekavīratā**

The condition associated with the single Vīra.

The commentary distinguishes this through:

**स्वरूपानन्दविश्रान्तियोग**

*Svarūpānandaviśrāntiyoga.*

"Union through repose in the bliss of one's own nature."

Now that's an extraordinary contrast.

The paired ritual symbolism has one expression.

The single-practitioner interpretation emphasizes intrinsic blissful repose.

The distinction doesn't necessarily mean that every practitioner follows one identical physical procedure.

It expresses different ritual and contemplative modalities.

### The triangle in the male body

The text also describes a triangular locus related to the lower bodily region and the source of generative power.

This is an explicitly embodied reference, not merely a diagram of abstract logic.

But the commentary ultimately relates its significance to the concentration and unfolding of blissful awareness.

Now here's the philosophical point.

**The bodily symbol and the supreme conscious reality are related without being identical descriptions at the same level.**

The bodily process belongs to manifestation.

The supreme Heart is its nondual ground.

### The incomplete quotation

Abhinavagupta next invokes a scriptural fragment beginning:

**वह्नेर्विषस्य मध्ये तु…**

*Vahner viṣasya madhye tu…*

But the Kashmir Series has explicit marks for missing text, and GRETIL transmits only the opening with an ellipsis.

We cannot reconstruct the remaining line.

Nor can we securely establish its full ritual meaning from the surviving words alone.

The phrase begins:

"In the middle of fire and poison…"

But the complete syntactic relation is lost in these witnesses.

So we stop at the attested beginning.

No invented continuation.

And now Abhinavagupta gives a clear quotation from a named authority.

Knowledge.

### Antaḥ - अन्तः

Within, inwardly.

### Bahiḥ - बहिः

Outside, outwardly.

### Udrikta - उद्रिक्त

Predominant, intensified, overflowing.

The knowledge-mudrā predominates inwardly.

**क्रियामुद्रा बहिः**

The action-mudrā is outward.

### Viparyaya - विपर्यय

Reversal.

Don't import the earlier epistemological sense automatically.

Context matters.

### Anupraveśa - अनुप्रवेश

Entry, penetration, inward realization.

The two powers participate in one relationship of manifestation.

### Knowledge and action are not two independent minds

The same consciousness knows.

## 20. First interpretation - Four stages of cognition gathered into one Heart

The first reading begins with the nondual harmony of the known and the knower:

**वेद्यवेदकयोः सामरस्यं।**

"The harmonious unity of the knowable and the knower."

Now the important words are:

**वेद्य - vedya**

That which is to be known.

**वेदक - vedaka**

Knower, that which apprehends.

**सामरस्य - sāmarasya**

Homogeneous unity, shared essence, mutual harmony.

Let's learn the grammar.

### Vedyavedakayoḥ - वेद्यवेदकयोः

A compound containing:

**वेद्य - vedya**

Knowable.

**वेदक - vedaka**

Knower.

The ending:

**-योः - -yoḥ**

Genitive or locative dual.

Thus:

**वेद्यवेदकयोः - vedyavedakayoḥ**

Of the known and the knower.

Now that dual is beautiful.

Two distinguishable poles of cognition.

But the text describes their *sāmarasya*.

### Sāmarasya - सामरस्य

Harmony, identity of essence, unification.

From:

**समरस - samarasa**

Of equal essence, sharing one relish.

The abstract noun:

**सामरस्य - sāmarasya**.

Now this isn't the simple abolition of the distinction between subject and object at every conventional level.

### The four states

The commentary then refers to:

**चतसृणां दशानामुद्योगादीनाम्**

*Catasṛṇāṃ daśānām udyogādīnām.*

"The four states beginning with Udyoga."

Now here the Sanskrit is using:

**चतसृणाम् - catasṛṇām**

Of four, feminine genitive plural.

**दशानाम् - daśānām**

Of states or conditions, from *daśā*.

This is not the numeral *daśa*, ten, in this phrase.

That's a crucial distinction.

The written forms can resemble each other, but the grammar and meaning differ.

### The four moments return

**उद्योग - udyoga**

Initiative, orientation toward manifestation.

**अवभास - avabhāsa**

Appearance.

**संक्रम - saṃkrāma**

Transition or assimilation.

**विलापन - vilāpana**

Dissolution or inward reabsorption.

These were the four cognitive modes of Part XXI.

Now they're interpreted through the phonemic Heart.

### The *caturdaśa* wordplay

A phrase containing:

**चतस्रः दशाः - catasraḥ daśāḥ**

Four states.

Can be related through Tantric *nirvacana* to the phonemic expression involving *caturdaśa*.

This is not ordinary proof that the numeral fourteen historically means "four stages."

It's a deliberate doctrinal reading.

But it's intelligible in context.

The Heart includes the fourfold movement of consciousness.

### The primary unity

The commentary calls the initial condition:

**अविभागभूः प्राथमिकी**

*Avibhāgabhūḥ prāthamikī.*

"The primal ground of nondivision."

### Avibhāga - अविभाग

Nondifferentiation.

**अ - a-**

Not.

**विभाग - vibhāga**

Division.

### Bhū - भू

Ground, foundation, place of becoming in the relevant compound.

### Prāthamika - प्राथमिक

Primary, first.

Now the argument is clear.

Before the distinguishable stages of knowing, the supreme ground is not itself constituted by their division.

And the four stages arise through its manifestation.

### The phonemic reading

The same interpretation invokes the vowel-powers ending in:

**ऊकार - ūkāra**

The phoneme *ū*.

And then four designated:

**अमृतवर्णाः - amṛtavarṇāḥ**

Nectar-phonemes.

Now we should be precise.

The Sanskrit identifies a set of four as *amṛtavarṇas* here.

The passage does not itself give a fresh explicit list of their individual names at this exact point.

We should not invent a list merely to make the account look tidy.

The important structure is the relation of the primary vowel-powers, the four nectar-powers and the unifying Heart.

### The Bhagavad Gītā quotation

The commentary then cites:

**सर्वभूतस्थमात्मानं सर्वभूतानि चात्मनि।**

"The Self abiding in all beings, and all beings in the Self."

Now the connection is clear.

The knower and the known are distinguishable in ordinary experience.

But the contemplative realization involves recognizing their ultimate relation within consciousness.

### Prasaṃkhyāna - प्रसंख्यान

Intensive contemplation, reflective enumeration or sustained discriminative investigation.

The commentary also mentions:

**अभ्यास - abhyāsa**

Practice, repetition.

Now the important qualification.

The point isn't to physically merge a pot with your body.

It's to understand the relation of subject and object within the conscious ground.

### The beautiful conclusion

The first interpretation relates:

The four stages of cognition.

The knowable and knower.

The nectar-powers.

The nondivided ground.

And the supreme Heart.

All to the same Anuttara.

**A threefold supreme Śakti.**

Together:

Forty.

**-त्व - -tva**.

That-ness, principle.

Bhairava.

Division, classification, differentiation.

**भैरवभेद - bhairavabheda**

A classification according to Bhairava.

### The quoted verse on initiation

Abhinavagupta then quotes a two-line teaching beginning:

**षट्त्रिंशच्छोधनीयानि।**

*Ṣaṭtriṃśac chodanīyāni.*

"The thirty-six are to be purified."

The quoted teaching distinguishes:

The thirty-six principles undergoing purification.

The supreme Bhairava as purifier.

And the supreme Trika as the means.

It concludes by identifying this with the highest initiation.

This is a genuine quotation in Abhinavagupta's prose. However, in the Kashmir Series a long bracket interrupts it and introduces a separate supporting explanation, including the commentary's earlier opening invocation. That bracket must not be counted as part of the quoted verse. ; \]

### Śodhanīya - शोधनीय

To be purified.

From √**शुध् - śudh**, to become pure, with the relevant causative and gerundive formation.

**शोधनीय - śodhanīya**

To be purified.

Neuter nominative plural:

**शोधनीयानि - śodhanīyāni**.

### Śodhaka - शोधक

Purifier, one who purifies.

A causative agent noun.

### Dīkṣā - दीक्षा

Initiation, consecration.

An important technical term in Śaiva ritual systems.

The verse identifies the arrangement as:

**दीक्षेयं पारमार्थिकी - dīkṣeyaṃ pāramārthikī.**

"This is the initiation pertaining to ultimate reality."

### The philosophical meaning

The numerical structure is not simply an arbitrary attempt to create forty separate independent substances.

It expresses:

The differentiated ontological field.

Its supreme conscious ground.

And the powers through which the field is understood and purified.

The distinction between what is purified, the purifier and the means is pedagogically and ritually meaningful.

But the ultimate metaphysics remains nondual.

### The cosmic accounting department

The accountant says:

"Thirty-six tattvas."

Abhinavagupta replies:

"Add the supreme Bhairava."

"Thirty-seven."

"Add the triad of supreme powers."

"Forty."

"Wonderful. Is that the final total?"

"No."

"Why?"

"Because the text admits other interpretive constructions."

The accountant leaves the building.

But here's the point.

**Different numerical systems can represent different relations among manifestation, power and the supreme ground.**

We must not conflate them.

Nor should we claim that the scripture literally teaches only one number of independently ultimate entities.

### The distinction between number and nonduality

This is one of the central lessons of the entire commentary.

A classification can have thirty-six members.

Or forty.

Or sixteen.

Without the supreme reality becoming thirty-six, forty or sixteen independent Absolutes.

The numerical systems map manifestations.

They don't manufacture the conscious ground.

And now Abhinavagupta gives an important qualification.

The highest condition is primarily approached through recognition.

Different practitioners may also approach it through repeated practice or yogic methods.

But he explicitly mentions people attracted to **aindrajālika**, magical or illusionistic operations.

Which is a rather funny interruption.

Because after all these extraordinary ritual claims, Abhinavagupta reminds us not to mistake fascination with magical technique for the ultimate philosophical aim.

### Aindrajālika - ऐन्द्रजालिक

Pertaining to conjuring, illusionistic display or magical operation.

Related to:

**इन्द्रजाल - indrajāla**

Magical illusion, conjuring.

The passage contrasts the primary nondual recognition of the Heart with certain interests in extraordinary methods and effects.

The distinction is not that all ritual is worthless.

We've just seen Abhinavagupta defend its capacity to loosen bondage.

But the ultimate realization isn't identical with acquiring a spectacular magical result.

**The Heart is more fundamental than the powers attributed to the Heart.**

That's the second interpretation.

And now comes the third.


## 22. Third interpretation - The third *brahma* is Icchā, the will-power

READING 3 · P. 226

Abhinavagupta begins:

**इच्छाभिप्रायेण तृतीयमिच्छा।**

*Icchābhiprāyeṇa tṛtīyam icchā.*

"With reference to will, the third is Icchā."

Now the root text's:

**तृतीयं ब्रह्म - tṛtīyaṃ brahma**

is being reinterpreted.

At the elementary phonemic level, it led us to **sa**.

But here the "third" is understood through divine power.

Specifically:

**इच्छा - icchā**

Will.

### Icchā - इच्छा

Will, desire, intention.

An important divine power in Trika.

### Abhiprāya - अभिप्राय

Intention, purport, interpretive standpoint.

From the *abhi-pra-√i* family, to go toward or aim at.

Instrumental singular:

**अभिप्रायेण - abhiprāyeṇa**.

Thus:

**इच्छाभिप्रायेण - icchābhiprāyeṇa**

With reference to Icchā.

### Tṛtīya - तृतीय

Third.

Now the philosophical importance.

The "third" can refer to a position within a divine triad.

Not merely the third consonant of a given alphabetic class.

### Bṛṃhita - बृंहित

Expanded, made great.

### Iṣyamāṇa - इष्यमाण

Being desired or intended.

Related to √**इष् - iṣ**, to desire.

The commentary emphasizes that the intended object is not ultimately independent of the power of willing.

### The fortyfold reading continues

Now the word **caturdaśa** is again related to **forty**.

But the semantic emphasis differs.

Here the word *yuta* can refer to a relation among differentiated entities, including a sense of separately established terms in certain grammatical or philosophical uses.

Abhinavagupta invokes examples involving:

**युतसिद्ध - yutasiddha**

Established as joined or independently established in the relevant technical context.

The Kashmir Series supplies a long square-bracketed grammatical note referring to the *Siddhāntakaumudī*. That explanatory note is not Abhinavagupta's own uninterrupted prose at this location; GRETIL does not reproduce it.

### The philosophical point

The will-power can manifest objects as distinguishable.

But the supreme nature of Icchā isn't defined by an independently existing object standing outside consciousness.

**Desire and its object can be distinguished within manifestation while remaining grounded in one conscious power.**

Now the interpretation connects the lord of the lunar days with:

**अकुल - Akula**

The transcendent or unsurpassable Śiva-principle.

And:

**आनन्द - ānanda**

Bliss.

The sequence is an ordering of powers.

Not a historical phonological derivation of the numeral fourteen.

### The divine will department

Imagine Icchā announcing:

"I desire."

"What?"

"Manifestation."

"Where is the object?"

"Not outside the supreme conscious ground."

"So you're desiring yourself?"

"The distinction between desire and desired appears through my own power."

The philosopher's headache has returned.

But the argument is coherent within the system.

**Will is not dependent upon an independently ultimate external object.**

And now the fourth interpretation turns to another divine designation.

**Īśāna.**

The Lord.

The expansive form of will.


## 23. Fourth interpretation - Icchā becomes Īśāna through expansion

READING 4 · PP. 226-227

The text begins:

**ईशानापेक्षया तृतीयमिच्छारूपम्।**

*Īśānāpekṣayā tṛtīyam icchārūpam.*

"With reference to Īśāna, the third has the nature of will."

Now here's the important distinction.

The third *brahma* isn't always being read through exactly the same symbolic system.

In this reading, the relevant divine power is **Icchā as Īśāna**.

### Īśāna - ईशान

Lord, sovereign, a Śaiva divine designation.

Related to √**ईश् - īś**, to rule.

### Apekṣā - अपेक्षा

Reference, relation, regard.

Instrumental:

**अपेक्षया - apekṣayā**.

Thus:

**ईशानापेक्षया - īśānāpekṣayā**

With reference to Īśāna.

### Prasara - प्रसर

Expansion, unfolding.

### Bṛhadbhūta - बृहद्भूत

Become great or expanded.

From:

**बृहत् - bṛhat**

Great.

**भूत - bhūta**

Become.

Thus:

**बृहद्भूत - bṛhadbhūta**

Expanded.

Now the commentary explains that Icchā assumes the condition of Īśāna through its unfolding.

### Vyāmiśratā - व्यामिश्रता

Intermixture, mutual blending.

From:

**वि - vi**

Apart or distinctly.

**आ - ā**

Toward.

**मिश्र - miśra**

Mixed.

With the abstract suffix:

**-ता - -tā**.

Thus:

**व्यामिश्रता - vyāmiśratā**

Intermixture.

Now this connects directly to the interpretation of *yuta*.

It may express mutual blending rather than merely arithmetic addition.

### The first Spanda of Kula Śakti

The commentary names:

**कुलशक्तिप्रथमस्पन्द**

*Kulaśaktiprathamaspanda.*

"The first pulsation of Kula Śakti."

Now that is a beautiful return to Part XXII.

Spanda.

The intrinsic upsurge of conscious power.

Not ordinary spatial displacement.

Here it appears as the first stirring of Kula Śakti.

### The philosophical relation

Will expands.

The powers intermix.

The first pulse of manifestation appears.

And the entire process remains grounded in the nondual reality of Śiva and Śakti.

The "third" is therefore not merely a fixed numerical slot.

It's a stage in the articulation of divine power.

### The cosmic expansion permit

Imagine Icchā requesting permission to expand.

"Who authorizes this?"

"Īśāna."

"Who is Īśāna?"

"The sovereign form of divine power."

"Is this a second independent deity outside consciousness?"

"No."

"Then who signs the permit?"

"Svātantrya."

The permit is approved immediately.

Because the power of manifestation doesn't require an external authority.

That's the fourth interpretation.

Now the fifth is closely related.

But it brings **Jñānaśakti**, knowledge-power, into the picture.

And the famous word:

**Unmeṣa.**

The opening of manifestation.


## 24. Fifth interpretation - Unmeṣa and the first differentiation of knowledge

READING 5 · P. 227

The text begins:

**उन्मेषात्मकज्ञानशक्तियोगेन।**

*Unmeṣātmakajñānaśaktiyogena.*

"Through conjunction with knowledge-power whose nature is Unmeṣa."

Now this is a marvellous compound.

Let's dismantle it.

### Unmeṣa - उन्मेष

Opening, unfolding, emergence.

The word can mean the opening of the eyes.

Within Kashmir Śaivism, it becomes a technical description of the first emergence of manifestation.

### Ātmaka - आत्मक

Having the nature of.

Thus:

**उन्मेषात्मक - unmeṣātmaka**

Having the nature of Unmeṣa.

### Jñāna - ज्ञान

Knowing.

### Śakti - शक्ति

Power.

Thus:

**ज्ञानशक्ति - jñānaśakti**

Knowledge-power.

### Yoga - योग

Union, conjunction.

Instrumental singular:

**योगेन - yogena**.

Thus the whole compound identifies the relationship between the opening of manifestation and divine knowledge-power.

### The first differentiation

The commentary refers to:

**प्रथमविभाग - prathamavibhāga**

First differentiation.

**प्रथम - prathama**

First.

**विभाग - vibhāga**

Division.

Now the important distinction.

This isn't a moment on a clock that existed before consciousness.

The temporal structure is itself part of manifestation.

The "first" here concerns the explanatory ordering of powers.

### Tithīśānta as the first pulse

The ending associated with the lord of the lunar powers is again related to:

**कुलशक्तिप्रथमस्पन्द - kulaśaktiprathamaspanda**.

The first Spanda of Kula Śakti.

And now a new verbal form appears:

**प्ररुरुक्षुता - prarurukṣutā**.

The wish or readiness to rise.

### Rurukṣu - रुरुक्षु

Desiring to ascend.

A desiderative formation associated with √**रुह् - ruh**, to rise or grow.

The form expresses inclination toward ascent or emergence.

Now the philosophical point.

The power is poised to manifest.

The first differentiation appears through the opening of knowledge.

But the conscious ground isn't thereby fragmented into independently existing substances.

### The eyelid analogy

Imagine an eye opening.

Before the opening, the visual field isn't manifest in the same way.

Then the eye opens.

Forms appear.

Now the physical act of opening an eye is not identical with supreme Spanda.

But it illustrates the notion of **unfolding**.

The manifestation of distinctions within an already existing capacity.

And that's why *unmeṣa* is such an evocative term.

### Why the root-word interpretation matters

The expression:

**तृतीयं ब्रह्म**

can be understood in relation to Īśāna and knowledge-power.

**Caturdaśa** can be brought into relation with a broader fortyfold tattva structure.

**Tithīśānta** can represent the first stirring of Kula Śakti.

The same root wording is mapped across multiple divine relationships.

This isn't ordinary arithmetic alone.

It's a structured Tantric interpretation of the phonemic and ontological powers.

And now the sixth reading returns to the vowel **ऊ - ū**.

The long *ū*.

Which Abhinavagupta has already associated with an important point in the expansion of divine powers.


## 25. Sixth interpretation - The long vowel Ū and the relation of pure and impure creation

READING 6 · KASHMIR SERIES P. 227

The key expression is:

**ऊकारकला - ūkārakalā.**

"The power associated with the vowel ū."

Now the passage places this power at a boundary between knowledge and action.

It then distinguishes:

**अशुद्ध - aśuddha**

Impure or limited.

**शुद्धाशुद्ध - śuddhāśuddha**

Pure-impure, mixed.

**शुद्ध - śuddha**

Pure.

These are familiar Śaiva ontological distinctions.

### Ūkāra - ऊकार

The vowel **ऊ - ū**.

Now remember the full form:

**उ - u**

Short *u*.

**ऊ - ū**

Long *ū*.

The vowel length matters.

### Kalā - कला

Power, phase, aspect.

Thus:

**ऊकारकला - ūkārakalā**

The phase or power of the long vowel *ū*.

### Madhyakoṭi - मध्यकोटि

Middle point, intermediate limit or junction.

**मध्य - madhya**

Middle.

**कोटि - koṭi**

Limit, point, extreme.

Thus:

**मध्यकोटि - madhyakoṭi**

Intermediate point or boundary.

The passage connects the vowel-power to the relationship of knowledge and action.

### The three levels of manifestation

In the Śaiva tattva system:

The pure level expresses the highest divine principles.

The pure-impure level concerns the emergence of limitation.

The impure level concerns the fully differentiated field of finite experience.

The boundaries and classifications are important.

But their ultimate ground remains one consciousness.

### The whole moving and unmoving universe

The commentary uses:

**चराचर - carācara**

Moving and unmoving.

From:

**चर - cara**

Moving.

**अचर - acara**

Unmoving.

Thus:

**चराचर - carācara**

The entire moving and unmoving world.

Now here's the philosophical significance.

The long *ū*-power is interpreted through the transition between divine knowledge, action and the differentiated universe.

But the "third" Brahma remains associated with pure creation in the relevant arrangement.

### Kādikṣānta - कादिक्षान्त

Extending from *ka* to *kṣa*.

We know this term from Part XV.

**क - ka**

The beginning of the consonantal series.

**क्ष - kṣa**

The final element of the sacred sequence.

Thus:

**कादिक्षान्त - kādikṣānta**

From *ka* to *kṣa*.

Now the text connects the pure source with the differentiated consonantal manifestation.

Again.

The entire alphabet has returned.

### The universe is back inside one vowel

Imagine the long *ū* attending a philosophical conference.

"I represent a specific divine power."

"Excellent."

"And I participate in the relation of knowledge and action."

"Wonderful."

"And the manifestation of the moving and unmoving universe."

The conference chair looks alarmed.

"All of that?"

"Yes."

"You're one vowel."

"That hasn't stopped Abhinavagupta."

A fair observation.

But the point is not that ordinary phonetic *ū* mechanically produces all phenomena.

It's that the Tantric phonemic system uses the vowel to represent a generative divine power.

**The phonological form serves as a sacred designation of the metaphysical relation.**

And now the seventh reading becomes especially curious.

Because the number four returns.

But this time it refers to four kinds of **śūnya**.

Four voids or spaces.

And the interpretation turns toward the destruction of the four elemental conditions.

The argument is becoming increasingly cosmological.



## 26. Seventh interpretation - Four elemental destructions and the space beyond them

READING 7 · PP. 227-228

Abhinavagupta now says:

**अथ शून्यचतुष्कानुसृत्या।**

*Atha śūnyacatuṣkānusṛtyā.*

"Now, following the fourfold void."

This is another major reinterpretation.

The *catur* element is no longer merely the numeral fourteen or four cognitive stages.

It refers to a fourfold series of voids.

### Śūnya - शून्य

Empty, void, space.

Now the word has different technical meanings in different Indian philosophical and contemplative systems.

We must not automatically equate every Śaiva *śūnya* with Buddhist *śūnyatā*.

Nor with complete nonexistence.

The context matters.

### Catuṣka - चतुष्क

A group of four.

From:

**चतुर् - catur**

Four.

With the grouping suffix:

**-ष्क - -ṣka**.

Thus:

**चतुष्क - catuṣka**

A fourfold set.

### Anusṛti - अनुसृति

Following, proceeding along.

From *anu-√sṛ*, to follow or move along.

Now:

**शून्यचतुष्कानुसृत्या - śūnyacatuṣkānusṛtyā**

By following the fourfold void.

### The four elemental conditions

The commentary speaks of four principles beginning with earth.

And their destruction or dissolution.

The key word is:

**विनाश - vināśa**

Destruction, dissolution.

From:

**वि - vi**

Apart.

**√नश् - naś**

To perish.

Now Abhinavagupta describes the *vyoman*, space, in which the destruction of the four elemental conditions occurs.

### Vyoman - व्योमन्

Space, sky.

The passage uses:

**व्योम - vyoma**.

Now the important philosophical distinction.

Space is not simply being described as a fifth physical container independent of the elements.

It is a level of manifestation within the contemplative and ontological system.

### The third Brahma

The third *brahma* is now interpreted through **Icchā** again.

And the terminal power associated with the lord of the lunar days is interpreted through the Sun's external radiance.

### Arka - अर्क

Sun.

### Tejas - तेजस्

Radiance, energy, brilliance, fire-like luminosity.

The phrase:

**बाह्येन तेजसा - bāhyena tejasā**

Through external radiance.

Both words are instrumental singular.

### The philosophical structure

Now the reading connects:

The dissolution of four elemental conditions.

Space as the remaining field.

Icchā as the third principle.

And solar radiance as the terminal power.

Different symbols.

Same Heart.

### The cosmic demolition company

Imagine Earth, Water, Fire and Air attending a demolition ceremony.

One by one, their differentiated forms are withdrawn.

Then Space remains.

A philosopher asks:

"Is that Space the supreme Absolute?"

Abhinavagupta replies:

"Not simply. The supreme conscious ground is not exhausted by one manifested element."

And that's the point.

**The elemental void is an interpretive and contemplative level, not necessarily the final reality of Anuttara.**

Now the eighth reading is unusually brief.

And that's important.

We shouldn't manufacture an enormous passage where the original contains one short statement.


## 27. Eighth interpretation - The same Īśāna reading applies

Then:

**एतदपि एवमेव।**

"This too is to be understood in the same way."

Now here's a lovely lesson in philological discipline.

But the Sanskrit itself gives only a compact reference back to the previously explained procedure.

It doesn't introduce a new ten-line analysis.

### Vyākhyāta - व्याख्यात

Explained.

From *vi-ā-√khyā*, to explain or declare.

### Krama - क्रम

Order, sequence.

Instrumental:

**क्रमेण - krameṇa**.

Thus:

**व्याख्यातक्रमेण - vyākhyātakrameṇa**

According to the sequence already explained.

### Īśāna - ईशान

The sovereign divine principle.

Now the sense is that the previously established interpretation can be applied again.

The reader is expected to retain the preceding argument.

### The exhausted commentator

Imagine the cosmic accountant preparing for another twenty-page explanation.

Abhinavagupta says:

"This one follows the previously explained sequence."

The accountant stares.

"That's it?"

"Yes."

"Thank God."

Then he turns the page.

And finds interpretation nine.

The relief lasts approximately three seconds.

But the joke illustrates something important.

**Not every numbered interpretation has equal textual length.**

We mustn't invent additional detail merely because the structure contains sixteen headings.

The eighth is brief.

Its function is to carry an established interpretation into another relation.

Now the ninth turns back toward earth and the inner transformation of four elements.

The terminal power becomes fiery again.

And we encounter a different interpretation of the relation between space and earth.


## 28. Ninth interpretation - Earth as the site of four inner elemental conditions

READING 9 · P. 228

The text again identifies the third *brahma* through Icchā.

Then it speaks of four elements beginning with space.

The key phrase is:

**चतुर्णां नभःप्रभृतीनाम्।**

*Caturṇāṃ nabhaḥprabhṛtīnām.*

"Of the four beginning with space."

Let's learn it.

### Caturṇām - चतुर्णाम्

Of four.

Genitive plural.

### Nabhas - नभस्

Sky, atmosphere, space.

### Prabhṛti - प्रभृति

Beginning with.

Related to *pra-√bhṛ*, to bear forth.

Now the compound:

**नभःप्रभृति - nabhaḥprabhṛti**

Beginning with space.

Genitive plural:

**नभःप्रभृतीनाम् - nabhaḥprabhṛtīnām**.

### Antardaśā - अन्तर्दशा

An inward state or internal condition.

**अन्तर् - antar**

Within.

**दशा - daśā**

State, condition.

Thus:

**अन्तर्दशा - antardaśā**.

Now the commentary relates the inward condition of the four elements to earth.

### Dharā - धरा

Earth.

Now here's the interesting inversion.

In the preceding interpretation, the four lower elemental conditions were considered through dissolution into space.

Here the structure starts with space and relates the inner stages to earth.

The direction of interpretation changes.

### The fiery terminal power

The passage invokes:

**वह्नि - vahni**

Fire.

And:

**तेजस् - tejas**

Radiance.

The terminal power is interpreted through a fire-related manifestation.

### The point

This is another correspondence between:

The third *brahma*.

The fourfold elemental series.

The terminal power.

And the relationship between space and manifest density.

The same root words support a different ontological mapping.

### The elemental elevator

Imagine the elements inside an elevator.

One interpretation travels upward.

Earth toward Space.

Another travels downward.

Space toward Earth.

The passengers ask:

"Which floor is the Heart?"

Abhinavagupta replies:

"The Heart isn't an independently ultimate floor in the elevator."

The passengers are becoming increasingly concerned.

But the point is clear.

**The different elemental arrangements are expressions of one conscious ground, not separate ultimate realities.**

And now we come to the tenth interpretation.

This one is genuinely extraordinary.

Because the third *brahma* is plunged into the complete void.

And the commentator describes a state of such profound absorption that even the sound of drums and cymbals may not bring a practitioner back into ordinary waking consciousness.

That's an authentic quoted line.

And we should read it carefully.


## 29. Tenth interpretation - The complete void and the yogin unmoved by drums

READING 10 · KASHMIR SERIES PP. 228-229

The first important expression is:

**परिपूर्णशून्यरूपप्लुत्या।**

*Paripūrṇaśūnyarūpaplutyā.*

"Through immersion in the form of the complete void."

**Yoni:** The matrix through which differentiated manifestation becomes expressible.

The two are not independent ultimate substances.

Their relation expresses the inseparable powers of Śiva and Śakti.

And that's why the next section isn't a completely unrelated new subject.

It is the complementary half of the generative exposition.

### Prativarṇam - प्रतिवर्णम्

For each phoneme.

Phoneme.

Thus:

**प्रतिवर्णम् - prativarṇam**

Phoneme by phoneme.

### Prāg eva - प्रागेव

Already, previously.

**प्राक् - prāk**

Before.

**एव - eva**

Indeed.

### The exact stopping point

We stop at the transition from sixteenfold **Bīja-vyāpti** to the upcoming detailed **Yoni-vyāpti**.

Not in the middle of the sixteenth interpretation.

Not after jumping ahead to bodily ritual substances.

And not before acknowledging the difficult thirteenth and fourteenth interpretations.

## 38. The final insight - You cannot enter a Heart you were never ultimately outside

Now think about the whole installment.

The root Tantra calls *sauḥ* the Heart of Bhairava.

Abhinavagupta begins by identifying the universe itself as Bhairava-form.

Then he describes its essence as Śiva-nature embraced by Parā Śakti.

The divine power is not absent from the manifestation.

The Heart is not an independent object separated from the universe.

Its nature is the conscious fullness through which the entire differentiated field appears.

And then comes the extraordinary warning.

**Saying “I have entered the Heart” is not necessarily entering the Heart.**

The sentence can become another limited conceptual construction.

You can turn the supreme Heart into an imagined object.

Then spend years searching for it somewhere beyond the awareness through which the search is occurring.

Now this is the remarkable philosophical move.

The difficulty isn't that you need to acquire a second consciousness.

It's that the differentiated mode of apprehension can fail to recognize its own ground.

And that is why Abhinavagupta can say external ritual may progressively loosen bondage.

The practice can transform the conditions of apprehension.

But the final reality isn't manufactured by the rite.

It is recognized.

### Why the Kaula symbolism matters

Then we encountered the Goddess's generative matrix.

The triangle.

Three goddesses at its corners.

Parā in the centre.

Divine union.

Bliss.

Visarga.

The knowledge-mudrā.

The action-mudrā.

These are not unrelated ritual curiosities.

They express the relation between the generative powers and their conscious ground.

The ritual symbolism makes the metaphysics embodied.

The philosophical interpretation makes the bodily and ritual symbolism intelligible as expressions of Śakti.

Neither level should be erased.

### The sixteen interpretations

Then Abhinavagupta subjected the root words to sixteen different constructions.

Four stages of cognition.

Forty tattvas.

Icchā.

Īśāna.

Unmeṣa.

The long *ū*.

The fourfold void.

Earth and space.

Different rhythms of Spanda.

The seed-powers of desire and speech.

Bindu.

Visarga.

The whole system returns to one central insight.

**The phonemic powers are differentiated expressions of one conscious freedom.**

The numbers differ according to the explanatory arrangement.

The supreme ground doesn't become divided by those enumerations.

And at the end, the entire network of manifestation is gathered into the recognition of Bhairava.

The poverty of limitation is removed.

Not because objects become conventionally identical.

Not because the world ceases to appear.

But because their ultimate relation to consciousness is recognized.

### The most beautiful irony

You may spend years trying to enter the Heart.

You may learn the mantra.

Study every Sanskrit compound.

Memorize the sixteen interpretations.

Perform traditional forms of worship.

Investigate the tattvas.

And eventually realize that the supreme Heart was never a second conscious reality from which you had been fundamentally excluded.

The methods don't manufacture the Heart.

They can disclose it.

And that's the distinction Abhinavagupta has been building toward since the first verse of the *Parātrīśikā*.

**The Heart of Bhairava isn't a place where consciousness eventually arrives. It is the self-revealing fullness through which the very experience of arrival becomes possible.**

And now the seed has been explained.

Sixteenfold.

But the complementary matrix remains.

*Yoni-vyāpti.*

The next section will take us from the sacred alphabet directly into the structure of embodiment, breath, the subtle body and the different levels of the knowing subject.

The entire human being becomes a representation of the cosmic Heart.

And the mathematical accounting gets even more complicated.

Abhinavagupta is apparently determined to make sure no accountant ever achieves liberation.
