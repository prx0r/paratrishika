# Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa

PART XIX - AHAM: The Divine I and the Universe in Reverse

*Abhinavagupta takes the entire universe, folds it into three Sanskrit phonemes, reveals why the Supreme Lord's “I” is not the same as your ego having a particularly ambitious Tuesday, quotes Utpaladeva on the resting place of consciousness, recites his own magnificent hymn to the Goddess, and then explains how sixty years of divine activity can somehow be contemplated within a single breath.*

Kashmir Series pp. 198-200 · Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa, commentary on verses 5-8


## Previously, in the cosmic madhouse…

We've spent eighteen installments studying the divine Sanskrit alphabet.

We've witnessed the birth of vowels, the condensation of consonants, the emergence of the thirty-six *tattvas*, the extraordinary powers of Mātṛkā, the relation between speech and consciousness, and the questionable philosophical decision to make a clay pot responsible for explaining practically everything.

Then came the problem of linguistic convention.

Why does **गो - go** mean cow?

Who establishes that relationship?

And what makes it possible to understand a convention in the first place?

Abhinavagupta's answer led us back to the nonconventional power of consciousness.

The ordinary word doesn't manufacture the consciousness that understands it.

Rather, the word, its meaning, and their recognition all become intelligible within the self-revealing power of awareness.

And the whole phonemic universe-vowels, consonants, words, objects, categories, gods, animals, calendars, everything-was traced back through Bindu into the supreme fullness of Anuttara.

Then Abhinavagupta made one final warning.

**There is no ultimate temporal sequence dividing this reality.**

We describe the unfolding of powers in order.

But the Absolute doesn't require that sequence in order to be complete.

And now comes the great revelation.

All this can be expressed by a single Sanskrit word.

# अहम्

## aham - I

Not merely the ordinary personal ego.

Not simply the body saying "I."

Not your collection of memories, preferences, possessions and psychological habits.

But the supreme self-revealing **I-consciousness** of Bhairava.

And here's the extraordinary thing: Abhinavagupta reads the very phonemic arrangement of *aham* as an expression of the universe's manifestation.

Its reversal represents withdrawal.

All the powers we've studied are gathered into one structure.

The first sound of the alphabet.

The final expressive power.

The point of reabsorption.

And the whole universe between them.

We'll resume exactly at Kashmir Series p. 198 and follow the unbracketed Sanskrit to the end of the argument on p. 200, stopping as the discussion turns toward the sixteen *tuṭis* and the lunar calendar. I've checked the Kashmir Series transcription against Somadeva Vasudeva's independent GRETIL transcription and Utpaladeva's *Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi*. There are substantive differences in the reverse *aham* notation, the title of Vāmana's work, and the description of *akāra*. We'll keep those differences visible. ; ; \]

Right.

Let's learn what “I” means.

And why, according to Abhinavagupta, it's doing considerably more work than your entire personality.


## 1. तदेतदुच्यते अहम् - This is called *Aham*

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN PROSE · KASHMIR SERIES P. 198

**तदेतदुच्यते अहमिति।**

*Tad etad ucyate aham iti.*

"This, then, is called *Aham*."

Now that is a spectacularly economical statement.

After all the metaphysical classifications, Abhinavagupta says:

"This is I."

And immediately adds the reverse condition of cosmic withdrawal.

The GRETIL reading gives:

**विपर्यये तु संहृतौ मह इति।**

*Viparyaye tu saṃhṛtau maha iti.*

"But in the reverse direction, in reabsorption, $$it is designated$$ *maha*."

The Kashmir Series instead prints the difficult segmentation **अह् अम्** at this point, rather than the continuous **maha** found in GRETIL. The reverse reading is also supported by Abhinavagupta's earlier explanation around Kashmir Series pp. 55-56. We can confidently identify the contrast between manifestation and withdrawal, but we shouldn't silently pretend the two witnesses display the same phonemic notation. ; ; \]

Let's take the Sanskrit apart.

### Tad etad - तदेतद्

**तत् - tat**

That.

**एतत् - etat**

This.

The combination:

**तदेतत् - tad etat**

This very thing, that which has just been established.

Notice the final consonant of *tat* becoming voiced before the following vowel in the connected expression.

Abhinavagupta is referring back to the entire preceding account of the supreme conscious fullness.

### Ucyate - उच्यते

Is said, is called, is designated.

From √**वच् - vac**, to speak.

The present passive has the form:

**उच्यते - ucyate**.

This is a useful irregular-looking formation.

Don't mechanically expect every passive of √*vac* to preserve the written consonant **वच्** unchanged.

### Aham - अहम्

I.

The first-person singular nominative pronoun.

In ordinary Sanskrit:

**अहम् - aham**

I.

But in Abhinavagupta's phonemic interpretation, the expression is considered through:

**अ - a**

The supreme beginning, Anuttara.

**ह - ha**

The culminating expressive power associated with Visarga.

**ं / म् - ṃ / m**

Bindu, the concentrated point of manifestation and return.

These correspondences are **Tantric phonemic interpretations**, not a claim about the historical linguistic derivation of the Indo-European pronoun *aham*.

That's an important distinction.

The historical Sanskrit word meaning "I" already exists.

Abhinavagupta uses its phonemic form to disclose a theological meaning.

### Viparyaya - विपर्यय

Reversal, inversion, the opposite direction.

We've encountered this word before in the sense of mistaken cognition.

But here it refers to the reverse movement of the phonemic sequence.

The context determines the meaning.

### Saṃhṛti - संहृति

Withdrawal, reabsorption, gathering in.

From:

**सम् - sam**

Together, completely.

**√हृ - hṛ**

To take, carry or gather.

The locative singular:

**संहृतौ - saṃhṛtau**

In reabsorption.

Notice **-au**.

Another feminine *-i* stem locative singular.

### The distinction between *aham* and *maha*

Now the point is subtle.

In the movement of creation, the phonemic exposition runs from the unsurpassable beginning through divine emission toward the manifested totality, gathered in Bindu.

In the reverse movement, the structure is considered from the standpoint of withdrawal.

But we should not confuse the transmitted *maha* with a casually reconstructed Sanskrit adjective **महा - mahā**, meaning great.

The reverse sequence is a **symbolic phonemic ordering**. It is not a claim that simply reversing the written letters of *aham* produces an ordinary Sanskrit word with an identical grammatical function.

And Bindu is not just an interchangeable spelling of the full consonant *ma* in every context.

The doctrinal scheme relates the two, but the phonetic and orthographic distinction remains.

### The cosmic rewind button

Imagine the entire universe as an unimaginably elaborate film.

The stars.

The oceans.

The thirty-six tattvas.

Every thought.

Every perception.

Every sound.

Every act of cognition.

Now Abhinavagupta compresses the whole production into one symbolic expression:

**Aham.**

And then considers it in reverse.

**Mah(a).**

Not because the cosmos is literally a three-letter video file.

But because the phonemic sequence expresses two aspects of one conscious reality:

Manifestation.

And reabsorption.

And now comes his crucial qualification.

**These aren't two different Absolutes.**


## 2. द्वैधमपि चेयमेकैव संवित् - Two directions, one consciousness

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S PROSE · P. 198

**द्वैधमपि चेयमेकैव वस्तुतः संवित्।**

*Dvaidham api ceyam ekaiva vastutaḥ saṃvit.*

"Even in this twofold presentation, this is in reality one consciousness alone."

Now this sentence is absolutely central.

Because we've been describing one direction of manifestation and another of withdrawal.

And the danger is obvious.

We could imagine:

One consciousness produces the world.

Another consciousness destroys it.

Or perhaps one supreme entity exists during creation and a different one during reabsorption.

Abhinavagupta refuses that.

The difference concerns the mode of manifestation.

Not the ultimate conscious ground.

### Dvaidha - द्वैध

Twofold division, dual aspect.

Related to:

**द्वि - dvi**

Two.

And the adjectival family *dvaidha*, twofold.

The form:

**द्वैधम् - dvaidham**

Twofoldness.

### Api - अपि

Even, although.

So:

**द्वैधमपि - dvaidham api**

Even though twofold.

### Iyam - इयम्

This.

Feminine nominative singular.

It refers to:

**संवित् - saṃvit**

Consciousness.

Which is feminine.

### Ekā eva - एका एव

One alone.

Feminine nominative singular:

**एका - ekā**.

And the particle:

**एव - eva**.

Through sandhi:

**एकैव - ekaiva**.

Because:

**ā + e → ai**.

### Vastutaḥ - वस्तुतः

Really, in reality, actually.

The form is adverbial.

It qualifies the claim of unity.

And finally:

### Saṃvit - संवित्

Consciousness, awareness.

Now the philosophical statement is direct.

**The differentiation between manifestation and reabsorption doesn't introduce a division into ultimate consciousness.**

### The philosophical problem of two directions

Think about an ordinary experience.

You begin imagining a pot.

The image becomes clearer.

You sustain it for a moment.

Then the image fades.

You can distinguish the emergence and disappearance of the image.

But the two events aren't thereby evidence that the capacity for awareness has become two independently existing realities.

Now the analogy is limited.

The psychological imagination of an ordinary person is not identical in capacity with Bhairava's universal *svātantrya*.

But the structural distinction helps.

Different modes of appearance can be grounded in one conscious reality.

And that is what Abhinavagupta is claiming.

### Creation doesn't improve Śiva; withdrawal doesn't repair him

Imagine someone saying:

"At the beginning, the Absolute was incomplete."

"Then it created a universe."

"Then it became full."

"Then the universe dissolved."

"And it became incomplete again."

What an absolute administrative disaster.

The Supreme Lord now requires a cosmic construction project merely to possess his own nature.

But Abhinavagupta's view is the opposite.

The supreme consciousness is already full.

Its expressive power doesn't manufacture its completeness.

And withdrawal doesn't restore a completeness previously lost.

**Both are manifestations of a freedom whose underlying reality remains undivided.**

Now the next sentence identifies exactly what makes this possible.

**Aham is the resting place of awareness in itself.**

And that is where Pratyabhijñā becomes extraordinarily precise.


## 3. स्वात्मविश्रान्ति - Awareness rests in itself

Abhinavagupta now says that even in the manifestation of a pot, pleasure and other objects, the fundamental *Aham*-condition is:

**स्वात्मविश्रान्तिसर्वस्वभूतः**

*Svātmaviśrāntisarvasvabhūtaḥ.*

Having repose in its own Self as its very essence or totality.

Let's understand the Sanskrit.

### Sva - स्व

Own.

### Ātman - आत्मन्

Self, nature.

Together:

**स्वात्मन् - svātman**

One's own Self.

### Viśrānti - विश्रान्ति

Repose, rest, settling into.

From *vi-√śram*, to rest.

Now the compound:

**स्वात्मविश्रान्ति - svātmaviśrānti**

Repose in one's own Self.

### Sarvasva - सर्वस्व

One's entire possession, totality, very essence.

From:

**सर्व - sarva**

All.

**स्व - sva**

Own.

Thus:

**सर्वस्व - sarvasva**

Everything belonging to oneself; one's very all.

### Bhūta - भूत

Become, being, having the nature of.

From √**भू - bhū**, to be or become.

So:

**सर्वस्वभूत - sarvasvabhūta**

Having become one's entire essence.

Now combine it.

**Svātmaviśrānti-sarvasvabhūta.**

Having repose in its own Self as its very substance.

And what is being described?

**अहंभाव - ahaṃbhāva**

I-consciousness.

### Ahaṃbhāva - अहंभाव

**अहम् - aham**

I.

**भाव - bhāva**

Being, condition, state.

Thus:

**अहंभाव - ahaṃbhāva**

The state of I-consciousness.

Now here's the crucial distinction.

This is not simply **अहंकार - ahaṃkāra**, the ordinary finite ego-function in the tattva scheme.

The terms can be related in wider Sanskrit usage, but their functions here aren't identical.

The universal *ahaṃbhāva* concerns the self-revealing nature of consciousness.

The limited *ahaṃkāra* belongs to differentiated individual manifestation.

And Abhinavagupta is describing the former.

### Why a pot is relevant again

Imagine you see a pot.

You have a cognition:

"This is a pot."

The pot appears as an object.

Now you recognize:

"I am seeing the pot."

The act of seeing is related to a subject.

But according to Abhinavagupta, the deepest nature of subjectivity isn't exhausted by the limited personality saying, "I own this body and I am looking at that object."

The very possibility of conscious manifestation is grounded in a self-revealing awareness.

And this awareness doesn't need an additional independent object in order to know that it exists.

**Its nature includes its own self-apprehension.**

That is the meaning of *svātmaviśrānti*.

And now Abhinavagupta quotes the most important line of the entire section.

It comes from Utpaladeva.

And the original source can be independently verified.


## 4. प्रकाशस्यात्मविश्रान्तिः - Utpaladeva's magnificent definition of I-consciousness

QUOTED VERSE · UTPALADEVA, AJAḌAPRAMĀTṚSIDDHI 22cd

**प्रकाशस्यात्मविश्रान्तिरहंभावो हि कीर्तितः।**

*Prakāśasyātmaviśrāntir ahaṃbhāvo hi kīrtitaḥ.*

"The repose of luminosity in itself is called I-consciousness."

This is one of the greatest definitions in the Pratyabhijñā tradition.

And its attribution is secure: it is the second half of verse 22 of Utpaladeva's *Ajaḍapramātṛsiddhi*. That earlier work also gives the contextual claim that the establishment of objects cannot be adequately explained through an obstruction of I-consciousness. ; \]

Now, what makes this formulation so powerful?

Because it does not define the Self through an object.

It doesn't say:

"I am the body."

Or:

"I am my personal history."

Or:

"I am the particular thought I am having."

It defines *Aham* through **the self-repose of conscious manifestation**.

Let's dismantle the Sanskrit.

### Prakāśasya - प्रकाशस्य

From:

**प्रकाश - prakāśa**

Light, manifestation, illumination, the power of appearing.

Related to:

**प्र - pra**

Forth.

**√काश् - kāś**

To shine, appear or become visible.

The noun:

**प्रकाश - prakāśa**

Illumination.

And the genitive singular:

**प्रकाशस्य - prakāśasya**

Of illumination.

Now here's the important philosophical difference.

In ordinary language, *prakāśa* can refer to physical light.

In Pratyabhijñā philosophy, it becomes a technical term for the self-revealing luminosity of consciousness.

A physical lamp illuminates a room.

But consciousness is the reality through which the lamp, its light and the illuminated room become apprehensible.

So don't reduce *prakāśa* to photons.

The metaphor is epistemological and metaphysical.

### Ātmaviśrāntiḥ - आत्मविश्रान्तिः

Now the wonderful compound.

**आत्मन् - ātman**

Self, own nature.

**विश्रान्ति - viśrānti**

Repose, rest, settling into.

Together:

**आत्मविश्रान्ति - ātmaviśrānti**

Repose in one's own nature.

Feminine nominative singular:

**आत्मविश्रान्तिः - ātmaviśrāntiḥ**.

Notice the final visarga.

And notice that the compound doesn't necessarily mean a chronological event in which awareness first lacks itself and subsequently discovers somewhere to rest.

Utpaladeva is identifying the nature of I-consciousness.

The repose belongs intrinsically to consciousness.

### Ahaṃbhāvaḥ - अहंभावः

The condition of I-consciousness.

**अहम् - aham**

I.

**भाव - bhāva**

Being, condition, nature.

So:

**अहंभाव - ahaṃbhāva**

The I-condition.

Masculine nominative singular:

**अहंभावः - ahaṃbhāvaḥ**.

### Hi - हि

Indeed, certainly, for.

An emphatic particle.

### Kīrtitaḥ - कीर्तितः

Declared, described, celebrated, named.

From √**कीर्त् - kīrt**, to proclaim or celebrate.

The past participle:

**कीर्तित - kīrtita**.

Masculine nominative singular:

**कीर्तितः - kīrtitaḥ**.

It agrees with *ahaṃbhāvaḥ*.

Thus the grammatical statement is clear:

**The repose of Prakāśa in its own nature is called Ahaṃbhāva.**

### Why this isn't the ordinary ego

Now imagine the ordinary finite ego.

It says:

"I am tall."

"I am hungry."

"I am British."

"I am studying Sanskrit."

"I am annoyed."

All perfectly intelligible statements.

But notice that each identifies the I with some particular characteristic or condition.

A body.

A sensation.

A nationality.

An activity.

A feeling.

Now the question is whether the self-revealing nature of awareness is exhausted by any one of these.

Utpaladeva's answer is no.

The intrinsic *Aham* is more fundamental than the particular predicates attached to it.

It's not merely the collection of things somebody can truthfully say about themselves.

### The most ridiculous ego-inflation imaginable

Imagine reading this verse and concluding:

"Excellent. I am Bhairava. Therefore my current personality is omniscient."

Your personality?

The thing that forgot where it put its glasses?

The thing that gets annoyed when the Wi-Fi drops?

The thing that can barely remember whether **घटः - ghaṭaḥ** is nominative or accusative?

That one?

No.

The distinction is not between a mediocre human and an enormously impressive human.

It's between **limited self-identification** and **the self-luminous ground of experience**.

The universal I-consciousness isn't merely the finite ego scaled up to cosmic proportions.

It is the reality through which limited identities become manifest.

### Prakāśa and Vimarśa

Now here's the philosophical heart.

*Prakāśa* expresses the luminous nature of consciousness.

*Vimarśa* expresses its intrinsic self-apprehension.

If awareness were merely a passive light incapable of any reflexive apprehension, it would resemble an inert instrument.

A lamp can illuminate a pot.

But it doesn't thereby recognize:

"I illuminate the pot."

The self-revealing consciousness of Pratyabhijñā is not like that.

Its luminous nature includes an intrinsic capacity for self-apprehension.

That's why the repose of Prakāśa in itself is called *Ahaṃbhāva*.

**The light doesn't need to borrow another independent light in order to appear to itself.**

Now Utpaladeva continues immediately in his own text to connect that repose with freedom, agency and lordship.

Abhinavagupta quotes only the specific half-verse we just read.

We should therefore distinguish the **quoted sentence** from the broader interpretation supplied by its original philosophical context.

And now Abhinavagupta states the conclusion in his own prose.

This *Aham* is **sarvātmaka**, all-encompassing.

And its nature is Bhairava, permeated by the supreme Goddess.


## 5. सर्वात्मकः - The I that contains the whole world

Abhinavagupta says:

**स च वस्तुतः सर्वात्मकः।**

*Sa ca vastutaḥ sarvātmakaḥ.*

"And that is, in reality, of the nature of everything."

Now let's unpack it.

### Saḥ - सः

He, that.

Here referring back to *Ahaṃbhāva*.

### Vastutaḥ - वस्तुतः

Really, in truth, essentially.

### Sarvātmaka - सर्वात्मक

From:

**सर्व - sarva**

All.

**आत्मक - ātmaka**

Having the nature of.

Thus:

**सर्वात्मक - sarvātmaka**

Having everything as one's nature.

Masculine nominative singular:

**सर्वात्मकः - sarvātmakaḥ**.

Now, this isn't the claim that a particular body physically contains every mountain, ocean and planet inside its skin.

That would be an absurd interpretation.

The universal *Aham* encompasses manifestation as its conscious ground.

And here's Abhinavagupta's next description:

**पराभट्टारिकानुविद्धः - parābhaṭṭārikānuviddhaḥ**

Permeated by the venerable supreme Goddess.

And:

**भैरवात्मकः - bhairavātmakaḥ**

Having the nature of Bhairava.

Let's learn both.

### Parābhaṭṭārikā - पराभट्टारिका

**परा - parā**

Supreme.

**भट्टारिका - bhaṭṭārikā**

Venerable Lady, exalted Goddess.

Thus:

**पराभट्टारिका - parābhaṭṭārikā**

The supreme venerable Goddess.

### Anuviddha - अनुविद्ध

Penetrated, permeated, interwoven.

Related to *anu-√vyadh*, to penetrate or pierce.

The participle:

**अनुविद्ध - anuviddha**

Permeated.

Masculine nominative:

**अनुविद्धः - anuviddhaḥ**.

Now this is a splendid word.

It doesn't merely suggest two independent objects sitting next to each other.

It expresses pervasive interpenetration.

And here it describes the relation of I-consciousness with Parā Śakti.

### Bhairavātmaka - भैरवात्मक

**भैरव - bhairava**

Bhairava.

**आत्मक - ātmaka**

Having the nature of.

Thus:

**भैरवात्मक - bhairavātmaka**

Bhairava-natured.

Now the philosophical statement becomes clear.

Universal I-consciousness is not a finite ego separate from the supreme Goddess.

It is Bhairava's conscious reality inseparable from Parā Śakti.

And that is precisely why the manifestation and withdrawal of the universe can be described through *Aham*.

The divine "I" isn't one object among its manifestations.

It is their conscious ground.

### A beautiful comparison with ordinary knowing

Imagine an ordinary perception.

"This is blue."

The blue appears as an object.

Now:

"I know blue."

The cognition is related to a subject.

But at the universal level, Abhinavagupta does not posit a subject that is externally dependent upon an independently ultimate blue substance.

The known appearance belongs to the self-revealing freedom of consciousness.

And the entire relation can be understood through universal *Ahaṃbhāva*.

Now here's the crucial point.

The term *sarvātmaka* doesn't mean every distinct appearance is functionally identical.

Blue isn't the same experienced colour as red.

A pot isn't an elephant.

Happiness isn't pain.

But their manifest differences do not establish ultimate independence from the conscious reality through which they are known.

That's the relationship.

And now Abhinavagupta quotes himself.

A hymn.

An extraordinary verse about the Goddess flowering through the entire universe as the uninterrupted wonder of supreme reality.

This is one of the most beautiful passages we've encountered in the whole reading.


## 6. विश्वत्र भावपटले - Abhinavagupta's own hymn to the Goddess

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN STOTRA · QUOTED IN HIS COMMENTARY, P. 198

**विश्वत्र भावपटले परिजृम्भमाण-**

**विच्छेदशून्यपरमार्थचमत्कृतिर्या।**

**तां पूर्णवृत्त्यहमिति प्रथनस्वभावां**

**स्वात्मस्थितिं स्वरसतः प्रणमामि देवीम्॥**

*Viśvatra bhāvapaṭale parijṛmbhamāṇa-*

*vicchedaśūnyaparamārthacamatkṛtir yā.*

*Tāṃ pūrṇavṛtty aham iti prathanasvabhāvāṃ*

*svātmasthitiṃ svarasataḥ praṇamāmi devīm.*

A close poetic rendering:

*I bow, from the spontaneous relish of my own being, to that Goddess: the self-abiding reality whose nature is to shine forth as the complete “I,” whose supreme wonder, free of interruption, unfolds throughout the whole expanse of existence.*

Now that is magnificent.

And importantly, Abhinavagupta himself introduces it with:

**यथोक्तं मयैव स्तोत्रे।**

*Yathoktaṃ mayaiva stotre.*

"As I myself have said in a hymn."

So we are not dealing with a root verse of the *Parātrīśikā*.

Nor with a later editor's explanatory poem.

This is explicitly presented as **Abhinavagupta quoting his own devotional composition**. ; \]

Let's learn its language.

### Viśvatra - विश्वत्र

Everywhere, throughout the universe.

The element:

**विश्व - viśva**

All, universal, the whole world.

And the adverbial ending **-tra** gives the sense of location or extension.

Thus:

**विश्वत्र - viśvatra**

Everywhere.

### Bhāvapaṭala - भावपटल

**भाव - bhāva**

Being, entity, manifested state.

**पटल - paṭala**

Layer, expanse, mass, collection.

Thus:

**भावपटल - bhāvapaṭala**

The expanse or multitude of manifested existents.

Locative singular:

**भावपटले - bhāvapaṭale**

Within the expanse of beings.

Notice **-e**.

This is the locative singular of a masculine or neuter *-a* stem.

### Parijṛmbhamāṇa - परिजृम्भमाण

Now what a beautiful word.

Related to:

**परि - pari**

All around, fully.

**√जृम्भ् - jṛmbh**

To expand, open, unfold, gape.

The participle:

**परिजृम्भमाण - parijṛmbhamāṇa**

Expanding, unfolding, blossoming forth.

Now here's something rather wonderful.

The root can have the ordinary sense of opening or yawning.

And Abhinavagupta uses it to describe the universe-wide unfolding of divine conscious power.

The Goddess is effectively unfolding through existence.

The whole universe is her blossoming.

Not a bad upgrade for a root that can describe someone yawning.

### Viccheda - विच्छेद

Division, interruption, severance.

From:

**वि - vi**

Apart.

**√छिद् - chid**

To cut.

The noun:

**विच्छेद - viccheda**

Cutting apart, interruption.

Now:

### Śūnya - शून्य

Empty, devoid, absent.

Thus:

**विच्छेदशून्य - vicchedaśūnya**

Devoid of interruption or division.

Now here's the essential point.

The supreme conscious reality isn't fundamentally cut into independent pieces by the multiplicity of manifestations.

The world is varied.

But the underlying self-revealing awareness isn't interrupted by every new object.

### Paramārtha - परमार्थ

**परम - parama**

Supreme.

**अर्थ - artha**

Meaning, reality, purpose, thing.

Through sandhi:

*parama + artha → paramārtha.*

Thus:

**परमार्थ - paramārtha**

Ultimate reality.

### Camatkṛti - चमत्कृति

Wonder, astonishment, ecstatic self-revelation.

Related to:

**चमत्कार - camatkāra**

Wonder, marvellous relish.

The precise derivational history of these expressive words is more complex than merely attaching an ordinary abstract suffix to *camatkāra*, but their semantic relationship is clear.

Now the compound:

**परमार्थचमत्कृति - paramārthacamatkṛti**

The wonder of ultimate reality.

And the feminine nominative:

**चमत्कृतिः - camatkṛtiḥ**.

So the first half describes the Goddess through the marvellous self-expression of supreme reality across the entire manifested expanse.

### Pūrṇavṛtti - पूर्णवृत्ति

**पूर्ण - pūrṇa**

Full.

**वृत्ति - vṛtti**

Operation, mode of being, state, activity.

Thus:

**पूर्णवृत्ति - pūrṇavṛtti**

Complete mode of being.

In the phrase:

**पूर्णवृत्त्यहमिति - pūrṇavṛtty aham iti**

The interpretation is that the Goddess's nature becomes manifest as the complete **Aham**.

### Prathana - प्रथन

Manifestation, expansion, making evident.

From √**प्रथ् - prath**, to spread or become manifest.

We've encountered this root repeatedly.

Remember:

**प्रथते - prathate**

Appears.

**प्रथमान - prathamāna**

Appearing.

Now:

**प्रथन - prathana**

Manifestation.

And:

### Svabhāva - स्वभाव

**स्व - sva**

Own.

**भाव - bhāva**

Nature, being.

Thus:

**स्वभाव - svabhāva**

Intrinsic nature.

The accusative feminine compound:

**प्रथनस्वभावाम् - prathanasvabhāvām**

Whose nature is manifestation.

### Svātmasthiti - स्वात्मस्थिति

**स्वात्मन् - svātman**

One's own Self.

**स्थिति - sthiti**

Abiding, establishment.

Thus:

**स्वात्मस्थिति - svātmasthiti**

Abiding in one's own nature.

Accusative singular:

**स्वात्मस्थितिम् - svātmasthitim**.

### Svarasataḥ - स्वरसतः

Now this is especially beautiful.

**स्व - sva**

Own.

**रस - rasa**

Essence, flavour, relish, aesthetic experience, delight.

The adverbial expression:

**स्वरसतः - svarasataḥ**

Spontaneously, through one's own nature or intrinsic relish.

In this devotional context, it conveys an especially intimate quality.

The worship is not simply a mechanical recitation imposed from outside.

It arises through the intrinsic delight of recognizing the Goddess.

### Praṇamāmi - प्रणमामि

I bow, I pay reverence.

From:

**प्र - pra**

Forth, intensely.

**√नम् - nam**

To bend, bow.

First-person singular present:

**प्रणमामि - praṇamāmi**.

And:

### Devīm - देवीम्

The Goddess.

Accusative singular of:

**देवी - devī**.

Notice the long final vowel before the accusative ending.

So:

**प्रणमामि देवीम् - praṇamāmi devīm**

I bow to the Goddess.

### Why this hymn matters philosophically

Now the entire meaning becomes clear.

The supreme Goddess isn't simply one object among others.

She is the uninterrupted conscious reality manifesting throughout the totality of existence.

And her nature is described as **the complete Aham**.

That's why Abhinavagupta quotes this verse immediately after defining *Ahaṃbhāva*.

The hymn is not an unrelated devotional ornament.

It expresses the same philosophical claim in poetic language.

The Sanskrit prose tells us:

Universal I-consciousness is the self-repose of Prakāśa.

The hymn tells us:

That very I-consciousness is the Goddess's uninterrupted wonder unfolding throughout existence.

**Philosophy and devotion converge on the same reality.**

And now the commentator introduces Vāmana.

Not the mythological dwarf incarnation of Viṣṇu.

A historical Śaiva author whose work concerns nondual attainment.

The entire discussion is about to return from divine poetry to the rather inconvenient mechanics of Sanskrit phonemes.


## 7. Vāmana's *Advayasampattivārttika* - A beautiful little textual trap

Abhinavagupta says:

**एष एव श्रीवामनविरचिते अद्वयसम्पत्तिवार्तिके उपदेशनयो बोद्धव्यः।**

*Eṣa eva śrīvāmanaviracite advayasampattivārtike upadeśanayo boddhavyaḥ.*

This very method of instruction should be understood in the *Advayasampattivārttika* composed by the venerable Vāmana.

Now here's an important point.

**The Kashmir Series reads *Advayasampatti*.**

The GRETIL transcription at this location gives:

**dvayasaṃpattivārtike**

Without the initial *a*.

And that isn't merely a modern transliteration error we can ignore.

Navjivan Rastogi discusses this precise textual issue in his research on Kashmir's Krama tradition, explaining that the missing initial *a* may reflect scribal omission. ; \]

So the work's title is transmitted in two forms:

**अद्वयसम्पत्तिवार्तिक - Advayasampattivārttika**

Commentary on the attainment of nonduality.

And:

**द्वयसम्पत्तिवार्तिक - Dvayasampattivārttika**

The alternative transmitted form lacking the negative prefix.

The first is contextually and historically supported.

But that doesn't entitle us to erase the second from the evidence.

### Advaya - अद्वय

Nondual.

**अ - a-**

Negative prefix.

**द्वय - dvaya**

Pair, duality.

Thus:

**अद्वय - advaya**

Nondual.

### Sampatti - सम्पत्ति

Attainment, accomplishment, coming into possession.

Related to *sam-√pad*, to arrive at or attain.

Thus:

**अद्वयसम्पत्ति - advayasampatti**

Attainment of nonduality.

### Vārttika - वार्त्तिक / वार्तिक

A supplementary explanatory or critical treatise, often discussing what is said, left unsaid or inadequately explained in an earlier text.

Now the term doesn't necessarily mean a casual note.

It can designate a substantial philosophical composition.

### Upadeśanaya - उपदेशनय

**उपदेश - upadeśa**

Instruction, teaching.

**नय - naya**

Method, principle, interpretive approach.

Thus:

**उपदेशनय - upadeśanaya**

Method of instruction.

### Boddhavyaḥ - बोद्धव्यः

To be understood.

From √**बुध् - budh**, to know or awaken.

The gerundive:

**बोद्धव्य - boddhavya**

To be understood.

Masculine nominative singular:

**बोद्धव्यः - boddhavyaḥ**.

Now here's the crucial distinction.

Abhinavagupta is **referring to Vāmana's teaching**, not reproducing a complete quotation from the Vārttika.

The surviving passage supplies the title and identifies the doctrinal agreement.

It doesn't authorize us to invent a stanza by Vāmana explaining *Aham*.

The philosophical lineage matters, but the quoted material must remain distinguished from the commentator's own exposition.

And now Abhinavagupta makes a statement about the first Sanskrit vowel.

The humble **अ - a**.

Because apparently eighteen installments of the alphabet have only just prepared us to appreciate the first letter.


## 8. अकार एव सर्वाढ्यः - A is already full of everything

Now Abhinavagupta says, according to the Kashmir Series reading:

**अकार एव सर्वाढ्यः।**

*Akāra eva sarvāḍhyaḥ.*

The vowel *a* alone is rich in or endowed with everything.

But the GRETIL transcription reads:

**akāra eva sarvādyaḥ**

The vowel *a* is the beginning of everything.

Now that's a genuine difference.

**सर्वाढ्य - sarvāḍhya**

Rich in everything.

**सर्वाद्य - sarvādya**

First or original with respect to everything.

The two expressions are philosophically compatible in the present context.

But they're not grammatically identical.

And it's precisely the sort of difference that shouldn't be silently harmonized. ; ; \]

Let's understand both.

### Akāra - अकार

The vowel *a*.

**अ - a**

And:

**कार - kāra**

A naming element used for phonemes.

Thus:

**अकार - akāra**

The sound *a*.

### Sarva - सर्व

All, everything.

### Āḍhya - आढ्य

Rich, endowed, abundant.

Thus:

**सर्वाढ्य - sarvāḍhya**

Rich in all, containing the fullness of everything.

### Ādya - आद्य

First, original, primordial.

From:

**आदि - ādi**

Beginning.

Thus:

**सर्वाद्य - sarvādya**

First of everything.

Now the philosophical point.

The initial *a* symbolizes Anuttara.

And in the Tantric exposition, Anuttara is not merely the first item in a chronological queue.

It is the underlying reality from which the phonemic powers become manifest.

So the first sound has both symbolic priority and universal scope.

### The examples: Harṣa, Ghaṭa and Nīla

Abhinavagupta immediately says that this is true even where words begin with other consonants.

He names:

**हर्ष - harṣa**

Joy, delight.

**घट - ghaṭa**

Pot.

**नील - nīla**

Blue.

Now here's the difficulty.

The words don't all begin with the written letter **अ - a**.

*Harṣa* begins with **ह - ha**.

*Ghaṭa* begins with **घ - gha**.

*Nīla* begins with **न - na**.

So how can *a* be fundamental even there?

The answer involves distinguishing the phonemic manifestation from its underlying power.

### The ordinary Sanskrit literacy lesson

Now, this connects beautifully with something very important about Devanāgarī.

A Sanskrit consonant letter generally includes an inherent **a** when written independently.

For example:

**क - ka**

**ग - ga**

**घ - gha**

**न - na**

To remove the inherent vowel, we use the virāma:

**क् - k**

**ग् - g**

**घ् - gh**

**न् - n**

And to attach another vowel, we use the appropriate vowel sign.

For example:

**कि - ki**

**कु - ku**

**के - ke**

Now this is an ordinary feature of the writing system.

It helps explain why Sanskrit consonant names are traditionally expressed as syllables containing *a*.

But don't confuse that orthographic convention with the totality of Abhinavagupta's metaphysics.

The doctrine is not simply:

"Devanāgarī has inherent a, therefore Śiva is the universe."

The writing system supplies a useful illustration.

The metaphysical argument concerns the inseparability of differentiated phonemic powers from their ultimate source.

### A consonant doesn't become independent of its conditions

Imagine pronouncing:

**हर्ष - harṣa**.

You can distinguish its consonants.

Its vowels.

Its phonetic transitions.

But the resulting word isn't created by a pile of wholly unrelated sounds with no integrated structure.

Its articulation belongs to a coordinated event of speech.

And Abhinavagupta extends that relation into his theory of divine phonemic manifestation.

The *ha*-sound and subsequent letters imply the powers relevant to their manifestation.

Their ultimate conscious ground isn't absent merely because the written word begins with a consonant other than *a*.

That's the philosophical point.

And now he makes the next, more difficult claim.

The powers are present **simultaneously** in cognition.

They do not need to wait for one another as though they were independent workers arriving at a construction site.


## 9. पूर्वापरवर्णसमाक्षेप - Every phoneme draws the others into its own manifestation

Now we reach Kashmir Series p. 199.

And one of the densest compounds in this entire reading.

**पूर्वापरवर्णसमाक्षेप**

*Pūrvāparavarṇasamākṣepa.*

The drawing together or inclusion of preceding and subsequent phonemes.

Let's dismantle it.

### Pūrva - पूर्व

Previous, earlier.

### Apara - अपर

Later, subsequent.

Together:

**पूर्वापर - pūrvāpara**

Earlier and later.

### Varṇa - वर्ण

Phoneme, letter, sound-category.

### Samākṣepa - समाक्षेप

Drawing together, gathering, inclusion, pulling into relation.

From:

**सम् - sam**

Together.

**आ - ā**

Toward.

**√क्षिप् - kṣip**

To throw, cast or project.

Thus:

**समाक्षेप - samākṣepa**

Gathering into one configuration.

Now the complete compound describes the inclusion of the relevant phonemic powers within a given manifestation.

### The crucial philosophical distinction

Suppose a word is spoken.

Ordinarily, its phonetic events unfold through time.

One sound follows another.

And different articulatory gestures contribute to the utterance.

That is a genuine feature of spoken language.

But Abhinavagupta is not simply analysing the chronological sequence of acoustic events.

He's examining the underlying unity of the conscious power that makes their coordinated manifestation possible.

And at that level, the relevant powers are not independent substances that only begin existing when their sound is physically produced.

### Antarnilīna - अन्तर्निलीन

Now the next important word.

**अन्तर् - antar**

Within.

**निलीन - nilīna**

Absorbed, hidden, merged.

From *ni-√lī*, to become absorbed.

Thus:

**अन्तर्निलीन - antarnilīna**

Hidden or absorbed within.

The passage says that the powers, if treated as merely added later in an external sequence, would remain concealed and not yet enter the domain of determinate cognition.

### Vikalpagocaratva - विकल्पगोचरत्व

Now here's the wonderful compound.

**विकल्प - vikalpa**

Conceptual distinction.

**गोचर - gocara**

Range, field, domain of apprehension.

**-त्व - -tva**

Abstract condition.

Thus:

**विकल्पगोचरत्व - vikalpagocaratva**

The condition of becoming an object or content of conceptual cognition.

And:

**अप्राप्त - aprāpta**

Not attained, not reached.

The argument is that a merely external chronological explanation doesn't capture the integrated conscious manifestation in which the differentiated phonemes become intelligible.

### The musical analogy

Imagine a pianist playing a phrase.

The notes occur in time.

One after another.

But the musical phrase isn't merely an accidental heap of independent noises.

Its intelligibility involves their relationship.

A listener can apprehend the phrase as one structured event.

Now the ordinary psychological details of that apprehension involve memory, attention and auditory processing.

Abhinavagupta's claim is more comprehensive.

The conscious power underlying the structured manifestation isn't itself merely one further sound occurring at the end of the sequence.

**The whole relation belongs to consciousness.**

And that's why the phonemic powers can be described as mutually implicated.

### The alphabetical office queue

Imagine every Sanskrit letter waiting outside the office of consciousness.

*Ka* arrives.

"Is *a* here?"

"No, he's at the beginning of the alphabet."

"Can I manifest without him?"

"No."

"Well, where's *ha*?"

"Near the end."

"And *ṃ*?"

"Different department."

Apparently the universe cannot begin until everybody has clocked in.

Abhinavagupta's point is that this is a ridiculous model of supreme freedom.

The powers aren't independently existing workers who need to be assembled from outside.

They're distinguishable expressions of one conscious reality.

And now he makes the statement explicit.

**All the Goddesses are present in every cognition.**


## 10. सर्वा एव देवताः - Every cognition contains the powers of all the Goddesses

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S PROSE · P. 199

**अत एव सर्वत्र विज्ञाने सर्वा एव देवताः सममेव समुदायं दधत्यः।**

*Ata eva sarvatra vijñāne sarvā eva devatāḥ samam eva samudāyaṃ dadhatyaḥ.*

"Therefore, in every cognition, all the divine powers arise together."

Now that's a remarkable statement.

And it follows directly from the preceding argument.

If the phonemic powers are intrinsically implicated in the fullness of consciousness, then no particular cognition needs to be manufactured by a set of completely independent divine entities.

The powers can be differentiated without being ultimately separate.

### Ata eva - अत एव

Therefore, precisely for this reason.

### Sarvatra - सर्वत्र

Everywhere, in every case.

### Vijñāne - विज्ञाने

In cognition.

From:

**विज्ञान - vijñāna**

Differentiated knowing, cognition.

Locative singular:

**विज्ञाने - vijñāne**.

### Devatāḥ - देवताः

Goddesses, divine powers.

The feminine nominative plural.

Now don't simply imagine this means all the figures from every religious mythology are independently sitting inside every thought.

The context concerns the divine phonemic powers.

### Samam - समम्

Together, equally, simultaneously.

### Samudāya - समुदाय

Arising, gathering, collection.

The accusative singular:

**समुदायम् - samudāyam**.

### Dadhatyaḥ - दधत्यः

Bearing, holding, possessing.

From √**धा - dhā**, to place or hold, through the present participial formation.

Feminine nominative plural:

**दधत्यः - dadhatyaḥ**.

It agrees with:

**देवताः - devatāḥ**.

Now the grammar supports the philosophical statement.

All the powers, feminine plural, bear their collective manifestation together.

And Abhinavagupta continues:

**चित्रां संवित्तिवृत्तिं वर्तयन्ति।**

*Citrāṃ saṃvittivṛttiṃ vartayanti.*

They manifest the variegated activity of consciousness.

### Citrā - चित्रा

Variegated, diverse, wonderful.

Feminine accusative singular:

**चित्राम् - citrām**.

### Saṃvittivṛtti - संवित्तिवृत्ति

**संवित्ति - saṃvitti**

Consciousness, cognition.

**वृत्ति - vṛtti**

Operation, mode of activity.

Thus:

**संवित्तिवृत्ति - saṃvittivṛtti**

A mode or movement of conscious apprehension.

### Vartayanti - वर्तयन्ति

They cause to operate, manifest or bring about.

Related to √**वृत् - vṛt**, to turn or occur, through its causative formation.

Third-person plural.

Now the philosophical point is extremely precise.

**The powers are simultaneous in their ultimate conscious ground, while their manifested activities can be distinguished through time.**

That distinction is absolutely essential.

Otherwise we end up with a contradiction.

We observe that sounds unfold sequentially.

But Abhinavagupta says the powers exist together.

The contradiction disappears when we distinguish the level of the phonetic event from the nondual ground of its manifestation.

### The three Goddesses and the ordinary pot

Remember the earlier discussions of:

Parā.

Parāparā.

Aparā.

And the differentiated modes of speech and cognition.

The divine powers can be considered through their distinct functions.

But the supreme conscious reality is not simply partitioned into separate compartments.

When a pot is apprehended, the different aspects of the knowing event are manifest.

Yet their ground remains one.

The same principle applies to every cognition.

And now Abhinavagupta brings time into the argument.

Because if the powers are fundamentally simultaneous, why does the universe appear to unfold through cycles?

Why do we have years?

Days?

Breaths?

Births and deaths?

Gods appearing and dissolving?

And how can vast spans of cosmic time be considered within a single breath?

This is where the commentary becomes extremely strange.

And philosophically very interesting.


## 11. One breath, sixty years, a hundred and twenty years - Cosmic time enters the lungs

At Kashmir Series p. 199, Abhinavagupta refers to the *Kālādhikāra*, or section dealing with time, in scriptural literature such as the *Svacchanda* tradition.

He says that systems describe remarkable cycles of emergence and dissolution within:

**एकस्मिन्नेव प्राणे - ekasminn eva prāṇe**

A single breath or movement of vital energy.

And even:

**प्राणषोडशांशेऽपि - prāṇaṣoḍaśāṃśe 'pi**

In one-sixteenth of a breath.

Then he mentions:

**षष्टि - ṣaṣṭi**

Sixty.

And:

**तद्द्विगुण - taddviguṇa**

Twice that.

So sixty years, one hundred and twenty years, and related periods become connected to the emergence and dissolution of different classes of divine beings.

Now, this is an attested traditional cosmological claim, not a modern scientific account of time measurement. Abhinavagupta is invoking it to explain the relationship between cycles of manifested time and the more fundamental reality of consciousness. ; \]

Let's dismantle the Sanskrit.

### Prāṇa - प्राण

Breath, vital energy, life-function.

From:

**प्र - pra**

Forth.

A verbal family involving √**अन् - an**, to breathe.

The noun:

**प्राण - prāṇa**.

Now, *prāṇa* can mean ordinary breath in some contexts.

But in Śaiva subtle-body and cosmological discussions, it can designate a much broader vital process.

We shouldn't automatically equate every use with a precisely measured respiratory cycle.

### Ekasmin - एकस्मिन्

In one.

Locative singular of the numeral/pronominal adjective:

**एक - eka**.

And:

**प्राणे - prāṇe**

In a breath, locative singular.

Thus:

**एकस्मिन्नेव प्राणे - ekasminn eva prāṇe**

In a single breath.

### Ṣoḍaśa - षोडश

Sixteen.

Now recall:

**षोडशी कला - ṣoḍaśī kalā**

The sixteenth lunar power.

The numeral is returning.

### Aṃśa - अंश

Part, portion, fraction.

Thus:

**षोडशांश - ṣoḍaśāṃśa**

A sixteenth part.

And:

**प्राणषोडशांश - prāṇaṣoḍaśāṃśa**

One-sixteenth of a breath.

Locative singular:

**प्राणषोडशांशे - prāṇaṣoḍaśāṃśe**.

Now:

**अपि - api**

Even.

Thus:

**प्राणषोडशांशेऽपि - prāṇaṣoḍaśāṃśe 'pi**.

Even in a sixteenth part of a breath.

### Ṣaṣṭi - षष्टि

Sixty.

Now the Devanāgarī is worth learning.

**ष - ṣa**

**ष्टि - ṣṭi**

The retroflex cluster.

Thus:

**षष्टि - ṣaṣṭi**.

### Dviguṇa - द्विगुण

Double, twofold.

**द्वि - dvi**

Two.

**गुण - guṇa**

Multiplier, factor, constituent.

Thus:

**द्विगुण - dviguṇa**

Twice.

And:

**तद्द्विगुण - taddviguṇa**

Twice that.

### Abda - अब्द

Year.

So:

**षष्ट्यब्द - ṣaṣṭyabda**

Sixty years.

And the subsequent compound allows doubled and other enumerated periods.

Now the important thing is that Abhinavagupta doesn't present a modern table of exact astronomical durations.

He invokes traditional cycles to illustrate how the manifestation of time can be considered on different scales.

### The classes of beings

The Sanskrit mentions:

**मातृ - mātṛ**

Mothers or Mātṛkā powers.

**रुद्र - rudra**

Rudras.

**लोकपाल - lokapāla**

Guardians of the world or directions.

**ग्रह - graha**

Planets or seizing celestial powers in traditional cosmological classifications.

**नाग - nāga**

Nāgas.

And other classes.

The text is connecting their various cycles of emergence and dissolution with time-periods used in Tantric cosmology.

Now, some explanatory translations supply specific traditional counts for these groups.

But the main sentence doesn't itself enumerate every class's membership in an independent list.

So we should not manufacture a complete calendrical table from the short passage.

The secure point is the relation between **multiple cosmic cycles and one underlying breath-time framework**.

### The universe on a stopwatch

Imagine a cosmic accountant approaching Bhairava.

"Lord, I've calculated sixty years for this class."

"Very good."

"One hundred and twenty for another."

"Excellent."

"And now the scripture says these cycles can be contemplated within a breath."

The accountant looks at his spreadsheet.

Then at his lungs.

Then at the spreadsheet again.

His spreadsheet has become metaphysically inadequate.

But Abhinavagupta's point isn't that the physical Sun has secretly completed sixty years while you've breathed in for three seconds.

That would be an empirical claim unsupported by the passage.

The point is that **different orders of temporal manifestation can be related through a single conscious ground that is not itself constituted by those temporal divisions**.

And that is the philosophical issue.


## 12. कालशक्ति - Time is a power of differentiation, not the master of Bhairava

Now we've reached one of the most difficult transitions.

Abhinavagupta has described several temporal scales.

And now he argues that their multiplicity doesn't establish time as an independent ultimate reality standing outside consciousness.

The key expression is:

**अकालकलितत्वमेव तत्त्वम्।**

*Akālakalitatvam eva tattvam.*

The ultimate principle is not intrinsically constituted or limited by temporal differentiation.

Now let's be very careful.

This doesn't mean that the ordinary world contains no temporal processes.

Nor that a human being can simply ignore biological duration.

The doctrine concerns the relation between **manifested time** and **supreme consciousness**.

### Kāla - काल

Time.

But remember, within the Śaiva tattva scheme:

**काल - kāla**

Time as a limiting principle.

And:

**कला - kalā**

A distinct principle involving limited agency.

The words look similar.

But their vowel lengths and philosophical functions differ.

**काल - kāla** has long ā.

**कला - kalā** has short first a and long final ā.

Now:

### Kalanā - कलना

Articulation, differentiation, measurement, delimitation.

Related to √**कल् - kal**, to count or calculate in the relevant lexical family.

Now the expression:

**अकालकलित - akālakalita**

Not constituted by temporal measurement or delimitation.

And:

**-त्व - -tva**

Abstract state.

Thus:

**अकालकलितत्व - akālakalitatva**

The condition of being free from intrinsic temporal determination.

Now the philosophical argument.

Suppose you're watching a film.

A scene lasts ten seconds.

Another lasts a minute.

The whole film lasts two hours.

The events have different durations.

But the act of apprehending their relationship isn't simply identical with any one event inside the film.

Now the analogy is limited.

Consciousness in ordinary embodied experience is certainly related to temporal processing.

But Abhinavagupta's supreme consciousness is not treated as one more finite event requiring an earlier event to bring it into existence.

The temporal series belongs to manifestation.

The ultimate conscious ground does not depend upon the temporal series for its own reality.

### The difference between akrama and frozen time

Here's an important distinction.

**अक्रम - akrama**

Nonsequential.

Doesn't mean that every event in the manifested universe must happen physically at once.

It means that the supreme ground isn't divided by the explanatory and experiential succession of its manifestations.

Similarly:

**अकाल - akāla**

Not bound by time, in this doctrinal context.

Doesn't mean that an ordinary person's watch will stop working after they understand Sanskrit.

The watch is still quite capable of telling you that you've spent the last six hours studying one paragraph.

And the next paragraph is still there.

### The thorny sentence about what cannot be conceptualized

At this point, the Sanskrit discusses the danger of treating what isn't grasped by Māyic determination as simply nonexistent.

The transmitted prose is unusually compressed, involving:

**मायीयाध्यवसाय - māyīyādhyavasāya**

Determinate conceptual judgment belonging to the domain of Māyā.

And:

**नास्तिताभिमान - nāstitābhimāna**

The conviction of nonexistence.

Now the philosophical issue is subtle.

If something cannot be exhaustively determined by an ordinary conceptual category, it doesn't automatically follow that it is absolutely nonexistent.

In the Śaiva system, supreme consciousness isn't merely one object that must satisfy the same criteria as an ordinary pot.

But the text also doesn't give us a licence to assert that every unimaginable entity exists.

The relevant point concerns the **limits of Māyic conceptual determination as an account of the supreme conscious ground**.

And Abhinavagupta immediately insists that the reality remains manifest in Parā Saṃvid.

### Parasaṃvidi - परसंविदि

In supreme consciousness.

**परा - parā**

Supreme.

**संविद् - saṃvid**

Consciousness.

Locative singular:

**संविदि - saṃvidi**.

Thus:

**परसंविदि - parasaṃvidi**.

Now the claim is that the temporal distinctions are not outside consciousness.

Their manifestation belongs to it.

And the conscious ground isn't itself limited to one particular time-scale.

### The important epistemic distinction

Abhinavagupta's argument is metaphysical.

It doesn't establish that every traditional calendrical assignment can be treated as an experimentally verified fact.

And it doesn't imply that historical time has no practical significance.

It means that time, like space and the other limiting structures, is interpreted through the manifestation of Śakti.

**The supreme reality is not one more thing trapped inside the temporal series it makes manifest.**

And now he returns to the example of multiple simultaneous activities.

Remember the Śrīkaṇṭha verse from Part XVIII?

Seeing one thing.

Thinking another.

Speaking a third.

Doing a fourth.

Now Abhinavagupta recalls that argument to explain the inseparability of the three divine powers.


## 13. पश्यत्यन्यद्विकल्पयत्यन्यत् - Seeing one thing, thinking another

At the end of p. 199, Abhinavagupta refers back to the teaching:

**पश्यत्यन्यद्विकल्पयत्यन्यत्।**

*Paśyaty anyad vikalpayaty anyat.*

One sees one thing and conceptualizes another.

Now this is closely related to, but not identical in wording with, the Śrīkaṇṭha verse we studied last time.

That verse began:

**पश्यत्यन्यच्छृणोत्यन्यत्...**

*Paśyaty anyac chṛṇoty anyat...*

One sees something and hears something else.

Here the prose emphasizes:

Seeing.

And conceptualization.

The distinction matters because the present argument concerns the plurality of cognitive functions within one awareness.

### Paśyati - पश्यति

Sees.

From √**पश् - paś**.

Third-person singular present.

### Anyat - अन्यत्

Something else.

Neuter nominative or accusative singular.

Now the sandhi:

**पश्यति + अन्यत् → पश्यत्यन्यत्**

*paśyati + anyat → paśyaty anyat.*

The final *i* becomes the glide *y* before the following vowel.

### Vikalpayati - विकल्पयति

Conceptualizes, distinguishes, constructs a determinate representation.

Related to:

**विकल्प - vikalpa**

Conceptual distinction.

Now:

**विकल्पयति - vikalpayati**

He or she conceptualizes.

Third-person singular present.

And:

**विकल्पयत्यन्यत् - vikalpayaty anyat**

Conceptualizes something else.

### The three divine powers

Abhinavagupta then refers to:

**देवतात्रयाधिष्ठानम्**

*Devatātrayādhiṣṭhānam.*

The establishment or support of the triad of divine powers.

Now:

**देवता - devatā**

Divine power, Goddess.

**त्रय - traya**

Triad.

**अधिष्ठान - adhiṣṭhāna**

Support, locus, establishment.

Thus:

**देवतात्रयाधिष्ठान - devatātrayādhiṣṭhāna**

The supporting presence of the triad of divine powers.

Now, given the context of the previous chapters, the most relevant structure concerns the threefold manifestation of consciousness and the powers associated with differentiated knowing and action.

We shouldn't infer that the short clause independently spells out every specific deity-name and ritual correspondence.

The argument is about their inseparability.

### The critical word anapāyin

**अनपायिन् - anapāyin**

Not departing, inseparable, never absent.

From:

**अन् - an-**

Negative prefix.

**अपाय - apāya**

Departure, loss, disappearance.

Thus:

**अनपायिन् - anapāyin**

Inseparable, not departing.

The text says the triadic support remains present everywhere.

### The philosophical meaning

Imagine looking at a pot while thinking about yesterday's conversation.

The visual content and conceptual content are different.

Yet the occurrence doesn't require two independent ultimate consciousnesses.

The differentiation belongs to one field of awareness.

Now, this isn't a claim that ordinary attention can perform every cognitive task perfectly at once.

It concerns the ontological relationship among differentiated cognitive manifestations.

And Abhinavagupta's conclusion is that the triadic powers are never ultimately absent.

### The connection to time

Now return to the cosmic cycles.

Sixty years.

One hundred and twenty years.

A movement of breath.

Different manifested temporal patterns.

They can be distinguished.

But the supreme conscious power isn't itself divided into separate ultimate realities by those patterns.

The same principle we've just applied to different cognitive contents now applies to different time-scales.

**Multiplicity of manifestation does not require multiplication of the ultimate ground.**

And at this point we cross into Kashmir Series p. 200.

The argument reaches its decisive conclusion.

Every cognition ultimately has the nature of Aham.

Then the entire world is emitted and swallowed by Visarga.

And the full phonemic universe returns through *ha*, *kṣa*, Nāda and the supreme ground.

This is the climax of Part XIX.


## 14. सर्वाण्येव संवेदनानि - Every cognition ultimately rests in *Aham*

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN PROSE · KASHMIR SERIES P. 200

**सर्वाण्येव च संवेदनानि वस्तुतोऽहमितिपरमार्थानि विमर्शमयान्येव।**

*Sarvāṇy eva ca saṃvedanāni vastuto 'ham iti paramārthāni vimarśamayāny eva.*

"All cognitions, in reality, have *Aham* as their ultimate nature and consist of reflexive awareness."

Now that is a genuinely extraordinary conclusion.

And it follows directly from everything we've studied.

The cognition of a pot.

The apprehension of happiness.

The recognition of knowledge.

The operation of linguistic convention.

The conceptualization of one object while seeing another.

All are different manifestations.

But the supreme conscious ground isn't multiplied into separate realities by those distinctions.

### Sarvāṇi - सर्वाणि

All.

Neuter nominative plural.

And:

### Saṃvedanāni - संवेदनानि

Cognitions, occurrences of conscious apprehension.

From:

**संवेदन - saṃvedana**

Awareness or cognition.

Neuter nominative plural:

**संवेदनानि - saṃvedanāni**.

Notice that both words agree:

**सर्वाणि संवेदनानि - sarvāṇi saṃvedanāni**.

All cognitions.

### Vastutaḥ - वस्तुतः

Really, essentially, in truth.

### Aham iti - अहमिति

The word *Aham*, I.

And:

### Paramārtha - परमार्थ

Supreme reality, ultimate nature.

The neuter nominative plural:

**परमार्थानि - paramārthāni**.

So:

**अहमितिपरमार्थानि - aham iti paramārthāni**

Having "I" as their ultimate reality.

### Vimarśamaya - विमर्शमय

**विमर्श - vimarśa**

Reflexive awareness.

**मय - maya**

Consisting of, constituted by.

Neuter nominative plural:

**विमर्शमयानि - vimarśamayāni**.

Thus:

**विमर्शमयान्येव - vimarśamayāny eva**.

They consist precisely of reflexive awareness.

### This is not the claim that every thought is true

Now a crucial distinction.

Suppose somebody has a mistaken perception.

They see a rope and think it's a snake.

The cognition is erroneous with respect to its object.

But it is still an occurrence of awareness.

So the claim that all cognitions belong ultimately to consciousness does **not** imply that every cognition accurately represents its object.

Nor that all beliefs are equally valid.

The distinction between error and valid knowledge remains meaningful within manifestation.

What Abhinavagupta denies is that such differences establish a second ultimate conscious substance outside Parā Saṃvid.

### The finite ego problem returns

Now imagine someone saying:

"All cognitions are Aham. Therefore all my opinions are the voice of Bhairava."

No.

The universal *Aham* is not identical in every respect with the limited contents of personal thought.

The fact that a belief appears within consciousness doesn't make the belief infallible.

Abhinavagupta has spent the entire commentary distinguishing the supreme conscious ground from the limiting forms through which it becomes manifest.

The divine I-consciousness is the self-revealing reality underlying cognition.

It isn't a guarantee that every sentence your ego produces is divinely authorized.

Which is fortunate, because the average ego is a truly unreliable commentator.

### The actual philosophical conclusion

Now we can state the structure.

**Prakāśa** - the luminosity of consciousness.

**Vimarśa** - its intrinsic self-apprehension.

**Ahaṃbhāva** - its self-repose as I-consciousness.

And all differentiated cognitions become intelligible through that underlying reality.

That's why *Aham* is universal.

And that's why the entire universe can be interpreted as a manifestation of the same conscious freedom.

Now Abhinavagupta states the final image.

And it's magnificent.

**The Supreme Lord vomits and swallows the universe.**

Yes.

That's the actual Sanskrit.

We've reached the cosmic digestive tract.


## 15. वमन् ग्रसमानश्च - Bhairava emits and swallows the universe

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S PROSE · P. 200

**एतद्विश्वमन्तःस्थितमानन्दशक्तिभरितो वमन् ग्रसमानश्च विसर्ग एव परमेश्वरः।**

*Etad viśvam antaḥsthitam ānandaśaktibharito vaman grasamānaś ca visarga eva parameśvaraḥ.*

"The Supreme Lord, as Visarga, filled with the power of bliss, emits and swallows this universe which abides within him."

Now, that is a spectacular image.

And the verbal forms are worth learning properly.

### Etad viśvam - एतद्विश्वम्

This universe.

**एतत् - etat**

This.

**विश्व - viśva**

All, universe.

Neuter nominative or accusative singular:

**विश्वम् - viśvam**.

### Antaḥsthita - अन्तःस्थित

Abiding within.

**अन्तः - antaḥ**

Inside.

**स्थित - sthita**

Standing, established, abiding.

From √**स्था - sthā**, to stand.

Thus:

**अन्तःस्थित - antaḥsthita**

Situated within.

### Ānandaśakti - आनन्दशक्ति

**आनन्द - ānanda**

Bliss.

**शक्ति - śakti**

Power.

Thus:

**आनन्दशक्ति - ānandaśakti**

The power of bliss.

### Bharitaḥ - भरितः

Filled, replete.

Related to √**भृ - bhṛ**, to bear, support or fill.

The participial adjective:

**भरित - bharita**

Filled.

Masculine nominative singular:

**भरितः - bharitaḥ**.

So:

**आनन्दशक्तिभरितः - ānandaśaktibharitaḥ**

Filled with the power of bliss.

### Vaman - वमन्

Vomiting, emitting, ejecting.

From √**वम् - vam**, to vomit, expel or emit.

The present participle:

**वमन् - vaman**

Emitting.

Now yes, the root has a very ordinary bodily sense.

But in this theological context it describes the outward projection of the universe.

It's a deliberately concrete image of emission.

### Grasamānaḥ - ग्रसमानः

Swallowing, consuming, engulfing.

From √**ग्रस् - gras**, to swallow or devour.

Present middle participle:

**ग्रसमान - grasamāna**.

Masculine nominative singular:

**ग्रसमानः - grasamānaḥ**.

And:

**च - ca**

And.

Thus:

**वमन् ग्रसमानश्च - vaman grasamānaś ca**

Emitting and swallowing.

### Visarga - विसर्ग

Emission.

Here the supreme divine power of manifestation.

### Parameśvaraḥ - परमेश्वरः

The Supreme Lord.

Now the philosophical image is clear.

The universe is not an alien substance that Bhairava acquires from outside.

It abides within the divine conscious reality.

Its manifestation can be described as emission.

Its reabsorption as swallowing.

Both are expressions of the same power.

### The cosmic digestive system

Imagine Bhairava saying:

"Right. Universe out."

The galaxies appear.

The oceans appear.

The thirty-six tattvas unfold.

Somewhere, somebody starts arguing about whether the Sanskrit alphabet contains fifty or sixty-four letters.

Bhairava then says:

"Right. Universe in."

Everything is gathered.

And the supreme reality remains.

Now, of course, this isn't a literal anatomical description of a deity's stomach.

The verbs are metaphors for the manifestation and reabsorption of differentiated reality.

But the imagery is wonderfully memorable.

**Bhairava doesn't need to manufacture an external world from alien material. He manifests and withdraws what is already grounded in his own conscious freedom.**

And that is why the passage connects the movement with Ānandaśakti.

The manifestation isn't born from an intrinsic lack in the Absolute.

It is an expression of its fullness.

Now the phonemic sequence resumes.

The Lord becomes the *ha*-power.

Then *kṣa*.

And then the entire articulated universe emerges.

We've reached the final phonemic movement of Part XIX.


## 16. हकारात्मता - The Lord becomes the power of *ha*

The prose says:

**घनीभूय हकारात्मतां प्रतिपद्य।**

*Ghanībhūya hakārātmatāṃ pratipadya.*

"Having become condensed, he assumes the nature of the *ha*-sound."

Now this is the same process we've been studying for many installments.

The subtle conscious power becomes more explicitly differentiated.

And the phonemic representation moves into the consonantal series.

### Ghanībhūya - घनीभूय

Having become dense or condensed.

From:

**घन - ghana**

Dense.

And:

**भू - bhū**

To become.

The absolutive:

**घनीभूय - ghanībhūya**

Having become condensed.

Now the important point.

This is **not** a literal statement that consciousness acquires physical mass.

It's a Tantric interpretation of the movement from subtle power into more explicit manifestation.

### Hakāra - हकार

The phoneme *ha*.

**ह - ha**

**कार - kāra**

Phonemic naming element.

Thus:

**हकार - hakāra**.

### Ātmatā - आत्मता

The condition of having a particular nature.

From:

**आत्मन् - ātman**

Self, nature.

With the abstract suffix:

**-ता - -tā**.

Thus:

**आत्मता - ātmatā**.

Now:

**हकारात्मता - hakārātmatā**

The condition of having the nature of *ha*.

Accusative singular:

**हकारात्मताम् - hakārātmatām**.

### Pratipadya - प्रतिपद्य

Having attained, having entered into, having assumed.

From *prati-√pad*, to attain or arrive.

The absolutive:

**प्रतिपद्य - pratipadya**.

So:

**hakārātmatāṃ pratipadya**

Having assumed *ha*-nature.

Now the philosophical meaning.

The divine power becomes manifest through the phonemic form associated with the final expressive power.

And the movement into *ha* is followed by a further multiplicity of combinations.

Which leads to **क्ष - kṣa**.


## 17. क्षरूपता - The entire consonantal universe culminates in *kṣa*

The Sanskrit continues:

**अनन्तसंयोगवैचित्र्येण क्षरूपतामप्येति।**

*Anantasaṃyogavaicitryeṇa kṣarūpatām apy eti.*

"Through the variety of endless combinations, it even assumes the form of *kṣa*."

Now let's learn the terms.

### Ananta - अनन्त

Endless.

**अन् - an-**

Negative prefix.

**अन्त - anta**

End.

Thus:

**अनन्त - ananta**.

### Saṃyoga - संयोग

Combination, conjunction, union.

From:

**सम् - sam**

Together.

**√युज् - yuj**

To join.

Thus:

**संयोग - saṃyoga**

Combination.

Now the word is also a technical grammatical term.

In Sanskrit phonology, *saṃyoga* can designate a consonant cluster.

And that's particularly relevant here.

### Vaicitrya - वैचित्र्य

Variety, differentiation, variegation.

Related to:

**विचित्र - vicitra**

Varied, remarkable.

Instrumental singular:

**वैचित्र्येण - vaicitryeṇa**

Through variety.

Now the compound:

**अनन्तसंयोगवैचित्र्येण - anantasaṃyogavaicitryeṇa**

Through the diversity of endless combinations.

### Kṣa - क्ष

Now the important phonetic detail.

In ordinary Sanskrit orthography:

**क्ष = क् + ष**

*k + ṣa*.

It's a consonant conjunct, traditionally also treated as a special alphabetic element in some enumerations.

We must distinguish its **orthographic composition** from its **Tantric symbolic role**.

The Kashmir Series includes an editorial bracket explaining *kṣa* through a more elaborate phonemic combination involving *ka*, *sa*, *a* and Visarga.

That bracket is an interpretive gloss.

It should not be presented as the ordinary historical derivation of the Sanskrit conjunct *kṣa*.

And it is not Abhinavagupta's uninterrupted prose.

The secure main-text statement is that the Lord, through the diversity of endless combinations, assumes the *kṣa*-form. ; \]

### The philosophical significance

Now remember the beginning of the alphabetic exposition.

**अ - a**

The unsurpassable source.

Then the intermediate vowel-powers.

Then the consonants.

And the final alphabetic culmination in *kṣa*.

The sequence symbolizes the full range of divine manifestation.

But this is not a merely linear procession of letters through clock-time.

The underlying power is one.

The different sounds are expressions.

And the full spectrum of manifestation can be contemplated through their relationships.

### The terrible cosmic spelling bee

"Ā."

Then:

"I."

The grammarians stare at their notes.

He begins to weep.

But the philosophical point is sound.

**The plurality of the phonemic series does not require independent plurality in the supreme conscious ground.**

And now Abhinavagupta adds a final Kaula description of the manifested sound.

It concerns the generative matrix, the conjunction of Śakti's powers, and the condition of **Anāhata Nāda**.

This is the last difficult passage before the natural textual boundary.

And it's important not to omit it.


## 18. The Kaula conjunction - Sound arises through the Śākta generative matrix

After *kṣa*, Abhinavagupta continues with an unusually dense compound.

The key components are:

**दूत्यात्मक - dūtyātmaka**

Associated with the Dūtī, the female intermediary or partner in the Kaula context.

**शाक्तयोनि - śāktayoni**

The Śākta generative matrix.

**सङ्घट्ट - saṅghaṭṭa**

Conjunction, encounter, union.

**क्षोभ - kṣobha**

Stirring, agitation, excitation.

And:

**अनाहतनाद - anāhatanāda**

Unstruck sound.

Now let's be clear about the textual situation.

The unbracketed prose invokes the union of the Śākta generative powers as part of the manifestation of sound.

The Kashmir Series then inserts several verses about Kaula ritual involving a female partner.

Those verses are inside square brackets.

They provide ritual context, but they are not uninterrupted words of Abhinavagupta at this point.

### Dūtī - दूती

A female messenger, intermediary or ritual partner.

In Kaula Tantric contexts, the term can have an explicitly sexual and ritual significance.

We shouldn't pretend it merely means an abstract philosophical category.

But the present passage is using the established generative symbolism to explain the emergence of sound.

### Yoni - योनि

Womb, source, matrix.

### Saṅghaṭṭa - सङ्घट्ट

Union, collision, joining.

Now remember Part XVI.

Śiva's seed became the generative flower of Śakti.

The triangle of apprehender, apprehension and apprehended was interpreted through the Śākta matrix.

And the union of Śiva and Śakti was understood as the generative ground of manifestation.

The present sentence belongs to that same structure.

It's not a new unrelated doctrine.

### Kṣobha - क्षोभ

Disturbance, stirring, agitation.

Related to √**क्षुभ् - kṣubh**, to become disturbed or agitated.

Now the word is important in the cosmological symbolism.

It expresses the emergence of differentiated activity from a more subtle condition.

But again, we shouldn't turn the metaphor into a measurable mechanical vibration supposedly observed at the beginning of the physical universe.

The theological interpretation concerns the expressive movement of Śakti.

### Anāhata - अनाहत

Unstruck.

From:

**अन् - an-**

Negative prefix.

**आहत - āhata**

Struck, beaten, impacted.

From *ā-√han*, to strike.

Thus:

**अनाहत - anāhata**

Unstruck.

### Nāda - नाद

Sound, resonance, subtle sonic principle.

Together:

**अनाहतनाद - anāhatanāda**

Unstruck sound.

Now here's the important distinction.

Ordinary sound in the physical world involves vibratory processes.

The Tantric term *anāhata nāda* refers to a subtle level of sonic manifestation within a doctrinal and contemplative scheme.

It isn't simply a claim that physical sound can occur without any causal process.

The phrase must be understood within its tradition.

### Why the unstruck sound matters here

The commentary has moved from:

The first vowel.

To the divine powers.

To Visarga.

To *ha*.

To the diversified alphabet.

And now it refers to the subtle sound-state underlying and connecting those manifestations.

So the phonemic series isn't merely an externally pronounced alphabet.

Its deeper reality belongs to the inner manifestation of consciousness.

And that is why the passage now invokes the Suṣumṇā-related condition of Madhyamā.

The movement is already turning inward.


## 19. Madhyamā and Suṣumṇā - The universe of sound returns inward

Abhinavagupta continues by associating the power with:

**मध्यम - madhyama**

Intermediate or inner speech.

**सौषुम्न - sauṣumna**

Pertaining to Suṣumṇā.

And:

**अनन्तभावपटल - anantabhāvapaṭala**

The endless expanse of manifested beings or states.

The key idea is that the manifesting Visarga-power, assuming the subtle Nāda condition, returns toward its supreme ground.

Now let's learn the terms.

### Madhyamā - मध्यमा

Intermediate.

Feminine form of:

**मध्य - madhya**

Middle.

In the fourfold doctrine of speech, *Madhyamā* is a subtler level than externally articulated Vaikharī.

### Sauṣumna - सौषुम्न

Pertaining to Suṣumṇā.

The derivative form begins with the strengthened vowel **au**.

And:

**सुषुम्णा - suṣumṇā**

The central subtle channel.

Now the important point.

The passage associates the intermediate speech-condition with a Suṣumṇā-related mode.

But it doesn't provide a complete anatomical instruction for practising breath retention or manipulating subtle energy.

The context remains the metaphysics of speech.

### Bhāvapaṭala - भावपटल

The expanse of manifested states or beings.

We've encountered this compound in Abhinavagupta's hymn.

Now it returns.

The same endless field of manifestations can be understood as emerging from and returning to the conscious ground.

### Viśliṣyan - विश्लिष्यन्

Separating, loosening, relaxing its conjunction.

From a verbal family involving separation or release.

And:

### Dhruvadhāman - ध्रुवधामन्

The stable, immutable abode.

**ध्रुव - dhruva**

Fixed, stable, enduring.

**धामन् - dhāman**

Abode, luminous domain, state.

Now the movement is clear.

The differentiated sonic manifestation is gradually considered through subtler forms.

It is no longer simply the outwardly articulated consonantal universe.

And the power returns to the stable supreme ground.

### Anuttarapada - अनुत्तरपद

The unsurpassable state.

Now the passage concludes:

**अनुत्तरपद एव प्रविशति।**

*Anuttarapada eva praviśati.*

"It enters the unsurpassable state itself."

And then Abhinavagupta remarks that this has already been explained previously.

That's a significant sign that the argument is reaching a genuine boundary.

He's not introducing an entirely new phonemic principle here.

He's completing the movement already established.

Manifestation.

Differentiation.

Subtle sound.

Reabsorption.

Anuttara.

### The ultimate philosophical point

Now imagine all the letters returning to silence.

The audible sounds cease.

The conceptual distinctions are no longer being explicitly rehearsed.

But the conscious ground hasn't been manufactured by this cessation.

Nor is it necessarily absent during speech.

The difference is the mode of manifestation.

And that is why the same supreme reality can be described through:

Parā.

Paśyantī.

Madhyamā.

Vaikharī.

The levels are distinguishable.

But their ultimate ground remains one.

**The outward word and the inward power aren't independently existing conscious substances.**

And that is the completion of the phonemic movement at p. 200.

The universe emerges through divine freedom.

Its letters and objects are differentiated.

Their conscious reality is Aham.

And their withdrawal is represented through return to Anuttara.

The entire passage has been an explanation of the supreme **I**.

Not the personal pronoun as an isolated piece of grammar.

But the self-revealing conscious ground to which all the phonemic powers belong.


## 20. Where the text now changes subject

We stop on Kashmir Series p. 200, immediately before the next words:

**अमी चाकाराद्याः स्थितिमन्तः प्राणे...**

*Amī cākārādyāḥ sthitimantaḥ prāṇe...*

"And these sounds beginning with *a*, established within Prāṇa..."

Abhinavagupta now turns from the immediate *Aham* argument to the detailed relationship among:

**प्राण - prāṇa**

The vital breath or energy.

**तुटि - tuṭi**

A minute unit of time.

**षोडश - ṣoḍaśa**

Sixteen.

And the lunar structure of:

**तिथि - tithi**

Lunar day.

**कला - kalā**

Lunar digit or power.

The next pages introduce the special seventeenth *kalā*, the relation between Visarga and fractions of *ha*, and the difficult question of how something ultimately without parts can nevertheless be discussed through fractions.

That is a genuinely new technical argument.

And it deserves its own installment.

We should not rush through it merely because we're excited about the great I-consciousness.

The natural stopping point is here.

The *Aham* exposition has culminated.

The phonemic universe has returned to Anuttara.

And now the commentary will ask how the same reality is expressed through breath and lunar time.


## 21. The complete movement of Part XIX

Let's gather the argument in the exact order we've followed.

Now the philosophical achievement is enormous.

Aham is not merely a label attached to a particular body.

Nor is it simply a thought that appears occasionally.

In the Pratyabhijñā account, it expresses the intrinsic self-apprehension of consciousness.

And because the entire universe appears through the freedom of that consciousness, the fullness of *Aham* is not limited to any particular object.

That's why the same power can manifest:

A pot.

A feeling.

A thought.

A language.

A phoneme.

A divine world.

A cosmic cycle.

And the whole universe.

Yet it doesn't become independently divided by those manifestations.

**The universal “I” is not one of the objects appearing within consciousness. It is the self-revealing reality through which their manifestation is possible.**

That's the essential point.

### Four especially useful writing exercises

**1. The Divine I**

# अहम् - अहंभावः

aham - ahaṃbhāvaḥ

*I* and *the state of I-consciousness*. Notice **म्** in the independent pronoun and the nasal sign **ं** in the compound.


**2. Utpaladeva's definition**

## प्रकाशस्यात्मविश्रान्तिः

prakāśasyātmaviśrāntiḥ

**प्रकाशस्य + आत्मविश्रान्तिः**. The long ā at the joining comes from the vowel sandhi.


**3. The Lord's emission and withdrawal**

vaman - grasamānaḥ

Emitting and swallowing. Two different present participial formations.

a - ha - kṣa - bindu

## 23. The final insight - The I doesn't have to become itself

Now here's the central insight I want to retain.

Normally, when we say "I," we identify ourselves through particular conditions.

I am hungry.

I am reading.

I am thinking.

I am happy.

I am annoyed.

I am this body.

I am this biography.

The contents change.

But the possibility of experiencing them as *mine*, or as belonging to a conscious subject, reveals a more fundamental question.

**What is the nature of the awareness through which these changing contents become manifest?**

Utpaladeva gives a remarkable answer.

The repose of Prakāśa in itself is I-consciousness.

Abhinavagupta then interprets the entire phonemic universe through that same reality.

The world is emitted.

The world is reabsorbed.

The vowels and consonants appear.

The tattvas differentiate.

Time unfolds through innumerable cycles.

Yet none of these transformations creates an independently ultimate second consciousness.

And that is why he can say:

**Aham and its reverse represent two directions of one reality.**

Manifestation and withdrawal aren't separate Absolutes.

The underlying conscious freedom remains undivided.

But here's the really important part.

The universal I-consciousness doesn't become complete after it has finished manifesting the universe.

And it doesn't need to destroy the universe in order to become complete again.

Its fullness is intrinsic.

The acts of manifestation and withdrawal express that fullness.

They don't manufacture it.

**Śiva does not need to become himself. His self-revealing freedom is already the ground of every becoming.**

Now that is a beautiful distinction.

Because it means the entire universe, in Abhinavagupta's metaphysics, is not a journey from the absence of consciousness into consciousness.

It is a manifestation of the conscious reality that was never absent.

And the supreme *Aham* isn't merely one more word in the dictionary.

It's a sacred expression of the self-apprehending nature of awareness.

The written word is a symbol.

The phonemes are a symbolic structure.

The philosophical teaching is an explanation.

But the conscious ground is what the explanation is meant to disclose.

And now, after nineteen installments, we've finally reached the word that contains the entire alphabet.

**अहम् - aham.**

I.

The first vowel.

The final expressive power.

The concentrated point.

Manifestation.

Reabsorption.

One consciousness.

And the whole bloody universe in between.
