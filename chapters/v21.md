# Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa

PART XXI - How Śakti Invents Space and Time

*Abhinavagupta puts every letter of Sanskrit into every act of knowing, watches the Goddesses compete to manifest the universe, asks why Mount Meru isn't the size of an atom, explains how differentiated knowledge gives rise to space and differentiated activity to time, and turns the simple act of seeing something into a four-stage cosmic drama. Then the Sun and Moon begin swallowing one another's powers, and the whole thing returns to the magnificent divine “I.”*

Kashmir Series pp. 204-207 · Commentary on verses 5-8 · Primary Sanskrit checked against Somadeva Vasudeva's transcription


## Previously, in the cosmic madhouse…

Twenty installments.

We've learned the sacred vowels, the thirty-six *tattvas*, the phonemic body of Śakti, the generative triangle, the metaphysics of ordinary language, and the supreme mystery of:

# अहम्

Aham - I-consciousness

Then Abhinavagupta introduced sixteen subdivisions of breath, fifteen lunar days, a sixteenth emissive power, and the seventeenth Goddess-who, rather inconveniently for the accountants, was described as half of half the phoneme *ha*.

An opponent asked how an indivisible phoneme could have parts.

Abhinavagupta answered that, at the highest level of his philosophy, **the manifestation of distinguishable parts does not divide the one conscious reality**.

He extended the argument to syllable duration, articulation, voice, emotion and music.

And at the end of Part XX, the vowels had become the expressive powers through which sorrow, love, tranquillity, crying, praise and musical tones could be made manifest.

Now he draws the conclusion.

Every cognition-every moment of seeing, hearing, thinking, remembering, desiring, acting-contains the divine powers of the entire phonemic system.

And then something rather extraordinary happens.

The Goddesses, all present together in nonsequential consciousness, produce the appearance of **space**.

Then, through the differentiation of action, they manifest **time**.

But Śiva himself doesn't become trapped inside an external space-time container.

Because the distinctions arise through his own conscious freedom.

That's the philosophical problem of Part XXI.

Right.

The Goddesses have arrived.

All of them.

In every cognition.

And they're apparently competing to make the universe manifest.


## 1. एवं सर्वत्र संवेदने - Every cognition contains all the powers

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN PROSE · KASHMIR SERIES P. 204

**एवं सर्वत्र संवेदने सर्वा एवैता वैचित्र्यचर्याचारचतुराः शक्तय आदिक्षान्ताः।**

*Evaṃ sarvatra saṃvedane sarvā evaitā vaicitryacaryācāracaturāḥ śaktaya ādikṣāntāḥ.*

"Thus, in every cognition, all these powers-from *a* to *kṣa*-are skilful in the diverse modes of manifestation."

This is our exact opening.

And it's one of the densest and most beautiful Sanskrit expressions we've encountered.

Let's dismantle it carefully.

### Evaṃ - एवम्

Thus, in this manner.

An indeclinable.

It refers back to the preceding explanation of the vowel-powers and their expressive capacities.

### Sarvatra - सर्वत्र

Everywhere, in every instance.

From:

**सर्व - sarva**

All.

And the locative adverbial element:

**-त्र - -tra**

Thus:

**सर्वत्र - sarvatra**

Everywhere.

### Saṃvedane - संवेदने

In cognition.

From:

**संवेदन - saṃvedana**

Conscious apprehension, cognition, experience.

Locative singular:

**संवेदने - saṃvedane**.

Now the important point.

Abhinavagupta isn't speaking only of extraordinary mystical visions.

He says **every cognition**.

The apprehension of a pot.

The recognition of a colour.

The awareness of pleasure.

The understanding of a word.

Any ordinary cognitive event.

### Sarvā eva etāḥ - सर्वा एवैता

All these very powers.

The underlying forms are:

**सर्वाः - sarvāḥ**

All, feminine nominative plural.

**एव - eva**

Precisely, indeed.

**एताः - etāḥ**

These, feminine nominative plural.

They agree with:

**शक्तयः - śaktayaḥ**

Powers, Goddesses.

Now here's a useful grammatical distinction.

**शक्ति - śakti**

Feminine singular stem.

**शक्तयः - śaktayaḥ**

Feminine nominative plural.

Don't confuse this with the masculine nominative plural of an *-a* stem, usually ending in *-āḥ*.

### Vaicitryacaryācāracaturāḥ - वैचित्र्यचर्याचारचतुराः

Now behold this monster.

A compound worthy of Abhinavagupta.

Let's separate its elements.

**वैचित्र्य - vaicitrya**

Variety, differentiation, multiplicity of forms.

From the *vicitra* family, meaning variegated or remarkable.

**चर्या - caryā**

Conduct, movement, practice, mode of operation.

Related to √**चर् - car**, to move or proceed.

**आचार - ācāra**

Conduct, practice, established mode of action.

Related to *ā-√car*, to conduct or practise.

**चतुर - catura**

Clever, skilful, adept.

Feminine nominative plural:

**चतुराः - caturāḥ**.

So the whole compound expresses the powers as extraordinarily skilful in their variegated modes of activity.

Notice the lovely accumulation of **ca** sounds.

*Caryā.*

*Ācāra.*

*Catura.*

The passage almost makes the Goddesses sound like virtuoso performers of manifestation.

And that's precisely the idea.

### Ādikṣāntāḥ - आदिक्षान्ताः

Now the range of the phonemic powers.

**आदि - ādi**

Beginning.

**क्षान्त - kṣānta**

Ending with *kṣa* in this alphabetic context.

Thus:

**आदिक्षान्ताः - ādikṣāntāḥ**

Extending from the beginning to *kṣa*.

Now be careful.

The form *kṣānta* can resemble an ordinary Sanskrit adjective meaning patient or endured.

But this context is unmistakably phonemic.

It concerns the sacred sound-series from *a* through *kṣa*.

### The philosophical meaning

Every cognition manifests through a structured relation of conscious powers.

And the complete divine phonemic system is understood as present within that manifestation.

This doesn't mean that the human brain audibly pronounces every Sanskrit syllable each time somebody recognizes a potato.

Nor does it mean that a person who has never learned Sanskrit secretly knows the dictionary.

The claim is metaphysical.

The powers represented by the phonemes are intrinsic to consciousness.

Therefore a particular cognition doesn't require a separate, independently ultimate consciousness for every differentiated function.

**The particular appearance is one expression of the complete divine power.**

And now Abhinavagupta makes that even more vivid.

The powers arrive with:

**अहमहमिका - ahamahamikā**.

A rather wonderful word.

Because it makes the Goddesses sound as if they're competing to be first.


## 2. अहमहमिका - The Goddesses are all saying, “Me! Me!”

The sentence continues:

**समापतन्त्योऽहमहमिकया।**

*Samāpatantyo 'hamahamikayā.*

"Coming together with an *ahamahamikā*-an eager mutual assertion of 'I, I!'"

Now the term has an ordinary Sanskrit usage involving rivalry or competitive precedence.

It's not simply a repetition of the supreme metaphysical pronoun *aham* without any idiomatic force.

And the editor actually supplies a lexicographical definition in square brackets.

But the word itself belongs to Abhinavagupta's unbracketed prose.

Let's learn it.

### Aham - अहम्

I.

The first-person pronoun.

### Ahamaham - अहमहम्

"I, I."

A repeated assertion.

Now:

### Ahamahamikā - अहमहमिका

The state of saying "I, I" in competition, vying with one another.

Instrumental singular:

**अहमहमिकया - ahamahamikayā**.

Through an eager competitive assertion.

Now this is delightful, because the context involves the powers appearing together.

The expression suggests a rush of mutually eager manifestation.

### Samāpatantyaḥ - समापतन्त्यः

Coming together, arriving, converging.

From:

**सम् - sam**

Together.

**आ - ā**

Toward.

**√पत् - pat**

To fall, fly or move toward.

The present feminine nominative plural participle:

**समापतन्त्यः - samāpatantyaḥ**.

It agrees with:

**शक्तयः - śaktayaḥ**.

Now the sandhi:

*samāpatantyaḥ + ahamahamikayā*

Becomes:

**समापतन्त्योऽहमहमिकया**

*samāpatantyo 'hamahamikayā.*

Notice the **ओऽ - o '** sequence arising from visarga sandhi.

### What does the editor add?

The bracketed note says, in effect, that *ahamahamikā* is a mutually competitive expression of *ahaṃkāra* or "I-assertion."

That's a lexical explanation.

Useful.

But not a new verse of Abhinavagupta's commentary.

Nor evidence that the supreme Goddesses possess finite psychological egos in exactly the same sense as humans.

### The cosmic talent show

Imagine every divine phonemic power simultaneously approaching the stage.

*A* says:

"I'm first."

*Ā* says:

"I'm the expansion of bliss."

*I* says:

"Excellent, but I'm will."

The knowledge-powers arrive.

Then the action-powers.

Then the consonants.

Then *ha*.

Then *kṣa*, who refuses to be left out despite being an awkward conjunct.

Every Goddess says:

"Me!"

"I'll manifest this cognition!"

"No, I will!"

And Abhinavagupta says:

**They all manifest together.**

Because their ultimate reality is one conscious power.

The comedy illustrates the Sanskrit idiom.

But philosophically the point is serious:

**No single limited phonemic power independently manufactures cognition from outside the others.**

Their distinguishable functions are mutually implicated within the one divine consciousness.

And that's why the next word matters.

**Akrama.**

Nonsequence.

All these powers are present together, before any explanatory ordering into earlier and later.

And yet, through their manifestations, the appearance of sequence arises.

How?

Now we're getting to the extraordinary part.


## 3. अक्रममेव भासमानाः - All the Goddesses manifest without sequence

The sentence continues:

**अक्रममेव भासमानाः।**

*Akramam eva bhāsamānāḥ.*

"Appearing precisely without sequence."

Now that's the crucial qualification.

Because the Goddesses have just been described through *ahamahamikā*, as if eagerly competing to manifest.

But their ultimate manifestation isn't a queue of independent divine agents.

### Akrama - अक्रम

**अ - a-**

Negative prefix.

**क्रम - krama**

Sequence, order, successive progression.

Thus:

**अक्रम - akrama**

Nonsequence.

Here:

**अक्रमम् - akramam**

Adverbially, without sequence.

### Bhāsamānāḥ - भासमानाः

Appearing, shining forth.

From √**भास् - bhās**, to shine or appear.

The present participle:

**भासमान - bhāsamāna**.

Feminine nominative plural:

**भासमानाः - bhāsamānāḥ**.

Now the important philosophical point.

**The divine powers are simultaneously implicit in the fullness of consciousness, even when their differentiated expressions appear sequentially.**

And we must distinguish those two claims.

The first concerns the ultimate unity of the powers.

The second concerns their manifest operation.

Abhinavagupta isn't denying that a spoken Sanskrit word unfolds through time.

The consonants do not all emerge as one undifferentiated acoustic blast.

Nor is he denying that one bodily action may occur before another.

His claim is that the powers are not independently ultimate entities that need to be manufactured in temporal order.

The manifest sequence belongs to the expression of the powers.

Not to the constitution of the supreme conscious reality.

### The difference between a phrase and its source

Imagine speaking:

**नमः शिवाय - namaḥ śivāya**.

You articulate the syllables successively.

The physical sound has a beginning and end.

But you can intend the complete phrase before finishing its pronunciation.

You can remember the whole phrase after the sound has ceased.

You can recognize it as one meaningful utterance.

The temporal articulation and the unity of its conscious apprehension are distinguishable.

Now that isn't a complete account of the ultimate *akrama* of Bhairava.

But it offers an ordinary illustration of why unity and succession need not be mutually exclusive.

### The important Pratyabhijñā distinction

Consciousness doesn't become a pile of disconnected flashes merely because its objects are successively manifested.

The power of recognition relates differentiated presentations.

And in the supreme form, the entire field of manifestation belongs to one self-aware freedom.

So:

**Akrama doesn't mean that sequential events are impossible.**

It means that the ground isn't itself constituted by their succession.

And now Abhinavagupta makes a genuinely radical move.

He says the very powers through which objects become differentiated also give rise to the appearance of **place**.

Not just which object you see.

But where that object appears to be.

Space is about to become a manifestation of *kalanā*.


## 4. कलनामयतया - The power that differentiates also gives things their apparent places

The next phrase is:

**कलनामयतयैव ज्ञानक्रमसङ्क्रमणमेव दिश्यमानं देशमुत्थापयन्त्यः।**

*Kalanāmayatayaiva jñānakramasaṃkramaṇam eva diśyamānaṃ deśam utthāpayantyaḥ.*

A close rendering:

"Through their character as the power of differentiation, they bring forth place as it is disclosed through the movement among successive cognitions."

Now that needs careful explanation.

Because the Sanskrit is tremendously compressed.

And we shouldn't simply announce that Abhinavagupta had invented modern theoretical physics.

He's making a **metaphysical argument about the manifestation of spatial distinctions within consciousness**.

Let's dismantle it.

### Kalanā - कलना

Differentiation, measuring, articulating, delimiting.

Related to √**कल् - kal**, to count or calculate in the established lexical family.

This is a crucial Trika term.

It concerns the capacity to distinguish and articulate within the otherwise undivided ground of consciousness.

### -Maya - मय

Consisting of, characterized by.

Thus:

**कलनामय - kalanāmaya**

Consisting of differentiating activity.

And the abstract noun:

**कलनामयता - kalanāmayatā**

The condition of being characterized by differentiation.

Instrumental singular:

**कलनामयतया - kalanāmayatayā**.

Now join:

**कलनामयतयैव - kalanāmayatayaiva**.

Because:

**ā + e → ai**.

That's genuine vowel sandhi.

### Jñāna - ज्ञान

Knowing, cognition.

From √**ज्ञा - jñā**, to know.

### Krama - क्रम

Sequence, succession.

### Saṃkramaṇa - सङ्क्रमण

Transition, movement across, passage.

From:

**सम् - sam**

Together.

**√क्रम् - kram**

To step or proceed.

Thus:

**सङ्क्रमण - saṃkramaṇa**

Transition or passage.

Now the compound:

**ज्ञानक्रमसङ्क्रमण - jñānakramasaṃkramaṇa**

The transition among sequential presentations of knowledge.

### Deśa - देश

Place, region, location, spatial domain.

Now the accusative singular:

**देशम् - deśam**.

### Utthāpayantyaḥ - उत्थापयन्त्यः

Causing to arise, bringing forth.

From:

**उद् - ud**

Up, forth.

**√स्था - sthā**

To stand.

The causative:

**उत्थापयति - utthāpayati**

Causes to rise, raises.

Feminine nominative plural participle:

**उत्थापयन्त्यः - utthāpayantyaḥ**.

It agrees with the divine powers.

So the sentence describes the Śaktis as bringing forth the presentation of space.

### What does that mean?

Imagine perceiving several objects.

A tree.

A stone.

A building.

You apprehend them as distinguishable.

You also apprehend them as differently situated.

The tree is here.

The building is farther away.

The stone is between them.

Now the philosophical question is:

**Must this spatial order be an independently self-revealing substance outside consciousness?**

Abhinavagupta says no.

The differentiation of objects and their relations is manifest through the power of consciousness.

And the appearance of place belongs to that differentiation.

This is not the banal assertion that you can change the physical distance between two objects merely by thinking about it.

Nor does it abolish ordinary practical geography.

It's a claim about the ontological and epistemic ground of spatial manifestation.

### Why the transition among cognitions matters

We ordinarily learn about spatial relations through changing perspectives and discriminations.

You see one side of a building.

Then move around it.

You recognize the relation between those appearances.

You distinguish near from far.

Inside from outside.

Here from there.

Abhinavagupta's account emphasizes that the intelligible relation is manifested through consciousness.

And the Goddesses' power of *kalanā* makes those differences available.

**Space, in this doctrinal account, isn't an independently conscious container standing beyond Bhairava.**

It is one form in which conscious freedom manifests differentiation.

Now here's the objection.

If spatial difference were entirely absent from manifestation, how could we distinguish the greatest imaginable mountain from the smallest imagined particle?

Enter:

**Meru.**

And:

**Paramāṇu.**

The largest and smallest actors in the cosmic philosophical sitcom.


## 5. अन्यथा मेरुपरमाण्वोरविशेषात् - Otherwise Mount Meru would be indistinguishable from an atom

SHORT REASONING FORMULA CITED IN ABHINAVAGUPTA'S MAIN PROSE · P. 204

**अन्यथा मेरुपरमाण्वोरविशेषात्।**

*Anyathā meruparamāṇvor aviśeṣāt.*

"Otherwise there would be no distinction between Meru and an atom."

Now this is one of the best philosophical examples in the entire passage.

Because it dramatizes the necessity of differentiating manifestation.

Let's learn it.

### Anyathā - अन्यथा

Otherwise.

An indeclinable.

### Meru - मेरु

Mount Meru.

The immense cosmological mountain of traditional Indian religious geography.

Now we should treat its role carefully.

The point here is the **greatest imaginable spatial extent**, not a verified scientific measurement of the mountain.

Meru is the large-scale contrast.

### Paramāṇu - परमाणु

The minute or ultimate atom in traditional Indian philosophical terminology.

**परम - parama**

Ultimate, supreme.

**अणु - aṇu**

Minute, tiny.

Thus:

**परमाणु - paramāṇu**

The ultimate minute unit.

Now, don't equate it uncritically with the modern atomic model.

Ancient Indian philosophical schools gave different accounts of minute particles.

Here the term supplies the extreme of smallness.

### Meruparamāṇvoḥ - मेरुपरमाण्वोः

Now the grammar is particularly useful.

The compound joins:

**मेरु - meru**

Meru.

**परमाणु - paramāṇu**

Ultimate atom.

The ending:

**-ओः - -oḥ**

Genitive or locative dual.

Thus:

**मेरुपरमाण्वोः - meruparamāṇvoḥ**

Of Meru and the atom.

Notice the transition from **-u** to the **-v-** glide in the inflection.

### Aviśeṣa - अविशेष

Absence of distinction.

**अ - a-**

Negative prefix.

**विशेष - viśeṣa**

Difference, particularity.

Thus:

**अविशेष - aviśeṣa**

Nondistinction.

Ablative singular:

**अविशेषात् - aviśeṣāt**.

Because of nondistinction.

### The philosophical objection

If all manifestation were simply an undifferentiated identity with no possible articulation of differences, the enormous and the minute could not be distinguished as such.

But Abhinavagupta doesn't deny the difference.

He explains it through *kalanā*.

The power of conscious differentiation manifests spatial relations.

So a mountain and an atom can be apprehended as different in extent.

But their difference doesn't create two independently ultimate conscious realities.

### Meru meets the atom

Imagine Mount Meru complaining:

"I am unimaginably vast."

A tiny atom replies:

"Yes, but apparently we're both consciousness."

Meru becomes furious.

"Then why am I enormous?"

The atom replies:

"Because nonduality doesn't abolish differentiated manifestation, you colossal idiot."

And that is the point.

The large and small are distinguishable.

Their ultimate ground remains one.

**Abhinavagupta is rejecting both an independently existing spatial Absolute and a featureless nonduality incapable of manifestation.**

And now he moves to the second great axis of the ordinary world.

Time.

The power of action produces sequence.

And the whole universe becomes a drama of before and after.


## 6. क्रियावैचित्र्यात्मकं क्रमरूपं कालम् - Time appears through differentiated action

Now the Sanskrit continues:

**गर्भीकृतदेशात्मकवैचित्र्यं क्रियावैचित्र्यात्मकं क्रमरूपं कालं बहिर्योजनयोल्लासयन्त्यः।**

*Garbhīkṛtadeśātmakavaicitryaṃ kriyāvaicitryātmakaṃ kramarūpaṃ kālaṃ bahiryojanayollāsayantyaḥ.*

A close rendering:

"They unfold time outwardly as sequence, constituted by the differentiation of actions and containing within itself the diversity expressed as spatial form."

Now that's a very dense sentence.

And its structure is important.

We've just studied the emergence of spatial differentiation.

Abhinavagupta now describes time through the manifestation of differentiated activities.

### Garbhīkṛta - गर्भीकृत

Contained, enclosed, incorporated.

From:

**गर्भ - garbha**

Womb, interior, what is contained.

**कृत - kṛta**

Made, placed.

Thus:

**गर्भीकृत - garbhīkṛta**

Made internal, incorporated.

Now, this is a beautiful word.

The temporal manifestation includes, rather than simply abolishes, the previously described spatial distinctions.

### Deśātmaka - देशात्मक

**देश - deśa**

Place, space.

**आत्मक - ātmaka**

Having the nature of.

Thus:

**देशात्मक - deśātmaka**

Spatial in nature.

### Vaicitrya - वैचित्र्य

Variety, differentiation.

Now the first compound concerns differentiated spatial appearance.

And the second:

### Kriyāvaicitrya - क्रियावैचित्र्य

**क्रिया - kriyā**

Action, operation, activity.

**वैचित्र्य - vaicitrya**

Diversity.

Thus:

**क्रियावैचित्र्य - kriyāvaicitrya**

Differentiation of actions.

Now this is the key distinction.

**Spatial sequence is associated with diversity of forms.**

**Temporal sequence is associated with diversity of activities.**

The most explicit traditional formulation occurs in Utpaladeva's *Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā* 2.1.5, which the Kashmir Series editor inserts in square brackets here.

We will read it separately in a moment.

### Kramarūpa - क्रमरूप

Having the form of succession.

**क्रम - krama**

Sequence.

**रूप - rūpa**

Form.

Thus:

**क्रमरूप - kramarūpa**

Sequential in nature.

### Kāla - काल

Time.

Accusative singular:

**कालम् - kālam**.

### Bahiryojanā - बहिर्योजना

An outward projection or arrangement.

**बहिः - bahiḥ**

Outside, outwardly.

**योजना - yojanā**

Arrangement, placing, planning, projection.

Related to √**युज् - yuj**, to join or arrange.

### Ullāsayantyaḥ - उल्लासयन्त्यः

Causing to shine forth, unfold or expand.

Related to:

**उल्लास - ullāsa**

Joyful manifestation, expansion, shining forth.

The feminine nominative plural participle:

**उल्लासयन्त्यः - ullāsayantyaḥ**.

Again agreeing with the Śaktis.

Now the philosophical meaning is clear.

The divine powers differentiate activities.

Their manifestation creates an intelligible succession.

We apprehend something beginning.

Continuing.

Changing.

Ending.

And through those distinctions, temporal order appears.

### Time isn't an external foreman

Imagine an independent cosmic clock standing outside Śiva.

It points at the Supreme Lord and says:

"Right, Bhairava. At 9:00 AM you may create the universe."

"At 9:15, maintain it."

"At 9:30, withdraw it."

"You're late on the quarterly creation schedule."

That would make the supreme conscious freedom dependent upon an external temporal authority.

But that's not Abhinavagupta's doctrine.

Time itself is a manifested order.

It belongs to the differentiation of divine activity.

Now, that doesn't deny that ordinary events have temporal relations.

The clock still works.

The tea still cools.

The Sun still rises according to physical processes.

But the ultimate reality of the manifestation is not a second independent conscious substance called Time.

**The Lord manifests temporal differentiation through Śakti; he is not produced by the clock that measures it.**

And now we have a genuinely interesting source distinction.

Because the Kashmir Series editor supplies a quotation from Utpaladeva explaining precisely this relationship.

The quote is authentic.

But its insertion at this location is editorial.

That's the kind of distinction our reading requires.


## 7. Utpaladeva's independently attested explanation - Space from forms, time from actions

EDITORIALLY INSERTED QUOTATION IN THE KASHMIR SERIES · INDEPENDENT ORIGINAL: ĪŚVARAPRATYABHIJÑĀKĀRIKĀ 2.1.5

**मूर्तिवैचित्र्यतो देशक्रममाभासयत्यसौ।**

**क्रियावैचित्र्यनिर्भासात्कालक्रममपीश्वरः॥**

*Mūrtivaicitryato deśakramam ābhāsayaty asau.*

*Kriyāvaicitryanirbhāsāt kālakramam apīśvaraḥ.*

"Through the variety of forms, the Lord manifests spatial order; through the manifestation of the variety of actions, he also manifests temporal order."

This is not a fabricated verse.

It is independently attested as Utpaladeva's *Īśvarapratyabhijñākārikā* 2.1.5, and his accompanying *Vṛtti* explains the distinction between the mutual differentiation of forms and the succession of activities. ; \]

But the textual placement matters.

In our Kashmir Series *Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa*, the verse occurs **inside a square-bracketed editorial expansion**.

So we can say:

Utpaladeva's authentic verse illuminates Abhinavagupta's present argument.

We cannot say:

Abhinavagupta himself explicitly quotes it in the uninterrupted prose of this passage.

Those are different historical claims.

Now let's learn the Sanskrit.

### Mūrti - मूर्ति

Form, embodiment, determinate configuration.

From the *mūrta* family, involving being formed or embodied.

The word here concerns the distinct forms of manifested things.

### Mūrtivaicitrya - मूर्तिवैचित्र्य

**मूर्तिवैचित्र्यतः - mūrtivaicitryataḥ**.

Through or because of the diversity of forms.

### Deśakrama - देशक्रम

**क्रम - krama**

**देशक्रमम् - deśakramam**.

### Ābhāsayati - आभासयति

**√भास् - bhās**

**आभासयति - ābhāsayati**.

Third-person singular.

### Asau - असौ

That one.

Masculine nominative singular, here referring to the Lord.

### Kriyāvaicitrya - क्रियावैचित्र्य

We know both components.

### Nirbhāsa - निर्भास

Manifestation, appearance.

Through the manifestation of different actions.

### Kālakrama - कालक्रम

Temporal sequence.

**काल - kāla**

Time.

Sequence.

**कालक्रम - kālakrama**.

### Īśvaraḥ - ईश्वरः

The Lord.

Nominative singular.

Now the statement is grammatically simple but philosophically enormous.

**The manifestation of different forms gives rise to spatial ordering.**

**The manifestation of different actions gives rise to temporal ordering.**

### The example of a courtyard

Imagine a house.

A courtyard.

A garden.

A tree.

A well.

Different forms are apprehended as occupying different places.

Now the *Īśvarapratyabhijñāvimarśinī* explains the spatial distinction through precisely this sort of differentiated appearance.

The house isn't identical in form to the garden.

The well isn't identical to the courtyard.

Their mutual differentiation constitutes the manifestation of a spatial order.

But this doesn't make space an independently self-revealing thing outside consciousness.

### The example of an action

Now imagine opening a door.

First it's closed.

Then you reach toward it.

Then you open it.

Then it is open.

The action contains distinguishable phases.

Their relation is temporal.

Earlier.

Later.

Before.

After.

Now here's the subtle point.

It isn't enough for a form merely to be different in some static respect to establish temporal succession.

Temporal order concerns the manifest differentiation and relationship of activities or presentations.

And the Pratyabhijñā tradition makes that distinction explicit.

### The Lord isn't located in a universal box

Imagine space and time as two enormous containers.

One contains all objects.

The other contains all events.

Then imagine Śiva standing outside both.

What contains Śiva?

Another larger space?

And when did that larger space begin?

An earlier time?

The regress returns.

Abhinavagupta's system avoids making space and time independently ultimate containers.

Their intelligible forms belong to the manifestation of consciousness.

**Bhairava doesn't live inside space and time as though they were independently existing landlords.**

The powers through which space and time appear belong to him.

Now the verse doesn't itself prove the entire ontology.

It expresses the Pratyabhijñā position with extraordinary precision.

And it's exactly the doctrine Abhinavagupta is deploying in the present passage.

But here's where things become even more interesting.

The Śaktis don't simply project space and time outward.

They also absorb them back into themselves.

So the universe is not merely being emitted.

It's being swallowed.

Again.

The cosmic digestive system is back.


## 8. स्वात्मनि युञ्जानत्वेन ग्रसमानाः - Space and time are swallowed back into the Self

We now cross from Kashmir Series p. 204 to p. 205.

The main Sanskrit says:

**स्वात्मनि युञ्जानत्वेन ग्रसमानाः।**

*Svātmani yuñjānatvena grasamānāḥ.*

"Swallowing $$those manifestations$$ by joining them into their own Self."

Now, that is a striking continuation.

We've just established that differentiated cognition manifests spatial relations, and differentiated action manifests temporal sequence.

But those manifestations do not become independent of consciousness.

The same divine powers gather them back.

Let's dismantle the Sanskrit.

### Svātmani - स्वात्मनि

In one's own Self.

**स्व - sva**

Own.

**आत्मन् - ātman**

Self.

Locative singular:

**आत्मनि - ātmani**.

Thus:

**स्वात्मनि - svātmani**.

Notice the ending **-ani**.

This is a useful feature of the *ātman* declension.

### Yuñjāna - युञ्जान

Joining, uniting, bringing into relation.

From √**युज् - yuj**, to join.

The present middle participle:

**युञ्जान - yuñjāna**.

Now the abstract:

**युञ्जानत्व - yuñjānatva**

The condition of joining.

Instrumental:

**युञ्जानत्वेन - yuñjānatvena**.

Through the condition of uniting.

### Grasamānāḥ - ग्रसमानाः

Swallowing, consuming, engulfing.

From √**ग्रस् - gras**, to swallow.

The present middle participle:

**ग्रसमान - grasamāna**.

Feminine nominative plural:

**ग्रसमानाः - grasamānāḥ**.

It agrees with the divine powers.

Now here's the philosophical structure.

The Śaktis manifest differentiated spatial and temporal appearances.

Then they are said to swallow those manifestations through their intrinsic unity with consciousness.

But this is not simply a chronological claim that every object must physically disappear immediately after becoming known.

It concerns the relationship between manifest differentiation and the conscious ground.

### What does swallowing mean here?

Think about the recognition of an object.

You see a pot.

You distinguish its shape.

You recognize where it is.

You think about what you might do with it.

Then you understand:

"That is a pot."

The object has been apprehended through a conscious relation.

In the interpretation supplied by the tradition, the cognition can be considered as an assimilation of the apparently external content into the knowing subject's awareness.

The object doesn't necessarily vanish from the room.

But the cognitive event has brought it into the field of what is known.

This is the ordinary analogy for a deeper nondual claim.

**What appears as an external, differentiated world is never ultimately outside consciousness.**

The swallowing symbolizes recognition of that nonseparateness.

### The universe's terrible eating habits

Imagine the Goddesses doing two jobs simultaneously.

One department projects the universe outward.

"Space."

"Distance."

"Mountains."

"Atoms."

"Action."

"Time."

Another department immediately swallows the whole arrangement back into conscious fullness.

The operations appear opposed.

But they belong to the same power.

And the universe is therefore described through:

**वमन - vamana**

Emission.

And:

**ग्रसन - grasana**

Swallowing.

We've met this pairing in Part XIX.

Now it's applied specifically to the differentiation and reabsorption of space and time.

But the next phrase adds something important.

The powers retain their inward unity **even at the very moment of first manifestation**.

The word is:

**Prollāsa.**

The first exuberant unfolding.

And it's one of the most beautiful terms in Kashmir Śaivism.


## 9. प्रोल्लाससमयेऽपि - Even at the first flowering of manifestation

The main prose continues:

**प्रोल्लाससमयेऽपि।**

*Prollāsasamaye 'pi.*

"Even at the moment of the first unfolding."

Now the Kashmir Series inserts a very large editorial explanation here.

And its opening is beautiful.

It describes the supreme Anuttara-reality as resembling a **waveless ocean**.

EDITOR'S SQUARE-BRACKETED EXPLANATION · NOT ABHINAVAGUPTA'S CONTINUOUS PROSE

**निस्तरङ्गजलधिप्रख्येऽनुत्तरात्मनि।**

*Nistaraṅgajaladhiprakhye 'nuttarātmani.*

"In the Anuttara Self, comparable to a waveless ocean."

The gloss explains that the first manifestation of the supreme *Aham*-apprehension shines forth before any separate object has become explicitly differentiated.

That's an illuminating explanation.

But it's the **editorial interpretation**, not a sentence we should uncritically attribute to Abhinavagupta at this exact point. ; \]

Let's learn the Sanskrit.

### Nistaraṅga - निस्तरङ्ग

Without waves.

**निस् - nis**

Without.

**तरङ्ग - taraṅga**

Wave.

Thus:

**निस्तरङ्ग - nistaraṅga**

Waveless.

### Jaladhi - जलधि

Ocean, literally a holder of waters.

**जल - jala**

Water.

**धि - dhi**

A holding or placing element in the compound's established interpretation.

Thus:

**जलधि - jaladhi**

Ocean.

### Prakhya - प्रख्य

Resembling, comparable to, appearing like.

So:

**निस्तरङ्गजलधिप्रख्य - nistaraṅgajaladhiprakhya**

Like a waveless ocean.

Now here's the beautiful image.

Before an individual wave appears, the ocean isn't absent.

A wave is a distinguishable formation of the ocean.

But the existence of a wave doesn't introduce a second independent substance unrelated to water.

Now the metaphor has limits.

Consciousness isn't literally H₂O.

And an ordinary ocean is a physical aggregate with parts.

But the image helps explain how differentiated manifestation need not be independent of its ground.

### Prollāsa - प्रोल्लास

A vigorous shining forth, swelling, joyous unfolding.

Related to:

**प्र - pra**

Forth.

**उल्लास - ullāsa**

Expansion, delight, illumination.

Thus:

**प्रोल्लास - prollāsa**

The exuberant emergence or first unfolding.

Locative singular compound:

**प्रोल्लाससमये - prollāsasamaye**

At the time of the first unfolding.

And:

**अपि - api**

Even.

### The important philosophical distinction

Don't imagine *prollāsa* as an event occurring at 00:00:00 on an independent universal clock.

The preceding argument has explicitly made time part of manifestation.

The "first" here concerns a logical and contemplative ordering of the powers.

The supreme reality isn't constituted by an earlier moment in which it lacked consciousness and a later moment in which it finally acquired it.

The freedom is intrinsic.

The unfolding is its manifestation.

Now, the editor expands the point through an example of ordinary cognition.

And the explanation is too valuable to ignore.

But it must be labelled correctly.

Because the four stages we're about to study are named in **Abhinavagupta's own prose**, while the detailed pot-like example is supplied in the square brackets.

That's the crucial distinction.


## 10. उद्योग, अवभास, संक्रम, विलापन - The four moments of knowing

Now comes the fourfold structure.

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN PROSE · KASHMIR SERIES PP. 205-206

**उद्योगावभाससङ्क्रामविलापनरूपेण।**

*Udyogāvabhāsasaṃkrāmavilāpanarūpeṇa.*

"Having the form of initiative, manifestation, transition and dissolution."

These four names are genuinely present in Abhinavagupta's main Sanskrit sentence.

But the detailed definitions that occupy most of p. 205 belong to the editor's square-bracketed explanation.

The distinction is worth keeping in mind as we study them.

THE FOUR MODES OF COGNITIVE MANIFESTATION

## उद्योग

*Udyoga*

Orientation or initiative toward revealing an object

## अवभास

*Avabhāsa*

The object becomes manifest or apparent

## संक्रम

*Saṃkrāma*

Transition, assimilation or inward appropriation of the apprehended content

## विलापन

*Vilāpana*

Dissolution of the object's apparent externality into self-repose

Now let's actually learn the Sanskrit rather than simply memorizing four translations.

### Udyoga - उद्योग

**उद् - ud**

Up, outward, forth.

**योग - yoga**

Joining, application or exertion, from √**युज् - yuj**.

The established noun:

**उद्योग - udyoga**

Effort, undertaking, initiative, active orientation.

In the present context, it expresses the first movement toward manifestation of an object.

Now the editor gives a particularly useful gloss:

SQUARE-BRACKETED EDITORIAL DEFINITION

**उद्योगोऽर्थावविभासयिषा।**

*Udyogo 'rthāvavibhāsayiṣā.*

"Udyoga is the wish to make an object manifest."

The critical word is:

**अवविभासयिषा - avavibhāsayiṣā**

The desire to reveal or cause to appear.

It's a desiderative formation associated with *avabhāsana*, manifestation.

Now that is a beautiful distinction.

Before the object becomes explicitly apparent, there is already an orientation toward its disclosure.

**The will to know is itself an operative power.**

Remember Somānanda from Part XV?

We learned that willing, knowing and acting are distinguishable yet inseparable divine powers.

Now we're encountering a corresponding structure within the individual act of cognition.

### Avabhāsa - अवभास

Manifestation, appearance, shining forth.

From:

**अव - ava**

Down, toward, in the verbal compound.

**√भास् - bhās**

To shine or appear.

Thus:

**अवभास - avabhāsa**

Appearance.

This is the second movement.

The object becomes present.

Something appears as:

"That."

"This."

"Blue."

"Pot."

"Pleasure."

Now, don't assume that every perceptual episode always contains a separately observable instant of pure objectless will followed by a second instant of fully developed visual perception.

The fourfold scheme is a philosophical analysis of cognitive manifestation.

The stages need not correspond one-to-one with four experimentally separable brain events.

### Saṃkrāma - संक्रम

Transition, passage, transfer.

From:

**सम् - sam**

Together.

**√क्रम् - kram**

To step or proceed.

Thus:

**संक्रम - saṃkrama**.

This concerns the movement through which an apprehended object is brought into a fuller relation with knowing.

The editor's expansion describes the object being considered, determined, savoured or appropriated by consciousness.

Now here's an important textual nuance.

Some explanatory traditions use:

**चर्वणा - carvaṇā**

Tasting, relishing or savouring.

That captures an aspect of the process.

But the unbracketed Sanskrit here actually says:

**सङ्क्राम - saṃkrāma**.

We should preserve that word.

It emphasizes transition and passage.

The relishing interpretation is useful but not identical in morphology.

### Vilāpana - विलापन

Dissolution, melting away, causing to disappear.

Related to the verbal family of *vi-√lī*, to dissolve or become absorbed, with the relevant causative formation.

Thus:

**विलापन - vilāpana**

Dissolution.

This is the fourth movement.

The object that appeared as distinct is gathered into the knowing subject's own repose.

Now, again, we're not saying the physical object must cease existing whenever you finish looking at it.

The claim concerns the cognitive and metaphysical relation.

The object is no longer merely an unresolved external presentation.

It has been apprehended.

And the experience can find repose within awareness.

### The pot goes through four departments

Imagine seeing a pot.

**Udyoga:** "What is that?"

**Avabhāsa:** "Something is appearing before me."

**Saṃkrāma:** "Ah, it's a pot. I understand its shape and function."

**Vilāpana:** "The object is now known; the cognitive movement finds repose."

A nice little miniature drama.

And in Abhinavagupta's theology, it expresses the same fundamental movement we've studied at the cosmic level.

Manifestation.

Articulation.

Appropriation.

Return.

Now this is a significant claim.

**Ordinary knowing is interpreted as a miniature expression of the divine process of emission and reabsorption.**

Not because a person creates an entire independent universe every time they see a pot.

But because the structure of cognition resembles the broader pattern of manifestation.

### The four stages are not four unrelated minds

Here's the key.

Udyoga doesn't need one independently existing consciousness.

Avabhāsa another.

Saṃkrāma a third.

Vilāpana a fourth.

The same conscious power can become manifest through distinguishable activities.

And therefore the sequence doesn't fracture its ultimate unity.

That's why Abhinavagupta can speak of the four moments while maintaining the nonsequential nature of the supreme ground.

Now the editor gives an elaborate experiential explanation.

And it's rather good.

So let's read it explicitly as an editorial expansion, instead of pretending it's a new paragraph authored by Abhinavagupta.


## 11. The editor's example - How a knower digests an object

SQUARE-BRACKETED EXPLANATION IN THE KASHMIR SERIES · P. 205

The editor describes an ordinary knower who wants to accomplish some purpose.

He encounters an object through a means of knowledge.

First he apprehends it.

Then determines what it is.

Finally he recognizes that he knows it, and the object's apparent exteriority is dissolved into the Self.

The most useful short phrases are:

**प्रथममालोचयेत्।**

*Prathamam ālocayet.*

"First, one would apprehend or inspect."

**अनन्तरमिदमित्थमिति विकल्पयेत्।**

*Anantaram idam ittham iti vikalpayet.*

"Then one determines, 'This is thus.'"

And:

**ज्ञातोऽयं मयार्थः।**

*Jñāto 'yaṃ mayārthaḥ.*

"This object has been known by me."

Now those are genuinely present in the Kashmir Series.

But all three are **inside the editor's bracket**.

They're not an uninterrupted quotation from Abhinavagupta's original Sanskrit at this location. ; \]

Let's learn them.

### Prathamam - प्रथमम्

First.

An adverbial use of the ordinal:

**प्रथम - prathama**

First.

### Ālocayet - आलोकयेत्

One would look at, examine or apprehend.

From:

**आ - ā**

Toward.

**√लोक् - lok**

To see, behold.

The optative:

**आलोकयेत् - ālocayet**.

This is a particularly useful verb for philosophical texts.

*Ālocana* can denote the initial apprehension or presentation of something before a fuller conceptual determination.

### Anantaram - अनन्तरम्

Afterward, next.

Literally involving the condition of no intervening interval in the established adverbial sense.

### Idam ittham - इदमित्थम्

**इदम् - idam**

This.

**इत्थम् - ittham**

Thus, in this way.

So:

**इदमित्थम् - idam ittham**

### Vikalpayet - विकल्पयेत्

From:

**विकल्प - vikalpa**

Conceptual distinction.

The optative:

**विकल्पयेत् - vikalpayet**.

Now the distinction between *ālocana* and *vikalpa* matters.

The first concerns the object's being apprehended.

The second its conceptual identification.

They're distinguishable, even if in ordinary experience the process can feel rapid or integrated.

### Jñātaḥ - ज्ञातः

Known.

From √**ज्ञा - jñā**, to know.

The past passive participle:

**ज्ञात - jñāta**

Known.

Masculine nominative singular:

**ज्ञातः - jñātaḥ**.

Now:

**अयम् - ayam**

This.

Through visarga sandhi:

**ज्ञातोऽयम् - jñāto 'yam**.

### Mayā - मया

By me.

Instrumental singular of the first-person pronoun.

Now that's a beautiful form.

**अहम् - aham**

I.

**मया - mayā**

By me.

### Arthaḥ - अर्थः

Object, meaning, purpose.

The connected phrase:

**मयार्थः - mayārthaḥ**.

Because:

**mayā + arthaḥ → mayārthaḥ**.

The adjacent *ā* and *a* coalesce.

Now the full statement:

**ज्ञातोऽयं मयार्थः।**

*Jñāto 'yaṃ mayārthaḥ.*

"This object has been known by me."

That's an excellent sentence to learn.

### Why the example matters

The object initially appears as something to be known.

Then it is apprehended.

Then identified.

Finally, the knower can say:

"I know it."

There is a satisfaction or completion in the act.

Now this doesn't mean every cognition is pleasurable, ethically good or metaphysically liberating.

You can know something distressing.

You can recognize a danger.

You can acquire knowledge that causes grief.

The point concerns the completion of the cognitive act, not necessarily a pleasant emotional state.

And the editor interprets that completion as a form of self-repose.

The object that was initially apprehended as external now belongs to the knower's understood world.

This is the experiential analogy for the divine swallowing of manifestation.

### The crucial distinction

Now here's where it would be easy to overstate the argument.

The object doesn't literally need to be destroyed for understanding to occur.

If you recognize a pot, the pot can remain on the table.

The "swallowing" is a metaphor for the object's cognitive assimilation and the doctrine of nonseparateness from consciousness.

And that's why this example is philosophically useful.

It's not a denial of the practical distinction between a pot and its cognition.

It is an account of how a differentiated presentation finds its place within awareness.

### The editor's extraordinary sacrifice quotation

The same bracket includes a verse attributed to Utpaladeva, beginning:

**सर्वभावमयभावमण्डलं...**

*Sarvabhāvamayabhāvamaṇḍalaṃ...*

It describes the whole field of beings offered in a universal sacrifice involving Śakti.

The theological image is beautiful.

Every object of experience becomes an offering within the universal field of consciousness.

But the textual point is essential:

**The verse is inserted by the Kashmir Series editor as a supporting illustration. It is not quoted in Abhinavagupta's uninterrupted prose here.**

The main commentary returns from that long bracket directly to the distinction between a twelvefold lean condition and a sixteenfold full condition.

And now the Sun and Moon enter.

Not simply as astronomical objects.

But as different organizations of the powers of knowing.


## 12. द्वादशात्मिका कृशरूपता - The twelvefold lean condition

Twelve.

And sixteen.

A comparatively lean condition.

And a full condition.

The Sun.

And the Moon.

Let's learn the terms.

Emptiness, lack.

### Kṛśa - कृश

**√श्रि - śri**

To resort to, take refuge in.

**आश्रयन्त्यः - āśrayantyaḥ**.

Feminine nominative plural.

Again referring to the Śaktis.

Now the important philosophical meaning.

The same divine powers can manifest a twelvefold, comparatively lean configuration.

And the four moments of cognition help express the differentiated operation.

### Why twelve?

The editor's bracket explains the solar configuration through twelve vowel-powers, excluding four traditionally designated **ṣaṇḍha** vowels from the expanded sixteenfold scheme.

The relevant four are the vocalic *ṛ*, long *ṝ*, *ḷ* and its extended counterpart in the traditional sacred inventory.

That gives the familiar contrast:

**Sixteen vowel-powers in the fuller enumeration.**

**Twelve in the restricted solar grouping.**

But the detailed assignment of the twelvefold grouping to the *pramāṇa*-Sun occurs in the explanatory bracket.

The unbracketed prose itself gives the **twelvefold lean condition**, followed by the **sixteenfold full condition**.

We should not conflate the two textual levels.

### The Sun as a power of cognition

Within the editor's explanation, the Sun represents:

**प्रमाण - pramāṇa**

A means of cognition.

Now remember the epistemological triad:

**प्रमाता - pramātā**

Knower.

**प्रमाण - pramāṇa**

Means of knowing.

**प्रमेय - prameya**

Object to be known.

The solar *pramāṇa* expresses a function through which the object becomes manifest.

And the twelvefold grouping is used to characterize that function.

Now here's the caution.

This is a symbolic and doctrinal arrangement.

It doesn't mean the physical Sun possesses exactly twelve measurable cognitive organs.

Nor does it establish that all human perception literally cycles through twelve neurons.

The point is the theological correspondence.

### The fourfold relation

Now the four moments of cognition appear within the twelvefold structure.

This is where the scheme becomes rich.

Udyoga.

Avabhāsa.

Saṃkrāma.

Vilāpana.

Different modes of the appearance and assimilation of objects.

And the lean solar condition represents one configuration of those powers.

Now the name *kṛśa* is important.

The power is relatively contracted or reduced.

It doesn't possess the full sixteenfold lunar manifestation in the same mode.

But here's the key.

**Relative contraction doesn't imply absence of the supreme conscious ground.**

The same Goddesses are present.

Their mode of manifestation differs.

And that brings us to the full lunar form.

Sixteen.

The Moon.

The nectar.

The blissful completion.

And one of Abhinavagupta's favourite images.

The cosmic swing.

Because a swing moves between two directions while remaining one connected movement.

### The cosmic playground

Full.

Nectar-bearing.

Within the doctrinal system, both modes participate in the manifestation of Śakti.

**The swing expresses divine freedom, not a mechanical limitation imposed upon it.**

### An important orthographic distinction

This is **not** the historical linguistic derivation of the Sanskrit pronoun *aham*.

It's a Tantric phonemic interpretation.

The threefold structure symbolically encompasses the power of manifestation.

The written word serves as a concentrated representation.

Its theological referent is the universal self-revealing consciousness of Bhairava.

## 19. One genuinely troublesome compound we must not pretend is easy

There is one difficult phrase on p. 206 that deserves explicit acknowledgement.

The witnesses transmit a compound corresponding to:

**तद्गृहीतप्रमदादिगतोद्योगादिकलाचतुष्टयपरिपूर्णतयापि।**

*Tadgṛhītapramadādigatodyogādikalācatuṣṭayaparipūrṇatayāpi.*

The secure elements include:

**तद्गृहीत - tadgṛhīta**

Grasped or taken up by that.

**उद्योगादि - udyogādi**

Beginning with *udyoga*.

**कलाचतुष्टय - kalācatuṣṭaya**

A group of four powers.

**परिपूर्णता - paripūrṇatā**

Complete fullness.

**अपि - api**

Even, also.

But the internal segmentation around **pramadādi** is difficult in the available transcriptions. It should not be silently replaced with the clearly different technical words *pramāṇa*, *prameya* or *pramātṛ* merely because those would fit the philosophical context conveniently.

The larger sentence describes the movement from the lean twelvefold configuration toward the full sixteenfold manifestation, in association with the fourfold cognitive structure.

That much is clear.

The exact internal interpretation of this troublesome compound is less secure.

And that's an important lesson in reading Sanskrit texts seriously.

**A coherent philosophical interpretation does not entitle us to rewrite an ambiguous word without evidence.**

The same caution applies to the distinction between **ददती - dadatī** and **दधती - dadhatī** at the final *Aham* statement, and to the substantial square-bracketed explanations inserted between the parts of this unusually long original sentence.

Both the doctrinal argument and the philological uncertainties deserve to remain visible.

## 22. The final insight - Space and time are not the landlords of consciousness

Now this is the philosophical insight I want to retain.

Imagine an ordinary act of knowing.

You look at an object.

It appears somewhere.

You distinguish its form from other things.

Your attention moves toward it.

You apprehend it.

You recognize it.

You act upon what you've understood.

And the cognitive movement comes to a kind of completion.

That ordinary event contains spatial and temporal relations.

The object is here rather than there.

One act occurs before another.

The appearance changes.

The cognition develops.

But Abhinavagupta asks what makes the entire differentiated process manifest.

His answer is the conscious freedom of Śiva.

And therefore space and time cannot be treated as independently ultimate realities standing above that freedom.

Their differentiation belongs to Śakti's manifestation.

This does not mean that ordinary space and time are meaningless.

The difference between Mount Meru and an atom remains intelligible.

The difference between earlier and later remains intelligible.

The difference between a first perception and a subsequent recognition remains intelligible.

But their ultimate ground is not another independently existing consciousness.

Now the really profound move comes through the four stages of cognition.

Udyoga.

Avabhāsa.

Saṃkrāma.

Vilāpana.

The object is approached.

It appears.

It is assimilated.

Its apparent exteriority finds repose within awareness.

This miniature movement reflects the broader cosmic structure.

Manifestation.

Differentiation.

Self-apprehension.

Return.

And the lunar-solar symbolism gives it a sacred phonemic form.

Twelvefold contraction.

Sixteenfold fullness.

The emission and swallowing of powers.

The swing-play of Śakti.

And at the end:

**Aham.**

The entire manifested universe is interpreted through universal I-consciousness.

Not the finite ego making grandiose claims.

Not an individual personality becoming the physical creator of the cosmos.

But the self-revealing ground of experience.

**Space and time are modes in which consciousness manifests differentiated experience; they are not independently ultimate containers that manufacture consciousness.**

That's the claim of the text.

And now the extraordinary thing is that Abhinavagupta ends the whole movement with the first vowel, the generative consonantal powers and Bindu.

Everything gathered into one sacred phonemic expression.

**अहम् - aham.**

The universe has appeared.

Differentiated.

Expanded.

Contracted.

Played between Sun and Moon.

Then returned to the conscious reality that was never fundamentally absent.

And the next passage will ask an even more difficult question.

If the universe is the self-expression of consciousness, what exactly is **Spanda**, the divine movement that isn't merely physical movement from one place to another?

Now we're getting to one of the deepest distinctions in all of Kashmir Śaivism.

Because the Absolute doesn't need to travel somewhere else in order to manifest.

Its freedom is already present.

And the world is the extraordinary unfolding of that freedom.
