# Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa

PART XXVII - The Body as the Heart of Bhairava

*Abhinavagupta has finished sixteen explanations of the divine seed. Now he turns toward the human body and asks a rather dangerous question: what if the very thing you've been treating as a miserable, temporary meat-prison is actually a complete manifestation of Bhairava?*

*Childhood, youth, old age, death and rebirth. Breath flowing in and out. The subtle body's eightfold machinery. The different levels of the knower. Fourteen faculties. Forty principles. Eighty configurations. Fifteen lunar days. Eleven Rudras. Three divisions of time. And somehow, one hundred and twelve mysterious points of the embodied Heart.*

*Then the philosophical argument gets properly scandalous. Wine, bodily secretions, ritual purity, forbidden substances, social custom, religious injunctions and the difference between genuine recognition and merely frightening yourself with your own concepts.*

*And finally, Kālī arrives to devour the very power of limitation.*

Kashmir Series pp. 230-237 · GRETIL/Gnoli pp. 265-267 · From Yoni-vyāpti through the conclusion of the Vijñānakrama


## 1. Previously, in the cosmic madhouse…

Twenty-six installments.

We've somehow travelled from the unsurpassable reality of consciousness to the complete Sanskrit alphabet, the thirty-six *tattvas*, Mātṛkā, divine emission, lunar phases, the supreme *Aham*, and the three phonemic components of Bhairava's Heart:

**सौः - sauḥ.**

Then, in Part XXVI, Abhinavagupta gave sixteen different interpretations of the seed.

And the end of that enormous explanation brought us to this sentence:

**एवं षोडशात्मिका बीजव्याप्तिरुक्ता।**

*Evaṃ ṣoḍaśātmikā bījavyāptir uktā.*

"Thus the sixteenfold pervasion of the seed has been explained."

Wonderful.

Finished.

Surely we can go home.

Absolutely not.

Because the next sentence says:

**योनिव्याप्तिस्तु प्रतिवर्णं प्रागेवोक्ता।**

*Yonivyāptis tu prativarṇaṃ prāg evoktā.*

"But the pervasion of the generative matrix has already been explained for each phoneme."

Now he's going to explain it again.

Through an entirely different arrangement.

And the arrangement is the **human body itself**.

But before we begin, a vital textual warning.

The Kashmir Series contains enormous square-bracketed explanations in this section, especially concerning ritual purity, prohibited substances and passages from other authorities. Somadeva Vasudeva's GRETIL transcription retains Abhinavagupta's main prose without reproducing these long additions. If we attributed the bracketed material to Abhinavagupta, we'd substantially misrepresent his position-particularly the relation between antinomian ritual, personal recognition and adherence to scriptural rules. ; ; \]

There's also a numerical problem.

The passage names fourteen, forty, eighty, fifteen, eleven, three and 112. The transmitted arithmetic isn't a clean set of addends that can simply be summed without further interpretation.

We'll preserve that problem rather than invent a correction.

And one more point: the current unit ends at **“vijñānakramo vistarata uktaḥ”**, the conclusion of this particular knowledge-oriented exposition. The next text begins a fresh phonemic interpretation of the root Tantra's words. That's our stopping point, after the *Sarvācāra*, *Spanda*, *Kramastotra* and ritual-calendar material.

Right.

The seed has been explained.

Now the matrix.

And its first manifestation is your entire bloody biography.


## 2. योनिव्याप्तिः - The pervasion of the generative matrix

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN PROSE · KASHMIR SERIES P. 230

**योनिव्याप्तिस्तु प्रतिवर्णं प्रागेवोक्ता।**

*Yonivyāptis tu prativarṇaṃ prāg evoktā.*

"But the pervasion of the Yoni has already been explained with respect to each phoneme."

Let's take this slowly.

### Yoni - योनि

Womb, source, generative matrix.

Feminine noun.

In the Kaula literature it can refer to an actual bodily locus, a ritual matrix, or the metaphysical source of differentiated manifestation.

The meaning isn't automatically reducible to one of those levels.

Here the immediate context is the complement to the already explained *bījavyāpti*, the pervasion of the seed.

### Vyāpti - व्याप्ति

Pervasion, encompassing, extension.

From:

**वि - vi**

Throughout, apart.

**√आप् - āp**

To reach, attain or pervade in the relevant formation.

Thus:

**व्याप्ति - vyāpti**

Pervasion.

The compound:

**योनिव्याप्ति - yonivyāpti**

Pervasion of the generative matrix.

### Tu - तु

But, however.

Now this is an important particle.

The topic is changing.

The seed-exposition has been completed.

The matrix-exposition remains.

### Prativarṇam - प्रतिवर्णम्

For each phoneme.

**प्रति - prati**

With respect to each, individually.

**वर्ण - varṇa**

Letter or phoneme.

Thus:

**प्रतिवर्णम् - prativarṇam**

Phoneme by phoneme.

### Prāg eva - प्रागेव

Already, previously.

**प्राक् - prāk**

Before.

**एव - eva**

Indeed.

### Uktā - उक्ता

Explained, stated.

Feminine nominative singular past passive participle of √**वच् - vac**, to speak.

It agrees with *yonivyāptiḥ*.

The feminine agreement matters.

The noun *vyāpti* is feminine.

Now the next phrase supplies the new method:

**वर्गीकरणाभिप्रायेण तु निरूपणीया।**

*Vargīkaraṇābhiprāyeṇa tu nirūpaṇīyā.*

"But it is now to be explained with a view to classification."

### Varga - वर्ग

Group, class, category.

### Karaṇa - करण

Making, forming.

From √**कृ - kṛ**, to do.

Thus:

**वर्गीकरण - vargīkaraṇa**

Classification, arranging into groups.

### Abhiprāya - अभिप्राय

Intention, purport, interpretive aim.

Instrumental singular:

**अभिप्रायेण - abhiprāyeṇa**.

Now the whole compound:

**वर्गीकरणाभिप्रायेण - vargīkaraṇābhiprāyeṇa**

With the intention of classifying.

### Nirūpaṇīyā - निरूपणीया

To be explained, defined or determined.

Feminine nominative singular gerundive.

Related to *nir-√rūp*, to determine or characterize.

Now here's the crucial point.

Abhinavagupta isn't saying the earlier interpretation was wrong.

He's changing the **classificatory framework**.

The phoneme-by-phoneme matrix had already been explained.

Now the same pervasive power is going to be understood through:

The living body.

The subtle body.

Vital breath.

The knower.

And the entire hierarchy of embodied manifestation.

### The cosmic classification department

Imagine Mātṛkā arriving at the university.

"I've already explained the letters."

"Excellent."

"Now I'd like to explain the body."

"Anatomy department?"

"Not exactly."

"Psychology?"

"Partly."

"Cosmology?"

"Also."

"Metaphysics?"

"Naturally."

"Can we assign you one departmental code?"

"No."

And that's precisely why the compound *vargīkaraṇābhiprāyeṇa* matters.

The same divine reality can be organized through several classifications.

But we must not mistake the classifications for independently ultimate substances.

**The arrangement changes; the conscious ground does not.**

Now the first classification is wonderfully ordinary.

Childhood.

Youth.

Old age.

And the assumption of another body.

The supreme philosophical system has suddenly become a human life story.


## 3. बाल्ययौवनस्थाविरदेहान्तरग्रहण - Four conditions of embodied existence

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S PROSE · P. 230

The enormous compound begins:

**बाल्ययौवनस्थाविरदेहान्तरग्रहणरूपदशाचतुष्टय…**

*Bālyayauvanasthāviradehāntaragrahaṇarūpadaśācatuṣṭaya…*

"The fourfold series of states consisting of childhood, youth, old age and the assumption of another body…"

Now this is delightful.

After two hundred and thirty pages of cosmic vowels, Abhinavagupta has introduced the familiar problem of getting older.

Even the divine alphabet cannot escape the passage of time at the manifest level.

Let's dissect the compound.

### Bālya - बाल्य

Childhood.

From:

**बाल - bāla**

Child, young.

With an abstract formation:

**बाल्य - bālya**

Childhood.

### Yauvana - यौवन

Youth, youthful condition.

Related to:

**युवन् - yuvan**

Young person.

Thus:

**यौवन - yauvana**

Youth.

### Sthāvira - स्थाविर

Old, aged, senior.

Related to:

**स्थविर - sthavira**

Elderly, venerable elder.

The form in the compound is:

**स्थाविर - sthāvira**.

Now this is worth noting.

It isn't simply the word *vṛddha*, old.

Abhinavagupta uses the *sthāvira* family.

### Dehāntara - देहान्तर

**देह - deha**

Body.

**अन्तर - antara**

Other, different.

Thus:

**देहान्तर - dehāntara**

Another body.

### Grahaṇa - ग्रहण

Taking, grasping, acquiring.

From √**ग्रह् - grah**, to grasp.

Thus:

**देहान्तरग्रहण - dehāntaragrahaṇa**

The assumption or taking of another body.

Now here's the theological context.

This refers to transmigration or rebirth as understood within the tradition.

It isn't simply a euphemism for someone's birthday.

Nor does the passage independently demonstrate rebirth as an empirically established fact.

It's part of the Śaiva account of embodied existence.

### Daśā - दशा

State, condition, stage.

Now don't confuse this with:

**दश - daśa**

Ten.

Here the long final **ā** makes it a different noun.

### Catuṣṭaya - चतुष्टय

Set of four.

From:

**चतुर् - catur**

Four.

With the collective suffix:

**-ष्टय - -ṣṭaya**.

Thus:

**चतुष्टय - catuṣṭaya**

A fourfold group.

### Samāhāra - समाहार

Gathering together, totality, collection.

From *sam-ā-√hṛ*, to bring together.

Now the complete sense:

**A composite encompassing four stages of embodied existence.**

Childhood.

Youth.

Old age.

Taking another body.

### Why no separate adulthood?

The verse isn't presenting a modern demographic classification.

It's making a fourfold doctrinal arrangement.

Youth includes the relevant mature embodied condition, while the fourth stage is the transition to another body.

We shouldn't correct the Sanskrit by inventing an additional category.

### The philosophical meaning

Now here's the subtle point.

The body is not a single frozen object.

It appears through changing conditions.

A child becomes a youth.

A youth becomes old.

The tradition then describes the continuation of the embodied process through the assumption of another form.

Yet all of these stages belong to the manifestation of consciousness.

Abhinavagupta is not saying the body remains physically unchanged.

Quite the opposite.

The body's changing conditions are precisely what make it useful as an expression of differentiated Śakti.

### The cosmic ageing department

Imagine the individual arriving at birth.

"Congratulations. You have a body."

"Wonderful."

"Stage one: childhood."

"Fine."

"Stage two: youth."

"Excellent."

"Stage three: old age."

"Excuse me?"

"Stage four: another body."

"Can I opt out?"

"That is precisely why the doctrine of liberation is being discussed."

A rather grim customer-service exchange.

But the philosophical issue is serious.

**The finite bodily condition changes; the ultimate nature of consciousness is not defined by one temporary bodily stage.**

And this brings us directly to the material basis of the body.

The five great elements.


## 4. पाञ्चभौतिकम् - The body is a five-element manifestation

The next word is:

**पाञ्चभौतिकम्**

*Pāñcabhautikam.*

"Composed of the five elements."

Now let's learn the morphology.

### Pañca - पञ्च

Five.

### Bhūta - भूत

Being, existent, element.

From √**भू - bhū**, to become.

In the five-element classification:

**पृथिवी - pṛthivī:** Earth.

**आपस् - āpas:** Water.

**तेजस् - tejas:** Fire or radiance.

**वायु - vāyu:** Air.

**आकाश - ākāśa:** Space.

Now:

**पाञ्चभौतिक - pāñcabhautika**

Pertaining to or constituted by the five elements.

Neuter singular:

**पाञ्चभौतिकम् - pāñcabhautikam**.

### The crucial distinction

This is a traditional cosmological classification.

It is not the modern periodic table.

Earth doesn't mean only the chemical element silicon.

Fire isn't simply oxygen combustion.

And space is not one more ordinary material substance in the same scientific sense as water.

The classification belongs to a theory of differentiated manifestation involving sensory qualities and elemental principles.

### Why the body matters

The body is constituted through the elemental manifestation.

It changes through time.

It possesses sensory and action faculties.

It participates in the movement of breath.

And it is related to the subtle body and the conscious subject.

The body therefore offers a concrete locus in which the wider metaphysics of Śakti can be contemplated.

### The body isn't an embarrassing exception

Now think about a possible misunderstanding.

One might assume:

"The supreme reality is spiritual. The body is merely material. Therefore the body must be entirely outside the divine."

Abhinavagupta's ontology doesn't permit that simple separation.

The embodied form belongs to the manifested field.

And the manifested field is grounded in consciousness.

That doesn't make every bodily impulse automatically wise.

Nor does it abolish the need for ethical judgment.

But it means the body isn't an independently existing metaphysical enemy of the Heart.

**Embodiment can be approached as a manifestation of Śakti rather than a substance fundamentally outside Bhairava.**

Now the text adds the movement that makes the body visibly alive.

Breath.

Inward and outward.

Prāṇa and Apāna.

And the relation between the two is described through the same *tithīśānta* we've been studying in the sacred phonemic seed.

The body is about to become the alphabet again.


## 5. प्राणापान - The two movements of vital breath

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN PROSE · KASHMIR SERIES P. 230

The commentary describes the body as joined with:

**प्रवेशनिर्गमनात्मना प्राणापानरूपेण।**

*Praveśanirgamanātmanā prāṇāpānarūpeṇa.*

"That whose nature is entering and departing, in the form of Prāṇa and Apāna."

Now this is an exceptionally important expression.

Because it connects the root Tantra's lunar and phonemic terminology with a living process.

Breathing.

Let's learn the components.

### Praveśa - प्रवेश

Entry, entering.

From:

**प्र - pra**

Forth, into.

**√विश् - viś**

To enter.

Thus:

**प्रवेश - praveśa**.

### Nirgamaṇa - निर्गमन

Going out, departure.

From:

**निर् - nir**

Out.

**√गम् - gam**

To go.

The action noun:

**निर्गमन - nirgamaṇa**.

Thus:

**प्रवेशनिर्गमन - praveśanirgamaṇa**

Entering and departing.

### Ātman - आत्मन्

Self, nature.

Instrumental singular:

**आत्मना - ātmanā**.

So:

**प्रवेशनिर्गमनात्मना - praveśanirgamanātmanā**

Having the nature of entry and departure.

### Prāṇa - प्राण

Vital breath, life-force.

Related to *pra-√an*, to breathe.

Now we should distinguish traditional *prāṇa* from modern respiratory physiology.

Ordinary breathing is certainly relevant.

But the Sanskrit term participates in a wider theory of vital functions.

### Apāna - अपान

A downward or outward-moving vital function within traditional Indian physiology.

Related to *apa-√an*, breathing away.

In many yogic contexts it is associated with elimination and descending vital movement.

But here the pair is interpreted through entry and exit.

The exact mapping of physical inspiration and expiration should not be assumed to exhaust all traditional uses of these terms.

### Prāṇāpāna - प्राणापान

Prāṇa and Apāna.

The sandhi:

*prāṇa + apāna → prāṇāpāna.*

Because:

**a + a → ā**.

### Rūpa - रूप

Form, nature.

Instrumental singular:

**रूपेण - rūpeṇa**.

Thus:

**प्राणापानरूपेण - prāṇāpānarūpeṇa**

In the form of Prāṇa and Apāna.

### The relation to Tithīśānta

The preceding phrase invokes:

**तिथीशान्त - tithīśānta**

The terminal or governing element associated with the lunar days.

Now this is fascinating.

The same phonemic-lunar principle previously interpreted through the divine seed is brought into relation with the body's vital movement.

That is a doctrinal correspondence.

Not the claim that normal breathing literally changes the astronomical Moon's orbital position.

### The embodied Visarga

Recall:

**विसर्ग - visarga**

Emission.

The movement out.

And the inward return.

Now the body is described through the continuous alternation of entry and exit.

Breath becomes an especially compelling expression of this process.

Something is received.

Something is released.

And the entire pattern participates in the body's living manifestation.

### The cosmic respiratory department

Imagine the universe running a breathing exercise.

"Prāṇa."

"In."

"Apāna."

"Out."

"And what is the supreme ground of the movement?"

"Consciousness."

"Does consciousness physically travel out through the nose every time I exhale?"

"No."

"Then what is the relation?"

"The vital process is a manifestation in which the power of emission and return can be contemplated."

Excellent.

The metaphysics remains intact.

And the physiology hasn't been mistaken for an independent Absolute.

### A practical distinction

This passage describes traditional vital principles. It does not give a breath-retention schedule, a safe ratio for *kumbhaka*, or instructions to suppress breathing.

We should not invent a respiratory practice from the metaphysical description.

The relevant teaching is about **the body's vital movement as an expression of the Heart**.

And now Abhinavagupta brings in something even subtler.

The *puryaṣṭaka*.

The eightfold city of the subtle body.

The machinery through which embodied identity continues beyond gross bodily change in the tradition's account.


## 6. पुर्यष्टक - The eightfold subtle city

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S PROSE · PP. 230-231

The text says:

**तृतीयं च पुर्यष्टकात्म।**

*Tṛtīyaṃ ca puryaṣṭakātma.*

"And the third has the nature of the eightfold subtle city."

Now this is one of the most useful words in Kashmir Śaivism.

Because it connects the subtle body with the continuity of finite experience.

### Pur - पुर्

City, enclosure, inhabited place.

Now:

**पुर् - pur**

A city.

This becomes important in Sanskrit compounds referring to the body as an inhabited city.

### Aṣṭaka - अष्टक

A group of eight.

From:

**अष्ट - aṣṭa**

Eight.

The collective formation:

**अष्टक - aṣṭaka**.

Thus:

**पुर्यष्टक - puryaṣṭaka**

An eightfold city or subtle complex.

Why **pury-**?

Because the stem *pur* appears in this compound with a glide before the following vowel.

The written form isn't simply *puraṣṭaka*.

### What are its eight constituents?

In a common Śaiva enumeration, the *puryaṣṭaka* comprises the five subtle sensory elements, together with mind, intellect and ego-function:

- Five *tanmātras*.
- *Manas*.
- *Buddhi*.
- *Ahaṃkāra*.

But it's important that **this immediate sentence names the puryaṣṭaka without independently listing its eight members**. Different traditional discussions may organize subtle-body components differently.

The enumeration above is contextual explanation, not a quotation from this line.

### Tanmātra - तन्मात्र

Subtle element or sensory potential.

**तत् - tat**

That.

**मात्र - mātra**

Only, mere.

Thus:

**तन्मात्र - tanmātra**

A subtle qualitative principle.

The traditional five concern sound, touch, form, taste and smell.

### Manas - मनस्

Mind.

The coordinating mental faculty.

### Buddhi - बुद्धि

Intellect, determinative cognition.

From √**बुध् - budh**, to know or awaken.

### Ahaṃkāra - अहङ्कार

The I-making principle.

**अहम् - aham**

I.

**कार - kāra**

Making, producing.

Thus:

**अहङ्कार - ahaṃkāra**

I-making or ego-function.

Now don't confuse *ahaṃkāra* with the supreme **Aham**.

The former is a differentiated principle associated with finite identification.

The latter is interpreted in Pratyabhijñā as universal I-consciousness.

That's an extremely important distinction.

### Why the subtle city matters

Imagine an embodied subject.

They perceive.

Remember.

Desire.

Recognize objects.

Construct an identity.

And perform actions.

The subtle-body theory provides a way to describe the continuity and organization of those functions beyond the gross physical elements.

Now this is a traditional metaphysical model, not an independently established anatomical map.

But within Trika, it plays an important role in explaining embodiment and transmigration.

### The city that thinks it's the king

Imagine the *puryaṣṭaka* as a city.

Five sensory departments.

Mind coordinating traffic.

Buddhi issuing judgments.

Ahaṃkāra announcing:

"I am the sovereign ruler of the universe."

The other departments applaud.

Then universal consciousness asks:

"Through what are all these operations manifest?"

The entire city goes quiet.

Ahaṃkāra suddenly discovers it may not be the Supreme.

An uncomfortable morning.

But that's the philosophical distinction.

**The subtle functions are manifestations within consciousness; they do not constitute an independently ultimate conscious source.**

Now the commentary adds the fourth major term.

**Śūnya.**

The void.

And it identifies the remaining Heart-principle through Śakti.

The embodied hierarchy is becoming a set of five levels.


## 7. शून्यम् - Why Brahma becomes the void

The text continues:

**ब्रह्म बृहत्त्वाच्च शून्यम्।**

*Brahma bṛhattvāc ca śūnyam.*

"And Brahma, because of its vastness, is the void."

Now this is remarkable.

The word *brahma* has already been interpreted through expansion and greatness.

Here the same notion becomes connected with *śūnya*.

Let's unpack it.

### Brahma - ब्रह्म

The expansive or supreme principle.

Neuter noun.

Do not confuse with the masculine creator-god **Brahmā**.

### Bṛhattva - बृहत्त्व

Greatness, magnitude, vastness.

From:

**बृहत् - bṛhat**

Great, expansive.

**-त्व - -tva**

Abstract condition.

Thus:

**बृहत्त्व - bṛhattva**

The condition of greatness.

### Bṛhattvāt - बृहत्त्वात्

Because of vastness.

Ablative singular.

### Śūnya - शून्य

Void, empty, open, free of particular content.

Now here's the philosophical difficulty.

Does *śūnya* mean absolute nonexistence?

Not necessarily.

In this setting it can designate a level of undifferentiated or seemingly empty manifestation.

The word's technical use must be distinguished from a blanket denial of conscious reality.

### The problem of the void

Suppose someone enters a contemplative state without ordinary object-content.

They report:

"Nothing appeared."

Now that is a particular experiential description.

But the absence of distinguishable objects does not by itself establish that awareness has become nonexistent.

And conversely, such a state isn't automatically identical with the supreme Bhairava-consciousness.

Remember Part XXVI.

Abhinavagupta discussed profound immobility and void-like absorption.

But the highest reality is not simply inert blankness.

### The next phrase: Śaktyātma

**अत्र च यद्धृदयं शक्त्यात्म।**

*Atra ca yad dhṛdayaṃ śaktyātma.*

"And here, what is the Heart has Śakti as its nature."

Now the structure is becoming clear.

Gross body.

Vital breath.

Subtle body.

Void.

Śakti as the Heart.

But the whole series remains grounded in conscious Bhairava.

### Śaktyātman - शक्त्यात्मन्

**शक्ति - śakti**

Power.

**आत्मन् - ātman**

Nature, Self.

Thus:

**शक्त्यात्मन् - śaktyātman**

Having Śakti as its nature.

### The central distinction

The text isn't presenting the void as a second Absolute standing above consciousness.

Nor is Śakti an external substance introduced after the void.

Rather, the classification expresses different levels of manifestation and apprehension.

The highest Heart is the conscious Śakti through which the differentiated levels become intelligible.

### The cosmic emptiness inspector

Imagine a yogin saying:

"I experienced a vast void."

"Excellent."

"Therefore I have attained the supreme reality."

"How do you know?"

"Because there were no objects."

"But is the absence of objects identical with recognition of self-revealing consciousness?"

The yogin becomes irritated.

This is an extremely inconvenient question.

But it is exactly the kind of distinction Abhinavagupta insists upon.

**Nonduality isn't simply the absence of manifested content.**

It concerns the conscious ground of both presence and absence.

Now the commentary gathers the entire series into one enormous compound.

And gives five corresponding names for the knowing subject.

This is the key to the whole Yoni-vyāpti exposition.


## 8. शरीर-प्राण-पुर्यष्टक-शून्य-तुर्यशक्ति - Five levels of the knowing subject

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN PROSE · P. 231

The critical sequence is:

**शरीरप्राणपुर्यष्टकशून्यतुर्यशक्तिरूपाः।**

### Śarīra - शरीर

Body.

### Prāṇa - प्राण

Vital breath.

### Puryaṣṭaka - पुर्यष्टक

Void.

Power.

Form, nature.

Plural adjective:

**Manifest differentiation proceeds through the graduated condensation of a conscious power.**

**व्यपदेश्याः - vyapadeśyāḥ**.

Knowers.

### Sampradāya - सम्प्रदाय

### Yukti - युक्ति

Instrumental:

### The body as a divine map

Imagine somebody looking at the body.

Ageing.

Thought.

Movement.

So our task is textual interpretation, not reconstruction of a potentially unsafe ritual.

And this is where Abhinavagupta gives one of the clearest scriptural statements of the problem:

**Ignorance generates doubt.**

**Mantras consist of phonemes.**

**And every phoneme has Śiva as its nature.**

That is the *Sarvācāra* quotation.

The entire preceding twenty-six installments are compressed into two lines.


## 27. वीरव्रतं चाभिनन्देत् - The *Tilaka* and *Bhargaśikhā* authorities

SCRIPTURAL REFERENCES IN ABHINAVAGUPTA'S PROSE · P. 235

After describing the dissolution of constricting doubt, Abhinavagupta mentions the **Śrītilakaśāstra**.

He says that the same principle is taught there.

Then he explicitly quotes another authority:

**श्रीभर्गशिखा - Śrībhargaśikhā**.

The attested fragment is:

**वीरव्रतं चाभिनन्देद्यथायोगं तथाभ्यसेत्।**

*Vīravrataṃ cābhinanded yathāyogaṃ tathābhyaset.*

"One should honour the vow of the Vīra and practise it according to the appropriate conditions."

Now this is important.

The passage hasn't abandoned ritual.

Nor has it announced that every ritual procedure is automatically necessary for every person.

The phrase **yathāyogam** matters.

Let's dismantle it.

### Vīra - वीर

Hero.

Within Kaula traditions, a technical designation for a practitioner with a particular ritual and initiatory status.

### Vrata - व्रत

Vow, observance, established discipline.

Thus:

**वीरव्रत - vīravrata**

Vīra observance or vow.

Accusative singular:

**वीरव्रतम् - vīravratam**.

### Abhinandet - अभिनन्देत्

One should honour, approve, rejoice in.

From:

**अभि - abhi**

Toward.

**√नन्द् - nand**

To rejoice.

The optative:

**अभिनन्देत् - abhinandet**.

### Yathāyogam - यथायोगम्

According to appropriateness, as suitable.

**यथा - yathā**

According to.

**योग - yoga**

Connection, suitability, circumstance.

Thus:

**यथायोगम् - yathāyogam**

As appropriate.

### Abhyaset - अभ्यसेत्

One should practise.

From the verbal family of:

**अभि - abhi**

Repeatedly toward.

**√अस् - as**

To throw or apply, in the formation of *abhyāsa*.

The optative:

**अभ्यसेत् - abhyaset**.

Now the important point.

The quotation speaks of honouring and practising the Vīra's observance appropriately.

It doesn't supply a complete independent manual of that observance.

Nor does it abolish the need for teacher, initiation or contextual rules.

### The difference between a scriptural injunction and an empirical prediction

The statement is normative within its religious framework.

It says what one should practise.

That's different from an empirical claim about what objectively occurs after a measurable action.

And that distinction helps prevent confusion when reading Tantric material.

### The Vīra certification department

Imagine someone reading the phrase:

**Vīravratam abhinandet.**

"Wonderful. I'm now a Vīra."

"Have you studied the appropriate ritual framework?"

"No."

"Received the relevant instruction?"

"No."

"Understood what *yathāyogam* means?"

"That sounds optional."

The teacher points to the text.

It really isn't optional.

**Suitability and context are part of the instruction.**

And now the next authority is the *Sarvācāra*.

Here the doctrine becomes remarkably explicit.

Ignorance gives rise to doubt.

Mantras are constituted by phonemes.

And every phoneme is Śiva-nature.

The entire *Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa* is compressed into a single scriptural sequence.


## 28. अज्ञानाच्छङ्कते मूढः - Ignorance gives rise to doubt

QUOTATION EXPLICITLY ATTRIBUTED TO THE ŚRĪSARVĀCĀRA · PP. 235-236

**अज्ञानाच्छङ्कते मूढस्ततः सृष्टिश्च संहृतिः।**

*Ajñānāc chaṅkate mūḍhas tataḥ sṛṣṭiś ca saṃhṛtiḥ.*

"Through ignorance, the deluded person entertains doubt; from there arise creation and withdrawal."

Now this is the direct scriptural formulation of the preceding philosophical argument.

Let's learn it.

### Ajñāna - अज्ञान

Ignorance.

**अ - a-**

Not.

**ज्ञान - jñāna**

Knowledge.

Ablative singular:

**अज्ञानात् - ajñānāt**.

Because of ignorance.

Now observe the sandhi:

*ajñānāt + śaṅkate → ajñānāc chaṅkate.*

The final dental stop and following palatal consonant undergo the regular consonantal adjustment.

### Śaṅkate - शङ्कते

Doubts, suspects, hesitates apprehensively.

From √**शङ्क् - śaṅk**, to doubt or suspect.

Third-person singular middle voice:

**शङ्कते - śaṅkate**.

### Mūḍha - मूढ

Confused, deluded, bewildered.

From √**मुह् - muh**, to become confused.

Masculine nominative singular:

**मूढः - mūḍhaḥ**.

Now before *tataḥ* the visarga changes according to the connected phonetic environment.

### Tataḥ - ततः

Then, from that, consequently.

Now the verse connects ignorance, doubt and the differentiated cycle.

### Sṛṣṭi - सृष्टि

Creation, emission.

### Saṃhṛti - संहृति

Withdrawal, reabsorption.

Related to *sam-√hṛ*, to gather together.

Now here's the important interpretive distinction.

The verse does not necessarily mean the supreme cosmic creation literally begins each time one individual experiences uncertainty.

It is describing the relation between ignorance, constricting differentiation and the cycle of appearance and withdrawal within the limited mode.

**The finite subject's creation of binding distinctions is the immediate philosophical issue.**

### The doubt that isn't healthy scepticism

Imagine somebody investigating a Sanskrit verse.

"Is this reading correct?"

"Let's compare the manuscripts."

That is careful philology.

Now imagine a finite subject responding:

"I am intrinsically severed from the supreme conscious ground, and my limiting categories are ultimate."

That is the kind of contracted apprehension being challenged.

Both may be called doubt in a very broad English sense.

But their roles are different.

**Inquiry can remove ignorance; constricting *śaṅkā* perpetuates it.**

And the *Sarvācāra* then gives an extraordinary declaration about language itself.


## 29. मन्त्राः वर्णात्मकाः - Every mantra is phonemic, every phoneme Śiva-nature

The next line is:

**मन्त्रा वर्णात्मकाः सर्वे वर्णाः सर्वे शिवात्मकाः।**

*Mantrā varṇātmakāḥ sarve varṇāḥ sarve śivātmakāḥ.*

"All mantras are constituted by phonemes, and all phonemes have Śiva as their nature."

There it is.

The entire ontology of sacred language in one line.

Let's learn it.

### Mantrāḥ - मन्त्राः

Mantras.

Masculine nominative plural.

### Varṇa - वर्ण

Phoneme, letter, sound-category.

### Ātmaka - आत्मक

Having the nature of, consisting of.

Thus:

**वर्णात्मक - varṇātmaka**

Consisting of phonemes.

Masculine nominative plural:

**वर्णात्मकाः - varṇātmakāḥ**.

### Sarve - सर्वे

All.

Masculine nominative plural.

### Śivātmaka - शिवात्मक

Having Śiva as one's nature.

**शिव - śiva**

Śiva.

**आत्मक - ātmaka**

Having the nature of.

Thus:

**शिवात्मक - śivātmaka**.

Plural:

**शिवात्मकाः - śivātmakāḥ**.

Now the philosophical progression is straightforward.

Mantras consist of phonemic powers.

Those powers are understood as manifestations of Śiva.

Therefore mantras are not independent ultimate substances existing outside the supreme conscious ground.

### Does this mean every ordinary word is automatically a liberating mantra?

Not quite.

The line expresses the ultimate Śiva-nature of phonemes.

That doesn't abolish the differences among ordinary language, ritual formulas, initiation and the recognition of the conscious ground.

Remember Part XXIII.

The same powers can participate in bondage or liberation depending upon their mode of manifestation and apprehension.

The divine ground remains.

But the finite knower's recognition can differ.

### The alphabet's theological identity crisis

Imagine the consonant **क - ka** attending a meeting.

"I am a phoneme."

"Yes."

"And a component of mantras."

"Potentially."

"And ultimately Śiva-nature?"

"Within this doctrine, yes."

"Then am I automatically a fully realized teacher?"

"No."

"Why not?"

"Because the metaphysical nature of a phonemic power is not identical with the actual realization of a finite subject who employs it."

The consonant is disappointed.

It had been hoping for a promotion.

But the distinction is essential.

**Ontological nonduality does not erase differences of function, understanding or qualification.**

Now the *Sarvācāra* continues with five-element correspondences.

And these are particularly important because they return directly to the body's material classification.

Water.

Earth.

Fire.

Air.

Space.

The question of pure and impure is mapped across the entire elemental field.


## 30. Water, food, form, touch and space - The *Sarvācāra*'s five-element argument

CONTINUATION OF THE ŚRĪSARVĀCĀRA QUOTATION · P. 236

The next couplet begins:

**पेयापेयं स्मृता आपो भक्ष्याभक्ष्यं तु पार्थिवम्।**

*Peyāpeyaṃ smṛtā āpo bhakṣyābhakṣyaṃ tu pārthivam.*

"What is drinkable and undrinkable belongs to water; what is edible and inedible belongs to the earthly."

The exact grammar is somewhat compressed, but the categorical contrast is clear.

Now the Sanskrit is beautiful because of the paired opposites.

### Peya - पेय

Drinkable, to be drunk.

From √**पा - pā**, to drink.

The gerundive:

**पेय - peya**.

### Apeya - अपेय

Not to be drunk.

Negative prefix:

**अ - a-**.

Thus:

**पेयापेय - peyāpeya**

Drinkable and undrinkable.

Now the phrase doesn't mean every liquid is safe.

It means the traditional conceptual distinction is articulated within the elemental field of water.

### Āpas - आपस्

Waters.

A plural-form noun.

The nominative plural:

**आपः - āpaḥ**.

In the connected form of the verse, the Sanskrit reads *āpo* before the following voiced sound.

### Bhakṣya - भक्ष्य

Edible, to be eaten.

From √**भक्ष् - bhakṣ**, to eat.

### Abhakṣya - अभक्ष्य

Not to be eaten.

Thus:

**भक्ष्याभक्ष्य - bhakṣyābhakṣya**

Edible and inedible.

### Pārthiva - पार्थिव

Earthly, pertaining to earth.

Related to:

**पृथिवी - pṛthivī**

Earth.

Now the statement maps eating distinctions onto the earthly element.

### The form and fire contrast

The verse continues:

**सुरूपं च विरूपं च तत्सर्वं तेज उच्यते।**

*Surūpaṃ ca virūpaṃ ca tat sarvaṃ teja ucyate.*

"What is beautiful in form and what is misshapen-this entire distinction is designated as fire or radiance."

Now the terms.

**सुरूप - surūpa**

Beautifully formed.

**विरूप - virūpa**

Different or irregular in form, misshapen.

**तेजस् - tejas**

Fire, radiance, luminous principle.

The relevant connection is to the visibility of form.

### The touch and air contrast

The next couplet says:

**स्पृश्यास्पृश्यौ स्मृतो वायुश्छिद्रमाकाश उच्यते।**

*Spṛśyāspṛśyau smṛto vāyuś chidram ākāśa ucyate.*

The transmission is grammatically compressed, but the contrast associates touchable and untouchable categories with air, while space is described through opening or interval.

### Spṛśya - स्पृश्य

Touchable.

From √**स्पृश् - spṛś**, to touch.

### Aspṛśya - अस्पृश्य

Not to be touched.

The same negative prefix.

Now here's an important historical distinction.

In some Indian social contexts, *aspṛśya* can acquire caste-related implications.

But within this immediate elemental contrast it concerns a general category of touchability.

We shouldn't automatically import a complete later social institution into every occurrence of the word.

Nor should we ignore the wider historical associations when they become relevant.

### Vāyu - वायु

Air.

### Chidra - छिद्र

Hole, opening, gap.

### Ākāśa - आकाश

Space.

Now the five-element mapping is complete.

Water and drink.

Earth and food.

Fire and visible form.

Air and touch.

Space and openness.

### The philosophical problem

These pairs express how differentiated experience becomes classified.

Drinkable.

Undrinkable.

Edible.

Inedible.

Beautiful.

Ugly.

Touchable.

Untouchable.

But the supreme conscious ground isn't intrinsically split by all the categories the finite subject applies to objects.

That's the point.

### The cosmic five-element restaurant

Imagine a restaurant with five departments.

Water says:

"We classify drinks."

Earth says:

"Food."

Fire says:

"Appearance."

Air says:

"Touch."

Space says:

"I provide the room."

Then a customer announces:

"These distinctions must be independently ultimate realities."

The elements exchange glances.

They're merely differentiated manifestations.

Abhinavagupta's *Sarvācāra* argument isn't denying that food and drink have practical properties.

It's questioning whether the categories of purity and impurity possess independent ultimate being.

### The critical limitation

This is not a license to disregard food safety, consent, medical considerations or applicable law.

The traditional text concerns ritual and ontological categories.

It is not a modern public-health directive.

And the passage's conclusion makes the nondual principle explicit.

Everything is fivefold.

Nothing in the manifest field is entirely outside that framework.

Then it asks:

**How could doubt arise in the Self?**

That is the philosophical climax.


## 31. सर्वं पञ्चात्मकं देवि - Everything is fivefold, O Goddess

CONCLUSION OF THE ŚRĪSARVĀCĀRA QUOTATION · P. 236

**सर्वं पञ्चात्मकं देवि न तेन रहितं क्वचित्।**

*Sarvaṃ pañcātmakaṃ devi na tena rahitaṃ kvacit.*

"Everything is fivefold, O Goddess; nowhere is anything devoid of that."

Now this is the culmination of the elemental argument.

### Sarvam - सर्वम्

Everything.

### Pañcātmaka - पञ्चात्मक

Having the nature of five.

**पञ्च - pañca**

Five.

**आत्मक - ātmaka**

Having the nature of.

Thus:

**पञ्चात्मक - pañcātmaka**

Fivefold in nature.

### Devi - देवि

O Goddess.

Vocative singular.

Now notice the form.

**देवी - devī**

Goddess.

**देवि - devi**

O Goddess.

The final long *ī* becomes short in the vocative singular of this feminine class.

### Rahita - रहित

Devoid of, lacking.

### Kvacit - क्वचित्

Anywhere, in any case.

Now the point is that the manifest field is understood through the five elements.

And therefore the divisions applied to particular objects belong within that manifest arrangement.

### The final question

The quotation ends:

**इच्छामुत्पादयेदात्मा कथं शङ्का विधीयते।**

*Icchām utpādayed ātmā kathaṃ śaṅkā vidhīyate.*

"The Self may produce the power of will; how, then, is doubt to be established?"

Now this is a fascinating sentence.

### Icchā - इच्छा

Will.

Accusative singular:

**इच्छाम् - icchām**.

### Utpādayet - उत्पादयेत्

One might produce, bring forth or cause to arise.

From:

**उद् - ud**

Forth.

**√पद् - pad**

To arise or attain, in the causative formation.

The optative:

**उत्पादयेत् - utpādayet**.

### Ātmā - आत्मा

The Self.

Masculine nominative singular.

### Katham - कथम्

How?

### Śaṅkā - शङ्का

Doubt.

### Vidhīyate - विधीयते

Is established, prescribed, arranged.

From *vi-√dhā*, to arrange or establish.

Third-person singular passive.

Now the philosophical question is beautifully pointed.

If differentiated manifestation arises through the Self's power, why should its own expressions be treated as independently ultimate sources of limitation?

The doubt arises through contracted apprehension.

Not through the creation of another Absolute.

### The essential qualification

This doesn't mean every desire is automatically liberating.

The line identifies *icchā* as a power of the Self.

But ordinary desires can still function through limited identification.

We've studied that distinction repeatedly.

**Divine Icchā and finite craving should not simply be equated without qualification.**

Now the *Sarvācāra* quotation is complete.

And Abhinavagupta says that the same intention is found in another scripture:

**Śrīvīrāvalīśāstra.**

He doesn't provide a substantial separate quotation from it at this exact point.

So we shouldn't invent one.

Instead, he proceeds to a remarkably beautiful hymn.

The *Kramastotra*.

And its central figure is Kālī.

Not simply as a frightening goddess.

But as the power that devours limitation itself.


## 32. सर्वार्थसंकर्षण - Kālī who draws everything into herself

KRAMA STOTRA QUOTATION · KASHMIR SERIES P. 236 · INDEPENDENTLY ATTESTED AS VERSE 4

Now Abhinavagupta quotes the *Kramastotra*.

The verse begins:

**सर्वार्थसंकर्षणसंयमस्य यमस्य यन्तुर्जगतो यमाय।**

*Sarvārthasaṃkarṣaṇasaṃyamasya yamasya yantur jagato yamāya.*

And concludes with homage to Kālī, who draws the very constraining principle into the great devouring.

Now this is remarkable Sanskrit.

And it's one of the most beautiful examples of the Krama tradition's language of **saṃhāra**, withdrawal.

First, an important question of authorship.

This is not automatically Abhinavagupta's **own** *Kramastotra*. Scholarship distinguishes his compositions from the earlier *Kramastotra* on which he wrote the now-lost commentary *Kramakelī*. Alexis Sanderson associates that earlier work with Eraka, while its authorship has been debated. Abhinavagupta explicitly says he has explained the quoted verse in his *Kramakelī*. That establishes his relation to the hymn as commentator; it does not make this verse an original composition of his. ; ; \]

Now let's dismantle the first line.

### Sarva - सर्व

All.

### Artha - अर्थ

Object, referent, thing, meaning.

Thus:

**सर्वार्थ - sarvārtha**

All objects or all things.

### Saṃkarṣaṇa - संकर्षण

Drawing together, pulling inward, withdrawing.

From:

**सम् - sam**

Together.

**√कृष् - kṛṣ**

To draw or pull.

Thus:

**संकर्षण - saṃkarṣaṇa**

Attraction or retraction.

### Saṃyama - संयम

Restraint, control, holding together.

From *sam-√yam*, to restrain.

### Yama - यम

Restraint, regulator, controller.

Also the name of the deity Yama in other contexts.

The Krama poem deliberately exploits the semantic family involving restraint and withdrawal.

### Yantṛ - यन्तृ

Controller, restrainer.

An agent noun related to √**यम् - yam**, to restrain.

Genitive singular:

**यन्तुः - yantuḥ**.

### Jagat - जगत्

World.

Genitive singular:

**जगतः - jagataḥ**.

In the connected verse:

**जगतो - jagato** before the following voiced sound.

### The meaning

The verse constructs a hierarchy of restraining or withdrawing powers.

The manifest objects are drawn together.

The world is restrained.

And Kālī draws even the constraining principle into the supreme devouring.

The whole structure becomes a hymn to the withdrawal of limitation.

### The second line and an important variant

GRETIL and the independently available Sanskrit *Kramastotra* read:

**वपुर्महाग्रासविलासरागात्संकर्षयन्तीं प्रणमामि कालीम्।**

*Vapur mahāgrāsavilāsarāgāt saṃkarṣayantīṃ praṇamāmi kālīm.*

"I bow to Kālī, who draws in the very body or essence of the restraining power, out of her delight in the great devouring."

The Kashmir Series, however, reads **mahāgrāsavilāsasaktam** where the other witnesses have **mahāgrāsavilāsarāgāt**. That is a genuine difference in construction, not merely a difference in spelling. The independently transmitted version supports the *rāgāt* reading, but it would be improper to pretend the Kashmir Series agrees. ; ; \]

Let's learn the terms.

### Vapus - वपुस्

Body, form, essence.

Neuter noun.

The form:

**वपुः - vapuḥ**.

In connected speech before a voiced sound, the final consonant changes according to sandhi.

### Mahā - महा

Great.

### Grāsa - ग्रास

Mouthful, swallowing, devouring.

Related to √**ग्रस् - gras**, to swallow.

Thus:

**महाग्रास - mahāgrāsa**

Great devouring.

### Vilāsa - विलास

Play, sportive manifestation, graceful activity.

### Rāga - राग

Desire, attachment, passionate relish.

Now the ablative:

**रागात् - rāgāt**

Because of desire or delight.

Thus:

**महाग्रासविलासरागात् - mahāgrāsavilāsarāgāt**

From delight in the play of great devouring.

### Saṃkarṣayantīm - संकर्षयन्तीम्

Drawing in, pulling together.

Feminine accusative singular present participle.

It describes Kālī.

### Praṇamāmi - प्रणमामि

I bow.

From:

**प्र - pra**

Forth.

**√नम् - nam**

To bow.

First-person singular present:

**प्रणमामि - praṇamāmi**.

### Kālīm - कालीम्

Kālī.

Feminine accusative singular.

Now the whole line is an act of devotion.

The speaker bows to Kālī.

And Kālī is described through the enormous power of withdrawal.

### The philosophical point

This is not merely destruction as violence.

The Krama theology concerns the differentiated stages of manifestation and their reabsorption into conscious fullness.

Kālī draws back the objects.

Then the limiting principles that organize their appearance.

Then even the condition of restriction.

The entire process resolves toward the supreme conscious ground.

### The Goddess who devours the jailer

Imagine the universe as a prison.

Objects are separated.

The subject is limited.

The conceptual categories become walls.

The walls have a guard.

And the guard has a supervisor.

Then Kālī arrives.

She doesn't merely remove one prisoner.

She devours the very principle that maintains the prison's apparent ultimacy.

The guard disappears.

The supervisor disappears.

The walls lose their independently ultimate status.

And the conscious ground remains.

That is an evocative way of understanding the Krama image.

**Kālī's great devouring is the withdrawal of limiting differentiation into its conscious source.**

### The Kramakelī reference

Abhinavagupta then says:

**व्याख्यातं चैतन्मया तट्टीकायामेव क्रमकेलौ विस्तरतः।**

*Vyākhyātaṃ caitan mayā taṭṭīkāyām eva Kramakelau vistarataḥ.*

"And I myself have explained this extensively in its commentary, the Kramakelī."

Now that's a historically valuable statement.

It explicitly connects the present *Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa* with Abhinavagupta's own commentary on the Krama hymn.

That commentary is not fully extant today.

So references such as this help establish its existence and doctrinal significance.

### The relationship to the Heart

Now the philosophical movement is complete.

The limiting doubt produces constricted differentiation.

The *Spandakārikā* explains ignorance as the source of spiritual depletion.

The *Sarvācāra* relates ignorance, doubt and the Śiva-nature of phonemes.

And the *Kramastotra* depicts Kālī withdrawing the very principle of limitation.

All of this is brought into the interpretation of Bhairava's Heart.

**The Heart is not another object inside the prison. It is the conscious reality in which the prison's apparent independence is overcome.**

And now Abhinavagupta turns back toward actual ritual practice.

Because the metaphysics doesn't abolish the ritual system.

It explains why ritual forms can function differently according to the practitioner's condition.

The next passage concerns the *Ṣaḍardha* scriptures and the annual *pavitraka* rite.

Which is, rather unexpectedly, a form of ritual maintenance.

The divine accountant returns one last time.


## 33. षडर्धशास्त्र - When worship is largely free of external restriction

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN PROSE · P. 236

The commentary says:

**षडर्धशास्त्रेष्वेषैव क्रिया प्रायो नियन्त्रणारहितत्वेन पूजा।**

*Ṣaḍardhaśāstreṣv eṣaiva kriyā prāyo niyantraṇārahitatvena pūjā.*

"In the Ṣaḍardha scriptures, this very activity is generally worship by virtue of its relative freedom from restrictions."

Now this is important.

The text isn't claiming that every action in every context is automatically an appropriately performed Tantric rite.

It describes the relative character of practice within the particular *Ṣaḍardha* scriptural framework.

Let's learn the Sanskrit.

### Ṣaḍardha - षडर्ध

A technical designation of the scriptural tradition.

The compound literally evokes six halves, but its historical use as a title or classificatory label should not be replaced by a naive arithmetic translation alone.

### Śāstra - शास्त्र

Scripture, authoritative teaching.

Locative plural:

**शास्त्रेषु - śāstreṣu**.

Thus:

**षडर्धशास्त्रेषु - ṣaḍardhaśāstreṣu**

Within the Ṣaḍardha scriptures.

### Eṣā eva - एषैव

This very one.

The feminine demonstrative refers to the practice or action.

### Kriyā - क्रिया

Action, ritual activity.

### Prāyaḥ - प्रायः

Usually, generally, for the most part.

Now this is an important qualification.

**Prāyaḥ** does not mean absolutely without exception.

### Niyantraṇā - नियन्त्रणा

Restriction, regulation, control.

From *ni-√yam*, to restrain.

### Rahita - रहित

Without, free from.

Thus:

**नियन्त्रणरहित - niyantraṇarahita**

Free from restriction.

### Pūjā - पूजा

Worship.

Now the statement identifies a comparatively nonrestrictive mode of worship within the tradition.

### The philosophical context

Remember the whole argument.

Constricting doubt can sustain bondage.

Ritual practices may therefore seek to loosen certain conventional prohibitions under specific initiatory conditions.

But that doesn't mean every practitioner should arbitrarily violate rules.

The text has already distinguished different levels of practice, and the editor has supplied explicit cautions.

### The distinction between freedom and compulsion

A ritual tradition may interpret freedom as a significant spiritual principle.

But that freedom is not identical with impulsivity.

And it is not merely the absence of ethical consideration.

Within the philosophical doctrine, *svātantrya* refers ultimately to the sovereign freedom of consciousness.

That cannot simply be equated with a finite person's wish to do whatever comes to mind.

### The cosmic ritual compliance office

Imagine a practitioner approaching the office.

"I've heard this tradition is relatively free of restrictions."

"Yes, in the stated context."

"Wonderful. So there are no procedures?"

"Not exactly."

"No qualifications?"

"Not exactly."

"No annual obligations?"

"Actually, there is a *pavitraka* rite."

The practitioner is devastated.

Apparently even a tradition emphasizing freedom has ritual maintenance.

And that's where the next sentence goes.

The *pavitraka*.

An act associated with completing the ritual cycle.


## 34. पवित्रकविधि - The rite that completes what was omitted

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S PROSE AND A QUOTED RITUAL VERSE · PP. 236-237

Abhinavagupta explains that because all the relevant ritual substances may not always be available, the tradition prescribes a *pavitraka* rite during the year.

The frequency is expressed through:

**चतुस्त्रिर्द्विःसकृद्वा।**

*Catus trir dviḥ sakṛd vā.*

"Four times, three times, twice or once."

Now that's a wonderfully compact series.

Let's learn it.

### Catuḥ - चतुः

Four times, in the relevant adverbial numerical usage.

### Triḥ - त्रिः

Three times.

### Dviḥ - द्विः

Twice.

### Sakṛt - सकृत्

Once.

### Vā - वा

Or.

Now the context concerns repetition during:

**संवत्सरमध्ये - saṃvatsaramadhye**

Within the year.

### Saṃvatsara - संवत्सर

Year.

### Madhya - मध्य

Middle, within.

Thus:

**संवत्सरमध्ये - saṃvatsaramadhye**

Within a year.

### Pavitraka - पवित्रक

A ritual of consecration, purification or completion, according to the historical Tantric context.

Related to:

**पवित्र - pavitra**

Pure, purifying.

Now the meaning here isn't simply taking a bath or cleaning ritual objects.

It belongs to an established ritual framework concerning the completion and correction of worship.

### Vidhi - विधि

Prescription, rule, procedure.

Thus:

**पवित्रकविधि - pavitrakav idhi** in morphological segmentation, written continuously as *pavitrakavidhi*.

### The ritual quotation

Abhinavagupta then cites a verse beginning:

**क्रियायाः पूरणार्थाय यागाज्ञापूरणाय च।**

*Kriyāyāḥ pūraṇārthāya yāgājñāpūraṇāya ca.*

"For the completion of ritual action and fulfilment of the injunction governing worship."

Now let's dismantle it.

### Kriyāyāḥ - क्रियायाः

Of action, of ritual performance.

Genitive singular.

### Pūraṇa - पूरण

Filling, completion.

From √**पॄ - pṝ**, to fill.

### Artha - अर्थ

Purpose, sake.

Dative:

**अर्थाय - arthāya**.

Thus:

**पूरणार्थाय - pūraṇārthāya**

For the sake of completion.

### Yāga - याग

Worship, sacrifice, ritual offering.

### Ājñā - आज्ञा

Command, injunction.

### Pūraṇa - पूरण

Fulfilment.

Thus:

**यागाज्ञापूरणाय - yāgājñāpūraṇāya**

For fulfilling the ritual injunction.

### The textual difficulty

The second half of the quoted verse reads awkwardly in the Kashmir Series and GRETIL:

**चतुः सकृद्वा यः कुर्यान्न पवित्रं स निष्फलः।**

*Catuḥ sakṛd vā yaḥ kuryān na pavitraṃ sa niṣphalaḥ.*

The intended warning is evidently that neglecting the required *pavitraka* completion renders the ritual fruitless or incomplete.

But the transmitted wording is syntactically compressed and possibly corrupt, especially around the negative particle and the frequency expression.

We should not claim that it is a perfectly transparent, independently established metrical instruction in every detail. ; \]

### Niṣphala - निष्फल

Fruitless, without result.

**निस् - nis**

Without.

**फल - phala**

Fruit, result.

Thus:

**निष्फल - niṣphala**

Fruitless.

Now here's the doctrinal point.

The relatively nonrestrictive ritual system still contains procedures to complete its ritual obligations.

So the earlier statement about freedom from restriction cannot be turned into a universal rejection of liturgical structure.

### The divine annual review

Imagine the practitioner completing the year's worship.

The ritual inspector arrives.

"Flowers?"

"Sometimes."

"Incense?"

"Yes."

"Other materials?"

"Not always available."

"Have you performed the appropriate completion rite?"

The practitioner pauses.

"Was that mandatory?"

"The tradition discusses it for precisely this situation."

The inspector produces a Sanskrit quotation.

The practitioner realizes that even the cosmic ritual department has an annual audit.

But the philosophy is coherent.

**Freedom from ultimate metaphysical limitation does not imply the disappearance of all conventional ritual structures.**

And now the commentator announces that this particular sequence of knowledge has been explained in detail.

That is our natural boundary.

But there is one final sentence before the next major phonemic interpretation begins.

And it concerns another kind of conventional distinction.

Birth-status.

Caste.

And whether social categories possess an intrinsic ultimate reality.

This is an important final qualification, especially given the preceding argument about ritual purity.



## 35. विज्ञानक्रमो विस्तरत उक्तः - The knowledge-sequence is complete

ABHINAVAGUPTA'S OWN PROSE · KASHMIR SERIES P. 237

**विज्ञानक्रमो विस्तरत उक्तः।**

*Vijñānakramo vistarata uktaḥ.*

"The sequence of understanding has been explained in detail."

That's a clear statement of completion.

Not the end of the *Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa*.

But the conclusion of the current *vijñānakrama*.

Let's learn it.

### Vijñāna - विज्ञान

Knowledge, understanding, discriminative cognition.

From:

**वि - vi**

Distinctly, thoroughly.

**√ज्ञा - jñā**

To know.

Thus:

**विज्ञान - vijñāna**.

### Krama - क्रम

Sequence, progression, ordered method.

### Vijñānakrama - विज्ञानक्रम

The sequence or method of understanding.

### Vistarataḥ - विस्तरतः

In detail, extensively.

Related to:

**विस्तर - vistara**

Expansion, elaboration.

### Ukta - उक्त

Explained, stated.

From √**वच् - vac**, to speak.

Masculine nominative singular:

**उक्तः - uktaḥ**.

Now this is the terminological closure.

The exposition has traced the relationship between the body, subtle powers, ritual substances, doubt and the dissolution of limiting differentiation.

And then Abhinavagupta adds a final observation about social classifications.

An important one.


## 36. जातीनां ब्राह्मणादीनां नास्ति स्थितिः - Are birth-status distinctions ultimate?

The next sentence says:

**जातीनां च ब्राह्मणादीनां नास्ति स्थितिः - कल्पितत्वात्।**

*Jātīnāṃ ca brāhmaṇādīnāṃ nāsti sthitiḥ - kalpitatvāt.*

"And the established status of classifications such as Brahmins and the rest is not ultimately grounded, because of their constructed character."

Now this is a significant extension of the previous argument.

The same issue that applied to purity classifications also concerns social categories.

Let's learn the Sanskrit.

### Jāti - जाति

Birth, kind, class, category.

Related to √**जन् - jan**, to be born.

Genitive plural:

**जातीनाम् - jātīnām**.

### Brāhmaṇa - ब्राह्मण

Brahmin.

In this context, a traditional social and ritual classification.

### Ādi - आदि

Beginning with, and so forth.

Thus:

**ब्राह्मणादीनाम् - brāhmaṇādīnām**

Of Brahmins and the other categories.

### Nāsti - नास्ति

Does not exist, is not established.

From:

**न - na**

Not.

**अस्ति - asti**

Exists.

### Sthiti - स्थिति

Standing, establishment, stable condition.

### Kalpitatvāt - कल्पितत्वात्

Because of constructedness.

**कल्पित - kalpita**

Established, arranged, conceived.

**-त्व - -tva**

Abstract condition.

Ablative singular:

**कल्पितत्वात् - kalpitatvāt**.

Because of the condition of being constructed.

### The philosophical significance

Abhinavagupta is questioning the ultimate status of divisions such as birth-based social classifications.

Within the nondual metaphysics, the conscious ground isn't divided into intrinsically separate realities according to social categories.

Now, this doesn't mean that caste distinctions had no historical force.

They plainly did.

And it doesn't establish that every historical Śaiva practitioner rejected caste restrictions in every social or ritual context.

We must distinguish:

**Ontological critique:** A social classification is not independently ultimate.

**Historical practice:** Particular communities may still enforce or maintain such classifications.

The former doesn't by itself prove the disappearance of the latter.

### The Mukuṭasaṃhitā reference

Abhinavagupta attributes a fuller treatment of these questions to the:

**मुकुटसंहिता - Mukuṭasaṃhitā**.

He says that the matter has been extensively determined there.

But the current passage does not reproduce that full argument.

So we shouldn't invent its contents.

The point is that the present discussion is intentionally abbreviated.

### The next boundary

Immediately afterward, the prose returns to:

**गुणा इच्छाद्या निर्णीताः।**

*Guṇā icchādyā nirṇītāḥ.*

"The powers or qualities beginning with Icchā have been determined."

Then the text restarts an interpretation of the fourteen vowels, Visarga and the third *brahma*.

This is a new phonemic movement.

The body-oriented *vijñānakrama* has concluded.

That is where we stop.

## 39. The final insight - The prison was constructed from distinctions within consciousness

Now here's the philosophical heart of Part XXVII.

Abhinavagupta began with the generative matrix.

He turned toward the body.

And showed how the entire embodied subject can be understood through different manifestations of Śakti.

Childhood.

Youth.

Old age.

Transmigration.

The five elements.

Vital breath.

The subtle body.

The void.

The levels of the knowing subject.

The entire structure.

Then he introduced the 112 *marmas* and described bodily existence itself as Bhairava-form.

That's an extraordinary claim.

**The body is not fundamentally outside the conscious reality that the tradition identifies as Śiva.**

But its differentiated limitations still exist at the manifest level.

And that is why the practical problem is *śaṅkā*.

Constricting doubt.

The identification with differences as though they possessed independently ultimate reality.

Now consider the relation to the ritual substances.

A particular substance may be regarded as pure in one context.

Impure in another.

An object of worship in a third.

And prohibited in a fourth.

Those distinctions can have meaningful conventional functions.

But they don't establish a second independent Absolute.

And the spiritual problem arises when conceptual classifications are mistaken for the ultimate nature of the conscious field.

### The astonishing connection with the *Spandakārikā*

The *Spandakārikā* says ignorance is like a robber.

It plunders the individual of the recognized fullness of consciousness.

And when the relevant ignorance is dissolved through Unmeṣa, the associated spiritual depletion loses its cause.

Now the *Parātrīśikāvivaraṇa* applies that logic to the constricting doubt underlying bondage.

The dissolution of limiting apprehension is the entry into the Heart.

Not the mechanical production of another independent consciousness.

### The *Sarvācāra* gives the answer

Mantras consist of phonemes.

Phonemes have Śiva as their nature.

The five-element world is a differentiated manifestation.

And the Self is the source of conscious expression.

So why treat the limiting classifications as independently ultimate?

That's the question.

### Kālī's great devouring

And finally, the *Kramastotra* describes Kālī drawing in the very power that restrains the world.

Not merely removing one object.

But withdrawing the principle of contraction itself.

It's a breathtaking metaphor.

Because the finite subject is not liberated by acquiring one more limited object.

The constricting structure of apprehension is what dissolves.

The universal conscious ground remains.

### The ultimate distinction

This does **not** require us to deny ordinary practical differences.

The body still needs food.

The senses still distinguish objects.

Substances still have physical properties.

Social actions still have consequences.

And ritual traditions still possess established procedures.

The claim concerns their ultimate relation to consciousness.

**The distinctions are manifest; their independent ultimacy is denied.**

And that is why the entire embodied world can be contemplated as Bhairava.

The body doesn't have to vanish.

The phonemes don't have to vanish.

The world doesn't have to vanish.

The conscious ground must be recognized without reducing it to any one limited manifestation.

That's the heart of Yoni-vyāpti.

And after the entire numerical, bodily and ritual exposition, Abhinavagupta has brought us back to the same fundamental teaching:

**Śiva is not somewhere else.**

The apparent distance is sustained through contracted apprehension.

And the transformation of that apprehension is the entry into the Heart.

The seed was never separate from the matrix.

The matrix was never outside Śakti.

And Śakti was never another independent reality outside Śiva.

That is the entire philosophical movement of Part XXVII.
